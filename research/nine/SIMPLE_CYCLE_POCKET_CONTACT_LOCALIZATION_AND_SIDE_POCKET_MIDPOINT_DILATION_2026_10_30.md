# Physical contacts stay on their cycle-pocket boundary, and side-pocket occupants can be dilated to the circle

Date: October 30, 2026 UTC. Author: common-certificate continuation.

**Status: complete proposed uniform geometric continuation, frozen for
separate full review.** The first theorem localizes every actual contact
of a completion square, including weak junctions and zero-stress shared
segments. The second gives a full-packing motion of all occupants of

## 1. Original selected cycle, physical spokes and stable face labels

Let actual closed unit squares have pairwise disjoint open interiors
inside one centered disk D_R. Assume R>sqrt(2), and keep an entire
simple positive body-pair component C of the same common compatible
force-and-torque selection used in [S]. Every C vertex has full positive
body degree two. There are no omitted positive branches or chords at
its vertices. All original normals, applications, shared-side means,
physical circle corners and individual zero circle weights remain.

The compatible center/application drawing of C from [P] can be made
Jordan by arbitrarily small local junction and tube routings. Protect
the center-to-corner spoke of every physical circle corner of every
core body, including a zero-load physical boundary body and both
corners of a radial body. These finite spokes are disjoint away from
their own centers and do not pass through any contact junction. The
proof in [P], Section 6, applies to each spoke: its open segment is
inside its actual body, and a containing-circle point cannot be shared.

Add the containing circle to this drawing. Call the resulting plane
graph H. An additional square E has its open interior disjoint from
the closed core union. Its center and every fixed compact subset of
its open interior consequently have a stable face label for every
sufficiently small physical routing. This is the complete weak-boundary
convention in [C], Section 2. Keeping all the spokes can subdivide an
exterior pocket; it does not identify two different pockets.

In the project window with at least three loads, [C] identifies the
coarse exterior pocket between consecutive loaded bodies A,B with
their actual intervening cycle path and the outward circle arc.
Its facing-corner crosscut is entirely occupied. A finer H face in
that pocket has only bodies on that same path as core boundary
vertices. A small cap between the two corners of one radial core
body holds no unit, as proved in [C]. Direct-contact exterior pockets
are also empty at that source's exact hypotheses.

The following property of the selected core is crucial.

**No-opposite property.** No original positive core edge can be the
opposite pair at an actual four-square junction. Moreover this remains
true after arbitrary changes of the additional squares that keep the
core fixed and preserve physical packing in the same disk.

For the initial packing this is [S], Lemma 3.1. For persistence, a
putative new opposite pair would still be a fixed point-only
vertex/vertex contact of the two original core bodies. If an endpoint
were unloaded, its original complete singleton two-edge cut is
excluded by the same generic two-port argument used in [S], Section 3.
This restriction is already a property of the unchanged positive core
edges; it is not a fresh generic choice after moving an extra.
Otherwise both endpoints are loaded physical boundary bodies. They
remain fixed. The geometric aligned-four-square lemma [S], Lemma 2.1,
forbids them as opposite members of any containing four-square block
at R>sqrt(2). Thus persistence uses no post-motion algebraic
independence or normal reselection.

## 2. One additional physical contact can be drawn without changing the core faces

**Lemma 2.1.** Let E be an additional actual square, and suppose it
touches either a core body W or another additional actual square F.
The graph H can be extended by this one physical contact edge, using
center/contact strands, without crossings and without changing the
eventual face label of any additional center. Every old core
application and every protected circle spoke is retained.

The statement adds one geometric edge at a time. It assigns that
edge no force, normal or multiplier. It does not assert planarity
of the entire graph of all zero-weight physical contacts.

### 2.1. Choice of a physical point and the open strands

Choose a point p of the actual intersection. If it is a shared
segment, choose p in its relative interior and away from the finitely
many old junctions. This is a drawing point for a new zero-stress
geometric edge, not a replacement for any old application or mean.
For a singleton contact p is the unique actual point.

The open center-to-p segment in each incident square lies in that
square's open interior. Before local routing, it can meet a strand
of a different square only at p. Existing compact strand portions
away from the finitely many junctions can be kept disjoint by making
the junction disks and parallel tubes sufficiently small. A protected
spoke has no contact endpoint at p: open spokes lie inside their
core bodies, and p cannot be a shared circle corner. All local
routings can therefore avoid every protected spoke.

### 2.2. Complete local sector extension

At most four actual squares meet at p. With two or three incident
squares the local drawing can be extended in their physical cyclic
sector order, exactly as in [P], Sections 1 and 5. At least one
incident square is additional, so the old cycle has at most two
core vertices there; its possible old local edge causes no crossing.

If four meet, they are the four aligned right-angle sectors of an
actual 2 by 2 block. By Section 1 every old supported core edge
at p joins adjacent sectors. There are at most three core sectors,
because E is additional. Their old local cycle graph is a subgraph
of the consecutive-sector path; it has no opposite edge and no
local triangle through an opposite pair. Old adjacent strands may
be drawn next to their corresponding boundary sectors. Their
relative order within any core sector is unchanged.

The new single edge is either another adjacent-sector edge or the
sole opposite-sector edge. In the latter case draw it through the
interior of the junction disk and insert its strand between the
old adjacent strands at its endpoint sector. The old adjacent
strands remain near the disk boundary. This is the noncrossing
four-sector extension in [P]; no second opposite pair is present.
If the new edge joins two additional squares, at most two core
sectors occur, and the same construction applies. In particular
the old positive core pair, if present, is adjacent, so the two
additional sectors are adjacent too.

This includes an original supported shared-side mean equal to p.
Its geometric strand is routed arbitrarily near the occupied
shared side, while the application in the force-and-torque system
stays exactly p. More than one old strand in one sector is kept
in its old noncrossing order and separated in its existing tube.
No old application moves along a segment.

These constructions extend the old local drawing, rather than
changing which side of a core strand contains an additional center.
The center and any chosen compact interior paths are outside all
sufficiently small junction disks. Thus their stable face labels
are unchanged. All other junctions are handled by the original
routing, avoiding the new open strands. This proves Lemma 2.1.

## 3. Closed contact localization, including different exterior pockets

**Theorem 3.1.** An additional square whose center has an H-face
label F can physically touch a core body only if that body's core
vertex is incident to F. Two additional squares with different
H-face labels are closed-disjoint. Their compact distance is positive.

Proof. In Lemma 2.1 the open new edge avoids H. An edge from an
interior point of F to a core vertex can end only on the boundary
of that face. An edge joining two additional centers lies entirely
in one component of the complement of H, so its centers must have
the same face label. Taking the contrapositive gives closed
disjointness, since any point of a nonempty physical intersection
would supply the geometric edge. Compactness gives positive
distance. The stable labels ensure the argument is independent of
the sufficiently small routing used for the one contact. QED.

**Coarse-pocket corollary.** In the project domain of [C], an extra
in the actual exterior pocket of the consecutive-loaded path

    A--U1--...--Uk--B

can touch among core bodies only A,U1,...,Uk,B. Extras in two
different coarse exterior pockets are closed-disjoint, as are an
exterior extra and a central extra. Subdivision by further physical
spokes or zero contacts only strengthens these statements.

This covers every shared interval, mean endpoint, singleton side
owner and four-sector weak junction. It does not forbid contacts
with the pocket's own boundary bodies, contacts among extras in
the same pocket, or contacts between nonconsecutive core bodies.

## 4. Midpoint dilation with the two actual endpoint units retained

The next elementary estimate extends the common-point dilation in
[D], Section 1, to two obstacles at exact set distance one.

Let A,U,B be an actual width-one side channel, with unchanged
applications

    p_A=(ell,h),  p_B=(ell+1,h),  p=(p_A+p_B)/2.

Its original force-and-torque relations give p_A in A, p_B in B,
and the whole segment p_A p_B in U. In particular p belongs to U,
and its distances to the two displayed actual endpoint points
are exactly one half. No replacement contact or mean is introduced.

Let E1,...,Em be any finite family of actual unit squares,
interior-disjoint from A,U,B and from one another. Keep their
orientations fixed and move their centers by

    c_i(s)=p+s(c_i-p),  s>=1.                         (4.1)

**Lemma 4.1.** This motion preserves every pairwise separation within
the moving group and every separation from A,U,B. Separations from
U and between distinct moving units become strict for s>1.
Separations from A,B need only remain weak.

Proof. Write H_i(n) for the original support halfwidth of Ei in
a unit direction n, so H_i(n)>=1/2. Choose any original separator
n from A toward Ei. Its physical support inequality gives

    n.(c_i-p) >= H_i(n)+n.(p_A-p)
               >= H_i(n)-1/2 >=0.                   (4.2)

Thus the same inequality cannot worsen under (4.1). The identical
argument with p_B treats B. This holds for arbitrary independent
frames and for every side/corner equality of the actual separator.

For U use p in U. Its separator n toward Ei gives

    n.(c_i-p)>=H_i(n)>=1/2,                         (4.3)

so the gap strictly increases when s>1. For a separator n from
Ei to Ej, the original inequality is

    n.(c_j-c_i)>=H_i(n)+H_j(n)>0.

Its left side is multiplied by s, while its right side is fixed.
This also strictly increases the gap. These original separators
prove all claimed full-set nonoverlap statements. QED.

The half-unit estimate in (4.2) is the reason this lemma applies
to a width-one channel. It is not asserted for a longer channel,
and it does not make A and B touch. In particular it supplies no
virtual center/contact triangle or new stress edge.

## 5. Full-packing side-pocket motion to a new physical circle contact

Now restore the entire original packing and the full scope of [C].
Let P be one actual exterior width-one pocket A--U--B. Apply (4.1)
to **all** additional squares whose interiors belong to P. Keep
every core square and every additional square in other regions fixed.

**Theorem 5.1.** If P is occupied, there is a physically valid
packing in the same disk, with the same entire core and every other
pocket fixed, in which at least one occupant of P touches the
containing circle. If an original occupant already touches it,
the original packing is the required packing. Otherwise a finite
motion (4.1) supplies the first such contact. Every original core
normal, force, application and mean is unchanged.

### 5.1. No additional square or nonboundary core body is forgotten

Lemma 4.1 preserves the moving-group separations and all contacts
against the three actual boundary bodies A,U,B. By Theorem 3.1,
every other fixed core body and every additional square in another
coarse region initially has positive compact distance from every
moving Ei. Consequently a nontrivial initial motion is feasible
against all those remaining physical constraints as well.

For any feasible portion of the motion, each moving center remains
in the same coarse pocket. One way to make this precise is to
choose a fixed sufficiently small routing of the core graph H.
The center of any unit interior-disjoint from a core body has
distance at least 1/2 from that closed body, because its open
incircle has radius 1/2. Take the routed core strands in a smaller
fixed neighborhood of the core union. A feasible moving center
cannot cross those strands or a protected core spoke. It cannot
cross the containing circle either. Its face label is constant.
Equivalently, its open square cannot cross the occupied crosscut
of [C]. The finer subdivision need not be tracked for this
coarse-pocket conclusion.

Suppose before a moving square reaches the circle that one of
the still-unchecked fixed bodies supplied the first physical
obstruction. At that first time the complete packing is still
feasible by continuity, and the original core is unchanged.
Section 1's no-opposite property therefore still holds. Applying
Theorem 3.1 to this actual first contact is impossible: a fixed
core body outside A,U,B is not incident to P, and a fixed
additional square has a different coarse-region label. Thus
there is no such first contact. This argument retains, rather
than ignores, all actual zero-weight contact possibilities at
every stage of the motion.

Formally, if a maximal feasible parameter interval ended before
circle contact, all unchecked pairs would still be strictly
separated at its endpoint by the preceding argument. Finitely
many compact pairs then have a positive minimum clearance there.
Lemma 4.1 preserves the checked pairs beyond it, and circle
containment is still strict. The feasible interval would extend,
a contradiction.

### 5.2. A finite first circle contact exists

Initially all moving squares are strictly circle-clear in the
nontrivial case. Since p belongs to U, no moving center equals
p; in fact (4.3) implies |c_i-p|>=1/2. Their centers are unbounded
as s tends to infinity. There is consequently a first finite
parameter s_*>1 at which a moving corner reaches the circle.
Until that parameter all its corners remain inside the disk.
The full nonoverlap continuation in Section 5.1 holds up to and
including s_*, including every weak equality at the endpoint.
This proves the theorem.

The conclusion is a geometric packing existence statement. It
does not assert preservation of the original secondary minimum
of Phi, introduce a different canonical selector, or transfer a
canonical-only closure to the new packing. Pure geometric
obstructions for the resulting physical packing do pull back
to the original one. The core itself has not moved and its
original complete equations remain the same throughout.

## 7. Complete six-load radial-incident side-channel caller

Retain all nine actual units and an entire no-long simple C7

### 7.1. Exactly one central extra and one exterior extra

The whole central free union has capacity one by [N]. Every
direct exterior pocket is empty. The radial-incident side

additional units are exactly one central T and one exterior E

    E can touch among core bodies only A,U,B,
    E intersect T = empty,  dist(E,T)>0.             (7.1)

Every possible E contact with A,U,B, every T/core contact and
every nonconsecutive core contact remains physical. Theorem
5.1 can make E a physical boundary square while keeping
T and the entire core fixed. This is still only a geometric
motion, not an allowed replacement of the original Phi minimum.

### 7.2. U and T are the two strict critical-near units

All six loaded bodies are physical boundary units and have
distance greater than delta in (6.2). The complete actual
channel-cap theorem gives E in its half-plane of height h.
The whole side-channel theorem [G], Section 2, gives h>3/10
because the same nine units have R>2 by [N]. In the present
radial-incident caller the stronger h>1/2 follows from [R].
In particular dist(O,E)>delta.

There are therefore seven distinct original units with distance
greater than delta. Theorem D of [I], with its current complete

    dist(O,U)<delta,  dist(O,T)<delta.               (7.2)

These are the original U and T; no motion is used to infer
(7.2). They are physically circle-clear, since every physical
boundary unit has the larger distance bound just used. Thus
the original core has exactly six physical boundary bodies.

### 7.3. Full radial-incident chart and strict occupied-height bound

Use A's actual radial frame, reflecting the channel if needed:

    A=[d,d+1]e+[-1/2,1/2]f, f=Je,
    d=sqrt(R^2-1/4)-1, phi=atan(1/(2d)),
    p_A=d e+f/2,
    g=-c e+s f, N=s e+c f,
    c,s>0, c^2+s^2=1.

The complete support cover [R], Section 1, gives these actual
original data and makes p_A a strict relative-interior point
of U's side. The actual side-channel coordinates are

    ell=p_A.g=-dc+s/2,
    h=p_A.N=ds+c/2>1/2,
    p_B=p_A+g,
    U=[ell,ell+1]g+[b-1/2,b+1/2]N,
    |b-h|<1/2.                                     (7.3)

Occupancy implies the strict height restriction

    h<d.                                           (7.4)

To prove it, the full containing-unit support theorem [H],
Section 1, gives h<=d for E. If h=d, E is the unique
radial cap unit `[d,d+1]N+[-1/2,1/2]g`. The original
radial application has norm squared d^2+1/4, so ell^2=1/4.
The strict c>0 gives ell=-dc+s/2<1/2, hence ell=-1/2.
U and E then have the same longitudinal interval [-1/2,1/2].
The strict side-interior condition |b-h|<1/2 puts open U
points above height d. They overlap E's open interior, a
contradiction. This proves (7.4), including the only possible
support equality and the actual original U mean.

Write c=cos(theta), s=sin(theta), 0<theta<pi/2.
Since h=sqrt(d^2+1/4)*sin(theta+phi) and
d=sqrt(d^2+1/4)*cos(phi), (7.4) implies

    0<theta<pi/2-2phi,
    c>sin(2phi),  s<cos(2phi),
    -d<ell<-1/2,
    1/2-d<ell+1/2<0.                               (7.5)

For the angular implication, on the complementary interval
`pi/2-2phi<=theta<pi/2`, sin(theta+phi) is at least
cos(phi). The strict opposite inequality therefore gives the
displayed interval. Then ell=-sqrt(d^2+1/4)*cos(theta+phi)
gives (7.5), with its strict lower bound also following from
ell(0)=-d and ell'(theta)=h>0.

Finally b>h-1/2>0. Every point of U has N-coordinate
at least b-1/2. Combining this with (7.2) gives the uniform
strict transverse bound

    0<b<1/2+delta<93/128.                           (7.6)

If b<=1/2 it is immediate; otherwise dist(O,U)>=b-1/2
and the same conclusion follows. These are full-domain
necessary inequalities, not a sampled interval or a declaration
that the remaining U/T/loaded-return constraints are impossible.
