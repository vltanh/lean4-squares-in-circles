## 1. Complete B mask, owner and proper-concurrence alternatives

Let \(\rho=|F|\). Since \(0<\delta<a<1\),
\[
\rho^2=\delta^2+(1-a)^2<1<d^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Every point of a physical radial unit has norm at least d, so B cannot
be radial. It is physical unique. A strict exposed vertex of a unique
boundary square, other than its opposite-to-circle vertex, has norm
at least \(\sqrt{d^2+1/4}>d\), or is its unshareable circle corner.
Thus the complete actual direct feature is either a B-owned side axis
with its original full mean, or a strict opposite vertex. Owner labels
are decided before positions; a side mean at a vertex stays in the
axis alternative.

The two outgoing B normals are -m and -g. If they are parallel then,
since \(m_y,g_y<0\), they are equal. For a strict supporting vertex
both B applications are F, so the actual segment from Q to F has
Q=F-m. Just before F it lies strictly inside A, because -m has two
positive coordinates and F is A's southwest corner. That segment lies
in V. An interior point of A in V forces overlap of open interiors,
even if the original transverse V mean is at its side endpoint.

If instead B's original support is a side axis, the parallel pair makes
its unique circle resultant parallel that own axis. A physical unique
circle corner has both absolute own coordinates greater than1/2 and
cannot lie on an own axis through the origin. This is also impossible.
Hence both actual loaded force lines are nonparallel. The accepted
proper unique concurrence applies without changing the degree of A:

\[
q_B=\tau z_B=F+\lambda m,\qquad 0<\tau\le1,\quad\lambda\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]

## 2. The entire q>=2 domain has an actual B point too close to Q

For either original direct feature the point \(W=F+m\) belongs to B.
For a B-axis support it is the point on its opposite parallel side
with the same actual transverse coordinate. For a strict opposite
vertex, m has two positive components in B's inward own axes, each
at most one, so moving by the unit vector m remains in B.

Suppose \(q\ge2\). Put \(h=K/M,j=b/M\). From (1),
\[
s<9/50,\quad c>49/50,\quad K>119/60,
\quad h>8/9,\quad j<19/42.
\tag{9}
\]
The last two comparisons use \(b<1\), so
\(h>119/\sqrt{17761}\) and \(j<60/\sqrt{17761}\).
Their positive-square reserves are
\(119^2\,81-64\,17761=10337\) and
\((19/42)^2-3600/17761=61321/31330404\).

Write \(W-Q=(A_0,-B_0)\). Then
\[
A_0=\delta+dc+s/2-h,\qquad
B_0=b+ds-c/2+j=1-(q-d)s-c/2+j.
\]
Both are positive: \(s/2>1-c\) gives \(A_0>0\), while
\(b>16/25\) gives \(B_0>16/25-1/2>0\). Since \(q>d\),
\[
A_0<1+1/30+9/100-8/9=211/900,

\[

## 3. A strict opposite vertex cannot have q<=1/3

\(a^2<1/90<1/81\), so \(b>8/9\). Write
\(z_B=(z_x,-B_0)\), where (8) implies \(B_0>0\). In (8),
\(m=(-h,-j)\), \(h/j=K/b<q/b\), and \(\delta<q\).

\[
\frac{|z_x|}{B_0}
\le\frac{\delta+\lambda h}{b+\lambda j}

\tag{12}
\]
This includes either possible sign of the actual z_x. Hence
\[
B_0^2>\frac{64}{73}R^2>\frac{272}{73}>(191/100)^2.

Using \(R<21/10\),
\[
F\cdot z_B=\delta z_x+bB_0

## 4. The exact owned-axis chart and its full small-q exclusion

Suppose B owns the original direct normal. Put \(v=(b,-K)/M\),
the actual perpendicular axis. Then
\[
X=F\cdot m=\frac{b^2-\delta K}{M}
 =\frac{1-2a+q\delta}{M}>0,
\quad T=F\cdot v=\frac{bq}{M}>0.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed \(\delta K<\delta q<a^2\), so its numerator exceeds
\(b^2-a^2=1-2a>7/25\). Properness (8) fixes the positive
transverse circle side. For the actual B, exactly
\[
B=[X,X+1]m+[Y-1,Y]v,
\quad Y=\sqrt{R^2-(X+1)^2}>1/2,
\quad T\in[Y-1,Y],
\]
\[
                         (X+1)T\ge XY.
\tag{16}
\]
The last inequality is the actual nonnegative direct proper length,
with \(\tau=T/Y\). It retains equality and the full mean interval.
Since \(X^2+T^2=\rho^2\), (16) gives the necessary scalar bound
\[
R\le\rho+\sqrt{1+(T/X)^2}.
\tag{17}
\]

If \(q\le1/3\), then \(a<1/8\), \(\delta<a\), and
\[
\rho<\sqrt{(1-a)^2+a^2},\qquad
\frac TX=\frac{bq}{b^2-\delta K}
 <\frac{1-a}{3(1-2a)}=:r(a).
\]
The resulting upper bound for the right side of (17) is
\[
f(a)=\sqrt{1-2a+2a^2}+\sqrt{1+r(a)^2},\quad0\le a\le1/8.
\]
Its first derivative term is at most -3/4. The second is at most
\(r(a)r'(a)\le(7/18)(16/27)=56/243\). Therefore
\(f'<-505/972<0\) on the whole interval. At its sole maximum,
\[
f(0)=1+\sqrt{10}/3<37/18<\sqrt{17}/2<R.
\tag{18}
\]
The strict square comparisons use \(10<(19/6)^2\) and
\(17/4-(37/18)^2=2/81\). This contradicts (17), proving q>1/3
also for every actual owned mean.

## 5. One convex comparison excludes the entire axis tilt s>=1/4

Suppose \(s\ge1/4\) in the actual axis chart. Then
\[
1/3<q<\frac{4+\sqrt{15}}{15}<8/15,
\quad a^2<4/225,\quad b>13/15,
\quad b\le1-q/4.
\tag{19}
\]
The bound on q follows from \(\delta/q=1-c\) and
\(c\le\sqrt{15}/4\). The others follow from (1).
In (17), \(\delta K<q/60\) and the function
\(bq/(b^2-q/60)\) decreases in positive b on this domain.
Consequently
\[
R<f(q):=
\sqrt{1/3600+(1-q/4)^2}
 +\sqrt{1+r(q)^2},
\]
\[
r(q)=\frac{(13/15)q}{169/225-q/60},\qquad1/3\le q\le8/15.
\tag{20}
\]
The denominator is positive. The first term of f is the norm of an
affine vector and hence convex. The positive rational r has positive
first and second derivatives. Composition with the increasing convex
function \(r\mapsto\sqrt{1+r^2}\) preserves convexity. Thus f is
bounded above by the larger endpoint value on this entire interval.

At q=1/3, the two radicals are less than23/25 and27/25; here
r=260/671. At q=8/15, they are less than87/100 and59/50; here
r=104/167. The four positive square reserves are respectively
\[
\frac{263}{45000},\quad
\frac{4575064}{281400625},\quad
\frac{31}{5625},\quad
\frac{319109}{69722500}.
\]
Therefore \(f(q)<41/20<\sqrt{17}/2<R\), with final squared
reserve19/400. This contradicts (20), proving (6) for every original
axis mean, including all permitted support and proper-length endpoints.
