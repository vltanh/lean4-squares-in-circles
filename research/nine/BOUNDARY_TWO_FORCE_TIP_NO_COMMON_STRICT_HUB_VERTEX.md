## 2. An exact full-shared-side boundary lemma

We first prove a separate local fact used below.

Suppose two actual physical boundary units V,W share one entire side. V has exactly two original positive pair forces, one toward W and the other toward an actual unit U. The actual V--U application P is an endpoint of their full shared side. Then these original data are impossible for R>sqrt(2), even without a positive circle-load premise at V.

Rotate about the unchanged circle origin to put the shared side horizontal. After reflecting vertically if necessary, their exact parallel frames are

    V=[a,a+1] times [b,b+1],
    W=[a,a+1] times [b-1,b].

Let X=max(a^2,(a+1)^2). Since both units are contained and physically touch the circle, their farthest-corner norms agree:

    X+max(b^2,(b+1)^2)
      =R^2
      =X+max((b-1)^2,b^2).

This equality forces b=0. If b>0, V's upper vertical endpoint has strictly larger absolute height than either W endpoint; if b<0 the reverse comparison applies. Hence

    V=[a,a+1] times [0,1],
    W=[a,a+1] times [-1,0],
    max(a^2,(a+1)^2)=R^2-1>1.                      (1)

The entire actual shared interval [a,a+1] consequently has one strict sign: if it met zero, both endpoint absolute values would be at most one, contradicting (1).

The unchanged endpoint P is (p,0), p=a or a+1. Write the original VW mean as M=(m,0), where the full interval a<=m<=a+1 is retained. Thus p and m have the same strict sign. The actual outward V-to-W normal is South.

Let n be the original U-to-V normal at P, directed inward into V, and let the positive UV and VW magnitudes be mu,nu. At either bottom V corner, every actual inward support normal has

    n_y>=0.

The full true V2 torque about the original circle origin, including all its radial circle atoms, is exactly

    -mu*det(P,n)+nu*det(M,South)=0,
    -mu*p*n_y-nu*m=0.                              (2)

If p,m>0, the left side of (2) is strictly negative. If p,m<0, it is strictly positive. The factor n_y may be zero; the unchanged positive nu and nonzero m make the total strict in both cases. This contradiction proves the full-shared-side boundary lemma.

The proof includes every original VW side mean and both endpoints, every original normal at P and all physical V/W circle masks. It uses no force equation at W and no selected circle-corner weight.

### 3.3. One unit has a side-interior point at P

Suppose P is a vertex of one of V,W and is in the relative interior of a side of the other. The tangent sector of the latter is a half-plane, while the former has an adjacent right-angle sector on the other side of the same supporting line. Their open sectors occupy a total angle of 3*pi/2. The actual U has P as a vertex and its inward open sector has angle pi/2. To avoid both other open sectors, it must be exactly the remaining right-angle sector. Its two own axes are therefore parallel to the axes of V and W.

For an explicit local coordinate check, place P at the local origin solely for this tangent calculation, put the shared line on y=0, and take V's local sector to be x>0,y>0 while W has the lower open half-plane. U's only possible sector is x<0,y>0. The normal toward the side-interior W is then South, an own axis of U. The other labelling is identical after swapping V and W. This local description neither translates the actual containing circle in any metric argument nor changes a retained normal.

But Section 1 assumes that both original U-outward normals strictly expose a U vertex and are not own axes. At the side-interior neighbor the separating normal is uniquely the normal of that side, now proved to be an own U axis. This is a contradiction.

Hence the entire partial shared-side feature is excluded, including an original VW mean anywhere along the shared segment. No endpoint mean is treated as a strict vertex normal.

### 3.4. Both V and W have vertices at P
