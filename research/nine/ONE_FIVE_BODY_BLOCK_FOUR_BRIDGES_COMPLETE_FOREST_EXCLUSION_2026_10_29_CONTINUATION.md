## 2. The complete bridge forest and its literal degrees

### 2.1. The outside graph is a rooted forest with four vertices

Every edge outside Q is a bridge. Consequently every component of
G minus V(Q) is a tree, joined to Q by exactly one bridge. Two distinct
attachments of one such component to Q would put its joining edges on
a cycle: use its internal tree path and a path in the connected Q,
or the same block vertex when the two attachments share their root.
That would contradict those edges being bridges.

Call each such outside tree together with its joining edge a branch,
and call its unique adjacent Q vertex its block root. Its number of
edges, including the joining edge, equals its number of outside
vertices. The sum of these positive branch sizes is exactly four.
Different branches have disjoint outside vertices. Their block roots
may coincide when permitted by the exact selection bound below.
