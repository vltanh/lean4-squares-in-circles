## 2. A complete radius-free quadrant theorem for the actual HC normal

The actual H has maximum x coordinate zero uniquely at P_H, and its
unchanged pose is

    H=P_H+[0,1](-a,-b)+[0,1](-b,a),
    a,b>0, a^2+b^2=1.                                  (5)

In particular H lies in x<=0 and has maximum y coordinate t+a<t+1<2.
Any actual H--C contact point S has

    S_x<0, S_y>=1.                                      (6)

Strictness of S_x follows because H's only point with x=0 is P_H,
whose height t is less than one. Let N be the unchanged original
unit normal from H into C. We prove

    N_x>=0, N_y>0.                                      (7)

### 2.1. Strict positivity of N_y

The actual supporting half-planes at S give

    N.P_H<=N.S<=N.P_C,
    s*N_x+(1-t)*N_y>=0.                                (8)

If N_y<0, (8) forces N_x>0. In the H pose (5), the dot product
of N with the upward axis (-b,a) is then strictly negative.
Every maximum-N support point of H consequently has zero coefficient
on that axis, and has height at most t. This contradicts S_y>=1.

If N_y=0, consider the two horizontal directions. For N=East, its
unique H support point is P_H, again too low. For N=West, the actual
separating line would equate the minimum x coordinate of H, which is
negative, with the maximum x coordinate of C, which is at least s>=0.
That is impossible. This proves N_y>0, with no force equation at H
or C used.

### 2.2. Nonnegativity of N_x at every C frame and feature

If C is not aligned, its minimum-y face is the vertex P_C and its
whole actual pose is

    C=P_C+[0,1]u+[0,1]v,
    u=(c,d), v=(-d,c), c,d>0, c^2+d^2=1.                (9)

The bottom vertex P_C cannot be S because its x coordinate s>=0.
The right vertex P_C+u has strictly positive x coordinate and also
cannot be S. At the upper vertex P_C+u+v, both incoming normal-cone
generators -u and -v have negative y component, contrary to N_y>0.
At the left vertex P_C+v, its incoming cone is generated by u and -v,
both with strictly positive x component. Thus a contact there has
N_x>0.

For the sides, the lower-right side P_C--P_C+u has nonnegative x
throughout and cannot contain S. The lower-left side P_C--P_C+v has
incoming normal u, whose x component is positive. The two upper sides
have incoming normals -v and -u, each with negative y component,
already excluded by Section 2.1. This exhausts every tilted vertex,
side and closed normal-cone endpoint.

If C is aligned, write its complete actual pose as

    C=[r,r+1] times [1,2], r<=s<=r+1.                   (10)

The right side has x=r+1>=s>=0 and cannot contain S. The top side
and its vertices have y=2, beyond the strict maximum y of H. The
remaining bottom side, left side and lower-left vertex have incoming
normal cones with nonnegative x component. All original bottom-side
means and both closed endpoints in (10) are retained. This proves
N_x>=0 also in every aligned case, completing (7).

## 4. Exact original-origin inequalities from E2 and whole supports

Let the original circle center have local D coordinates O=(xi,zeta),
and put k=1-xi. Let

    d_R=sqrt(R^2-1/4)-1<61/60.

in every direction. Thus every actual minimum support height about

    det(P_E-O,n_E)<0,
    -q*k+p*zeta<0.                                     (14)

    a_E=n_E.(P_E-O)=p*k+q*zeta<=d_R.

    a_E>zeta/q,
    zeta<q*d_R<d_R.                                    (15)

The last strict comparison uses 0<q<1 and d_R>0. No sign of zeta
was assumed. C's actual North support gives 1-zeta<=d_R, so altogether

    1-d_R<=zeta<d_R.                                   (16)

These are bounds about the original circle origin, not a local chart
translation assertion. They cover arbitrary original E support owners.

## 8. The auxiliary E top cannot lie half a unit above the original origin

Let Y=M-zeta be the actual top height of E about O. We prove

    Y<1/2.                                               (26)

    1/2<=Y<d_R<61/60.

its center height about O lies in the strict interval

    -1/4<(c_E)_y<31/60.                                 (27)

upper bound from Y<61/60.

O. If (c_E)_x<=0, its distance from this point, which is at most the

    |c_E|^2<1/2+(31/60)^2=2761/3600<25/16,

contrary to the strict boundary-center bound |c_E|>5/4. Consequently
(c_E)_x>0. If its y coordinate is negative, (27) and |c_E|>5/4
give sin(arg(c_E))>-1/5>-1/2. Hence in all cases

    -pi/6<arg(c_E)<pi/2.                                (28)

Use the actual representatives in (23), (28). Their difference lies
strictly between -2*pi/3 and pi/2. The positive counterclockwise
advance less than 2*pi from C to E is therefore either less than
pi/2 or greater than 4*pi/3. Equality of the representatives would
give no permissible distinct endpoint lift. Both alternatives contradict
the actual three-contact interval (13), because

    pi/2<3*pi/5, 4*pi/3>6*pi/5.

Thus the entire supposition Y>=1/2 is impossible. This proves (26),
including its closed equality endpoint, without an ownership or force
