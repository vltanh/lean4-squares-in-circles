### 1.2. A force-free shared-circle-point fact

Two contained unit squares with disjoint open interiors cannot share a point p on the containing circle. A circle point of a square must be one of its vertices, by strict convexity of the disk. If u,v are its two perpendicular inward unit side directions there, containment of p+u and p+v gives

    p.u<=-1/2, p.v<=-1/2.

Thus its full closed right-angle tangent sector lies strictly inside the inward tangent half-plane p.w<0 away from zero. Two right-angle sectors with disjoint angular interiors would have total angle pi, while both are contained in one open half-plane with strict angular margins. This is impossible. Their angular interiors overlap, giving actual open-interior overlap near p. This proves the fact without a force or load assumption on either unit.

This is the physical justification for excluding shared U/W or V/W points as W circle corners in Appendix A. No U boundary-load inference is needed.
