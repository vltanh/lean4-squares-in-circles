## 3. Two strict hub vertices must coincide, where the three units overlap

Suppose H is nonparallel to both actual roots. Its normal at
AH is then not an H side axis, so Q_A is a strict exposed H
vertex. Likewise Q_C is a strict exposed H vertex. Distinct
vertices of a unit square are at distance at least one. But
(3)--(4) give

    |Q_A-Q_C|<=|Q_A|+|Q_C|<2/5<1.

Hence the two original physical contacts are the same actual
H vertex:

    Q_A=Q_C=Q.                                       (5)

At A the point Q is in the relative interior of its owned +u
side. Here is the full endpoint check. Its actual side parameter
is sigma b, with

    0<=sigma b<d b<11/60<1.

Thus the far endpoint is impossible. If sigma b=0, Q would
be an A vertex as well as an H vertex. Because H is nonparallel
to A their physical intersection is a singleton, and the
original compatible strict point-normal convention forbids
the selected common normal from being the A side axis +u at
such a vertex/vertex contact. This is the same original
point-normal convention retained in the tilted local feature
cover, not a new selection after extraction. Therefore
0<sigma b<1, so Q is genuinely in the A side interior.
The same argument places Q in the C side interior.

In a sufficiently small neighborhood of Q, each of the actual
units A and C occupies a full half-plane on the inward side
of its own side. If these two inward half-planes are not
opposite, their open angular intersection gives interior
overlap of A and C arbitrarily near Q. Interior disjointness
therefore requires them to be opposite. Their actual union
then covers a full neighborhood of Q except for its single
common side line.

The actual unit H has vertex Q and a two-dimensional inward
right-angle sector there. Its interior points approach Q and
cannot all lie on that one line. In the same neighborhood
some such points lie in int A or int C. This contradicts
the original pairwise disjoint open interiors. All three
original squares stay fixed throughout this local argument.

Thus the complete two-strict-H-vertex domain is impossible.
