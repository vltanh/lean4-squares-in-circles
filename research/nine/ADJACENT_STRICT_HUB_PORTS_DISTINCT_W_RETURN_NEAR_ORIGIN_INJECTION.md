## 1. Radius-free strict-three-vertex force lemma

Let an actual unit square U have zero total circle force and exactly three original positive pair forces. In an actual own frame write

    U=[x-1,x] x [p-1,p].

Suppose two unchanged applications and their original outward unit normals are

    P=(x,p), n=(a,b),
    Q=(x,p-1), m=(c,-d),

    a,b,c,d>0, a^2+b^2=c^2+d^2=1.

Assume the third original outward normal is not an own U side axis. Its support on U is therefore a strictly exposed vertex. No owner, shape, degree, circle contact or force equation of the three neighboring bodies is needed for this lemma.

Let mu,nu,lambda>0 be the original force magnitudes at P,Q,S. The full U3 force and torque imply exactly one of the following two alternatives:

    S=(x-1,p),     n_T=(-e,f), e,f>0,
    mu*b=nu*(d-c), so d>c;                           (1)

or

    S=(x-1,p-1),   n_T=(-e,-f), e,f>0,
    nu*d=mu*(b-a), so b>a.                           (2)

Here e^2+f^2=1 because n_T is the original unit normal.

**Proof.** The first two force normals have strictly positive horizontal components. Their positive sum can be balanced only by a third normal with negative horizontal component. Since that normal is not a U axis, its vertical component is also nonzero. Thus its actual strict support is one of the two left vertices.

If S is the upper-left vertex, take the full torque about P. The UW contribution is nu*c and the third contribution is -lambda*f. Hence lambda*f=nu*c. Substitution into the vertical force equation

    mu*b-nu*d+lambda*f=0

gives (1), with strictness from mu,b,nu>0.

If S is the lower-left vertex, take the full torque about Q. The UV contribution is -mu*a and the third contribution is lambda*f. Hence lambda*f=mu*a. The vertical force equation is

    mu*b-nu*d-lambda*f=0,

which gives (2), with strictness from nu,d,mu>0. These alternatives exhaust the actual strict support. No normal or application has been moved. This proves the lemma.

Put

    K=ad+bc>0, G=ac-bd.

In either alternative,

    ac<K, G<K.                                      (3)

Indeed d>c implies ad>ac, while b>a implies bc>ac; in either case the remaining summand of K is positive. Subtracting bd>0 gives the second strict inequality.

For comparison with the concurrence formulation, the first two actual force lines meet at

    I=P-(ac/K,bc/K).

Since 0<bc/K<1, the two alternatives put I strictly to the right of U's left side. Equation (3) is precisely that horizontal conclusion. The direct torque proof above additionally identifies which incoming normal has a component exceeding its other component.

The lemma is radius-free. It has no boundary-contact, circle-load, simple-cycle, endpoint-properness or selected-circle-corner premise.
