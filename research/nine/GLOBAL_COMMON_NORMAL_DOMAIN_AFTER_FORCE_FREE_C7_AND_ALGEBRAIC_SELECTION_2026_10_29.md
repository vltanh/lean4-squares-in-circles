## 8. A single generic choice for all proper algebraic conditions

Let K0 be the field generated over Q by the coordinates of the fixed
circle center, radius, nine centers and side axes, and any fixed
physical constants in the retained prelists. Finitely many actual
coordinates are involved. Their rational-function field is countable:
its elements are values of the countably many finite rational
expressions in those coordinates. Adjoining their real algebraic
closure remains countable, since there are countably many polynomials
and each nonzero one has finitely many real roots. Call that field K.

This includes all algebraic quantities computed from fixed geometry,
such as epsilon, actual fixed support points, circle corners, side
directions and every uniquely geometry-determined algebraic scalar.
Unknown force magnitudes, circle-weight splits and variable shared
means are not adjoined as if fixed in advance. If a particular source
uses another fixed physical real coefficient, finitely or countably
many such specified coefficients can first be adjoined without changing
the countability proof. None may depend on the later chosen stress.

For each independent interval choose a rational unit-circle chart in
a physical orthonormal frame:

    n(t) = ((1-t^2)/(1+t^2))*u + (2t/(1+t^2))*v.

The relevant strict normal arc can be covered by one such chart
without its missing pole, after a fixed change of signs or quarter
turn in the physical frame. Its inverse image is a nonempty open
real interval. At each four-unit junction use 0<t<1 and

    a(t)=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2), b(t)=2t/(1+t^2)

for both matched normals in Section 3. Thus one t, not two, occurs.
The inequalities a>b or b>a simply shorten this same open interval.
Let J1 x ... x Jk be the inverse image of Omega in these genuine
parameters. It is a nonempty open product.

**Lemma 8.1.** Choose t1,...,tk in the prescribed intervals so that
they are algebraically independent over K.

*Proof.* For t1, the elements algebraic over countable K form a
countable set, whereas every nonempty real open interval is
uncountable. Choose t1 in J1 outside that set. After t1,...,t_(j-1)
have been chosen, the field K(t1,...,t_(j-1)) is still countable,
being the values of countably many finite rational expressions.
Choose tj in Jj transcendental over that field. Induction gives
algebraic independence of the resulting finite tuple. More explicitly,
a nonzero polynomial relation in the first j parameters, viewed as a
polynomial in tj, has a nonzero coefficient after substituting the
first j-1 parameters by the induction hypothesis, contradicting this
transcendence. If k=0 there is no choice and the assertion is vacuous.
QED.

Every normal component is now a rational function of the genuine t's
with coefficients in K and positive denominator 1+t^2. Therefore
any polynomial condition in the normal components, after the actual
matching substitutions and clearing positive denominators, becomes
a polynomial F in K[t1,...,tk]. If F is nonzero, Lemma 8.1 gives

    F(t1,...,tk) != 0.

This avoids all proper algebraic conditions at once, rather than
choosing separate normals for each eventual graph. Several conditions
may share the same parameter. They need not be independent conditions;
only their correctly substituted polynomials must be nonzero. The
fixed physical geometry may be special, so nonidentity is tested after
the actual physical coefficients and matching relations are imposed.

The same statement applies to rational factors on their legitimate
nonzero-denominator branches. A denominator that vanishes identically
is a separate geometric branch and is never divided out. The theorem
does not turn an identically zero factor into a proper avoidance.

**Corollary 8.2 (elementary finite version).** For the actual finite
collection of current physical prelists, algebraic independence is
optional. Multiply their nonzero polynomial numerators, together with
every legitimate nonzero denominator that must be avoided, to obtain
one nonzero polynomial F. A polynomial ring over a field has no zero
divisors, so this finite product is nonzero. A nonzero real polynomial
cannot vanish on an entire nonempty open box: in one variable it has
finitely many roots; induction in the number of variables first
chooses the other coordinates so one nonzero coefficient remains,
then chooses the last coordinate away from finitely many roots.
Apply this to J1 x ... x Jk. A point with F!=0 simultaneously
meets every current proper avoidance.

Thus the complete finite proof needs only polynomial arithmetic and
the already constructed open product. Lemma 8.1 is a stronger single
choice useful for a countable family of specified algebraic prelists;
it is not needed to make any current finite case-classification
certificate exist. Neither version chooses an unknown stress mean.
