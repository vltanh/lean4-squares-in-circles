## 3. A physical support-coordinate bound, including every owned mean

If P_0 minimizes n=(r,-t), r,t>0, r^2+t^2=1, on a unit S, then

\[
                     \min_{Z\in S}Z_x\ge(P_0)_x-t.
\tag{7}
\]

At a strict minimum vertex choose its two inward orthogonal unit
axes, each with positive scalar product with n. At most one can
have negative x component. Such an axis must have the form
(-a,-b), a,b>0, because n dot axis is positive. The inequality
-ra+tb>0, together with a^2+b^2=1, gives a<t. Hence the whole
unit's leftward extension is strictly below t. If no axis has
negative x component the assertion is immediate.

At an owned minimum side, retain the full mean eta in [0,1]. With
j=(t,r), the actual unit is

\[
                    S=P_0-\eta j+[0,1]n+[0,1]j.
\tag{8}
\]

Its minimum x coordinate is (P_0)_x-eta t. This proves (7), including
both side endpoints and every feature where the minimizing normal
is an actual side axis.

## 4. All B contacts when V is oblique

Suppose first that V is oblique to X. Its unique minimum-x vertex
is the actual R_0. Write its actual inward axes as

\[
 f=(a,b),\qquad j=(b,-a),\qquad a,b>0,\quad a^2+b^2=1,
 \qquad V=R_0+[0,1]f+[0,1]j.
\tag{9}
\]

The lower vertex R_0+j and the near side R_0+[0,1]j have height at
most zero and cannot meet B. The endpoint R_0 is excluded as well:
B's only possible zero-height point has first coordinate p<1.
The far vertex R_0+f+j has first coordinate 1+a+b>2, beyond B.

The far side R_0+f+[0,1]j has outward normal f. Since f dot u>0,
every minimum-f point of B has u coordinate zero and first coordinate
at most p<1. It cannot lie on that V side. The other far side has
outward normal j. Since j dot v=-bs-ac<0, every minimum-j point
of B has v coordinate one, and its first coordinate is at most
p-s+c<=c<1. This excludes that whole side mean too.

Therefore the actual B application on V is

\[
                          Q=R_0+\xi f,\qquad0<\xi\le1.
\tag{10}
\]

Its V outward normal has positive y component. Its x component
must be negative: otherwise every minimum point on B has u
coordinate zero and first coordinate at most p<1. Write its whole
actual force as (-beta,gamma), beta,gamma>0. Its positive scalar
product with B's v axis shows that

\[
 Q=P+\tau u,\quad0<\tau\le1,
                \qquad\frac\gamma\beta\le\frac cs.
\tag{11}
\]

The weak ratio is the actual B-owned side-normal case; tau is never
reset. Comparing (10),(11) yields

\[
 \begin{split}
 \xi a&\le s+\tau c-1,\\
 \xi b&\ge\tau s,\\
 \xi(a-b)&\le s-1+\tau(c-s)<0.
 \end{split}
\tag{12}
\]

The final strict inequality follows by considering c>=s and c<s;
the respective upper bounds are c-1 and s-1. Thus a<b.

If the actual V-to-B normal is its owned axis -j, retain its complete
mean xi. With force mu(-j), mu>0, full V3 makes the third force
lambda e_x+mu j. Both its own-axis components are positive, so its
V application is R_0+f+j. The unchanged V moment about R_0 is

\[
                    \mu(\xi-1)=\lambda(b-a)>0,
\tag{13}
\]

which is impossible. This includes xi=1. Hence the surviving actual
B application is the strict upper vertex R_0+f, and

\[
       b\gamma-a\beta>0,\qquad
       a\le c+s-1\le\sqrt2-1<5/12,\qquad b>9/10.
\tag{14}
\]

These conclusions use the whole V3, not a false two-force equation.

## 7. The actual Y must be below the original X

Since Y contains a point of A with x<=0 and a point of C with x>1,
its horizontal projection contains [0,1] and has length strictly
greater than one. In particular Y is oblique to X.

The separating-axis calculation in Section 4 of
`UNIT_BYPASS_NORMAL_CHANNEL.md` only uses that a unit's projection
contains the corresponding complete projection interval of another
unit. It does not require a force equation at the projecting unit.
Apply that physical calculation to Y and X. Since their interiors
are disjoint, their vertical projections are separated:

\[
                         Y\subseteq\{y\ge0\}
                   \quad\hbox{or}\quad Y\subseteq\{y\le-1\}.
\tag{24}
\]

The first alternative is impossible. A's actual maximum-x support
is x=0, so its center has first coordinate at most -1/2. Its center
has second coordinate at most 1/2 as well. Indeed, for oblique A
its unique rightmost vertex is E=(0,z), and its inward axes may be
written (-a_A,b_A),(-b_A,-a_A), with a_A,b_A>0. Its center height
is z+(b_A-a_A)/2<=1/2. For parallel A write
A=[-1,0] times [a_0-1,a_0]; its actual X mean gives a_0-1<=z<=0,
so a_0<=1 and its center height is also at most 1/2.

Both B and an above-X Y have center height at least 1/2. Each
has horizontal projection containing [0,1], and coordinate width
at most sqrt(2); hence each center first coordinate is at least
1-sqrt(2)/2>0. Both actual center displacements from A therefore
have strictly positive x component and nonnegative y component.
Their dot product is strictly positive.

But B--A--Y is an actual three-boundary path. The complete fresh
force-free theorem `THREE_BOUNDARY_PATH_MIDDLE_CENTER_OBTUSE.md`
gives the strict opposite dot-product sign. This retains all actual
degrees and physical masks, with no force equation at A. Therefore

\[
                              Y\subseteq\{y\le-1\}.
\tag{25}
\]

## 8. The entire oblique-A case has two incompatible physical tilts

We spell out the pure geometric upper-contact calculation, so the
subsequent reflection retains every feature and endpoint.

Suppose A is oblique, with unique rightmost vertex E=(0,z) and
inward axes

\[
             f_A=(-a_A,b_A),\qquad j_A=(-b_A,-a_A),
          \quad a_A,b_A>0,\quad a_A^2+b_A^2=1.
\tag{26}
\]

The actual lower vertex and lower near side have height at most
zero and cannot meet B. E is also excluded because B's unique
possible zero-height point has first coordinate p>0. The far
vertex has x=-a_A-b_A<-1, below B's minimum x.

The far f_A side cannot touch B: its normal has positive scalar
product with B's v, so every minimum B point has v coordinate zero
and x>=p>0. The far j_A side cannot touch B either: its normal
has negative scalar product with B's u, so every minimum B point
has u coordinate one and x>=p+c-s>=1-s>0. These exclusions include
their whole actual means and closed endpoints.

Thus the actual contact is E+xi_A f_A, 0<xi_A<=1. Its outward
normal has positive y component. If its x component were nonpositive,
every minimum B point would again have v coordinate zero and positive
x, impossible. The normal is therefore strictly Northeast. Every
minimum B point consequently has u coordinate zero, so the same
actual contact is P+tau_A v, with 0<tau_A<=1.

Comparing its two coordinates gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 \xi_Aa_A&=\tau_A s-p\le c-1+\tau_A s,\\
 \xi_Ab_A&=h+\tau_Ac-z\ge\tau_Ac,\\
 \xi_A(a_A-b_A)&\le c-1+\tau_A(s-c)<0.
 \end{split}
\tag{27}
\]

As before, the final upper bound is s-1 if s>=c and c-1 otherwise.
Hence

\[
                                  a_A<b_A.
\tag{28}
\]

Now reflect the whole physical coordinate chart by (x,y)->(x,-1-y).
X is unchanged as a set. The actual Y of (25) is an oblique unit
above y=0 with horizontal projection containing [0,1]. E becomes
(0,-1-z), whose height still lies in [-1,0]. The upper inward
axis of the reflected A is (-b_A,a_A), and its lower inward
axis is (-a_A,-b_A).

The complete geometric calculation just proved applies literally to
this reflected A and actual Y. It uses only that one boundary-side
unit is above X, spans [0,1], and physically touches A; it uses no
force, no neighbor degree and no identity of its far-right neighbor.
It therefore gives b_A<a_A, contradicting (28).

Both choices z=-1 and z=0, every actual upper or lower side-axis
endpoint, and all mean coordinates xi_A,tau_A in their complete
ranges are retained in this reflected argument.

## 9. The entire parallel-A case is impossible as well

Let A=[-1,0] times [a_0-1,a_0]. Since B is oblique and above y=0,
its unique lowest vertex has x strictly between zero and one.
Every B point with x<=0 therefore has height strictly greater than
zero. The actual AB contact gives a_0>0.

Likewise Y is oblique, below y=-1, and spans [0,1]. Its unique
highest vertex has x strictly between zero and one. Every Y point
with x<=0 has height strictly less than -1. The actual AY contact
therefore gives a_0-1<-1, or a_0<0, a contradiction.

These strict statements retain B's minimum height zero and Y's
maximum height -1: their single extreme vertices have positive
first coordinate and cannot be points of A.
