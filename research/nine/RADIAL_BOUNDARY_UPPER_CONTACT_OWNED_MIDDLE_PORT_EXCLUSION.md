# An owned incoming middle port cannot lie inside the radial inner-corner radius

Date: October 27, 2026.

**Status: complete hand local theorem, submitted for full root and
separate independent review.** The weak norm endpoint is included.
The proof keeps the full actual loaded M2 star, and treats its
possible physical radial mask by its complete circle cone, rather
than by an inside-unit concurrence assumption.

## 1. Exact local original-force hypotheses

Keep actual interior-disjoint unit squares in the original disk,

    sqrt(17)/2<R<=sqrt(1105)/16,
    d=sqrt(R^2-1/4)-1,       1<d<61/60.

Use an actual orthonormal frame n,f. The actual boundary unit C is

    C=[d,d+1]n+[-1/2,1/2]f.

The actual loaded degree-two middle unit M has its two actual
physical boundary neighbors B,C. Their original normals into M
are g and h. Assume that M genuinely owns its incoming normal g,
at its unchanged actual side mean Q, and that

    g_n>0>g_f,       Q_n>0, Q_f>0,
    |Q|^2<=d^2+1/4.                                  (1)

The original C-to-M normal satisfies h_f>0,h_n<=0. Its complete
actual support is therefore

    h_n<0: P=(d,1/2);
    h_n=0: h=f, P=(X,1/2), d<=X<=d+1.                (2)

Retain the full true-M2 conclusion: its two pair directions are
nonparallel, its full circle resultant has a positive radial
concurrence q_M, and the two original proper port lengths are
nonnegative. These are supplied in the S123 callers by the actual
boundary neighbors B,C, exactly as in the already used local
degree-two theorem. No degree or equation is assumed at B or C
beyond those actual premises.

The theorem asserts that (1)--(2) are impossible. All physical
circle masks, original side means, and zero individual corner
weights are retained.

Write

    g=(c,-y),       j=(y,c),       c,y>0, c^2+y^2=1.

Since g.h<0, the actual C port P is on M's far g face. If its
minimum g coordinate is H=Q.g, then P.g=H+1. Also, for every
P in (2),

    K:=P.j=X*y+c/2>1/2,                              (3)

where X=d in the strict-inner case. The inequality follows from
X>=d>1 and c+y>1.

## 2. The strict C-inner port, with its complete M feature cover

First suppose h_n<0, so P=(d,1/2). Because M owns g and g.h<0,
there are only three possibilities for h.j.

### 2.1. The C port is the lower-j endpoint

If h.j>0, its original minimum-h contact is the far-g, lower-j
vertex P. Thus

    M=[H,H+1]g+[K,K+1]j,
    Q=P-g+beta*j,       0<=beta<=1.                  (4)

Both coordinates of P-g=(d-c,1/2+y) are strictly positive,
and so are both coordinates of j. Hence (1),(4) give

    d^2+1/4>=|Q|^2>=|P-g|^2
                 =d^2+1/4+1-2P.g.

Consequently P.g>=1/2, or H>=-1/2.

If P.g>1/2, the actual unique farthest corner of M is P+j,
because H+1>|H| and K+1>|K|. Moreover

    2d*c>1+y
    => 2d*(1-y)>c,

using c^2=(1-y)(1+y). It follows that

    R^2-|P+j|^2=2d*(1-y)-c>0.                       (5)

Every actual M corner is therefore strictly inside the original
circle, contradicting its physical boundary load.

At equality P.g=1/2, every preceding inequality is an equality.
Thus beta=0, Q=P-g, and (3) becomes K=d. The actual M is the
physical radial square

    M=[-1/2,1/2]g+[d,d+1]j,
    P=(1/2,d),       Q=(-1/2,d) in the g,j frame.       (6)

Use the full unchanged M2 proper relations. The incoming line
q_M=Q+ell*g keeps q_M.j=d. The other incoming relation
q_M=P+rho*h with rho>=0 and h.j>0 then forces rho=0.
Thus q_M=P. But every positive combination of M's two actual
circle corners in (6), allowing either individual weight to be
zero, lies in the radial cone

    |g component|/(j component)<=1/[2(d+1)].

The ray through P has the strictly larger ratio1/(2d).
It cannot be the positive ray of M's full circle resultant.
This excludes the entire weak endpoint without asserting that
the concurrence of a radial two-corner unit lies inside it.

### 2.2. The C port is the upper-j endpoint

If h.j<0, P is the far-g, upper-j vertex, and

    M=[H,H+1]g+[K-1,K]j.

By (3), the positive-j face is farther from O than the other
j face. If H>=-1/2, a farthest M corner is P, whose norm is
strictly less than R. If H<-1/2, its farthest corner is P-g.
But P.g=d*c-y/2>-1/2, so

    |P-g|^2<d^2+9/4<R^2,

the last strict reserve being2d-1>0. The equality H=-1/2
merely gives two equally distant corners, both inside the
circle. Thus M is physically clear in every case, impossible.

### 2.3. The parallel endpoint

If h.j=0, then h=-g, contradicting the retained true-M2
nonparallel pair conclusion. This is an actual force endpoint;
it is not discarded by treating a side mean as a strict vertex.

These three cases exhaust the entire strict-inner C support.

## 3. The entire C-owned upper-side mean

Now h=f and P=(X,1/2), d<=X<=d+1. Since h.j=c>0,
P is again the far-g, lower-j vertex and (4) remains valid,
with K=X*y+c/2.

If H<=-1/2, then P.g=H+1<=1/2. Equation (4) and the
positive coordinates of P-g give

    |Q|^2>=|P-g|^2>=X^2+1/4>=d^2+1/4.

By (1) equality holds everywhere. Thus X=d,H=-1/2,beta=0,
and the actual radial equality (6) is exactly the already
excluded full-circle-cone endpoint. Therefore

    H>-1/2.

The sole physical circle corner of M must consequently be

    Z=P+j=(X+y,1/2+c).                               (7)

This mask is proved before using the unique-circle radial
relation. The original proper line from C is q_M=P+rho*f,
so its n coordinate is X. The positive radial relation gives

    q_M=[X/(X+y)]Z,
    rho=(X*c-y/2)/(X+y)=(H+1)/(X+y)>0.

The incoming original line q_M=Q+ell*g keeps its j coordinate.
Writing Q as in (4), one therefore has beta=rho*c. Its actual
f coordinate is forced to be

    Q_f=1/2+y+c^2*(X*c-y/2)/(X+y).                    (8)

We next give a complete elementary strict lower bound for
(8). If y>=3/4, its first two terms are at least5/4, while
the remaining term is positive because H+1>1/2.

For 0<y<=3/4, the fraction (X*c-y/2)/(X+y) increases with
X, since its derivative is y(c+1/2)/(X+y)^2>0. As X>1,
the right side of (8) is greater than

    1/2+y/2+y^2/2+(1-y)*sqrt(1-y^2).

On this interval sqrt(1-y^2)>=1-(2/3)y^2: squaring has
nonnegative reserve y^2(1/3-4y^2/9). Thus (8) exceeds

    3/2-y/2-y^2/6+(2/3)y^3>5/4.                    (9)

For the final strict sign, multiply the difference by12 and
write z=y-1/2, so -1/2<z<=1/4. The resulting polynomial is

    3-6y-2y^2+8y^3=1/2-2z+z^2*(10+8z)>0.

For z<=0 both displayed nonconstant contributions are
nonnegative. For 0<=z<=1/4 the first two terms have
nonnegative sum, and their only zero endpoint has a strictly
positive square contribution. This proves (9) on the whole
interval, with no omitted angle endpoint.

But the original weak norm bound (1) gives

    Q_f<=|Q|<=sqrt(d^2+1/4)<23/20<5/4,

where (23/20)^2-[(61/60)^2+1/4]=7/180>0.
This contradicts (8)--(9). Every original C-owned side mean,
including X=d and X=d+1, has now been excluded.

## 4. Exact reusable scope

The proof covers the whole C support in (2), arbitrary real
B degrees beyond the stated boundary-neighbor premise, and
every actual physical M mask. The only two-corner M endpoint
is proved radial and then excluded with its complete circle
