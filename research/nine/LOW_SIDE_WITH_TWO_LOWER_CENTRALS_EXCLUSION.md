# A low upper side unit excludes two lower central units

November 2, 2026. Complete hand caller for the independently accepted
two-central high-floor height cap. No Lean or CI.

Retain an actual horizontal four-chain of original physical unit squares,
with the consecutive main center comparisons also in the horizontal
cone, in the closed disk of squared radius \(Q=1105/256\). All physical
nonoverlap conditions and independent square frames are retained. Put

\[
 l=29/60,\quad g_0=15/31,\quad \delta=331/20000,
 \quad B=2g_0-1/10=269/310.
\]

**Theorem.** Suppose an original right-side outsider S is above its own
inner main and has center height below \(3/5\). There cannot be two
distinct central outsiders G,H below both inner main centers. Horizontal
and vertical reflections give the other three versions.

## 1. The low side unit lowers its own main

The original side and endpoint geometry from
`LOW_SIDE_UNIT_FORCES_SAME_SIDE_CENTRAL_FLOOR.md` gives

\[
 l\le x_S<49/80,
 \qquad y_2<y_S<3/5.
\]

The horizontal bound includes the entire height range: when
\(y_S<7/16\), its earlier universal lower bound
\(y_S>-113/600\) permits the zero-side bound
\(x_S<2211/4000<49/80\); for \(7/16\le y_S<3/5\),
the endpoint inset/disk calculation gives the stated bound directly.

Since \(29/60<x_2<31/60\), the horizontal center gap between S
and M2 is below \(31/240<g_2\). The center's horizontal coordinate
lies strictly inside the main inset. The contained radius-one-half disk
of S must therefore have vertical center clearance at least
\(g_2+1/2\). Consequently

\[
                   y_2<1/10-g_2.                 \tag{1}
\]

## 2. Both reflected lower squares have high whole floors

For every central original unit T, the coupled inner-main edge bound
\(x_2-g_2<\delta\) gives its horizontal distance to the main inset
strictly less than \(l+\delta<1/2\). If T is below M2, its
contained radius-one-half disk thus requires vertical center gap
strictly greater than \(g_2\). Combining with (1), its reflected
center height \(Y_T=-y_T\) obeys

\[
                 Y_T>2g_2-1/10>B.
\]

Every physical unit has \(h_T\le1/\sqrt2<17/24\), so

\[
 Y_T-h_T>B-17/24
      =1/10+221/3720>1/10.                       \tag{2}
\]

In particular both reflected G,H have centers above \(3/5\).
The upper-column theorem forces their horizontal center gap strictly
greater than l. Since both are central, they must occupy opposite strict
horizontal half-planes. Relabel H so that \(0<x_H\le l\).

## 3. The same-half lower unit exceeds the two-unit height cap

The positive central coordinate of H makes its horizontal distance from
the main inset at most \(\delta\). Its physical contained disk gives
the stronger clearance

\[
 y_2-y_H\ge g_2+\sqrt{1/4-\delta^2}.
\]

Combining this with (1) yields

\[
 Y_H>2g_0-1/10+\sqrt{1/4-\delta^2}>273/200.     \tag{3}
\]

For the last comparison the candidate square-root lower bound
\(273/200-2g_0+1/10\) is positive, and the exact squared reserve is

\[
 1/4-\delta^2-(273/200-2g_0+1/10)^2
             =945821879/384400000000>0.
\]

But (2) places both reflected original G,H under
`TWO_CENTRAL_HIGH_FLOOR_HEIGHT_CAP_HAND_TABLE.md`. That complete physical
two-unit theorem gives both center heights strictly below \(273/200\),
contradicting (3). This proves the theorem.

The argument never requires a physical vertical projection edge, a
common square frame, or a selected short-chain link. The two independent
lower squares are retained in the height-cap application.
