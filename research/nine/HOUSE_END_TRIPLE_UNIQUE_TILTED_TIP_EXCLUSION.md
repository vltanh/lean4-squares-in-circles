# The lower unique-corner tip of an end-triple house must be parallel to its transmitter

**Status: complete hand physical-mask and frame exclusion; root and separate
independent full hand audits passed.** Use the end-triple labels
N=1, B=2, T=3, U=4, H=5 and the positive edges
\[
                         UN,UB,HN,HB,HT,NT.
 \tag{1}
\]
Exactly N,B,T are loaded. The actual boundary edge NT is retained;
N has positive degree three. U and H are clear. All other conventions,
the radius window and the four completion squares are those of
`HOUSE_END_TRIPLE_TILTED_BYPASS_DOMAIN.md`.

**Theorem.** Suppose B has exactly one physical circle corner. Then B
must be parallel to the clear side-normal transmitter U. This excludes
the entire nonparallel B frame for every N/T physical circle mask,
every actual roof mean and every endpoint owner or free normal.

The parallel H frame has already been excluded by
`HOUSE_END_TRIPLE_PARALLEL_BYPASS_EXCLUSION.md`. We therefore use the
whole tilted H domain, without restricting the sign of its tilt.

## 1. Every endpoint normal lies in its full right-hand quadrant

Normalize as in the tilted-bypass source:
\[
 U=[0,1]^2,\quad B\subset\{y\le0\},\quad N\subset\{y\ge1\},
 \quad P_B=(\xi,0),\quad P_N=(\xi,1),\quad0\le\xi\le1,
 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 H=P+[0,1]u+[0,1]v,\quad P=(d,e),\quad
 u=(a,b),\quad v=(b,-a),\quad a,b>0,\quad a^2+b^2=1,
 \tag{3}
\]
\[
                  d\ge1,\qquad1-b\le e\le a.
 \tag{4}
\]
Its lower, upper and right vertices are
\[
 L=P+v,\qquad V=P+u,\qquad W=P+u+v.
\]
Both P and W have heights strictly between0 and1. Every HB or HN
contact has horizontal coordinate greater than d>=1>=xi.

Let n_B,n_N point from B,N respectively into H. The whole-domain
proof already gives their weak nonnegative horizontal components.
It now gives the sharper signs
\[
 (n_B)_x\ge0,\quad(n_B)_y>0,
 \qquad
 (n_N)_x\ge0,\quad(n_N)_y<0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed an HB point lies on the lower boundary PL or LW. On the
relative interior of LW the outward H normal is v, which would give
(n_B)_x=-b<0. Thus HB lies on PL or at L. On PL its normal is u;
at L its inward normal belongs to the cone generated by u and -v,
both with strictly positive vertical components. The reflected upper
argument places HN on PV or at V and gives its negative vertical
component. In particular
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 HB\text{ H-owned}:& n_B=u,\quad Q_B=P+t v,\quad0\le t\le1,\\
 HB\text{ not H-owned}:&Q_B=L,\\
 HN\text{ not H-owned}:&Q_N=V.
 \end{array}
 \tag{6}
\]
The whole-domain theorem also proves that HB and HN are singletons
and that they cannot both be H-owned. No side mean at UB,UN or HT is
removed by (5)--(6).

Here is a useful elementary endpoint consequence that includes side
applications. A unit square supported above y=1 at a point P_N, and
having a second support point Q to its right with outward normal
\(n_x\ge0,n_y<0\), satisfies
\[
                              |Q-P_N|\le1.
 \tag{7}
\]
If the square is parallel to U, both points are on its bottom side.
Otherwise P_N is its unique bottom vertex. Write its inward axes
as (c,s),(-s,c), with c,s>0. The second axis has strictly negative
scalar product with n. The support feature for n is therefore the
bottom vertex, its right adjacent vertex, or the side joining them.
The asserted horizontal order removes the first option and gives (7).
Thus every actual HN application satisfies (7), including every
possible N-owned side contact.

## 2. The nonparallel unique B has no free HB normal

Assume for contradiction that B is not parallel to U. Its actual UB
intersection is the unique top vertex P_B in (2). Its HB intersection
is another fixed physical point, because the tilted-domain theorem
excludes shared HB sides. They are distinct: Q_B has horizontal
coordinate strictly larger than xi.

The already compatible `GENERAL_FIXED_TIP_NORMAL_SELECTION.md`
therefore excludes a positive free HB normal at the unique-circle-corner
degree-two body B. This is exactly its distinct-fixed-application
hypothesis. It needs no circle-touching neighbor, and no shared mean
has been frozen to obtain it. In the final candidate graph U transmits
the actual vertical UB force; the point P_B is fixed by the physical
UB singleton itself.

Consequently the actual HB normal has a side-axis owner, B or H.
The retained strict point-only convention excludes an unlisted
axis endpoint of a vertex/vertex normal cone. We handle both owners.

## 3. A B-owned HB side contradicts its unique circle corner

Suppose B owns HB. It cannot simultaneously be H-owned: parallel
supporting sides would give a shared segment or a forbidden point-only
vertex/vertex axis endpoint. By (5), write
\[
 n_B=(c,s),\qquad c,s>0,\qquad c^2+s^2=1.
\]
The positive components are strict because B is not parallel to U.
Its top vertex P_B and its two inward side axes give
\[
 w=(-c,-s),\quad k=(s,-c),\qquad
 B=P_B+[0,1]w+[0,1]k.
 \tag{8}
\]
The HB side is the side from P_B to P_B+k. Since H is not the owner,
its contact is L. Write
\[
                         L=P_B+\tau k,\qquad0<\tau\le1.
 \tag{9}
\]
Allowing the final endpoint only enlarges the actual side-interior
family. Equations (2)--(4) yield
\[
 \tau s=d+b-\xi\ge b,
 \qquad
 \tau c=a-e\le a+b-1<b.
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus c<s.

Let mu>0 be the true UB magnitude and nu>0 the HB magnitude. The
outward circle resultant of B is
\[
 F_B=-\mu e_y-\nu n_B
      =(\nu+\mu s)w+\mu c\,k.
 \tag{11}
\]
Both own components are positive. Since B has one physical circle
corner, that corner is exactly z=P_B+w+k, and its force is a
positive multiple of z-O, with O the true disk origin.

Take B's original torque equation about P_B. The UB torque vanishes.
The HB force nu w at (9) contributes -nu tau. The circle force
-F_B at z contributes nu-mu(c-s), because det(w,k)=1. Hence
\[
                         \nu(1-\tau)=\mu(c-s).
 \tag{12}
\]
The left side is nonnegative, so c>=s, contradicting (10). This
retains every actual side endpoint and does not assume a midpoint.

## 4. An H-owned HB side forces the other endpoint farther than one unit

It remains that n_B=u. In the frame (8), now determined by the top
vertex of the nonparallel B, put
\[
                 A=ac+bs>0,
                 D=as-bc.
 \tag{13}
\]
The scalar product of u with w is -A. The other scalar product is D.
If D<0, the support point for u would be P_B itself, impossible.
If D=0, the HB supporting sides are parallel; their singleton
intersection would be vertex/vertex with an axis normal, forbidden by
the selected strict convention. A positive-length intersection is
already excluded. Thus D>0 and the actual HB application is
\[
                        Q_B=P_B+k=P+t v,
                             \qquad0\le t\le1.
 \tag{14}
\]
Comparing vertical coordinates and using (4) gives
\[
                         e=ta-c,\qquad
                         c\le a+b-1<\sqrt2-1.
 \tag{15}
\]
In particular s>c.

The actual B circle resultant in its own frame is
\[
               F_B=(\mu s+\nu A)w+(\mu c-\nu D)k.
 \tag{16}
\]
Its w-component is positive. Its k-component is nonzero: a unique
physical far corner has nonzero coordinates of absolute value greater
than1/2 in its own outward coordinates, and F_B is its positive
multiple. Thus the sole circle corner is either P_B+w+k or P_B+w.

In the first case, torque about P_B gives
\[
                             \mu(s-c)+\nu D=0,
\]
impossible because s>c and D>0. In the second case, that same exact
torque gives
\[
                         \nu(D-A)=\mu c>0.
 \tag{17}
\]
Hence
\[
                        (a-b)s>(a+b)c,
                         \qquad a>b.
 \tag{18}
\]

Both HB and HN cannot be H-owned. Therefore (6) forces the actual HN
point to be V. Compare it with the true UN point P_N. Equation (14)
gives exactly
\[
                V-P_N=(s+a-tb,\ ta+b-c-1).
 \tag{19}
\]
The squared norm of this vector has derivative with respect to t
\[
                         2\bigl(t-bs-a(c+1)\bigr).
 \tag{20}
\]
It is strictly negative for every t<=1. Indeed
\(ac+bs\ge b\): use a>b from (18), c>0 and
\(1-s=c^2/(1+s)<c\). Thus
\(bs+a(c+1)=A+a\ge a+b>1\).

The norm in (19) is consequently minimized over t<=1 at t=1.
Put p=a+b and k_0=a-b. Since c^2+s^2=1 and
\(k_0^2=2-p^2\), direct expansion gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 |V-P_N|^2-1
 &\ge (s+k_0)^2+(p-c-1)^2-1\\
 &=3-2p+2sk_0-2c(p-1)\\
 &>3-2p+2c>3-2\sqrt2>0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{21}
\]
The strict middle inequality is precisely (18). But the actual N
normal at V has the signs (5), so (7) gives |V-P_N|<=1. This
contradiction excludes the entire H-owned case, including all possible
owners, side features and means at HN.

## 5. Whole implication and retained families

Free, B-owned and H-owned HB exhaust the selected contact normal when
B is unique-corner and nonparallel to U. Sections2--4 exclude all of
them for the entire tilted H domain. The already completed parallel-H
source supplies the other H frame.

Thus every surviving end-triple house has either a physical radial-S
two-circle-corner B, or B parallel to U. The parallel B family retains
both physical masks and its variable UB mean; the nonparallel radial
family retains both circle weights. Neither family is declared closed
here. N and T kept every physical corner mask, and N was never treated
as degree two. The certificate, all independent physical frames and
all four arbitrary additional squares stayed fixed. No numerical
parameter cover, Lean compilation or CI is used as a premise.
