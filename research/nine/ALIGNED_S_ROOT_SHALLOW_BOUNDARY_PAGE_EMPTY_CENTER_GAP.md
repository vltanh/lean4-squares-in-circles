## 1. A complete degree-free shallow-support center cone

Let V be an actual unit square contained in the unchanged disk D_R about
O=0. Assume only

    R>=2, 0<=b<=1/2,
    V subset {y<=-b},
    P=(x,-b) in V, x>=-1/15,
    V physically touches the circle of radius R.        (1)

The point P may be any actual maximum-y support point: a vertex, a
side-interior point, or either endpoint. No force, total load, degree,
normal selection, support owner or circle-coefficient premise is used.
In particular every physical circle corner is retained even if its
individual stress coefficient is zero.

**Theorem 1.** Every actual circle corner Z of V satisfies Z_x>0.
Its actual center c satisfies

    c_y<0, c_x>(2/5)*|c_y|>0.                          (2)

Consequently its center direction is strictly between

    -arctan(5/2) and 0.

All endpoints in (1), including R=2, b=0, b=1/2 and x=-1/15,
are included. There is no upper bound on R in this theorem.

### 1.1. Every physical circle corner has strictly positive x coordinate

Put r=R-sqrt(2). Since V has a physical circle corner and diameter
sqrt(2), every point of V, including the unchanged P, has norm at least r.
In the whole radius range,

    r>=2-sqrt(2)>1/2,
    r^2>12/35.                                        (3)

The second comparison follows from sqrt(2)<99/70. If x<=0, (1) would
give

    |P|^2<=1/225+1/4<12/35,

contradicting (3). Thus x>0.

Let Z be any actual circle corner. If Z_x<=0, then P_y=-b and |Z_y|<=R
give P.Z<=bR. Hence the same actual two points of V would satisfy

    |Z-P|^2=R^2+|P|^2-2P.Z
           >=R^2+r^2-2bR
           =2+2R*(r-b)>2.                             (4)

The final inequality uses r>1/2>=b. It contradicts the diameter of V.
Thus every physical circle corner has positive x coordinate. This
argument needs neither uniqueness of the physical corner nor a positive
coefficient at that particular corner.

### 1.2. Every parallel frame and every original side mean

If V is parallel to the axes, (1) gives

    V=[z,z+1] times [-b-1,-b],
    c=(z+1/2,-b-1/2).

Its center has c_y<0. The preceding result forces c_x>0: a negative
c_x would put a farthest corner on the negative-x side, and c_x=0
would give a negative-x farthest corner as well. Such farthest corners
are physical circle corners because V physically touches the disk.

Suppose c_x<=(2/5)*|c_y|. Since |c_y|=b+1/2<=1, its actual farthest
corner would have squared norm at most

    (c_x+1/2)^2+(b+1)^2
       <=(9/10)^2+(3/2)^2=153/50<4<=R^2,              (5)

a contradiction. The complete side interval and its endpoints have
remained arbitrary. This proves (2) in all parallel frames.

### 1.3. The full tilted frame and both possible physical corners

Otherwise the actual top support P is V's unique top vertex. Write its
two actual inward orthonormal axes as

    u=(p,-q), v=(-q,-p), p,q>0, p^2+q^2=1,
    V=P+[0,1]u+[0,1]v.                                (6)

Both tilt directions are included; p and q are not ordered. Put

    S=p+q, delta=p-q,
    S^2+delta^2=2, 1<S<=sqrt(2)<17/12,
    -1<delta<1,
    c_x=x+delta/2, c_y=-b-S/2<0.                      (7)

Because P.u=xp+bq>0, adding u strictly increases the squared corner
norm, both before and after adding v. Thus every physical farthest
corner is one of

    Z_1=P+u, Z_2=P+u+v.                               (8)

This includes the possible physical tie. At least one of these actual
corners has norm R; by Section 1.1 each such physical corner has
strictly positive x coordinate.

Suppose now that the second inequality of (2) failed. Then

    c_x<=(2/5)*(b+S/2)=2b/5+S/5.                     (9)

If Z_1 is a physical circle corner, its coordinates and (9) give

    0<(Z_1)_x=c_x+S/2<=2b/5+7S/10<143/120,
    |(Z_1)_y|=b+q<=3/2.

Consequently

    |Z_1|^2<52849/14400
               =4-4751/14400<4<=R^2,                (10)

a contradiction over the entire p,q interval.

If Z_2 is a physical circle corner, its positive x coordinate satisfies

    0<(Z_2)_x=c_x+delta/2<=2b/5+S/5+delta/2,
    |(Z_2)_y|=b+S.

Both upper quantities are positive when used. Increasing b to 1/2 can
therefore only increase their squared sum. Exact expansion gives

    |Z_2|^2
      <=(1/5+S/5+delta/2)^2+(1/2+S)^2
       =237/100-(79/100)*delta^2
          +(27/25+delta/5)*S+delta/5.                 (11)

The coefficient 27/25+delta/5 is positive on the whole closed enlarged
interval -1<=delta<=1. Substituting S<17/12 in its positive term gives

    |Z_2|^2
      <39/10+(29/60)*delta-(79/100)*delta^2
      <=39/10+841/11376
       =4-1483/56880<4<=R^2.                         (12)

The second comparison is the maximum of the one displayed concave
quadratic, or equivalently its completed square, with center
delta=145/474. It covers both signs of delta and the entire physical
tie in (8). There is no angle subdivision or corner-weight assumption.

Both possible physical circle corners have been contradicted under
(9). Sections 1.2 and 1.3 exhaust all actual frames, so Theorem 1 follows.

**Lemma 2.** Such a center satisfies rho>17/20. Also no actual unit

Distinct interior-disjoint unit squares have center distance at least

    F(rho)=rho^2+A_0^2+t^2

    F(0)=A_0^2+t^2<=1061/3600<1.                      (17)

At rho=s_0 it increases with phi on [0,phi_0], so substitute
cos(phi_0)=2/sqrt(29), sin(phi_0)=5/sqrt(29). The resulting expression
increases with t>=0 and also with A_0 in (14): its A_0 derivative is

    2A_0-4s_0/sqrt(29)>13/15-17/25>0.

Here sqrt(29)>5 was used. Therefore its maximum over the whole enlarged
rectangle (14) is bounded at A_0=31/60,t=1/6, giving

    F(s_0)<=1831/1800-17/(50*sqrt(29))
           <1831/1800-17/275
            =18917/19800=1-883/19800<1.               (18)

The strict comparison uses sqrt(29)<11/2. Convexity and (17)--(18)
give F(rho)<1 throughout [0,s_0], contradicting actual center separation.
The rho=0 conclusion also proves the assertion about a center at O
without assigning an angular direction to the origin. This proves
Lemma 2 over its entire closed sector and both b endpoints.

## 4. A physical endpoint fact at the centered root slider

The following observation supplies the whole actual lower-support
premise even for a strict Southeast page at the leaf corner.

Suppose b=1/2 in (13), so

    A union L=[d-1,d+1] times [-1/2,1/2].

If a third actual unit W contains P=(d,-1/2) and its open interior
is disjoint from those of A and L, then

    W subset {y<=-1/2}.                               (21)

Indeed, if W had any point above y=-1/2, it would have an open-interior

open rectangle (d-1,d+1) times (-1/2,1/2). A small ball there contains
points in int(A) or int(L), including when the segment itself has x=d.
This contradicts disjoint interiors. Thus (21) holds for the entire
actual W, regardless of its frame or original contact normal.

No replacement of a strict Southeast normal by South is made. Equation
(21) is only a physical containment of the same actual unit.
