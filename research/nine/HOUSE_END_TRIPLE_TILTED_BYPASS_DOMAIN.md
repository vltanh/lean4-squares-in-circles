# The whole tilted-bypass domain of an end-triple house

**Status: complete hand implication at the stated full physical scope;
root and separate independent full hand audits passed.** This is a domain theorem for
the entire remaining tilted bypass. It does not exclude that family.

Use the end-triple labels and exact positive graph of
HOUSE_END_TRIPLE_PARALLEL_BYPASS_EXCLUSION.md: N,B,T are loaded, U is
the clear degree-two transmitter, and H is the clear degree-three
bypass. The edges are UN,UB,HN,HB,HT,NT. Work in the same radius window
and retained strict point-normal convention, with all nine bodies and

The parallel-bypass source excludes H parallel to U. The results here

## 1. Complete coordinates; no choice of a preferred tilt sign

The normal-channel and transverse-bypass theorem permits coordinates
\[
 U=[0,1]^2,\qquad B\subset\{y\le0\},\qquad
 N\subset\{y\ge1\},\qquad H\subset\{x\ge1\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Write the two actual U means as
\[
                         P_B=(\xi,0),\qquad P_N=(\xi,1),
                         \qquad0\le\xi\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
The unique leftmost vertex of the tilted H is P=(d,e). Its inward
orthonormal axes can be labeled
\[
 u=(a,b),\qquad v=(b,-a),\qquad
 a,b>0,\quad a^2+b^2=1.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus
\[
 H=P+[0,1]u+[0,1]v,
 \qquad d\ge1,\qquad 1-b\le e\le a.
\tag{4}
\]
The last interval is exactly the requirement that H's vertical
projection contain [0,1]. Both a>b and a<b remain. In particular
0<e<1. Its lower, upper and right vertices are
\[
 L=(d+b,e-a),\qquad V=(d+a,e+b),\qquad
 W=(d+a+b,e+b-a).
\tag{5}
\]

Every point of H at height at most0 has first coordinate strictly
greater than d. Indeed the leftmost point P has height e>0, and
the leftmost point in a horizontal slice below P lies strictly along
the edge PL. Likewise every point of H at height at least1 has
first coordinate strictly greater than d, because P has height e<1.
Consequently every actual HB and HN point Q satisfies
\[
                              Q_x>d\ge1\ge\xi.
\tag{6}
\]
This strict inequality includes e=a and e=1-b: the corresponding
endpoint contact is then the distinct vertex L or V.

## 2. Every endpoint reaction points weakly toward the bypass

Let n_B be the actual unit normal directed from B toward H. Then
\[
                              (n_B)_x\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]
Choose its actual HB application Q. The B support inequality and
(2),(6) give
\[
                n_B\cdot(Q-P_B)\ge0,
                 \qquad Q_x-\xi>0,\quad Q_y\le0.
\tag{8}
\]
If (n_B)_x<0, (8) forces (n_B)_y<0.

For any unit S let h_S=H_S(e_x)=H_S(e_y), so
\(1/2\le h_S\le1/\sqrt2\). The centers satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (c_H)_x&\ge1+h_H,& (c_B)_x&\le\xi+h_B\le1+h_B,\\
 (c_H)_y&\ge1-h_H,& (c_B)_y&=-h_B.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The vertical center difference is strictly positive. With both components
of n_B negative, (9) therefore gives
\[
 n_B\cdot(c_H-c_B)
       \le \max(h_B-h_H,0)
       \le\frac1{\sqrt2}-\frac12<1.
\tag{10}
\]
But the actual separating support gap is
\[
 n_B\cdot(c_H-c_B)=H_B(n_B)+H_H(n_B)\ge1,
\tag{11}
\]
a contradiction. This proves (7), with no assumed owner of HB.

Reflection in y=1/2 gives the upper-endpoint statement for its actual
normal n_N directed from N toward H:
\[
                              (n_N)_x\ge0.
\tag{12}
\]
The reflection is used only to prove this geometric assertion; it does
not exchange the roof's attachment or identify the two loaded bodies.

### 2.1. Exact equality alternatives

If (n_B)_x=0, the positive vertical center difference in (9) forces
n_B=e_y. Thus H has minimum height0, equivalently e=a, and the actual
HB point is its lower vertex L. An oblique B would have a unique top
vertex, forcing L=P_B, contrary to (6). Hence B is parallel to U.
Its two positive pair normals, to U and H, are both e_y.

Since B is loaded, its circle resultant is vertical downward. An aligned
unit with a unique physical circle corner cannot have a vertical
resultant: its farthest horizontal coordinate has absolute value at
least1/2. Thus B has the physical radial-S two-corner mask with vertical
radial direction. Both physical corners are retained; the vertical force
equation forces their two circle weights to be equal and positive.
The HB point lies in the relative interior
of B's top side; otherwise the vertical force would be a forbidden
point-only vertex/vertex axis endpoint.

Similarly, (n_N)_x=0 forces
\[
                         n_N=-e_y,\qquad e=1-b,
\tag{13}
\]
and N parallel to U. No radial mask is inferred for N, which has the
additional positive NT force.

Both equalities cannot occur together, since they would give
a+b=1, contrary to a,b>0 and a^2+b^2=1. At least one inequality
in (7),(12) is therefore strict.

## 3. Neither endpoint/bypass contact is a shared segment

Suppose HB contained a positive-length segment. Then B and H have
parallel square frames. Since B is below y=0, its unique top vertex
is P_B, and it can be written
\[
                     B=P_B+[0,1]v+[0,1](-u).
\tag{14}
\]
Only H's two edges incident to L can meet B: the other two edges have
strictly positive heights, using e>0 and e+b-a>=1-a>0.

For the left lower H edge PL, the parallel B supporting edge is
\(P_B+[0,1]v\). Collinearity would require P-P_B to be parallel to v.
Its coordinates are d-xi>=0 and e>0, with the first strictly positive
unless d=xi=1. No multiple of v=(b,-a) has these signs; the zero first
coordinate endpoint is also impossible because b>0.

For the right lower H edge LW, the parallel B supporting edge is
\(P_B+[0,1](-u)\), with the opposite side orientation. Collinearity
would require L-P_B to be parallel to u. Its first coordinate
d+b-xi is strictly positive and its second e-a is nonpositive,
again impossible because a,b>0.

Thus HB is a singleton. Reflection in y=1/2 proves that HN is a
singleton as well. This conclusion includes all side/vertex endpoint
limits and does not assume that either of the two unit normals is free.
The UB and UN means, and a possible shared HT mean, are still retained.

## 4. The roof reaction is strictly outward, and the two endpoint
contacts cannot both be owned by H

Let the positive H magnitudes toward B,N,T be nu_B,nu_N,nu_T, and let
m be its actual outward normal toward T. Force balance gives
\[
                       \nu_Tm=\nu_Bn_B+\nu_Nn_N.
\tag{15}
\]
Sections2--2.1 imply the strict full-family conclusion
\[
                                    m_x>0.
\tag{16}
\]

If H owns HB, its normal toward B must be -u. The alternative lower
side normal v has positive first coordinate, whereas H's normal toward
B has first coordinate at most0 by (7). The two upper H sides cannot
contact B. Thus n_B=u in this owner case. Likewise if H owns HN,
then n_N=v.

If both contacts were H-owned, (15) would give
\[
                            \nu_Tm=\nu_Bu+\nu_Nv.
\tag{17}
\]
But H,N,T form a physical unit-square triangle, and the H-owned HN
normal from H is -v. The whole triangle side-normal theorem therefore
requires \((-v)\cdot m\ge0\). Equation(17) gives instead
\[
                           (-v)\cdot m=-\nu_N/\nu_T<0.
\tag{18}
\]
This is a contradiction. Consequently at least one of HB,HN is owned
by its boundary endpoint or has a genuine free point normal. A free
point normal has not been discarded by this assertion.
