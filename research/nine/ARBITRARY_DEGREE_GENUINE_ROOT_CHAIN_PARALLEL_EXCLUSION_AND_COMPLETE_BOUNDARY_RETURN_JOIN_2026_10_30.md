## 3. The two equal-type combinations are physically impossible

In the original six-body block with three distinct genuine leaves,

Whenever each terminal has a positive physical-boundary private neighbor,
Theorem 2 excludes the entire family. The private graph may be a path
or triangle, every private degree and load pattern remains, and no
restriction is placed on additional circle-clear private neighbors.
This includes every such assignment with or without AC, without treating
a higher-degree terminal as a true3 root.

One complete graph/load application is useful explicitly. Suppose the
private path is x--y--z and both actual endpoints x,z are physically
boundary. Biconnectivity gives each endpoint a nonempty attachment set
in {A,C}. The full cap theorem [V], Theorem 1, allows each root at most
one distinct boundary private neighbor. Neither endpoint can attach to
both roots: then the other endpoint would have no permitted attachment.
After interchanging A,C the actual incidences are therefore x--A,z--C,
so both terminals have the positive boundary returns required by
Theorem 2. The entire family is excluded.

The middle private unit y keeps its entire load and physical mask. It
may have no terminal edge, one edge to A or C, or both edges. Its true
degree can consequently be two, three or four. An optional actual AC
edge remains. If y too is physically boundary, its cap restrictions
can only narrow the family; no such restriction is needed in this
application. Positive circle load at x,z is a sufficient condition for
their physical boundary status, but no load is assumed when the latter
status is already known.

Cases where one terminal has no positive physical-boundary private
neighbor retain their remaining obligations. The Type I/II disconnected
private partitions have a different genuine-root incidence pattern and
are not inserted into this caller. No complete C1.6.b3 claim or original
