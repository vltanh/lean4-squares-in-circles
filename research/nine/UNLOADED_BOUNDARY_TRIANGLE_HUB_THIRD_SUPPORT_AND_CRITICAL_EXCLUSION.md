## 2. An exact boundary-origin port-order lemma at d<=1

Assume d<=1. Let K be any actual unit with O on its boundary. Keep
two actual contacts with two distinct contained neighbors, with the
original outward unit normals and applications satisfying

    det(p_-,n_-)<0<det(p_+,n_+).

Their points are nonzero by these strict moment signs. Rotate properly
to put an actual origin side of K on x=0 and K to its right:

    K=[0,1]x[b,c], b<=0<=c, c=b+1.

Use the ordinary argument branch [-pi/2,pi/2] on this half-plane.

**Boundary-origin port lemma.** The argument of p_+ is at least that
of p_-. Equality can occur only if both points are one same far
corner q=(1,c) with c>0, or q=(1,b) with b<0. In either equality
case |q|>1 and q is radial maximal on K. Whenever the actual chord
[p_-,p_+] avoids O, this argument difference is its angular advance.

Here is the complete feature proof. A negative-moment normal at the
upper-right corner (1,c) with both components u,v>0 would give

    v<c*u,
    h_K(u,v)=u+c*v>u+v^2/u=1/u>1,

contrary to the contained neighbor support bound. The East endpoint
is possible only at d=1 and has the unique neighbor (1). Reflection
gives the full lower-right counterpart: a positive-moment strict
Southeast feature is impossible, while its East endpoint has that
same unique neighbor.

All negative ports consequently have height at most zero, except
possibly an East-axis port (1,Y) with Y>0. All positive ports have
height at least zero, except possibly an East-axis port (1,Y) with
Y<0. This is the full cover: North has moment x>=0, South has
moment -x<=0, West has its unchanged mean height, Northwest has
moment c*(-n_x)>=0, and Southwest has moment b*(-n_x)<=0.
The already checked right corners and East means complete it.

Both exceptional East alternatives cannot occur at the two distinct
neighbors, since (1) allows only one actual East neighbor. If neither
port is exceptional, their signed heights prove nonnegative order.
Equal arguments would require both heights zero. If b<0<c, the
only height-zero side features have zero moment. If c=0, the only
nonzero right-corner moments at height zero are positive; if b=0
they are negative. Thus two opposite strict moments cannot attain
equality in this nonexceptional case.

If p_-=(1,Y), 0<Y<=c, is exceptional, a remaining positive port
is on the top side (x,c), or on the West side at positive height.
The latter has argument pi/2 and gives strict increase. On the top,

    arg(x,c)>=arg(1,c)>=arg(1,Y),

and equality forces x=1 and Y=c. This is precisely q=(1,c), c>0.
Its radial direction is in that far-corner normal cone. The reflected
argument proves the other equality possibility. All actual side means
and endpoints are included, and no equation at K or a neighbor was
used.

The chord proviso is explicit: opposite points on an origin side could
have a chord through O in a different physical application. In the
triangle theorem below, the general strict third-support separation

that possibility is absent for a proved reason.

### 3.1. The entire origin-free U domain

If n_x>=0, the nonpositive exceptional support and positive moment

n_x=0,c=0. South has strictly negative moment. At a strict Southeast
port the positive moment forces b<0, making its support strictly
positive. East has already been excluded.

Consider n_x<0 first. If n_y<0, write n=(-u,-v), u,v>0. Its
positive moment at (a,b) forces b>0. Every nonpositive-moment return

strictly positive moment, and any Northeast return with nonpositive

    c*f>=(a+1)*t,
    h_U(f,t)>=(a+1)/f>1.

Its axis endpoints are the already excluded East or positive-moment

If n is West, its original positive-moment mean has positive height,

component at most zero. Vertical balance makes both returns horizontal;
East is impossible, so every force points West, another contradiction.

The remaining strict feature is n=(-u,v), u,v>0. Put w=(v,u).

It remains the earlier exceptional North row c=0. Its whole square
