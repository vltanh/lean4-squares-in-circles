## 2. A complete physical-triangle coefficient lemma

We prove, including every parallel and coincident alternative,
that the original forces satisfy

    u=b_U a+c_U F_U,       b_U>0, 0<=c_U<=1,
    v=b_W a+c_W F_W,       b_W>0, 0<=c_W<=1.             (5)

Consider first the actual triangle H-U-K. The purely physical
proper-port fact in `RADIAL_TRIANGLE_CONCURRENCE.md`, Section 1,
applies to any nonparallel pair at any of its three bodies. It
does not require zero moments for the external aggregates of
this smaller triangle, and no such moments are assumed here.

If H's two triangle forces u,a are parallel, they have the same
outward sign: opposite supports would prevent U and K touching.
Thus u=b_U a with b_U>0, giving c_U=0.

If K's two triangle forces -f,-a are parallel, the same physical
fact makes f=b_U a with b_U>0. Its genuine tip equation then
gives u=b_U a+F_U, so c_U=1. The H and K pairs cannot both be
parallel because that would make the true tip U pair parallel,
contrary to (4).

Suppose both H and K pairs are nonparallel. Denote their actual
geometric intersections by h_U and k_U. The physical proper-port
fact, applied at each end of the three actual edges, gives

    q_U-h_U=l u,
    k_U-q_U=m f,
    k_U-h_U=L a,             l,m,L>=0.                  (6)

These coefficients are all strictly positive unless all three
concurrences coincide. Indeed, if two concurrence points agree
and the third differs, the two force directions joining the
third to the coincident pair would be parallel. That contradicts
the nonparallel pair at that third body. This treats every zero
proper coefficient without imposing a strictness assumption on
an individual application mean.

The all-coincident alternative is impossible here. In that case
every actual edge application equals the common point p, since
each lies between its two proper concurrence endpoints. At U,
p=q_U is a positive multiple of F_U, and the positive sum of
its two outward contact forces is -F_U in its normal cone at p.

For one physical circle corner z, this makes p the opposite
corner on the same radial line, so

    p=(R-sqrt(2)) z/R.

For two physical circle corners, use U=[d,d+1] x [-1/2,1/2]
and write the direction of F_U as (T,eta), T=d+1,
|eta|<=1/2. If eta>0, the support for -F_U is (d,-1/2),
which cannot be a positive multiple of (T,eta); eta<0 is the
reflection. If eta=0, radiality forces p=(d,0), a relative
side-interior point. Its normal cone has one axis, which would
make both actual U forces parallel. Thus this two-corner case
is impossible too, including each individual zero-weight limit.

In the remaining one-corner form, the distinct boundary unit K
also contains p. Choose any actual circle corner z_K of K.
The inequalities

    R=|z_K| <= |p|+|z_K-p| <= R-sqrt(2)+sqrt(2)=R

are equalities. Hence z_K is the same radial circle point z.
Two distinct physical units cannot share a containing-circle
point. This excludes the all-coincident alternative. Therefore
l,m,L in (6) are strictly positive.

Using u=F_U+f in (6) now gives

    l u+m f=L a,
    u=[L/(l+m)]a+[m/(l+m)]F_U.

Thus b_U=L/(l+m)>0 and c_U=m/(l+m) lies strictly between
zero and one in this nonparallel case.

For H-K-W the same complete argument applies. At H, parallel
v,a gives c_W=0. At K, its actual outward pair is g,-a,
so a parallel pair has g=-b_W a with b_W>0 and v=F_W+b_W a,
giving c_W=1. In the nonparallel case the proper differences are

    q_W-h_W=l v,
    q_W-k_W=m g,
    k_W-h_W=L a,

with the same strictly positive coefficients after the same
coincident-point exclusion using the boundary tip W. Therefore
l v-m g=L a and v=[L/(l+m)]a+[m/(l+m)]F_W. This proves
all of (5) at the unchanged physical force magnitudes.
