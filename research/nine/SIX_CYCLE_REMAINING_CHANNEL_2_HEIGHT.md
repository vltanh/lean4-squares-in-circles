## 2. A hypothetical height at most one quarter reduces to one scalar

Suppose \(0<h\le1/4\). Since \(sa-cb=L\), positivity of
\(ca+sb\) fixes the exact rotation formulas
\[
 a=sL+c\sqrt{R^2-L^2},\qquad
 b=s\sqrt{R^2-L^2}-cL.
\tag{9}
\]
For fixed h,c, increasing the comparison radius to 2 increases both
a and b. In (8) the coefficient
\(\lambda=1-h^2/L^2\) lies in \((0,1)\), because \(A_0>0\).
Thus F decreases in this comparison. At radius 2 denote the resulting
positive coordinates by \(\bar a,\bar b\); then
\[
 F\ge F_2:=C^2-2C\bar a-\lambda\bar b^2,
                   \qquad \bar a^2+\bar b^2=4.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular \(\bar b\ge b>1/2\).

If \(\bar a\le\bar b\), then \(\bar a\le\sqrt2\), and
\[
 F_2=(C-\bar a)^2-4+(h\bar b/L)^2>0,
\]
since \(C-\bar a\ge2\). This already contradicts (8).

It remains to consider \(\bar a>\bar b\). Put
\[
                              k=A_0/h>0.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(c=\sin\alpha\), \(s=\cos\alpha\), then
\[
 \alpha=\pi/4-\arcsin(kh/\sqrt2),\qquad
 \bar a=2\sin\left(\pi/4+
              \arcsin\frac{h(1+k)}2-\arcsin\frac{kh}{\sqrt2}\right).
\tag{12}
\]
All angles in (12) are on their actual principal branches; both
rotated coordinates in (9) are positive. The inequality
\(\bar a>\bar b\) is consequently equivalent to
\[
        \frac{1+k}{2}>\frac{k}{\sqrt2},\qquad
                         0<k<1+\sqrt2<5/2.
\tag{13}
\]

Fix k and increase the scalar comparison height h to \(1/4\).
The difference of arcsines in (12) strictly increases with h:
for a fixed h the function \(v/\sqrt{1-v^2h^2}\) increases
with the positive coefficient v, and (13) orders the coefficients.
Throughout this interval the angle in (12) belongs to
\([\pi/4,5\pi/12)\), since \((1+k)/8<C/8<1/2\).
Thus \(\bar a\) increases. The coefficient
\(\lambda=1-1/(1+k)^2\) is fixed, and
\[
 \frac{d}{d\bar a}\{C^2-2C\bar a-\lambda(4-\bar a^2)\}
                   =-2C+2\lambda\bar a<0.
\]
Therefore it is enough to prove, for \(0\le k\le1+\sqrt2\),
\[
 \mathcal F(k):=C^2-2C\mathfrak a(k)
       -\left(1-\frac1{(1+k)^2}\right)
                    (4-\mathfrak a(k)^2)>0,
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
 \mathfrak a(k)=2\sin(\pi/4+\varepsilon(k)),\qquad
 \varepsilon(k)=\arcsin\frac{1+k}{8}
                         -\arcsin\frac{k}{4\sqrt2}.
\tag{15}
\]
These are scalar comparison intervals only. No interpolated packing,
force change, or replacement of the original mean is asserted.

## 3. One concave tangent bounds the whole function a(k)

           \quad}
\tag{16}

### 3.1. Complete concavity, with exact derivative signs

The auxiliary derivative computations can be made on the larger
interval \(0\le k\le5/2\), on which both radicals remain positive:
\[
 \varepsilon'=rac1{\sqrt{64-(1+k)^2}}-rac1{\sqrt{32-k^2}},
\]
\[
 \varepsilon''=rac{1+k}{[64-(1+k)^2]^{3/2}}
                         -\frac{k}{(32-k^2)^{3/2}},
\]
\[
 \varepsilon'''=rac{64+2(1+k)^2}{[64-(1+k)^2]^{5/2}}
                         -\frac{32+2k^2}{(32-k^2)^{5/2}}<0.
\tag{17}
\]
For the last strict sign, its first term is at most
\[
 \frac{177/2}{(207/4)^2\,7}
       =\frac{472}{99981}<\frac1{200},
\]
whereas its second is at least \(1/(32\sqrt{32})>1/192\).
The first rational reserve is \(5581/19996200>0\).
Thus \(\varepsilon''\) decreases, and
\[
                   \varepsilon''\le\frac1{63\sqrt{63}}
                              <\frac1{441}<\frac1{400}.
\tag{18}
\]

Also \(\varepsilon'\) is concave. At \(k=3/5\),
\[
 \varepsilon''(3/5)=\frac{25}{3072\sqrt6}
                            -\frac{75}{791\sqrt{791}}<0.
\]
The last comparison follows by squaring positive sides; its integer
reserve is \(6\cdot9216^2-791^3=14694265>0\).
The tangent inequality and \(k\ge0\) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \varepsilon'(k)
 &\le\varepsilon'(3/5)+(k-3/5)\varepsilon''(3/5)\\
 &\le\varepsilon'(3/5)-(3/5)\varepsilon''(3/5)\\
 &=\frac{315}{1024\sqrt6}-\frac{3910}{791\sqrt{791}}
                         <-\frac1{20}.
 \end{split}
\tag{19}
\]
For a fully rational check of the strict last sign, use
\(\sqrt6>2449/1000\) and \(\sqrt{791}<225/8\), whose squared
reserves are \(2399/1000000\) and \(1/64\). Replacing the
radicals in the displayed upper bound leaves its difference below
\(-1/20\) equal to \(325183/2231607168>0\).

On the required interval \(0\le k\le1+\sqrt2\), one has
\(0\le\varepsilon<\pi/4\). Put
\(\vartheta=\pi/4+\varepsilon\in[\pi/4,\pi/2)\).
Equations (18)--(19) prove
\[
 \mathfrak a''=2\cos\vartheta\,\varepsilon''
                    -2\sin\vartheta\,(\varepsilon')^2<0,
\tag{20}
\]
because \(\sin\vartheta\ge\cos\vartheta>0\) and
\((\varepsilon')^2>1/400>\varepsilon''\).
This proves concavity on the entire needed interval.

## 4. One positive quartic closes the scalar comparison

\tag{23}
\]

\[

\tag{24}

 \begin{split}
