## 1. A local opposite-quadrant common-neighbor cap

Let P be a fixed physical point. Suppose actual units E and F satisfy

    E subset {X>=P_X, Y<=P_Y},
    F subset {X<=P_X, Y>=P_Y}.

Let another actual unit T touch both E and F. Its actual center c_T is
at distance at most 1/sqrt(2) from each actual T contact. A contact
with E therefore gives

    (c_T)_X>=P_X-1/sqrt(2),
    (c_T)_Y<=P_Y+1/sqrt(2).

A contact with F gives the reverse two bounds. Consequently

    |(c_T)_X-P_X|<=1/sqrt(2),
    |(c_T)_Y-P_Y|<=1/sqrt(2),
    |c_T-P|<=1.                                     (1)

This uses the actual contact points and the unit circumradius only.
There is no force, degree, normal owner, common frame of T, or presumed
intersection of E and F in the lemma. All weak contact endpoints remain.

In particular, if |P|<1/40 and R>2, T cannot be a physical boundary
unit. Equation (1) gives |c_T|<41/40, while a genuine circle corner
of T gives

    |c_T|>=R-1/sqrt(2)>2-3/4=5/4>41/40.             (2)

The same statement holds after any rigid rotation or reflection.
