## 1. The precise theta obstruction

Suppose U,V physically touch the circle and UV is a positive
edge. Suppose two other positive U-to-V paths P and Q are
internally vertex-disjoint from one another and from the
direct edge. If each of P and Q contains an internal vertex
which is physically circle-touching or is a genuine leaf root,
the configuration is impossible. More generally, the same planar
argument applies to any two exterior witnesses in the same
embedding in which UV is exterior; an arbitrary exterior label
from a different drawing is not imported.

Restrict the drawing to the three U-to-V paths UV,P,Q. Any
surviving exterior vertex and the exterior side of UV remain
exterior after other edges and vertices are removed. The
three-path graph is a plane theta graph. Its unbounded face
has as its boundary the union of two of the three paths.
Since that face is incident to the direct edge UV, its other
boundary path is P or Q. The remaining path, including every
one of its internal vertices, lies on the bounded side of
that outside cycle. It cannot contain an exterior vertex.
This contradicts the assumed witness on whichever path is
internal.

This argument uses no local degree or force equation at a
