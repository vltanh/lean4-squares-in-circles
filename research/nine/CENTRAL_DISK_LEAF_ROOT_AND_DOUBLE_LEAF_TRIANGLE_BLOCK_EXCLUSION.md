## 2. Uniform central-disk leaf-root theorem

Let an actual square E satisfy

    D_epsilon subset int E,

and let E have a genuine positive leaf L. Then every actual cyclic
biconnected positive block in E's connected positive component contains
E. Moreover every positive bridge in that component is a direct genuine
leaf at E.

The leaf assumption is used only to supply the actual positive E--L
path to an actual physical boundary body outside any cyclic block not
containing E. It is not used as a substitute for any root force.

### 2.1. All actual nonroot bodies avoid the closed disk

Every distinct closed actual square U avoids D_epsilon. Otherwise it
would have a point in int E, and approximation by interior points of U
would give overlap of the two open interiors. Compactness also gives
strict positive clearance from that disk.

No actual radial contact can join two such nonroot bodies. To see this,
write its nonzero application as p=t e, t>0, with the inner body U in
e.x<=t and the outer body V in e.x>=t. The contained-neighbor support
bound from the first pinned source gives t<=d. Choose an own interval
of the origin-free U that misses zero, reflecting its axis if necessary
to write it [a,a+1] with a>0. Since p=t e is a maximum-e point of U,
its positive coordinate on that axis is a+1. Hence

    t>1,       a<=t-1<=epsilon.

If the other own interval also missed zero, the absolute value of the
other coordinate of p would also exceed one, so t>sqrt(2)>d. It
therefore contains zero, and U has the actual point with own coordinates
(a,0), of norm at most epsilon. This contradicts disk avoidance.

Consequently every original positive edge whose endpoints both differ
from E has a nonzero origin moment. This includes an arbitrary actual
side mean: radiality here means exactly that its original application
and common normal are parallel.

### 2.2. A cyclic block avoiding E would obstruct its genuine leaf

Suppose an actual cyclic biconnected block B does not contain E. All
of its actual units avoid D_epsilon, and all its original edge moments
are nonzero by Section 2.1.

The full origin-torque equations restrict to a circulation on B. Each
component outside a maximal biconnected block attaches to it at at most
one vertex; a second attachment would put its joining path in that same
block. Sum the original torque equations over each such outside
component. Internal pair moments cancel at their unchanged common
applications, and all circle atoms have zero origin moment. The complete
external moment at the sole attaching B vertex is therefore zero. This
argument retains every outside force, including every further cyclic
block. Subtracting those complete zero moments from each full B-vertex
equation leaves exactly all original B-edge moments summing to zero.

Orient each B edge by the direction in which its original moment is
positive. Its positive moment weight gives a finite nonempty positive
circulation. Following outgoing edges produces a directed simple cycle
C. The graph is simple and only one direction was selected on each
undirected edge, so C has at least three vertices.

The degree-free theorem in Sections 1--3 of the first pinned source
applies to all the actual C bodies and their two original cycle ports.
It gives strictly positive winding about O. The common compatible
physical embedding realizes the same cycle as a Jordan curve with that
winding; coincident contact strands are routed within the positive
clearance from O supplied by the actual bodies.

The genuine leaf L has positive circle force: its one nonzero pair
force could not balance if its full circle system vanished. Thus L is
a physical boundary body. Neither L nor E belongs to C, because L has
true degree one and B avoids E. The compact segment O--c_E lies in
int E, disjoint from all C bodies. Choose the common routing close
enough to preserve its clearance. Then c_E, like O, lies strictly inside
the embedded C. The actual positive edge E--L is disjoint from C in
this same embedding, so it puts c_L in that bounded region. This
contradicts L's protected incidence with the unbounded face. No cycle
specific reembedding or changed contact is made.

This proves that every actual cyclic block contains E.

### 2.3. Every original bridge is a direct genuine leaf at E

Summing all original torque equations on one cut side of any positive
bridge makes that bridge's original origin moment zero. Section 2.1
therefore requires it to be incident with E. For such a bridge E--U,
any other bridge incident with U would avoid E, which is impossible.
Any cyclic block incident with U must contain E by Section 2.2, and
would then give an E--U path avoiding the purported bridge. Thus U
has no other positive edge and is a genuine leaf. This proves the
complete stated bridge consequence with no altered force system.

## 3. Privately double-leaf triangle theorem

Let A,H,B be an actual positive triangle. Suppose A has exactly its
two triangle pair forces and two genuine leaves L_1,L_2. Suppose B has
true positive degree two. Let a distinct actual cyclic biconnected block
Q belong to the same positive component and not contain A.

**Theorem.** This entire original configuration is impossible.

H and Q retain arbitrary true degrees, every original force and physical
circle system. Q need not have a leaf and can have any number of
vertices allowed by the same nine actual bodies. In particular Q may
share H with the triangle.

First, B is actually circle-loaded. Otherwise its full degree-two
equation makes its two positive outward normals opposite. Its actual

by B's width, which is at least one. They could not physically touch,
contradicting the actual AH edge. This is the genuine B2 equation; it
is not imposed on A or H.
