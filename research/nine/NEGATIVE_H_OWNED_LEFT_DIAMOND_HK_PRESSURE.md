## 1. Original variables and the pressure to be bounded

\[

\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]

The classification proves that the actual HK point U is H's front/top
vertex and is on K's min-e side. Hence

\[
c_H=U-(n+j)/2,\qquad U\cdot e=\mathsf A-1,

\[
c_H\cdot e=\mathsf A-1-\frac{E+F}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(\mathsf A>1+1/\sqrt2\), Cauchy's inequality

\tag{4}

## 2. A uniform bound on the original normal asymmetry

We prove, on the whole interval \(3/2\le a\le\mathsf A\le171/100\),

\[
                         k\le\frac{\mathsf A-3/2}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]

Fix \(\mathsf A,a\) and compare q down to \(q_0\), using only the
unchanged scalar identities

\[
b=\sqrt{q-a^2},\qquad
\beta=\sqrt{q-a^2-2(\mathsf A-a)},\qquad
\delta=1-\beta/b.
\tag{6}
\]

This is a scalar comparison, not a new packing. Both radicals stay
positive throughout: their squares are at least
\(q_0-\mathsf A^2\). If \(\mathsf A=a\), then \(\delta=k=0\), and
(5) is immediate. Otherwise write \(d=\mathsf A-a>0\). As b increases,

\[
\delta=\frac{2d}{b(b+\beta)},\qquad
\delta_b=-\frac{2d}{b^2\beta}<0,
\]

and

\[
\{\delta(a+b)\}_b
=\frac{2d\{b\beta-(a+b)(b+\beta)\}}
 {b^2\beta(b+\beta)}<0.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the numerator \(\delta(a-b)\) decreases and the denominator
\(2-\delta(a+b)\) increases with q. They are positive on the comparison
interval; the explicit lower bound below verifies this already at
\(q=q_0\). Therefore k is at most its value at \(q_0\).

At \(q=q_0\), throughout the full rectangle in question,

\[
b,\beta>\frac{247}{250},\qquad
a-b<\frac{361}{500},\qquad a+b<\frac{14}{5}.
\tag{8}
\]

For the first bound, both squared radicals in (6) are at least
\(q_0-(171/100)^2\), and

\[
q_0-(171/100)^2-(247/250)^2=\frac{381}{10^6}>0.
\]

The second follows from \(a\le171/100\). For the third,
\(a+b\le\sqrt{2q_0}=79\sqrt2/40<14/5\), with squared reserve
\(112^2-2\cdot79^2=62>0\).

We also have the full-domain bound

\[
                            b-\beta<9/50.
\tag{9}
\]

At fixed a, this difference increases with \(\mathsf A\), so take
\(\mathsf A=171/100\). Its derivative with respect to a is
\(-a/b+(a-1)/\beta<0\): the equivalent positive comparison is
\((2a-1)b^2>2a^2(\mathsf A-a)\). The left side exceeds \(19/10\)
by (8) and \(a\ge3/2\), while the right side is below \(63/50\)
because \(a^2<3\) and \(\mathsf A-a\le21/100\).
The maximum is therefore at a=3/2. There
\(b<257/200\) and \(\beta>221/200\), with squared reserves
\(3/5000\) and \(6/625\), respectively. Their difference is less
than \(36/200=9/50\), proving (9).

Put \(L=2b-(b-\beta)(a+b)\). Equations (8)--(9) give

\[
b+\beta>247/125,\qquad L>184/125.
\]

In particular all denominators in the comparison were positive. From
the exact formula for k,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{k}{\mathsf A-a}
&=\frac{2(a-b)}{(b+\beta)L}\\
&<\frac{2(361/500)}{(247/125)(184/125)}
=\frac{2375}{4784}<\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The last reserve is \(17/4784\). Since \(\mathsf A-a\le\mathsf A-3/2\),
this proves (5) for every original q, with the zero case retained.

## 3. The physical corner bounds the actual tilt from below

Put \(w=\mathsf A-1\), so for the present case
\(w\le1/\sqrt2\). The exact original horizontal coordinate is

\[
x=\mathsf B s-wc.
\]

Since \(x=a-1\le w\), both sides in
\(\mathsf B s\le w(1+c)\) are positive. Squaring and cancelling
\(1+c\) gives

\[
c\ge\frac{\mathsf B^2-w^2}{\mathsf B^2+w^2}
=\frac{q-2w^2-2w-1}{q-2w-1}
\ge c_0(w):=1-\frac{2w^2}{q_0-2w-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

The last quotient increases with q, as its derivative is
\(2w^2/(q-2w-1)^2>0\). Because \(c<1/2\), the same inequality
implies \(q<4w^2+2w+1\). At \(w=51/80\) this last polynomial is
exactly \(q_0\). It is increasing for positive w. Therefore

\[
                         51/80<w\le1/\sqrt2.
\tag{12}
\]

On the enlarged closed interval in (12), \(0<c_0\le1/2\) and
\(c_0>1/4\). For example use \(w<71/100\) and
\(q_0-2w-1>37/25\) to obtain
\(c_0=1-2w^2/(q_0-2w-1)>1/4\).
Let \(k_0=(w-1/2)/2\). Then \(0<k_0<11/100\), so the function
\(\sqrt{1-c^2}+k_0c\) strictly decreases for \(c\ge c_0\): its
derivative is \(-c/\sqrt{1-c^2}+k_0<0\).

Equations (3)--(5), (11) consequently give

\[
\sqrt2\,c_H\cdot e
\ge F_0(w):=\sqrt2\,w-\sqrt{1-c_0(w)^2}
                        -c_0(w)(w-1/2)/2.
\tag{13}
\]

## 4. One exact derivative comparison on the entire interval

We prove \(F_0(w)>0\) throughout (12), without subdividing it.
Write \(D=q_0-2w-1\) and \(s_0=\sqrt{1-c_0^2}\). Direct
differentiation gives

\[
c_0'=-\frac{4w(D+w)}{D^2}<0,\qquad
c_0''=-\frac{4(q_0-1)^2}{D^3}<0.
\tag{14}
\]

At \(w_0=51/80\),

\[
c_0=1/2,\quad s_0=\sqrt3/2,\quad
k_0=11/160,\quad -c_0'=5680/2601>2.
\]

Since \(c_0''<0\), throughout (12) we have \(-c_0'>2\).
We also have \(-c_0''<11\): use \(q_0-1<291/100\),
\(D>37/25\), and the exact reserve

\[
11(37/25)^3-4(291/100)^2=111707/62500>0.
\]

Furthermore

\[
\frac{c_0}{s_0}-k_0
\le\frac1{\sqrt3}-\frac{11}{160}
<\frac7{12}-\frac{11}{160}<\frac{13}{25}.
\tag{15}
\]

Define

\[
M=-c_0'\left(\frac{c_0}{s_0}-k_0\right)+\frac{c_0}{2}.
\]

Then \(F_0'=\sqrt2-M\), and the exact derivative is

\[
M'=-c_0''\left(\frac{c_0}{s_0}-k_0\right)
       -\frac{(c_0')^2}{s_0^3}+c_0'.
\tag{16}
\]

Its first term is less than \(143/25\) by (15) and
\(-c_0''<11\); if the parenthesis is negative that upper bound is
immediate. Its second term is less than \(-4\), since
\(-c_0'>2\) and \(s_0\le1\), and its last term is less than \(-2\).
Thus \(M'<-7/25<0\) over the whole interval.

At its left endpoint, use \(1/\sqrt3<7/12\) to get

\[
M(w_0)<\frac{5680}{2601}
       \left(\frac7{12}-\frac{11}{160}\right)+\frac14
=\frac{42877}{31212}<\frac75<\sqrt2.
\tag{17}
\]

The middle comparison has reserve \(4099/156060\). Consequently
\(F_0'>0\) on the whole interval. Finally \(\sqrt2>140/99\) and
\(\sqrt3<26/15\) give the exact left-endpoint estimate

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(w_0)
&=\frac{51\sqrt2}{80}-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}-\frac{11}{320}\\
&>\frac{51}{80}\frac{140}{99}-\frac{13}{15}-\frac{11}{320}
=\frac1{2112}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

Equations (13),(17)--(18) prove (1) on the full remaining interval.
Together with the immediate larger-\(\mathsf A\) case this exhausts
every original pose in the low left-singleton diamond.

## 5. Two further signs for the unchanged root center

Write \(c_H=(x_H,y_H)\) in the original A frame. The proved sign
\(-cx_H+sy_H>0\), together with the unchanged negative pressure
\(ax_H+by_H<0\), implies
\[
x_H<0,\qquad c_H\cdot j>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed \(x_H\ge0\) would force \(y_H<0\), contradicting the first
strict sign. Since s>0, it also gives \(y_H>(c/s)x_H\), so
\[
c_H\cdot j=-vx_H+uy_H
 >(-v+uc/s)x_H>0.
\]
The coefficient is strictly negative because \(u\le v\) and \(c/s<1\),
including \(u=v\). These signs use the original center and normal;
they do not assert positivity of the still separate HA projection
\(c_H\cdot n\). Root and the independent reviewer checked this direct
corollary with all the original endpoints retained.
