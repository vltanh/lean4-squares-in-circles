## 2. A strict real-leaf vertex cannot coexist with (1)

Suppose the real leaf direction e is not an own side axis of A. Direct
A's own axes x,y toward its genuine support vertex q=d e. In these own
coordinates, for positive H,K,

\[
 A=[H-1,H]\times[K-1,K],\quad q=(H,K),\quad H^2+K^2=d^2.
\]

The vertex r cannot equal q, since |r|>d. It cannot be the opposite
vertex: that vertex has squared norm d^2+2-2(H+K)<d^2, because
H+K>=d>1. Hence r is adjacent to q. Exchange the two axes to write
r=q-x. Its squared norm is d^2+1-2H. Equation (1) gives

\[
 d^2<|r|^2\le d^2+1/4,\qquad 3/8\le H<1/2.
\]

Also K>sqrt(3)/2>epsilon. The other inequality follows without a sample:

\[
 (1-\varepsilon)^2-(d^2-9/64)=9/64-4\varepsilon>9/64-1/15>0.
\]

Thus K<1-epsilon. Both own intervals of A contain [-epsilon,epsilon]
strictly: H>epsilon, H-1<-epsilon, K>epsilon, K-1<-epsilon. Therefore
closed D_epsilon lies in int A. The distinct unit Y has an actual point
there and interior points arbitrarily close to it, contradicting physical
nonoverlap. This eliminates every strict real-leaf feature at A.

## 3. The aligned real-leaf chart forces exactly (2)
