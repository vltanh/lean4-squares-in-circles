### 1.1. All original private moments are nonzero if h>epsilon

Suppose h>epsilon. The actual reverse support at H is -h, so every
point of H avoids closed D_epsilon. Every actual boundary unit has
distance at least r from O. Hence all actual G units avoid closed
D_epsilon, including their boundaries.

Every contained unit has center norm at most d+1/2 and halfwidth at
least 1/2 in every unit direction. Therefore a contained neighbor
beyond an actual support `e.z>=t` requires t<=d.

If an original private contact had zero origin moment, its actual
application would be `p=t e`, t>0, with an inner unit U having this
radial maximum support and an outer neighbor beyond it. The case
p=O already contradicts private disk avoidance. Because U avoids O,
some own coordinate interval misses zero; reflect it to `[a,a+1]`,
a>0. The radial maximum-support point has that coordinate a+1, so

    1<t<=d,  a<=t-1<=epsilon.

If both actual own intervals miss zero, both absolute radial support
coordinates exceed one, so t>sqrt(2)>d. If just one does, the other
contains zero, and the actual point `(a,0)` lies in U and D_epsilon.
Either alternative is impossible. This is a statement about each
original shared mean or singleton point, with no normal reselection.

Thus every original edge of G has nonzero origin moment. Orient it
in its positive-moment direction.
