## 2. The complete KB feature cover

### 2.1. The top side, right side and upper-right vertex

The exact H extents are

    max_H g=h+wE<h+1,
    max_H f=X+d(1-w)<F.                                (6)

immediately next to the vertex. Convexity would put that segment in B,
giving forbidden interior overlap. Thus the entire upper-right vertex
is impossible, independent of its actual normal or B's mask.

### 2.2. Every bottom point beyond T is blocked by A or K

Consider any actual point Q=V+t f of B on K's bottom side with
delta<t<=1. This includes the bottom-right vertex.

## 3. In the left-side feature, H-owned HB must be j

Assume k=-f. Then all B points have f coordinate at most X, and its

### 3.1. An H-owned singleton n contact cannot occur

Its physical B vertex Y lies on H's front n side. The B half-plane
f<=X and the exclusion of V give

    Y=V+t j, 0<t<=w<1.                                 (8)

If B is aligned with K, every B vertex has f coordinate X or X-1.
But Y_f=X-dt lies strictly between these, a contradiction.

If B is tilted, its maximum-f support is the unique vertex Q. The two
distinct B vertices Y,Q would have distance 1 or sqrt(2). Instead, putting
z=y-h in [0,1], their exact distance obeys

    |Q-Y|^2=d^2t^2+(z-Et)^2<1.                         (9)

The right side is convex in z. At z=0 it is t^2<1; at z=1 it is
1-2Et+t^2<1, because E>v>=1/sqrt(2) and t<1<2E.
Thus (9) holds on the entire original interval, a contradiction.

### 3.2. The entire shared n contact is impossible

Q=V, already impossible for any third square. No original shared HB

The only possible H-owned HB axis in this whole left-side KB feature is
