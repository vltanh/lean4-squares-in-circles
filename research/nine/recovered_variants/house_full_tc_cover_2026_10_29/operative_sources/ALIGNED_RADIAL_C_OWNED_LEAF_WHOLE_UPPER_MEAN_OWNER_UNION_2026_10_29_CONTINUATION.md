## 3. Two force-free horizontal support facts

The following statements concern an actual unit and an actual support
point; they do not prescribe a normal owner or a force equation.

**Fact A.** If S is a minimum-support point of a unit in a unit direction
n with negative world x component, every point of that unit has world
x coordinate strictly less than `S_x+1`.

At a vertex, express the unit using its two inward orthonormal side
axes. Both have nonnegative scalar product with n. At most one can
have a positive x component: if both did, their positive cone contains
East, contradicting `n_x<0`. That one positive component is strictly
less than one, since an inward East axis would have negative product
with n. At a side support, the inward normal has negative x component,
so positive horizontal reach is at most the remaining fraction of the
actual tangent side, also strictly less than one. This includes both
side endpoints and every original mean.

**Fact B.** From an actual maximum-y point P of a unit, positive
horizontal reach is at most one. If the unit contains a point with
x coordinate `P_x+1`, the unit is exactly

    [P_x,P_x+1] x [P_y-1,P_y].                     (7)

At a top vertex the inward axes have nonpositive y components; at most
one has positive x component. Equality of reach forces that axis to be
East and the other to be South. At a top-side mean, equality forces P
to be the side's left endpoint and gives the same rectangle. Notice
that (7) does not force the second point to have height P_y.
