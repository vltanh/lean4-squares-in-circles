## 2. Elementary full-unit bounds and the actual height

For any contained unit with center c and own orthonormal axes u,w, the
maximum squared vertex norm is

\[
|c|^2+\tfrac12+|c\cdot u|+|c\cdot w|
 \ge |c|^2+|c|+\tfrac12.
\]

Therefore

\[
|c|\le d+\tfrac12<\tfrac{91}{60},\qquad
d<\tfrac{61}{60},\qquad 0\le\epsilon<\tfrac1{60}.
\tag{5}
\]

The strict upper comparison has exact reserve

\[
(91/60)^2+91/60+1/2-Q_*=31/57600>0.
\]

Every physical boundary unit has center norm at least rho, since its
actual circle vertex is within its circumradius c0 of the center.
Every actual point p of that boundary unit satisfies

\[
                             |p|\ge R-\sqrt2=r,
\tag{6}
\]

by comparison with that same circle vertex and the unit diameter sqrt2.

A contained unit beyond a unit-normal support n.z>=s has s<=d,
because its support half-width is at least 1/2. Applying this to
both endpoints in (2) gives

\[
                 a,b\le d,\qquad a,b\ge1-d\ge-\epsilon.
\tag{7}
\]

Let H_X,H_Y be their horizontal support half-widths. They lie in
[1/2,c0]. The actual center coordinates are

\[
(c_X)_x=-a-H_X,\qquad (c_Y)_x=b+H_Y,
\]
\[
(c_Y)_x-(c_X)_x=1+H_X+H_Y\ge2.
\tag{8}
\]

Since both center norms are strictly below 91/60<2, (8) forces
(c_X)_x<0<(c_Y)_x, proving the first conclusion.

At least one of |a|,|b| is at most 1/2. Indeed if both are nonnegative,
their sum is one. If one is negative, its absolute value is at most
epsilon<1/60. Use (6) at the corresponding original application in (2)
to obtain h^2>=r^2-1/4. Since R>=2 and sqrt2<99/70,

\[
r^2-\tfrac14\ge\tfrac{23}{4}-4\sqrt2
 >\tfrac{13}{140}>\tfrac9{100};
\tag{9}
\]

the last reserve is 1/350. Thus h is nonzero and reflection makes
h>3/10, without changing an actual application or force relative to
the transformed physical configuration.

## 3. Both actual endpoint centers have the sign of the common height

Work in the reflected frame h>3/10. We prove (c_X)_y>0; horizontal
reflection and exchange of X,Y give the other endpoint identically.

Suppose (c_X)_y=-z<=0, with z>=0. Relative to its actual center, the
original right-support application P_X has displacement

\[
                         (H_X,h+z).
\]

Every actual point of a unit is within c0 of its center, so

\[
H_X^2+(h+z)^2\le\tfrac12,\qquad H_X\ge\tfrac12.
\tag{10}
\]

In particular 0<h<=1/2 and 0<=z<=1/2-h. The strict negative center
x coordinate already proved and its physical boundary norm give

\[
a+H_X\ge\sqrt{\rho^2-z^2}.
\]

All square roots here are positive: rho>1 and z<=1/2. Combining
with (10),

\[
 a\ge f(z):=
 \sqrt{\rho^2-z^2}-\sqrt{\tfrac12-(h+z)^2}.
\tag{11}
\]

On the entire interval 0<=z<=1/2-h, f is increasing. Its derivative is

\[
 f'(z)=
 -\frac{z}{\sqrt{\rho^2-z^2}}
 +\frac{h+z}{\sqrt{1/2-(h+z)^2}}>0.
\]

The second numerator is strictly larger and its denominator strictly
smaller: their squared-denominator difference is
rho^2-1/2+h^2+2hz>0. Hence

\[
                       a\ge\rho-\sqrt{\tfrac12-h^2},
\qquad
                       b\le u:=1-\rho+\sqrt{\tfrac12-h^2}.
\tag{12}
\]

Here u is positive and larger than epsilon. Indeed h<=1/2 gives
sqrt(1/2-h^2)>=1/2, while R<21/10 and c0>7/10 imply rho<7/5.
Thus u>1/10>epsilon. Equation (7) supplies b>=-epsilon, so |b|<=u.
At the other actual boundary application,

\[
|P_Y|^2=b^2+h^2
 \le u^2+h^2
 =(\rho-1)^2+\tfrac12-2(\rho-1)\sqrt{\tfrac12-h^2}
 \le\rho^2-3\rho+\tfrac52,
\tag{13}
\]

where rho>1 and sqrt(1/2-h^2)>=1/2 were used in the final step.

But (6) gives |P_Y|^2>=r^2=(rho-c0)^2, and

\[
r^2-\left(\rho^2-3\rho+\tfrac52\right)
 =(3-\sqrt2)\rho-2
 \ge 5-\tfrac72\sqrt2>0.
\tag{14}
\]

The lower bound uses rho>=2-1/sqrt2; its last strict sign follows from
sqrt2<10/7. This contradicts (13). Therefore (c_X)_y>0, including
the alleged equality (c_X)_y=0 and the weak endpoint h=1/2.
Reflection proves (c_Y)_y>0.

Only the actual support displacement, circumradius, boundary center norm
and the other actual port were used. No force equation at X or Y occurs.

## 4. Full physical endpoint inequalities

For completeness, the port-margin estimates are proved over their whole
owner and mask domain. The proof below actually does not require the
center-height conclusion of Section 3.

Let an actual boundary unit X have right-support application p=(-a,h)
with a>0,h>0. Then

\[
                            R^2\ge(a+1)^2+h^2.
\tag{15}
\]

If X owns this side direction, write its actual unchanged form

\[
                       X=[-a-1,-a]\times[B-1,B],
                       \qquad B-1\le h\le B.
\]

The far horizontal coordinate is -a-1. The maximum absolute vertical
coordinate is max(|B|,|B-1|), which is at least B>=h>0.
Thus (15) holds, without choosing a new mean or requiring B>=1/2.

Otherwise p is the actual unique exposed right vertex. Choose its
unchanged own side vectors

\[
u=(c,s),\quad w=(-s,c),\quad c,s>0,\quad c^2+s^2=1,
\]
\[
                    \operatorname{Vert}(X)=\{p,\ p-u,\ p+w,\ p-u+w\}.
\]

Put k=-ac+hs and j=as+hc>0.
The far own-w face is the positive one. Its physical far circle corner
is p+w if k>=1/2, and p-u+w if k<=1/2. The physical two-corner tie
is k=1/2 and is covered by either calculation.

In the adjacent-corner case k>=1/2 implies h>ac/s, and therefore

\[
j=as+hc>a/s\ge a,\qquad
R^2=a^2+h^2+1+2j\ge(a+1)^2+h^2.
\]

In the opposite-corner case put C=c+s,D=c-s. The exact excess is

\[
R^2-[(a+1)^2+h^2]=1+2a(C-1)+2hD.
\tag{16}
\]

If c>=s it is nonnegative. If c<s, the corner condition k<=1/2 gives
h<=(1/2+ac)/s. Since D<0, substitution in (16) gives the lower bound

\[
                         c/s+2a(1-s)/s\ge0.
\]

This proves (15) for all original owners and physical masks, including
the two-corner tie and all axis limits.

A second full endpoint bound is

\[
                R^2\le(a+1)^2+(h+1)^2\quad\hbox{when }h\ge a>0.
\tag{17}
\]

For a side owner, every transverse coordinate is between h-1 and h+1,
so its absolute value is at most h+1. The horizontal extreme is a+1.
For the opposite-corner case,

\[
aC+hD=(a+h)c+(a-h)s\le a+h,
\]

which yields (17) after expanding the actual corner norm. In the
adjacent-corner case j<=sqrt(a^2+h^2)<=a+h, so its radius expression
is even smaller than the right side of (17).

These are inequalities for all actual physical circle corners; they
impose no unique-corner force identity on a physical two-corner face.

## 5. Strict margins at both original ports

We first show a,b>0. If a<=0, then b>=1. Apply (15) to the actual
right endpoint after horizontal reflection. It gives

\[
                     h^2\le Q_*-(b+1)^2\le81/256.
\]

By (7), |a|<=epsilon<1/60. Thus

\[
                 |P_X|^2<(1/60)^2+81/256<(23/40)^2.
\]

The actual X circle vertex lies within sqrt2 of P_X, whence

\[
                 R\le|P_X|+\sqrt2<23/40+57/40=2,
\]

contradicting R>=2. Reflection likewise rules out b<=0.

Suppose 0<a<=7/100. If h<=a, then

\[
R\le|P_X|+\sqrt2\le\sqrt2(1+a)
 <\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac{107}{100}<2,
\]

again impossible. Thus h>a. Apply (17) at X and (15) at the reflected
Y:

\[
               (2-a)^2+h^2\le R^2\le(a+1)^2+(h+1)^2.
\]

Expansion gives h>=1-3a>0. Consequently

\[
R^2\ge(2-a)^2+(1-3a)^2
 =5-10a+10a^2
 \ge4349/1000>1105/256.
\tag{18}
\]

The quadratic is decreasing on [0,7/100]; its final exact reserve is
1043/32000. This proves a>7/100, and reflection proves b>7/100.

Every unchanged actual vertex of T has x coordinate -a or b. Thus
its norm is strictly greater than 7/100, regardless of its vertical
slider, side means or mean endpoints. This completes the theorem.
