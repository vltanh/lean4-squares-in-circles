\[

\[
                      |z+w-O|^2\le R^2=|z-O|^2,

\[
 C=[A-1,A]\times[B-1,B],\quad
 A\ge B\ge1/2,\quad A^2+B^2=R^2.

              C=[s_0-1,s_0]\times[-1/2,1/2].

## 3. The complete parallel alternative is impossible

Suppose n and g are parallel. Since (6)'s resultant is nonzero, their
axis is the axis of F_C.

### 3.1. Opposite directed incoming forces

If the directions are opposite, at least one incoming normal equals
-f. Its actual contact or common group face is the maximum-f face of C.
Since zbar belongs to C and points along f,

\[
                       h_C(f)\ge f\cdot(\overline z-O)
                                  =|\overline z-O|\ge s_0>d.
\tag{14}
\]

Its real neighbor is beyond that support, contradicting h<=d. For a
group face any original N_i is a genuine width witness, as in Section 2.
The argument covers both signs of the net force and all unequal
magnitudes, including values arbitrarily close to cancellation.

### 3.2. All incoming forces have the same direction

They must then all equal f. If the circle corner is unique, use (11)
with A>=B>1/2. The incoming direction is proportional to (A,B), so
every actual minimum-support application is the same inner vertex

\[
                               p=(A-1,B-1).
\]

The complete origin torque is a positive multiple of

\[
                         \det(p,(A,B))=A-B.
\]

It vanishes only when A=B. Then p is radial about O, as is the
horizontal direction (4), so the original channel port has y=0.
This contradicts h>3/10. No three-boundary-unit incidence premise
is needed.

For the physical two-corner mask use (12)--(13). If eta!=0, every
incoming application is the same original inner corner

\[
                        p=(s_0-1,-\operatorname{sign}(\eta)/2).
\]

Its moment against the full circle-mean direction is

\[
 \det(p,(s_0,\eta))
 =(s_0-1)\eta+\operatorname{sign}(\eta)s_0/2,
\tag{15}
\]

which has the strict sign of eta because s_0>1. This contradicts the
full torque, including eta=+/-1/2.

If eta=0, the incoming normal is the actual radial own axis and every
original application is on the minimum-radial side with support d.
Consequently a=d for C=X, or b=d for C=Y. The other actual port margin
equals 1-d. But

\[
s_0\ge\sqrt{15}/2>193/100,\qquad
d>93/100,\qquad 1-d<7/100,
\tag{16}
\]

contrary to (3). The exact squared reserve for (16) is
15/4-(193/100)^2=251/10000>0.

All original means on that radial side were retained; none was assumed
equal to the weighted mean or its midpoint. The complete parallel

## 4. A full nonparallel two-group concurrence theorem

We now prove the exact rectangle statement needed once n and g are
nonparallel. This subsection needs only the loaded C equations (6),
the two original support points P,Pbar, and the real width witnesses
from Section 2. It imposes no physical boundary status at a neighbor.

Let q be the intersection of P+R*n and Pbar+R*g. Taking the origin
moment of (6) at that intersection gives

\[
                               \det(q-O,F_C)=0.
\tag{17}
\]

### 4.1. Coincident actual and weighted applications

If P=Pbar, then q is that actual point of C. For every physical circle
corner z,

\[
2(q-O)\cdot(z-O)
 =|q-O|^2+R^2-|q-z|^2
 \ge |q-O|^2+R^2-2>0.
\tag{18}
\]

Summing with all original nonnegative circle coefficients shows
(q-O).F_C>0. Equation (17) therefore makes q-O a positive multiple
of F_C, and both proper distances are zero. Every coincident mean
endpoint is included.

In the rest of the proof P and Pbar are distinct.

### 4.2. Unique physical circle corner

Use (11) with B>1/2. Write

\[
                              q-O=\tau(A,B).
\tag{19}
\]

For 1-1/A<=tau<=1, q belongs to C: its first coordinate is in
[A-1,A], and B*tau>=B-B/A>=B-1. Since A>=R/sqrt2>=sqrt2>1,
tau is strictly positive throughout this interval.

We exclude every other tau by the complete rectangle normal-line cover.
At a side interior the line is perpendicular to the actual side; at
a vertex its direction, up to sign, belongs to the full original
normal cone.

If tau>1, q is strictly above and right of C. Only the outer circle
corner and the opposite inner corner can have such normal lines;
no side-interior application is possible. The circle corner is
unavailable, leaving only one application, contrary to distinctness.

If tau<1-1/A, then q_x<A-1. For q_y<B-1 the same normal-cone check
leaves only the inner corner and outer circle corner, again impossible.
At q_y=B-1 there is also the lower-right horizontal outward contact,
whose support A>=R/sqrt2>R-1 excludes its real width witness.

In the remaining range

\[
                     q_x<A-1,\qquad B-1<q_y<B,
\tag{20}
\]

the only possible non-circle applications are a left-side point, a
right-side horizontal point, and the lower-right corner (A,B-1).
The right side has the same impossible support A>R-1. Two distinct
applications therefore require that lower-right corner. Its outward
unit normal is (cos xi,-sin xi), with

\[
                       0\le\tan\xi<(A-B)/A.
\tag{21}
\]

The endpoint zero has already been covered, but is kept in the following
closed estimate. Its support satisfies

\[
                  A\cos\xi-(B-1)\sin\xi
                              \ge R/\sqrt2>R-1.
\tag{22}
\]

For a full verification, put e=A-B>=0. The left side is positive on
0<=tan xi<=e/A: if B>=1, its positive cosine factor is multiplied
by A-(B-1)tan xi>=A-(B-1)>0; if B<1 positivity is immediate. Its
second derivative is its negative, so it is concave and has a minimum
at an endpoint. At xi=0 it equals A>=R/sqrt2. At tan xi=e/A put

\[
                   N=A^2+e(1-B)>0,\qquad D^2=A^2+e^2.
\]

The endpoint value is N/D, and the exact identity is

\[
2N^2-R^2D^2
 =e\{2B^2(2-B)+e(2+4B-3B^2)+e^2(4-2B)\}\ge0.
\tag{23}
\]

All bracket coefficients are positive for 1/2<=B<3/2, since
B<=R/sqrt2<3/2. Positivity of N follows directly if B<=1, and if
B>1 from N>A^2-A(B-1)=A(A-B+1)>0. Finally R/sqrt2>R-1 because
R<2+sqrt2. This proves (22) on the whole angle interval, including
A=B. The real width witness contradicts (22), completing (20).

Thus q is inside C and tau>0. At each actual minimum-support point
P or Pbar, the difference q-P or q-Pbar is along its inward normal
and has nonnegative coefficient, by the support half-plane. Therefore
all of (7) holds in the unique physical mask.

### 4.3. The entire physical two-corner mask

Use (12)--(13) and write

\[
                         q-O=\tau(s_0,\eta),\qquad|\eta|\le1/2.
\tag{24}
\]

If tau<(s_0-1)/s_0, then q lies to the left of C. For |q_y|<1/2,
the normal-cone cover permits only an inner-side application and an
outer horizontal-side application. The latter has support s_0>R-1,
contradicting its real width witness. For |q_y|>1/2 only one
non-circle inner vertex remains. At |q_y|=1/2 the additional outer
horizontal contact has the same impossible support. In each case
two distinct applications are impossible.

For (s_0-1)/s_0<=tau<=1, q lies in C, since s_0>1 and
|eta|<=1/2. The preceding support-half-plane argument gives both
proper distances and tau>0.

For tau>1, if |q_y|>1/2 the same rectangle feature check leaves
at most one non-circle application. If |q_y|<=1/2, every surviving
non-circle application is on the inner side or at one of its endpoints;
the outer horizontal face is excluded by its support s_0>R-1.
Because q_x>s_0, its vector from each such application points along
that application's inward normal with a nonnegative coefficient.
This includes |q_y|=1/2 and does not assert q belongs to C.

These alternatives exhaust every tau and every eta, including eta=0
and +/-1/2. Hence tau>0 and both proper-ray equations in (7) hold
for the entire physical two-corner mask, with zero individual circle
weights still allowed.

The proof also covers all original side-interior, endpoint and
vertex-normal applications at Pbar: it uses only the actual face,
non-circle property and width witness proved in Section 2.
