## 4. The actual near P must use the other shallow side of H

The three original triangle applications are V, Z_0 and T_H.
They are distinct: V,Z_0 have opposite heights on x=epsilon,

Section 1; it needs no stress, degree or circle premise on the
further unit.

Choose the actual q in P intersect D_epsilon. In own coordinates

    eta(q)<=-tau, xi(q)>-sigma, and tau<=epsilon.      (12)

Suppose eta(q)>-tau. If also xi(q)>-sigma then q is in
int H, since both far own sides are above epsilon. That is
impossible for a distinct actual unit P. If xi(q)<=-sigma,
q is on the exterior side of the min-v line through VT_H.
The disk also gives ordinary x(q)<=epsilon and y(q)>=-epsilon
>-1/2. These are precisely the two sides of the triangular
wedge between VZ_0 and VT_H, above the opposite edge Z_0T_H.
Thus q is in int Delta or in a relative interior of one of the
edges VZ_0,VT_H. It cannot be V, because |V|>epsilon, and
cannot be on the opposite edge below -1/2.

In the open-triangle case int P meets int Delta. In an edge case,
the appropriate occupied unit A or H fills the local half-plane
opposite Delta. Interior points of P approaching q avoid that
closed occupied unit, so also enter int Delta. Both conclusions
contradict the preceding complete area argument. Hence
eta(q)<=-tau, which gives tau<=epsilon.

    x(q)=epsilon-c(xi(q)+sigma)-s(eta(q)+tau)>=epsilon.

The inequality is strict unless both parentheses vanish. Strictness
contradicts q in D_epsilon; equality would give q=V, again outside
that disk. Thus xi(q)>-sigma, proving (12).
