## 2. Complete cut work, including every internal edge

Let K be a nonempty unloaded set with nonempty original positive cut.
Let T be its set of distinct external positive neighbor bodies. T is
disjoint from K; its members may have arbitrary full degrees and loads.

For an original positive pair i--j, orient the unit normal n from i
toward j and denote its magnitude by mu. If c_i is the actual center,
and u_i,v_i are orthonormal own axes, put

    h_i(n)=(|n.u_i|+|n.v_i|)/2 >= 1/2.

The actual common support and full unloaded translations are

    n.(c_j-c_i)=h_i(n)+h_j(n),
    sum_(all original positive neighbors j) mu_ij n_ij=0
        for every i in K.                                      (2.1)

For every cut pair orient n outward from K toward its actual terminal.
Every internal undirected pair is counted once in

    C = sum_cut mu > 0,
    H = sum_cut mu h_i(n),
    M = sum_internal mu,
    I = sum_internal mu (h_i(n)+h_j(n)).                        (2.2)

Thus H>=C/2 and I>=M. Different pairs with the same outside terminal
are distinct cut summands; no outside body is duplicated geometrically.

Suppose a closed disk of radius delta centered at an auxiliary point o
meets every terminal. Choose z_t in the actual terminal t with
|z_t-o|<=delta. For a cut i--t at its unchanged application p,
the terminal minimizes n at p and i maximizes n there. Consequently

    n.(c_i-o) = n.(p-o)-h_i(n)
               <= n.(z_t-o)-h_i(n)
               <= delta-h_i(n).                               (2.3)

Dot each complete equation (2.1) with c_i-o and sum over K. The two
terms of every internal pair combine to its negative support work.
Hence

    0 = -I + sum_cut mu n.(c_i-o) <= -I+delta C-H,
    W := delta C-H-I >= 0.                                    (2.4)

This is the full terminal-ball inequality, not a sum along selected
paths. Internal cycles, chords, branching, and shared means are still
present. Changing o changes no physical point or force.

If all external terminals have a common actual point, delta=0 in
(2.4) is impossible because C>0 and H>=C/2. This includes an unloaded
private component whose only outside terminal is one actual unit.
More generally any terminal hitting disk of radius less than 1/2
excludes every such unloaded module. At radius 1/2 it excludes a module
with an internal positive edge. These assertions need no connectivity;
when used for a component below, its positive cut is checked explicitly.

## 3. Exact force compensation on the actual cut-host bodies

For each i in K let C_i be the sum of magnitudes of its original cut
edges and let F_i be their vector sum. Set both to zero when i has no
cut edge. Define the actual external-force cancellation

    D = sum_(i in K) (C_i-|F_i|) >= 0.                         (3.1)

The quantity is zero at a body with exactly one cut edge. It measures
force cancellation at the real body, not at a fictitious replacement
of K. Define also

    E_cut = H-C/2 >= 0,
    E_int = I-M >= 0,
    Xi = sum_(i in K)
         [sum_(internal edges incident to i) mu
          - |sum_(internal edges incident to i) mu n_ij|]
       >= 0.                                                  (3.2)

Every original unloaded translation says that the internal vector sum
at i is -F_i. Each internal pair contributes its magnitude at both
ends, so

    sum_i |F_i| = 2M-Xi,
    D = C-2M+Xi = C-2I+2E_int+Xi.                             (3.3)

Substitute I=delta C-H-W from (2.4). This gives the exact identity

    D = 2(1-delta)C + 2E_cut + 2E_int + Xi + 2W.               (3.4)

All four terms after the first are nonnegative and retain actual
support-width and triangle-inequality reserves. In particular

    D >= 2(1-delta)C,
    sum_i |F_i| <= (2delta-1)C.                               (3.5)

The second inequality is useful for delta<1. It is not an assumed
lower bound on individual force coefficients or on internal path size.

## 4. One actual terminal-contact anchor

Suppose there is one actual unit square S such that every terminal in
T either equals S or physically touches S. The touching may be a
singleton, a shared segment, or a contact whose selected weight is
zero. The anchor S need not be loaded, need not have a positive pair
to each terminal, and need not itself belong to T. It can also belong
to K; no force equation at S is removed in that case.

Every actual point of S is at distance at most

    sigma=1/sqrt(2)

from c_S. Each terminal therefore meets the disk D_sigma(c_S), by
its actual contact with S or by being S itself. Apply (3.4) at that
unchanged anchor center. The complete necessary budget is

    D = (2-sqrt(2))C + 2E_cut+2E_int+Xi+2W
      >= (2-sqrt(2))C.                                      (4.1)

Let C_one be the total cut-force magnitude at bodies of K having
exactly one cut edge, and C_many its total at bodies having at least
two cut edges. Since D_i=0 at a one-exit body and 0<=D_i<=C_i at every
body,

    C_many >= (2-sqrt(2))C,
    C_one <= (sqrt(2)-1)C.                                  (4.2)

In particular:

**Terminal-anchor theorem.** An entirely unloaded module whose every
cut-host body has at most one original cut edge cannot have all its
terminal units physically touching one actual anchor unit (with the
anchor itself also permitted among the terminals).

This is a whole-module contradiction: if every host has at most one
cut edge, D=0 while C>0 and 2-sqrt(2)>0. It remains valid for any
number of cut edges, repeated terminals, arbitrary internal cycles,
and arbitrary external forces at the terminals.

There is also a literal angular consequence. By (3.5), at least one
cut host satisfies

    |F_i|/C_i <= sqrt(2)-1 < 1/2.                            (4.3)

Its cut normals cannot all lie in any closed cone of aperture 120
degrees: projection on that cone's bisector would make their resultant
at least C_i/2. This statement concerns all original cut normals at
one real body. It does not claim that every pair of those normals
has a large angle, or charge the body one extra channel width.

## 5. Three mutually touching terminals force a positive load

Suppose T is contained in three distinct actual units A,B,C that
physically touch pairwise. Choose actual contact points

    z_AB in A intersect B,
    z_BC in B intersect C,
    z_CA in C intersect A.

Each side of the triangle of these three points is a segment in one
of the actual terminal units. Its length is at most sqrt(2), the
diameter of a unit square. If the triangle is degenerate, the median
of its three collinear points belongs to all three closed side
segments, hence to all three terminals. Then delta=0 applies.

Otherwise take the triangle's incenter and inradius r. The three
perpendicular feet lie on its actual side segments, so the disk of
radius r meets every terminal. Write a,b,c for the side lengths and
s=(a+b+c)/2. Heron's identity and the arithmetic-geometric-mean
inequality give

    r^2 = (s-a)(s-b)(s-c)/s <= s^2/27 <= 1/6,                (5.1)

because s<=3sqrt(2)/2 and the three nonnegative factors sum to s.
Thus r<=1/sqrt(6)<1/2. Equation (2.4) excludes every nonempty unloaded
K with a positive cut only to these terminals.

This includes all cut multiplicities, shared means, and host patterns;
the terminals need not have positive selected pairs among themselves.
With one terminal, or two physically touching terminals, the common-
point argument already applies. Equivalently, any actual private
positive network supported only by a physical triangle of terminals
must contain a positive selected circle load. A physical circle point
with zero selected coefficient does not discharge that conclusion.

## 6. A force-free angular bound for two touching neighbors

Here U,A,B are three distinct actual unit squares. A and B touch each
other, and each touches U. Let n_1,n_2 be any of the actual outward
unit support normals from U to A and B, respectively, and p_1,p_2
their original applications or means. For all positive weights a,b,
we prove

    |a n_1+b n_2|/(a+b) >= 1/(1+sqrt(7)).                    (6.1)

No equilibrium, radius, circle contact, normal ownership, or degree
is assumed in this lemma. All shared means and axis endpoints remain.

If n_1.n_2>=0, the left side is at least 1/sqrt(2), which is larger
than the proposed bound. Suppose n_1.n_2<0 and place the auxiliary
origin at c_U, using any actual orthonormal own frame of U. At least
one product of corresponding normal components is negative. Both
components in that coordinate are therefore nonzero and have opposite
signs. Their two actual support applications lie on opposite own
faces in that coordinate. Consequently, with m=(p_1+p_2)/2,

    |p_1-p_2|>=1,       |m|<=1/2.                            (6.2)

The second assertion holds because that midpoint coordinate is zero
and its other own coordinate belongs to [-1/2,1/2]. This remains true
if either application is an arbitrary full mean on an owned side.

Choose any actual z in A intersect B. Both p_1,z belong to A and
both p_2,z belong to B, so their distances are at most sqrt(2).
The parallelogram identity then gives

    |z-m|^2=(|z-p_1|^2+|z-p_2|^2)/2-|p_1-p_2|^2/4
             <=7/4,
    |z| <= (1+sqrt(7))/2.                                  (6.3)

Since A is beyond U's n_1 support and B is beyond its n_2 support,

    (n_1+n_2).z >= h_U(n_1)+h_U(n_2) >= 1.

Together with (6.3), this implies
|n_1+n_2|>=2/(1+sqrt(7)). Finally, the elementary exact identity

    |a n_1+b n_2|^2/(a+b)^2
      = 1-2ab(1-n_1.n_2)/(a+b)^2
      >= (1+n_1.n_2)/2 = |n_1+n_2|^2/4

proves (6.1). In particular opposite normals are impossible for this
actual touching-neighbor configuration. No diagrammatic choice of a
common contact point at U was made.

## 7. Complete three-edge-cut host classification at a terminal path

Let K now be connected in the original positive graph, unloaded, and
have exactly three original positive cut edges. Suppose their three
distinct terminal units form an actual physical path A--B--C: A
touches B and B touches C. A may or may not touch C. The three
contacts leaving K need not be singletons; every owner and every
original shared mean is retained. There is no restriction on the
internal graph of K or on its true degrees.

The middle unit B is a terminal-contact anchor. Classify the number
of real K bodies carrying the three cut edges.

* Three distinct hosts are impossible by Section 4.
* If all three cut edges have one host U, connectedness forces K={U}.
  Indeed each nonempty positive component of K-{U} would have a
  nonempty positive cut only to U, because all original external
  cut edges are at U. Section 2 excludes every such unloaded component.
* Otherwise there are exactly two hosts U,V. One has two cut edges
  and the other has one. The paired terminal units at U cannot
  physically touch each other, by the complete proof below.

For the last assertion, write a,b for the two magnitudes at U and
lambda for the singleton magnitude at V. Put S=a+b. Summing the full
translations over K cancels all internal forces, so

    |F_U|=|F_V|=lambda,
    lambda=|a n_1+b n_2|.                                 (7.1)

Summing the internal triangle inequalities over K gives
2lambda<=2M<=2I. Thus I>=lambda, while (2.4) and H>=C/2, at
delta=1/sqrt(2), give

    (S+lambda)/sqrt(2) >= I+H
                          >= lambda+(S+lambda)/2.

Rearrangement with positive denominators yields

    lambda/S <= (sqrt(2)-1)/(3-sqrt(2))
               = (2sqrt(2)-1)/7 =: kappa.                 (7.2)

If the paired terminal units touched, Section 6 would instead give
lambda/S>=1/(1+sqrt(7)). These two bounds are incompatible. For a
fully explicit radical comparison,

    1/(1+sqrt(7))-kappa
      = [7sqrt(7)-12sqrt(2)-1]/42 > 0,                    (7.3)

because sqrt(7)>21/8 and sqrt(2)<10/7, and hence its bracket is
greater than

    147/8-120/7-1=13/56>0.

The two squared comparisons are 7-441/64=7/64>0 and
100/49-2=2/49>0; all compared quantities are positive.

Therefore, if K has at least two bodies, the only remaining terminal-
path pattern has the two nonadjacent end terminals A,C attached to
one original host U, and the middle terminal B attached to another
original host V. If A and C also touch, Section 5 excludes K entirely.
This is a necessary remaining pattern, not a claim that it is realized
or that its internal cycles can be discarded.

## 8. Exact force and angle restrictions on that remaining pattern

In the remaining multi-body pattern, retain a,b at the A,C exits,
lambda at the B exit, and their actual unit normals n_A,n_C. Then
(7.2) holds with no missing original force. The reverse triangle
inequality and the preceding weighted-unit-vector identity give

    |a-b|/(a+b) <= lambda/(a+b) <= kappa < 2/7,
    5/9 < a/b < 9/5,                                      (8.1)
    n_A.n_C <= 2kappa^2-1
              = -(31+8sqrt(2))/49 < -6/7.                 (8.2)

For (8.2), write theta for the principal angle between the two normals.
The resultant ratio is at least cos(theta/2); apply (7.2). The last
strict inequality follows from sqrt(2)>11/8, whose squared reserve
is 2-121/64=7/64. The upper estimate kappa<2/7 follows from
sqrt(2)<3/2. Equation (8.1) follows by solving the two strict bounds
on |a-b|/(a+b), with a,b>0.

These are coupled statements: the two paired forces are comparable
and nearly opposite, while the third force is small relative to their
sum. They do not claim that an arbitrarily weak individual force
controls an angle, and do not replace K by a single unit or a path.

## 9. Whole graph families across body orders

The terminal-anchor theorem applies to any full unloaded module with
arbitrarily many cut edges provided each internal host carries at most
one cut edge and all actual terminals touch one actual anchor. Thus it
excludes entire branch/cycle families without checking any free-normal
factor and without assuming the module is an induced tree.

One concrete caller is the following. Let H be any connected unloaded
subgraph and suppose exactly three positive joins leave its full vertex
set, at three distinct vertices of H. Let the three distinct terminal
units be consecutive vertices A,B,C of an actual retained exterior
cycle, so the actual positive A--B and B--C contacts make a physical
terminal path. Then the entire feature is impossible, regardless of
the internal chords and branches of H, the three join owners or means,
the terminal loads/degrees, and every other outside contact. Extra
positive joins leaving H would violate the full-cut premise and cannot
be omitted.

If H is two-connected with h>=3, and the retained outside cycle has
k>=3 distinct actual vertices, the graph obtained by the three joins
at distinct vertices on both sides is two-connected. After deleting
one vertex, each constituent remains connected and at least two joins
survive. This supplies a literal single-block graph-family caller.

| Example unloaded H | Outside cycle size k | Total selected bodies h+k |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Triangle, arbitrary original positive weights | 3,4,5,6 | 6,7,8,9 |
| Four-cycle or diamond | 3,4,5 | 7,8,9 |
| Five-vertex two-connected graph, including K2,3 | 3,4 | 8,9 |
| Any six-vertex two-connected graph | 3 | 9 |

Only three consecutive terminals on the stated outside cycle are
required; the rest of that cycle and every outside chord are retained.
The table is not a physical-existence assertion or a complete list of
any coarse parent. It is one entire feature exclusion at several body
orders. Whenever h+k<9, all 9-h-k actual completion units remain.

Separately, Section 5 excludes an arbitrary unloaded private module
