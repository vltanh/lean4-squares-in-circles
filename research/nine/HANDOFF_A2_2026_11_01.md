### N. Global strict-radius boundary normalization — new reduction, no original roots closed

- Source: \`resume/research/nine/computer_assisted/BOUNDARY_NORMALIZATION.md\`.
- For the unrestricted *existential* \(q<Q\) packing problem, radially expand square centers until some corner reaches the \(Q\)-circle. Every formerly weak separating inequality becomes **strict**, so the expanded **closed squares** are pairwise disjoint. Conversely, a \(Q\)-disk packing with strict separators can have all centers contracted a little, recovering a packing below \(Q\). This proves a global equivalence, not a fixed-root normalization.
- A common isometry moves the touching corner to \((R,0)\), \(R=\sqrt Q\), and places the chosen square's center at \((R-(a+b)/2,(a-b)/2)\), with \(a^2+b^2=1\), \(a\ge b\), \(2Rb\ge1\). Its center belongs **strictly** to existing hex cell 17 in the normalized coordinate system. No individual relative frame is fixed.
- Crucial caveat: the common transformation changes the old positional root. Never impose the boundary normalization on an already fixed original root without tracking this map. The normalized strict-pair formula is a **separate equivalent global search domain**, not proof that A.2 has closed.
