## 1. Exact inherited physical facts

The genuine-leaf theorem and compatible nonradial bridge selection in
`RADIAL_BRIDGE_AND_LEAF_REDUCTION.md`, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.2,
give an outward radial unit vector e such that the actual D leaf has
inner vertex r e, outer vertex R e and center (r+1/sqrt(2))e.
Its vertex r e meets the relative interior of a side of H. Therefore
e is an actual own-side axis of H, and its own-coordinate intervals
along e and Je are

    H=[r-1,r] e + [a-1,a] Je,       0<a<1.             (1)

The original radius window gives

    1/2<r<2/3,                 1/3<1-r<1/2.           (2)

For the lower bound, sqrt(2)<59/40 and R>=79/40 suffice. For the
upper bound, use R<52/25 and sqrt(2)>140/99, which give
r<1648/2475<2/3. The two strict radical comparisons follow from
(52/25)^2-Q=1599/160000>0 and 2-(140/99)^2=2/9801>0.
In particular (1) implies

    O belongs to int H.                                (3)

This conclusion retains every positive force at H. It is not the
equilibrium equation of a degree-one or degree-two root.

The selected physical embedding from
`PHYSICAL_BODY_GRAPH_UNCROSSING.md` places the physically boundary
leaf L on the exterior face. Its actual positive degree is one.
The unique face incident to its pendant edge HL is consequently the
unbounded face, so H is exterior to the full selected graph and to
every retained subgraph containing H. Equivalently, a positive edge
from a strictly enclosed H to this exterior L cannot cross a retained
positive Jordan cycle.

We use the degree-free enclosed-four-ring equality theorem in
`SPANNING_K23_LEAF_BLOCK_EXCLUSION.md`, Section 2. If a positive
four-cycle encloses an actual unit T, the five actual units form an
aligned cross: T is central, and the four ring units share its four
complete sides in their original cyclic order. That complete proof
retains coincident applications, shared-side mean endpoints and weak
junction routing. It imposes no leaf, boundary-count, force-degree
or radius hypothesis. We use only this unconditional equality lemma,
not the later more restrictive leaf-block theorem in that source.

Finally every actual contained unit E has

    |c_E| <= d+1/2.                                     (4)

This is the sharp contained-center bound in
`END_TRIANGLE_RESULTANT_SUPPORT_BOUND.md`, Section 1: its farthest
corner has squared norm at least |c_E|^2+|c_E|+1/2. No stress or
physical circle contact is needed for (4).

## 2. The forced aligned cross contains the original origin

Assume a contrary positive four-cycle encloses an actual unit T.
Use the equality theorem above without changing any physical square.
In its actual common frame, let (x,y) be the central center. Reflect
the axes and relabel the four ring members so that x,y>=0. Their
centers are

    (x+1,y), (x,y+1), (x-1,y), (x,y-1).                 (5)

The right and upper units have actual corners (x+3/2,y+1/2) and
(x+1/2,y+3/2). As R<=sqrt(Q)<21/10, containment gives

    0<=x<3/5,                 0<=y<3/5.                (6)

If x>1/2 and y>=1/2, the squared norm of the right corner is
strictly greater than 2^2+1^2=5>Q. The same argument with the
two axes exchanged excludes y>1/2 and x>=1/2. Consequently:

- If x,y<=1/2, the closed central square T contains O.
- If x>1/2, then y<1/2 and 1/2<x<3/5<3/2. The open left ring
  square contains O.
- If y>1/2, the open bottom ring square contains O.

This exhaustive alternative retains x=1/2 and y=1/2 as well. Thus
at least one of the five actual cross units contains O. By (3),
that unit must be the very same physical square H: every different
closed unit containing O has interior points arbitrarily near O,
and those points would lie inside the open neighborhood int H.
An origin-on-boundary cross alternative cannot survive, since its
identification with H would contradict (3).

We have proved that H is either the central unit T or a ring unit.
This identifies original physical bodies by their interior disjointness;
it does not merge equal-coordinate vertices in a reduced stress graph.

## 3. The genuine D root cannot be the central unit

If H=T, its center is strictly inside the embedded positive ring.
The genuine leaf L cannot be a ring member: each ring member already
has two distinct positive ring neighbors, whereas L has true degree
one. The physical boundary leaf L is exterior to the selected graph
and therefore outside the ring. The original edge HL would then join
the interior H to the exterior L, with no endpoint on the cycle,
contradicting the same plane embedding.

This is the true pendant-edge argument of Section 1. No other edge
is suppressed, and no boundary vertex is inferred merely from a
chosen positive atom when the physical circle contact is absent.

## 4. Every ring position forces an actual side of H to be shallow

Suppose H is a ring member. Rotate the actual common frame so the
central unit is immediately to its left. By O in int H, the cross
has the exact chart

    H=[-sigma,1-sigma] times [b,b+1],
    T=[-sigma-1,-sigma] times [b,b+1],
    W=[-sigma-2,-sigma-1] times [b,b+1],                (7)

with sigma>0 and W the opposite ring member. Apply (4) to this same
actual W. Its first center coordinate is -sigma-3/2, hence

    sigma+3/2 <= |c_W| <= d+1/2,
    0<sigma<=d-1<1/60.                                (8)

In particular d>1 is derived from the contrary cross, so the entire
domain d<=1 is already excluded. The theorem has not assumed a
smaller radius window at its start.

The D-leaf normal e in (1) is a side axis of H. If e were parallel
to the horizontal direction of (7), one of the two distances r or
1-r would have to equal sigma. Equation (2) makes both strictly
greater than 1/3, while (8) makes sigma<1/60. This is impossible.
Therefore e is vertical. Reflect the vertical axis if necessary so
e points downward. The actual root and actual D leaf are now

    H=[-sigma,1-sigma] times [-r,1-r],
    L={ (u,v): |u|+|v+r+1/sqrt(2)| <= 1/sqrt(2) }.    (9)

No leaf slider is fixed by choice here: the transverse interval of
H is exactly its actual interval (7), while the genuine radial leaf
already has its actual inner vertex (0,-r).

## 5. A fixed strict interior overlap excludes the ring position

The lower ring member adjacent to the central T is the actual unit

    B=[-sigma-1,-sigma] times [-r-1,-r].               (10)

Take the fixed point

    q=(-1/5,-r-3/10).                                 (11)

As 0<sigma<1/60<1/5, its first coordinate lies strictly between
-sigma-1 and -sigma. Its second coordinate lies strictly between
-r-1 and -r. Thus q belongs to int B.

On the other hand, 1/sqrt(2)>3/10 and (9) give

    |q_x|+|q_y+r+1/sqrt(2)|
       =1/5+1/sqrt(2)-3/10
       =1/sqrt(2)-1/10
       <1/sqrt(2).                                    (12)

Therefore q also belongs to int L. The units B and L are distinct:
B is a ring member with its two original distinct positive ring
neighbors, and the true positive degree of L is one. This is a
strict overlap of two different original occupied units, contradicting
their pairwise interior disjointness.

Sections 2--5 exhaust both possible positions of H in the forced
cross. They prove the full four-ring theorem, including all physical
circle masks, slider endpoints allowed by genuine D contact, contact
means and graph degrees.

## 6. The whole positive K2,3 domain is excluded

Suppose five actual graph vertices contain all six positive K2,3
edges. In the same selected plane embedding, restrict the drawing
to these six edges only as a topological proof step. Its three
internally disjoint hub-to-hub paths have one page each. The boundary
of the exterior face is the simple four-cycle through the hubs and
two pages. The third page center lies strictly inside that cycle.

This is an actual unit enclosed by an actual positive four-cycle.
The genuine D leaf anywhere in the full selected graph is still
present with its true degree one, so the theorem just proved forbids
it. Every outside edge, physical unit and circle weight stays present
in the original equations. No degree-two equation is assigned to any
K2,3 vertex after restricting its drawing.

This proves the K2,3 corollary without a boundary-count premise or
any requirement that H belong to the K2,3.
