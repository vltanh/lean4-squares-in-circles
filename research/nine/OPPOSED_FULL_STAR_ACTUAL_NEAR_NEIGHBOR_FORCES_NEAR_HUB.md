## 1. Exact full-star theorem

Let U be unloaded. Its entire actual positive pair-force star consists
of an exceptional neighbor X and at least two distinct other neighbors
V_j. Write their actual outward unit normals, applications and positive
magnitudes as n,p,lambda and g_j,q_j,mu_j. Assume

    lambda*n+sum_j mu_j*g_j=0,
    lambda*det(p,n)+sum_j mu_j*det(q_j,g_j)=0,

    det(p,n)>0,
    det(q_j,g_j)<0 for every j.                         (1)

Assume that the actual X meets closed D_epsilon and that each actual
V_j avoids closed D_(sqrt(2)*epsilon). The V_j may have arbitrary true
degrees, additional positive forces and circle weights.

**Theorem.** The actual U meets closed D_epsilon. It is consequently
physically circle-clear. The same conclusion holds with all determinant
signs in (1) reversed.

The actual separating support at X supplies only

    h_U(n)=p.n<=epsilon.                              (2)

Indeed X lies in n.z>=p.n and contains a point of norm at most epsilon.
No negative-support premise is imposed. In particular (2) can be an
equality or can be positive. It is not inferred that X contains O.

We prove the theorem by supposing that the entire U avoids closed
D_epsilon. All signs and all forces below belong to the original full
star (1). A proper own-frame rotation preserves the signs. A single
whole reflection covers the reversed-sign version.

### 2.2. The strict support-span fact and the actual inward segment

    max_{z in T} w.(z-P)<1.                           (7)

## 3. The full two-missing-interval case

Suppose both own intervals of U miss zero. Choose a proper own frame
so that

    U=[a,a+1] times [b,c], a,b>0, c=b+1>1.             (9)

has support greater than one and squared point norm greater than two.

(2), leaves just West, strict Southwest, or strict Northwest at (a,c).
East and North have support greater than one, South and Southeast have
negative moment, and Northeast was excluded physically. This is the
entire feature cover.

For strict Southwest exceptional n, every negative-moment return has
nonpositive y component. Northwest, West and North have positive
moment; Northeast is unavailable. Full vertical balance is strictly
negative, impossible. For West exceptional n, the same cover and full
vertical balance force every return horizontal, hence East. At least
two distinct returns would be beyond x=a+1>1, contradicting (5).

It remains that

    p=(a,c), n=(-u,v), u,v>0, u^2+v^2=1.

The possible negative-moment returns are East, South, strict Southwest
and strict Southeast. Keep the following sums over the entire original
return star. E is the sum of East magnitudes. When E>0, y is their
actual weighted height and delta=c-y in [0,1]. By (5), every East
atom belongs to one actual neighbor, so that weighted point belongs
to that actual unit; in a pair-aggregate certificate it is already
the original mean. F,T are the sums of positive x and negative y
components of Southeast forces; S,W are the sums of negative x and
negative y components of Southwest forces; N is the sum of South
magnitudes, and N_x is the same sum weighted by their original
x-a in [0,1]. Thus 0<=N_x<=N. Full force and torque about (a,c) give

    lambda*u=E+F-S,
    lambda*v=T+W+N,
    E*delta+F-T-S-N_x=0.                              (10)

If E=0, its mean term is zero and no mean is introduced. Equations
(10) imply u<=v. Put k=v/u>=1. The original bound (2) gives

    a>=c*k-epsilon*sqrt(1+k^2)
       >=c-sqrt(2)*epsilon>39/40.                    (11)

The middle function is increasing for k>=1, because its derivative
is c-epsilon*k/sqrt(1+k^2)>0. For the last inequality use c>1,
sqrt(2)<3/2 and epsilon<1/60. The actual U corner (a+1,c) then has
squared norm greater than

    (79/40)^2+1=7841/1600>1105/256,

a contradiction. Therefore E>0. Its real outer neighbor and (3) imply

    0<a<=d-1<=epsilon.                               (12)

In particular d>1. From (2),

    c*v<=a*u+epsilon<=2*epsilon<1/30,
    v<1/30, u>29/30, kappa=v/u<1/29.                 (13)

Every Southwest return applies at the actual point (a,b). The far
return premise and (12) imply b>a: otherwise a^2+b^2<=2*epsilon^2.
Its negative moment -a*w+b*s<0 therefore gives w>s. Hence W>=S,
with equality W=S=0 if no such return is present.

Subtract kappa times the first equation of (10) from the second
and use its third equation. The exact full-star identity is

    E*(delta-kappa)
      =(1-kappa)*S-W-(1-kappa)*F+N_x-N<=0.            (14)

Every sign follows from (13), W>=S and N_x<=N. Thus delta<=kappa
and the actual East mean has

    y=c-delta>1-1/29=28/29>14/15.

Its East support is a+1>1, so (4) contradicts this actual mean.
The full two-missing-interval case is impossible.
