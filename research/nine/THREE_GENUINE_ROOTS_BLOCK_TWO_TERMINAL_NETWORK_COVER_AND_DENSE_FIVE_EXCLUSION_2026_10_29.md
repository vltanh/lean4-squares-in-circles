## 2. Each actual private component has exactly two root neighbors

Let K be a nonempty connected component of Q-T. Its vertices are, by
definition, different from all three genuine roots. Any Q edge from K to
a vertex outside K ends at one of A,B,C: otherwise it would connect two
different components of Q-T. Put N_T(K) for this actual root-neighbor set.

First N_T(K) is nonempty because Q is connected. If N_T(K) were {A},
removing A from Q would separate the nonempty K from the remaining actual
roots B,C. This contradicts biconnectivity. The same argument applies to
either other singleton. Hence |N_T(K)|>=2.

Suppose N_T(K)=T. Choose one actual K-to-root edge for each of A,B,C.
Because K is connected, there is a finite tree inside K joining their
three chosen endpoints. Add the three chosen root edges and take the
minimal subtree containing A,B,C. The three roots are leaves: each was
added through just its one chosen edge, and none is in K. Prune all
unneeded nonroot leaves. A tree with precisely three leaves has exactly
one degree-three branch vertex and no higher-degree vertex, after its
degree-two vertices are left as path vertices. This follows directly from

    sum_v (degree(v)-2)=-2.

Call that branch vertex V. It lies in K and is different from all three
roots. The three V-to-root paths are internally disjoint. Their internal
vertices are all in K, so no root lies inside another path. No genuine
leaf is in Q: its true degree one prevents membership in a biconnected
block. Thus none is on these paths either.

All three paths are original positive graph paths, so Section 1 excludes
them. This contradiction proves |N_T(K)|<=2. Combined with the first part,

    |N_T(K)|=2

for every nonempty actual K, proving the first assertion of Theorem A.

This proof includes the possibility that the three chosen K endpoints
coincide, when V is that same original vertex. No graph edge or root
adjacency outside the chosen tree is needed or removed from a force sum.

## 3. Every pair class is nonempty, including all direct root edges

Suppose, for example, the BC class were empty: there is no positive BC
edge and no component of Q-T attached to both B and C. Every private
component attaches to exactly AB or AC by Section 2.

Remove A from Q. Each AB component can then attach only to B, and each
AC component only to C. Distinct private components have no mutual edge.
The original AB and AC edges disappear under this graph deletion, and
BC was assumed absent. Consequently B and C lie in distinct components
of Q-A. This contradicts biconnectivity.

The same proof applies cyclically. Every pair class is therefore nonempty.
A direct root edge may be the sole member of a class, may coexist with
several private components for the same pair, or may be absent when an
actual private component supplies that pair connection. All those options
are included in Theorem A.

For a component K attached to A,B, connectedness supplies an original
A-to-B path with all internal vertices in K. It does not follow that the
whole K is just a path. Its original branching vertices, chords and loads
remain in place and are retained in all later physical arguments.

## 5. Exact loaded-path consequence between any two of the near roots

Here is a useful separate force statement with its true-degree premise
made explicit. Suppose an original positive path joins two of the roots
and every internal vertex has true positive degree two and zero total
circle load. All other forces at the two roots remain arbitrary.

Force and torque balance at each such actual internal square gives the
discrete normal-transmitter theorem: the two unchanged positive forces
have equal magnitude and opposite direction, their actual means lie on
one line, and the square's reserve is exactly 1 or sqrt(2). Along the
whole path these original directions agree consistently. If there are
m>=1 internal squares, the resulting original channel separates its
root endpoints by support width

    W=sum_j w_j >= m >=1.

Choose the actual radial near points z_A=epsilon e_A and z_B=epsilon e_B.
In the channel's A-to-B unit direction n its actual root support planes
give

    n.z_B-n.z_A >= W >=1.

But |z_A|=|z_B|=epsilon implies

    n.(z_B-z_A) <= |z_B-z_A| <=2epsilon<1,

a contradiction. Thus every such original path with true degree-two
internal vertices has at least one positive circle-loaded internal square.
In particular a single private vertex attached to two near roots, when
its true degree is two, must be loaded and physically boundary.

This argument uses actual support planes and the real transmitter widths.
It does not replace a branching private vertex by a transmitter or remove
an extra positive edge to fit the premise. If the entire positive component
is Q plus its three genuine leaves, true private degrees are exactly their
degrees in Q, so the stated premise is immediate for every private path
component in that exact caller.

same five vertices, with at least those two physical boundary units and
