## 2. The full small-radial-support cover fixes H's second shallow side

Consider every possible support feature of the actual rectangle H
in (2) for a radial support of norm epsilon, as in (1).

The face opposite n has distance 1-epsilon>epsilon from O.
Every vertex on that far face also has norm greater than epsilon.
Each vertex on the n-face has squared norm epsilon^2+y^2 with
y an endpoint of [a,a+1]. Since a<0<a+1, both endpoints are
nonzero; these vertex norms are strictly greater than epsilon.

Consequently (1) cannot be a strict vertex support or the opposite
n side. A side-axis support along n would have e=n, already
excluded. The only remaining features are the two transverse
side axes. Hence e is perpendicular to n and the actual side
height in direction e equals epsilon. The opposite transverse
side then has distance 1-epsilon. Both transverse sides cannot
have height epsilon, because 2epsilon<1.

Thus H has exactly the four side distances

    epsilon, 1-epsilon, epsilon, 1-epsilon,

and contains the full closed disk D_epsilon. Exactly two points
of its boundary have norm epsilon:

    epsilon n and epsilon e.                          (4)

All other points of that radius lie strictly in int H. This is
the complete side/vertex cover, including axis endpoints and all
original transverse mean endpoints.

Thus that leaf is S. Write its actual radial direction v. Its
root C contains the actual point epsilon v by the full inward

Equation (4) therefore forces v=n or v=e.

If v=n, C's genuine physical S leaf is exactly the B in (2),

This proves the uniform theorem without summing, replacing, or
