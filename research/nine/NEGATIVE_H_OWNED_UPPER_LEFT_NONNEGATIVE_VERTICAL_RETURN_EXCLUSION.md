## 2. Two whole-support projection observations

Set

\[
E=g\cdot j=su+cv>0,\qquad F=g\cdot n=sv-cu.
\]

Then \(E^2+F^2=1\), so \(E\le1\) and \(|F|<1\). No sign of F is assumed.

**H-support observation.** If m is an actual nonzero supporting normal from H toward a neighbor and \(m_x\ge0\), every original application Q on that supporting face satisfies

\[
                             (Q-P)\cdot g<1.
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed write \(m_n=m\cdot n\), \(m_j=m\cdot j\). If \(m_n>0\), its supporting face lies on H's front n-side. Thus
\((Q-P)\cdot g\le rE<1\), including every endpoint or original mean on that side. If \(m_n\le0\), the identity

\[
m_x=u m_n-v m_j\ge0
\]

forces \(m_j<0\), since m is nonzero and \(u,v>0\). The application then lies on H's bottom j-side, and

\[
(Q-P)\cdot g
\le\max\{0,-F\}+(r-1)E<1.
\]

This proves (5) for the full supporting-face and mean fan, including zero components of m in H's axes.

**C-return observation.** Suppose an actual return point has the form

\[
Q=W_1+\eta k+\zeta Jk,
\qquad0\le\eta\le1,\qquad0<\zeta<1.
\tag{6}
\]

Since \(Jk=-t f-p g\) and \(T-P=(0,1)=cf+sg\), one has

\[
\begin{aligned}
(Q-P)\cdot g
 &=s+1+\eta t-\zeta p\\
 &>s+1-p\ge1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The strict inequality uses \(p>0\) and \(\zeta<1\). It does not require \(\eta>0\), so every side-mean endpoint remains.

Sections 3--4 prove that every possible HC application has (6), then exhaust its actual normal.

## 3. Complete physically unique C fan

Assume first that C has one physical circle corner. Put \(w=W_1\cdot k\). Its minimum-k side is at coordinate w and contains \(W_1\) in its relative interior. True C2 proper concurrence, using its physical boundary neighbor B, gives

\[
q_C=W_1+\ell_C k=\kappa z_C\in C,
\qquad\kappa>0,\qquad\ell_C\ge0.
\]

Its Jk-coordinate is the negative number \(W_1\cdot Jk=-M\). Hence the actual physical circle corner has negative far Jk-coordinate. The complete C rectangle and corner are

\[
\begin{gathered}
C=[w,w+1]k+[-V,1-V]Jk,\qquad z_C=U k-VJk,\qquad V>1/2,\\
V-1<M<V,\qquad
\kappa=M/V\in(0,1),\qquad
\delta_C=V-M\in(0,1),\\
U=w< -1/2\quad\hbox{or}\quad U=w+1>1/2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{8}
\]

The two U alternatives are precisely the two possible actual far k-endpoints. The tie \(w=-1/2\) would give two physical circle corners and belongs to Section 4. None of these physical corner choices is made by discarding a zero multiplier.

Let h be the actual C-to-H normal, and let \(\gamma,\nu,\lambda_C>0\) be the unchanged BC, HC and unique circle magnitudes. The genuine C equations give

\[
\lambda_Cz_C=\gamma k-\nu h,
\qquad
\nu(h\cdot Jk)=\lambda_C V>0,
\qquad
\nu\det(Q,h)=\gamma M>0.
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently HC uses the maximum-Jk side or one of its two endpoints. These are all actual C support features with positive Jk normal component. Each application has (6), with \(\zeta=1-\delta_C\in(0,1)\) and its original \(\eta\in[0,1]\).

### The side and the right endpoint

If \(h=+Jk\), the H-to-C normal is \(m=-Jk\), whose ordinary first component is \(k_y\ge0\). Equations (5) and (7) contradict one another. This covers the complete C-owned side case, including every genuinely shared HC mean and both mean endpoints.

At the strict right endpoint

\[
Q=(w+1)k+(1-V)Jk,
\]

the normal h has strictly positive components along both k and Jk. Since \(k_x<0\) and \((Jk)_x=-k_y\le0\), one has \(h_x<0\), so \(m_x=-h_x>0\). Again (5) contradicts (7). This entire vertex feature is excluded geometrically, regardless of its owner.

### The left endpoint, with both physical far-k alternatives

The remaining strict endpoint is

\[
L=w k+(1-V)Jk,
\qquad h\cdot k<0,\quad h\cdot Jk>0.
\]

By proper C concurrence, its actual normal is a positive multiple of \(L-q_C\). This vector is nonzero because its Jk-component is \(1-\delta_C>0\). In the positively oriented \((k,Jk)\) coordinates its exact origin moment is

\[
\det(L,L-q_C)
=\kappa\{wV+U(1-V)\}.
\tag{10}
\]

If \(U=w<0\), the right side equals \(\kappa U<0\), contradicting the positive actual moment in (9). Thus \(U=w+1>0\). Equation (10) then becomes \(\kappa(U-V)\), so

\[
                               U>V>0.
\tag{11}
\]

Write the original proper ray as

\[
L-q_C=-A_0 k+B_0 Jk,
\qquad A_0=1-\delta_C U/V,\quad B_0=1-\delta_C.
\]

The strict vertex normal gives \(A_0>0\), while (8), (11) give

\[
                             0<A_0<B_0.
\tag{12}
\]

The two C applications \(W_1,L\) are distinct fixed physical singletons. The compatible GENERAL_FIXED_TIP_NORMAL_SELECTION.md theorem therefore excludes a free HC. Its strict C-vertex normal is not a C axis, so H owns HC. TRIANGLE_C4_THREE_LOAD_PARALLEL_EXCLUSION.md applies to the unchanged H4/intact-petal component and excludes both normals parallel to n. Hence the actual H-to-C normal is one of \(\pm j\).

Set \(k_n=k\cdot n\), \(k_j=k\cdot j\). Since \(k_x<0\) and \(k_y\ge0\),

\[
k_j=-v k_x+u k_y>0.
\]

The strict left-endpoint sign \(h\cdot k<0\) rules out H-to-C normal \(-j\). Thus it is \(+j\), and h is \(-j\). The positive Jk component of h gives \(k_n<0\). But

\[
k_j+k_n=(u-v)k_x+(u+v)k_y\ge0,
\]

using \(u\le v\). Therefore the actual normal satisfies

\[
\frac{|h\cdot k|}{h\cdot Jk}
 =\frac{k_j}{-k_n}\ge1.
\tag{13}
\]

The same ratio from its positive proper ray equals \(A_0/B_0<1\) by (12), a contradiction. This excludes the last physically unique C feature, without assuming any sign of \(c_H\cdot z_C\).

## 4. Complete physical radial C fan and both original weight endpoints

Now suppose C has two physical circle corners. Set

\[
K=\sqrt{q-1/4},\qquad d=K-1>0.
\]

Because k is a side axis of C, its radial axis is parallel either to k or to Jk. If it is parallel to k, the minimum-k side at the incoming BC contact is either the outer radial face or the inner radial face. The outer-face unit-width obstruction excludes every unit neighbor there. The inner-face obstruction in C7_FORCE_POLYGON.md, Lemma 2.1, excludes the actual physical boundary neighbor B for every original inner-face point. These are physical exclusions; they impose no circle-weight equality.

Thus the radial axis is parallel to Jk. Since the actual incoming coordinate is \(W_1\cdot Jk=-M<0\), its sign is forced, and the entire original C is

\[
C=[-1/2,1/2]k+[-K,-d]Jk,
\qquad W_1=-k/2-MJk,
\qquad d<M<K.
\tag{14}
\]

Both strict inequalities come from the actual side-interior singleton BC. Normalize the unchanged radial circle resultant as

\[
\lambda_C(\eta_R k-KJk),\qquad
\lambda_C>0,\qquad -1/2\le\eta_R\le1/2.
\]

This retains both individual zero-weight endpoints. The true proper concurrence is

\[
q_C=\kappa(\eta_R k-KJk),\qquad
\kappa=M/K\in(0,1).
\tag{15}
\]

No unsupported interior-concurrence assertion for a general radial body is used: here (14)--(15) explicitly put this actual point inside C.

Its genuine C2 force equation again makes \(h\cdot Jk>0\), and its original torque equation again requires \(\det(Q,h)>0\). The full HC support is therefore C's inner radial face or one of its endpoints. Every original application has (6), with \(\zeta=M-d\in(0,1)\), including every inner-face mean and endpoint.

The face normal \(+Jk\) and the strict right inner vertex are excluded by exactly the support comparisons (5), (7): their H-to-C normals have nonnegative ordinary first coordinate. This does not require either a fixed-tip selection at radial C or an ownership assumption.

At the sole remaining left inner vertex

\[

\]

the unchanged proper ray is

\[
L-q_C=(-1/2-\kappa\eta_R)k+(M-d)Jk.

Its exact origin moment is

\[

 =-\frac M2-d\kappa\eta_R

\[
\frac K2+d\eta_R\ge\frac{K-d}{2}=\frac12>0.
\]

This contradicts the required positive actual moment and includes \(\eta_R=\pm1/2\). Every physical radial C return is therefore excluded.
