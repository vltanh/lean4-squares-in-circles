The prism is biconnected, so each face boundary is a simple cycle.
For completeness, deleting any one vertex leaves the opposite triangle
connected, with both remaining same-triangle vertices joined to it by
their matching edges; hence the graph has no cutvertex.

Each displayed triangle is facial. Indeed, deleting that triangle's
three vertices leaves the opposite connected triangle. The entire
remaining graph, including the open matching-edge strands, is connected
and disjoint from the first triangle, so it lies on one side of that
Jordan curve. The other side has no graph edge or vertex and is a face.
There are no other graph edges with both endpoints on the first
triangle. This proves the assertion whether the empty side is bounded
or unbounded.

The two triangles are distinct faces. The prism has six vertices and
nine edges, hence five faces by Euler's formula. Its total face-boundary

edge sides. The remaining three boundaries have length at least four:
the only graph triangles are the two already displayed. Their lengths
sum to twelve, so each has length exactly four.

Thus every prism face, including whichever face is unbounded, has
boundary length at most four. No classification by sampled embeddings
or external graph enumeration is used.
