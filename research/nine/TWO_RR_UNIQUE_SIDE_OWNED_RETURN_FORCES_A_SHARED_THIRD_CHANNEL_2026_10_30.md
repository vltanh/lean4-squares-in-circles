## 2. A uniform loaded-star span obstruction

Here is a local theorem independent of the RR geometry. Let m,t be
orthonormal with det(m,t)=-1. Suppose an actual loaded square is

    D=q+[0,1]m+[-r,1-r]t, 0<r<1,
    z=q+m+(1-r)t,

with exactly one physical circle corner z. Suppose

    |z|=R<21/10, z_m=z.m>1/2, z_t=z.t>0.

Its complete original positive pair star has exactly two forces:
-nu*m at the actual side point q, and -mu*g at the actual vertex
p=z-t, with mu,nu>0. Its full circle force is chi*z, chi>0.
Assume that this second application belongs to an unchanged reserve-one
normal channel from an actual point a, so

    p-a=g=-X*m+Y*t, X>0, Y>0, X^2+Y^2=1.

**Lemma 2.1.** These full force and torque equations imply

    |q-a|^2>3.

Proof. Put k=1-r. The full force equation is

    chi*z=mu*g+nu*m.

Torque about z is equally literal. The q force contributes nu*k,
and the p force contributes -mu*X, because det(m,t)=-1. Thus

    nu*k=mu*X,
    z_m/z_t = X*r/(k*Y).

The ratio is legitimate: its denominator is positive and the full
t-force equation gives chi*z_t=mu*Y. Since z_m>1/2 and
0<z_t<=R<21/10,

    X*r/(k*Y)>5/21,
    r*Y>Y^2/((21/5)*X+Y).                         (2.1)

Also the actual channel relation gives

    a=q+(1+X)m-(r+Y)t,
    |q-a|^2=(1+X)^2+(r+Y)^2
            =2+r^2+2X+2rY.                         (2.2)

Three fixed exact scalar ranges now exhaust X in (0,1).

* If X>=1/2, (2.2) is strictly greater than 3.
* If 1/4<=X<1/2, then Y^2>3/4 and the denominator in
  (2.1) is less than 31/10. Hence rY>15/62. Since Y<1,
  r^2>(rY)^2. Therefore (2.2) is greater than

      2+1/2+2*(15/62)+(15/62)^2
        =3+163/3844.

* If 0<X<1/4, then Y^2>15/16 and that denominator is less
  than 41/20. Thus rY>75/164, and (2.2) is greater than

      2+2*(75/164)+(75/164)^2
        =3+3329/26896.

This proves the entire lemma. The scalar ranges prove one uniform
inequality; they are not sampled orientations or physical pose boxes.
Every original force is retained. The theorem is valid for any such
full true-degree-two loaded star, not only a seven-cycle.

## 3. The original two radial ports are strictly closer than sqrt(3)

The actual RR applications at B and A are respectively their lower

    a=p_A=d*e_A-(1/2)*Je_A
      =rho*(cos(2pi-9phi),sin(2pi-9phi)).           (3.1)

For the direct D-owned contact, B's two outer vertices are physical

    det((d,-1/2),((d+1),eta))

    q=(d,1/2)=rho*(cos(phi),sin(phi)).              (3.2)

The full radial means, including zero corner coefficients, are used

    m=(-c,s), c,s>0, c^2+s^2=1.                    (3.3)

An axial endpoint would align the side frames; since q is in the

Put c=cos(theta), s=sin(theta), 0<theta<pi/2, and
t=(s,c). This is an explicitly left-handed orthonormal basis:
det(m,t)=-1. We do not invoke a positively oriented port theorem

## 4. Complete physical chart for the D-owned side

Since q is in the relative interior of the original D side whose
inward normal is m, there is one actual k in (0,1) such that

    D=q+[0,1]m+[k-1,k]t.                           (4.1)

Write

    q_m=q.m=-d*c+s/2,
    q_t=q.t=d*s+c/2>1/2.

The last strict inequality follows from d>1/2, c,s>0 and
c+s>1. D's center t-coordinate is q_t+k-1/2>0, so its
farthest corners have t-coordinate q_t+k.

We must determine its m mask, not assume it. If q_m<=-1/2,
the farthest m-coordinate is q_m (a tie at equality). Also
q_m^2+q_t^2=rho^2 implies q_t<=d. The maximum squared corner
norm would then be

    |q+k*t|^2=rho^2+k^2+2k*q_t
              <rho^2+1+2d=R^2,

since k<1. This contradicts D's physical circle corner, including
the proposed tie. Consequently

    q_m>-1/2,
    z=q+m+k*t,
    z_m=q_m+1>1/2, z_t=q_t+k>0.                    (4.2)

Both center coordinates are now strictly positive, so this is
indeed the unique physical farthest corner. The circle equation is

    k^2+2q_t*k+2q_m=2d.                            (4.3)

One more consequence is needed for a complete projection cover.
Since q_m=-rho*cos(theta+phi)>-1/2=-rho*sin(phi), monotonicity
of cosine on (0,pi) gives

    theta>pi/2-2phi.

Thus beta=4phi-theta lies strictly between 0 and pi/2: its
lower bound is 4phi-pi/2>0, and its upper bound is

to q therefore has coordinates

## 5. The full original projection and force equations exclude all other faces

    p_D-a=g, |g|=1, dist(A,D)=1.

    (min(L,1), max(W,k-1)).                         (5.1)

If W>=k-1 and L<=1, a itself belongs to D and the distance

    p_D=q+m+(k-1)t=z-t,

the full D star is exactly its force toward U3, its force

    chi*z=mu*t+nu*m,

    nu*q_t=mu*a_m,
    (q_m+1)*q_t=(q_t+k)*(q_m+L).                    (6.1)

    q_t*(1-L)=k*(q_m+L).                           (6.2)
