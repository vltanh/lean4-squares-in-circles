## 2. All shared-perimeter direction patterns are covered

Positive-length shared contact makes the two actual square frames agree
up to axis signs and permutation. The perimeter is connected, so all
four block units have one common frame. A shared normal on either axis
gives a signed center increment exactly \(1\) on that axis; the other
increment has absolute value strictly below \(1\).

An odd count of normals on either axis is impossible. Three signed
unit increments sum to an odd integer of absolute value at least one,
and the single remaining tangential increment has absolute value
strictly below one.

If there are two normals on each axis and the two horizontal ones
are consecutive, their signed increments cannot agree: a sum of
magnitude two cannot be canceled by the two strict tangential
increments of the remaining edges. Hence they cancel. The same is
true of the two consecutive vertical increments. The two opposite
cycle centers then coincide, contradicting disjoint interiors.

Therefore a nonparallel pattern must alternate. Its two signed
increments on each axis must be opposite, by the same strict
tangential-increment comparison. This is the purely geometric
direction cover from ADJACENT_C4_ALL_SHARED_SS_RIGIDITY.md,
Sections 1.1 and 1.3; it requires no H3 or K2 equation.

### 2.1. The all-parallel pattern is impossible with the actual C2/K3

Suppose all four perimeter normals are horizontal. The actual true
degree-two equation at C makes its nonzero circle resultant horizontal.
For an aligned unit with nonzero center height, all its physical circle
corners have that same strict vertical sign. Thus its center height
must be zero. The physical C is a horizontal radial S unit, with
center \((\pm(d+1/2),0)\).

The actual KC shared side gives a horizontal center difference exactly
one and \(|(c_K)_y|<1\). The outward horizontal alternative for K
has \(|(c_K)_x|=d+3/2>d+1/2\), contradicting the universal
center bound \(|c_K|\le d+1/2\). The inward alternative gives
\[
                        |(c_K)_x|=d-1/2.
\]
Every actual K corner then satisfies
\[
                  |z|^2<d^2+\frac94
                       <(d+1)^2+\frac14=R^2,
\tag{4}
\]
because \(2d-1>0\). This contradicts its physical boundary contact.

No K force equation was used. C's two-circle physical mask and either
individual zero weight were retained in deriving its horizontal
resultant; a weight endpoint incompatible with that direction is
excluded by the equation itself, not removed from the domain.
Vertical parallel normals are handled by rotation.

## 3. Keep the complete sliding family and the actual central contact

We may now orient the derived alternating frame so that HB points
right, BK up, KC left and CH down. There are original real sliding
coordinates \(x,y,p,q\) with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H&=[x-1,x]\times[p,p+1],&
 B&=[x,x+1]\times[q,q+1],\\
 C&=[y-1,y]\times[p+1,p+2],&
 K&=[y,y+1]\times[q+1,q+2].
 \end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The actual positive shared lengths in (3) are retained. In particular
\[
                              |x-y|<1,\qquad |p-q|<1.
\tag{6}
\]

Physical HK contact requires
\[
                                  x\ge y,\qquad p\ge q.
\tag{7}
\]
Otherwise its horizontal or vertical projection intervals are disjoint.
If both inequalities were strict, their intersection would have positive
area, forbidden by interior disjointness. Thus the actual HK contact
is one of:

* a horizontal shared segment, with actual normal \(e_y\);
* a vertical shared segment, with actual normal \(e_x\);
* the common upper-right/lower-left vertex, with its actual strict
  first-quadrant free normal.

Accordingly its actual normal \(n=(n_x,n_y)\) always has
\[
                           n_x,n_y\ge0,\qquad n\ne0.
\tag{8}
\]
No normal is chosen at this stage. The selected original HK force,
with magnitude \(\eta>0\), is kept in every following equation.

Write \(\mu,\beta,\nu,\alpha>0\) for the actual forces on
HB,BK,CK,HC, respectively. The full root equations give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_{L_H}&=-\mu e_x-\alpha e_y-\eta n,\\
 F_{L_B}&=\mu e_x-\beta e_y .
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Both have two strict coordinate signs. Therefore their genuine
radial S applications are the actual outer lower root vertices:
\[
                    P_H=(x-1,p),\qquad P_B=(x+1,q),
                    \qquad |P_H|=|P_B|=d.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular
\[
                  -1<x<1,\qquad -d<p,q<0,
                  \qquad p^2-q^2=4x.
\tag{11}
\]
The two strict negative heights give \(1-x<d\), \(1+x<d\),
and hence \(d>1\). Thus the lower-radius interval and its equality
endpoint are excluded within this actual feature family.

The unchanged true C2 and K3 equations are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_C&=-\nu e_x+\alpha e_y,\\
 F_K&=\nu e_x+\beta e_y+\eta n .
 \end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Their signs are strictly upper-left and upper-right, respectively.
The actual center heights \(p+3/2,q+3/2\) are positive by (2),(11).

For an aligned unit with positive center height, a physical two-corner
circle mask would require its center abscissa to vanish and make it
vertical radial S. At C this would give \(p+1=d\), and at K
\(q+1=d\). Both contradict \(p,q<0\) and \(d>1\).
Thus both physical masks are uniquely determined as one circle corner.
The actual corners, with their signs from (12), are
\[
                 z_C=(y-1,p+2),\qquad z_K=(y+1,q+2),
                 \qquad |z_C|=|z_K|=R.
\tag{13}
\]
This step keeps the physical mask distinct from the positive-weight
stratum. It uses K's full equation with its \(\eta n\) term.

Subtracting the circle equations gives
\[
                            (p-q)(p+q+4)=4y.
\tag{14}
\]
Subtract (11) from (14):
\[
                                   y-x=p-q.
\tag{15}
\]
But (7) makes the left side nonpositive and the right side nonnegative.
Consequently
\[
                             p=q,\qquad x=y,\qquad x=y=0.
\tag{16}
\]
The last equality follows from (11).

This excludes every nonzero original slider at once, including both
possible shared central-HK alternatives. The entire physical perimeter

## 5. The full H4/K3 equations force the diagonal bisector

Use positive actual scalars
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_{L_H}&=\lambda_H(-1,-b),&
 F_{L_B}&=\lambda_B(1,-b),\\
 F_C&=\kappa_C(-1,2-b),&
 F_K&=\kappa_K(1,2-b).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The leaves' genuine radial equations and the proved unique physical
circle corners justify these scalars.

The unchanged B3 and C2 equations give
\[
                 \mu=\lambda_B,\quad \beta=b\lambda_B,\qquad
                 \nu=\kappa_C,\quad\alpha=(2-b)\kappa_C.
\tag{21}
\]
The original H4 and K3 equations, still including \(\eta n\), give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lambda_H&=\lambda_B+\eta n_x,&
 b\lambda_H&=(2-b)\kappa_C+\eta n_y,\\
 \kappa_K&=\kappa_C+\eta n_x,&
 (2-b)\kappa_K&=b\lambda_B+\eta n_y.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Eliminate only these actual scalars. The two resulting expressions are
\[
 \begin{split}
 b\lambda_B-(2-b)\kappa_C
   &=\eta(n_y-bn_x)\\
   &=\eta((2-b)n_x-n_y).
 \end{split}
\tag{23}
\]
Since \(\eta>0\),
\[
                                  n_x=n_y.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus the actual free HK normal must be the common-frame diagonal
bisector. This is a necessary identity for the full original stress,
not an assertion obtained by deleting HK.
