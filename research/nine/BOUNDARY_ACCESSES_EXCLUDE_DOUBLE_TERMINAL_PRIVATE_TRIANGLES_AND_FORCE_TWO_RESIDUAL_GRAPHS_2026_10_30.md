## 1. A circle-access obstruction for any three-path theta

Use the original compatible selected physical certificate and its one
plane body-graph embedding with the protected spokes of every physical
boundary body [P]. Let A,C be distinct actual vertices. Suppose three
actual simple positive paths P_0,P_1,P_2 join A to C and are pairwise
internally disjoint. Each has an internal vertex with an actual path
to a physical boundary body such that this access path meets the
displayed theta only at that internal vertex. An access path of length
zero is allowed when its starting vertex is already boundary.

**Theorem 1.** This configuration is impossible.

This is the whole three-access theta obstruction of [N], Sections 3--4,
stated explicitly for the new caller. For completeness, the union of
the three paths is a plane theta. The unbounded face of that union
uses exactly two of its bridges. Every internal vertex of the third
bridge is separated from the unbounded face by the simple cycle of
the other two bridges. Append the actual protected circle spoke and
an outward radial continuation to its hypothesized access. The graph
drawing is strictly inside the containing circle, the spoke meets it
only at its incident body, and the continuation outside the disk meets
none of it. This is an access from that internal bridge to the
unbounded face without crossing the theta, a contradiction.

The three access paths need not be disjoint from one another. In
particular the accesses from P_1 and P_2 may join at one actual body
and then use the same spoke. Each must separately avoid the theta
away from its own starting vertex, which is all the separation proof
uses. Extra positive edges can only add constraints; they cannot
remove the separating Jordan cycle. A physical circle body with zero
selected circle coefficient supplies the same protected access.

### 1.1. One actual boundary body may obstruct two private bridges

    A--B--C, A--x--C, A--z--C,

equilibrium, load or boundary condition at x or z is needed. The

The terminal C must then attach to y or z, and cannot attach to x.
