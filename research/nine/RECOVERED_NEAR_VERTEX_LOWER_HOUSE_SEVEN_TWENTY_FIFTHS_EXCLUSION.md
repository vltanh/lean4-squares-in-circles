## 2. The coincident and parallel return alternatives

Suppose first that A's actual AT and AD applications coincide. The full positive A force equation makes their resultant West. Their common point is therefore the unique maximum-West vertex

    W=P+f+g.

Torque about P forces W-P horizontal, so alpha=beta=1/sqrt(2) and W=(k-sqrt(2),y). Every original return normal n at W has nonnegative f,g components. Therefore

    n_x<=-1/sqrt(2), |n_y|<=1/sqrt(2).

For a unit square with an actual minimum-n support point W, the point W+n belongs to that same square. At an owned side this is the unchanged mean translated to the opposite side; at a vertex the two inward own components of n lie in [0,1]. This fact uses neither a chosen owner nor a chosen mean.

Consequently one actual returned square contains a point satisfying

    |W+n|^2
      >= (sqrt(2)-1/60)^2
          +1+sqrt(2)*(sqrt(2)-1/60-Y)
      > 5-(3/2)*(3/60+Y)
      =901/200
      >1105/256.                                      (4)

In the second line the positive 1/3600 term was discarded and sqrt(2)<3/2 was used. The last reserve is 1207/6400. This contradicts containment. The whole coincident alternative is excluded.

If the two return normals are parallel, full vertical balance makes them horizontal. Any East normal uses P and is excluded by the boundary distance. Thus both are West and have the same unique A vertex W, already excluded. This covers both parallel signs and every physical application.

Henceforth the two actual return applications are distinct and their original normals nonparallel.

## 3. Complete A feature cover and actual labels

Full A force balance gives opposite strict vertical signs for its two return normals. A horizontal return would force the other to be horizontal, the excluded parallel case.

The A axes -f and -g cannot occur. For example, if one return normal is -f, the physical owned-axis triangle rule makes the other return normal have nonnegative dot product with -f. Its f component is therefore nonpositive. The original East normal also has strictly negative f component, contradicting the complete A force equation. The -g case is identical.

Strict normals exposing P are already excluded by boundary distance. Every remaining original support feature belongs to one of the two complete chains

    upper chain: P+q*f+g, 0<=q<=1,
    lower chain: P+f+p*g, 0<=p<=1.                      (5)

The upper chain includes the strict upper adjacent vertex, the complete g-owned side with both endpoint means, and the opposite vertex. The lower chain similarly includes the strict lower adjacent vertex, the complete f-owned side, and the opposite vertex. Their strict vertical normal signs distinguish upper from lower; the upper contact is geometrically above the lower unless both are W, already excluded.

The actual house labels are fixed by the protected spokes, not by exchanging a T2 with a D3. Use one sufficiently close compatible plane embedding. The B order L,A,C, together with the real boundary access of L, makes A-B-C-D-A clockwise: its other orientation would place BL in its bounded sector and trap L. At A the three distinct ports consist of the rightmost P and the two ports in (5). If D were the upper return, the clockwise bounded sector of that four-cycle would contain AT and trap the actual boundary square T. Protected access through T's interior to a physical circle point excludes this. Thus

    P_T=P+q*f+g, P_D=P+f+p*g, 0<=p,q<=1,               (6)

where T is the true loaded degree-two tip and D remains the true loaded degree-three return. In particular the A port rays have counterclockwise order B,T,D.

In the f,g coordinates the two actual contact points are

    P_T=(q-b,a), P_D=(1-b,a-1+p).

Their global determinant is

    det(P_T,P_D)
      =a*(1-b)-(q-b)*(a-1+p).                          (7)

This is bilinear in p,q. Its four corner values are a-b, a, 1-b, 0; the sole zero corner is p=q=1. All other values are strictly positive because a>a0>Y>|b|. Thus the actual chord P_T to P_D avoids O and has strictly positive angular advance.

## 4. Positive moment of the original upper T force

Let M_T,M_D denote the original positive-magnitude-weighted moments of A-to-T and A-to-D. If gamma>0 is the unchanged AB magnitude, full A torque gives

    M_T+M_D=gamma*y>0.                                 (8)

Suppose M_T<0. Then the unchanged directed triangle A-to-D-to-T-to-A has all three positive edge moments. At A its incoming contact is P_T and outgoing contact P_D; (7) gives a strictly positive advance along their actual chord. At each of the whole far bodies D,T, the degree-free far-body port theorem gives nonnegative advance from its negative incoming outward moment to its positive outgoing moment. Its distance premise holds because each point of a boundary square has norm greater than 1/2, whereas the relevant epsilon=d-1 is less than 1/60.

All three actual chords avoid O. The positive total argument change gives positive winding of the actual contact triangle P_T,P_D,Z, where Z is the unchanged TD application.

Independently, B lies outside the embedded A-D-T-A cycle. Indeed its genuine edge BL connects it to the real boundary leaf and cannot cross that cycle. At A the counterclockwise port order is B,T,D. If A-D-T-A had counterclockwise orientation, its bounded sector between the outgoing AD strand and incoming AT strand would contain AB. This would place B inside, a contradiction. Therefore that actual cycle is clockwise.

The orientation passes from the compatible embedding to its contact triangle. For distinct contacts, the center-to-contact polygon is simple: its open strands lie in disjoint square interiors, and the two strands within one square meet only at that square's center. Move each interior junction continuously toward the midpoint of its two boundary contacts. At every time before the final limit the polygon remains simple with the same clockwise orientation. The limit is precisely the polygon of contact chords. A nondegenerate limit retains the sign of its signed area. A degenerate contact polygon cannot have the positive winding already established. Coincidence of Z with another contact is likewise incompatible with that positive winding.

Thus the actual clockwise orientation contradicts the positive winding. It follows that

    M_T>0.                                             (9)

This argument does not assume O belongs to A or B, and uses no two-force equation at D.

## 5. Actual concurrence and exclusion of the opposite T vertex

The nonparallel return lines concur, by the complete A force and torque equations, at a point on the horizontal AB line. Write

    Q_A=P-delta*East.

A line at the strict upper or lower adjacent vertex has opposite coordinate signs, while the displacement from that vertex to Q_A has both coordinates negative. Such a line also cannot contain Q_A. The only possible contact feature would then be W for both returns, already excluded. This proves delta>=0 without an interior-concurrence assumption.

Write n=n_AT and let m=det(P_T,n), omitting the positive force magnitude. Since the true upper normal has positive vertical component, (9) gives

    0<m=det(P,n)-delta*n_y<=|P|<Y.                      (10)

    u<=d.                                              (11)

Here J is the counterclockwise quarter turn; Jg=f and Jf=-g, in agreement with det(f,g)=-1.
