## 1. Complete original theorem

Suppose an actual connected positive component has distinct bodies
`H,L,A,K` and possibly further bodies, with the following exact conditions.

* L is a genuine D leaf at H.
* H has exactly the three original positive neighbors L,A,K.
* A is physically boundary, and the actual units A,K physically touch;
  their mutual contact need not carry stress for the local geometry.
* K has zero total circle load and retains every original positive pair
  force of its full true star.
* Every other body of this positive component, except H,L,K, is physically
  boundary. Its total circle multiplier may be zero.
* The actual positive graph obtained by deleting H,L is connected and
  contains the distinct A,K.

**Theorem.** This whole original configuration is impossible.

The force-free common-neighbor theorem supplies

    K is disjoint from closed D_(1/4).                     (1)

Indeed H's entire nonleaf neighbor list is exactly K and the one
physical-boundary page A, with its required actual A--K contact. The
full-window theorem applies directly, keeping H's true degree three.
No claim that an arbitrary completing square is boundary is made here.

The proof will show that K must be an extremal vertex in the orientation
of the original nonroot edge moments. Its full unloaded star then
contradicts the geometric theorem in Section 2.

## 2. An explicit geometric theorem for a complete unloaded star

This section is radius-window geometry at the true original degree.
It extracts no artificial body force and assumes no intact triangle.

Let U be an actual contained unit with zero total circle force. Suppose

    dist(O,U) > sqrt(2)*epsilon.                         (2)

Let one actual positive outward U force have unit normal n, application
p and magnitude lambda>0. Assume

    h_U(n)=p.n < -epsilon,
    det(p,n)>0.                                         (3)

Suppose every other actual positive outward pair force has nonzero
origin moment of the opposite sign:

    det(q_j,g_j)<0,   mu_j>0,
    lambda*n + sum_j mu_j*g_j = 0.                      (4)

Here (4) is U's entire original unloaded equation. Its true degree is
arbitrary, and each q_j is the actual original mean. All neighboring
units are physically contained in the same circle.

**Full-star theorem.** Conditions (2)--(4) are impossible.

The reflected theorem, with all moment signs reversed, follows by
reflecting the whole physical configuration once.

### 2.1. Actual support bounds and a far own interval

For any actual contained unit V, the farthest-corner inequality gives

    |c_V|^2+|c_V|+1/2 <= R^2,
    |c_V| <= d+1/2.

If V is the actual neighbor beyond a unit-normal support `g.x>=h`,
its half-width in direction g is at least 1/2. Consequently

    h+1/2 <= c_V.g <= |c_V|, and therefore h<=d.        (5)

The complete original radius window gives

    9/10<d<61/60,  0<=epsilon<1/60,
    d<=1+epsilon.

The two exact squared reserves are `65/1600` for the lower d bound
and `31/57600` for the upper one. Only positivity and these coarse
bounds are used below.

By (2), at least one own coordinate interval of U misses zero by more
than epsilon. Otherwise the nearest-point squared distance would be
at most `2*epsilon^2`. Choose a positively oriented own frame with
that interval positive. If the other interval's upper endpoint is
below `-epsilon`, make a proper quarter turn. The resulting complete
chart is

    U=[a,a+1] x [b,c],   c=b+1,
    a>epsilon, c>=-epsilon,
    a+1>1+epsilon>=d.                                (6)

This is a coordinate choice, not a physical motion. It preserves all
chosen determinant signs.

### 2.2. The exceptional inward normal has negative first component

Suppose `n_x>=0`. If also `n_y>=0`, its support is at least
`-epsilon`, using (6), contrary to (3). The downward vertical axis
has negative determinant because its actual point has positive first
coordinate, contrary to (3). For `n_x>0,n_y=-v<0`, its support is
at `(a+1,b)`, and a positive determinant forces `b<0`. Its support
is then `(a+1)n_x-bv>0`, again impossible. These alternatives
exhaust `n_x>=0`; hence `n_x<0`.

If `n_y<0`, its actual lower-left application has positive moment
only if `b>0`, hence `c>0`. If `n_y=0`, its actual left-side
mean has positive height by (3), so again `c>0`.

When `c>0`, every negative-moment outward force has nonpositive
vertical component. The top and upper-left cones have positive moment.
The right side is impossible by `a+1>d`. At an upper-right normal
`g=(u,v)`, `u,v>0`, a negative moment would give

    (a+1)v<c*u,
    h_U(g)=(a+1)u+c*v > (a+1)/u >= a+1>d,

contrary to (5). Its two axis endpoints are already covered.
The remaining side and lower-cone features have `g_y<=0`.

Thus `n_y<0` makes the entire vertical force sum in (4) negative.
If `n_y=0`, vertical balance forces all other normals horizontal.
East is impossible by (5)--(6), so all actual normals point West,
contradicting full horizontal balance. These cases include every
actual left-side mean and both endpoints.

### 2.3. Every remaining force has one strict negative projection

Only

    n=(-u,v), u,v>0, p=(a,c),
    -a*u+c*v < -epsilon,
    a*v+c*u>0                                         (7)

remains. Put `w=(v,u)`, so `w.n=0`. Each other actual outward
normal g of negative moment has `w.g<0`, as the complete feature
cover shows.

* The right and top sides are excluded as above. At the upper-right
  vertex, negative moment is impossible when `c<=0`, and when `c>0`
  the preceding support calculation contradicts (5).
* In the upper-left cone, write `g=(-f,t)`, `f,t>=0`. Negative
  moment forces `c<0`, `f>0` and `t/f<-c/a`. The last inequality
  in (7) gives `-c/a<v/u`. Therefore `w.g=-v*f+u*t<0`.
* The left side, bottom side and lower-left cone have normals West,
  South, or with both components nonpositive. Each has strictly
  negative scalar product with w. All actual means are included.
* At the lower-right vertex take `g=(f,-t)`, `f,t>0`. If
  `t/f<=v/u`, then g lies in the cone between East and `-n`.
  Both directions expose this same actual lower-right vertex. Their
  supports satisfy

      h_U(East)=a+1>d,
      h_U(-n)=u+v-h_U(n)>1+epsilon>=d.

  Write `g=lambda_0*East+lambda_1*(-n)` with both coefficients
  nonnegative. Since all three normals are unit, their coefficients
  sum to at least one. Support is linear on this actual vertex cone,
  so `h_U(g)>d`, contrary to (5). Hence `t/f>v/u`, and
  `w.g=v*f-u*t<0`. The two axis endpoints were already covered.

This exhausts every original support feature of U. Dotting the full
equation (4) with w gives zero from its one exceptional force and a
strictly negative sum from every other force. That contradiction
proves the full-star theorem. No equation at the exceptional neighbor
or any other neighbor has been used.

The calculation is the complete far-interval geometry underlying
`GLOBAL_NEXT_INTACT_ROOT_UNLOADED_MOMENT_AND_BLOCK_REDUCTION.md`,
Section 2, but it is proved here under precisely (2)--(4). Neither that
source's intact-petal hypothesis nor its separate near-corner theorem
is imported as an unproved scope extension.

## 3. The genuine D root supplies the strict inward return support

Write the actual genuine D-root chart with its leaf East:

    H=[-s,r] x [-b,a], a,b>0, a+b=1,
    p_HL=(r,0), n_HL=East.

The root is physically clear and contains O openly. The complete
full-window geometric margins give

    r>11/20, s>1/3, r^2-s^2>1/10.

Let n be the original H-to-K normal. We prove

    h_H(n)>epsilon.                                  (8)

If both `a,b>epsilon`, all four root margins exceed epsilon, and
every unit-normal support of H exceeds epsilon. Thus (8) holds
without a force calculation.

Otherwise reflect the entire configuration vertically so

    0<a<=epsilon<1/60<1/4, b>=3/4.                    (9)

The full-window shallow-D kernel applies to the one physical boundary
page A. Its actual H-to-A normal m satisfies `m_y<=0`. In H's
complete original equation

    alpha*East + gamma*n + beta*m = 0,
    alpha,gamma,beta>0,                               (10)

this forces `n_y>=0`.

If `n_y=0`, both n and m are horizontal. Opposite outward normals
at a physical triangle are impossible by the owned-axis theorem.
The real East leaf then forces both to point West. Consequently
`h_H(n)=s>1/3>epsilon`, proving (8) in this complete axis case,
with every actual mean retained.

Suppose `n_y>0`. North is impossible: the H-owned axis theorem
would force `m_y>=0`, leaving a strictly positive full vertical
sum. A strict Northeast n excludes West/South m by the same owned
axis fact and excludes strict Southwest m by the physical opposite-
vertex triangle fact. The remaining East/Southeast m would make
the full horizontal sum in (10) strictly positive. Thus n is strict
Northwest. The same triangle cover now leaves m West or strict
Southwest, and the positive vertical component of n excludes West.
The complete actual configuration therefore has

    n=(-c,t), m=(-u,-v), c,t,u,v>0,
    c^2+t^2=u^2+v^2=1,
    p_HK=(-s,a), p_HA=(-s,-b).

H's complete y-force and origin-torque equations give

    gamma*t=beta*v,
    gamma*(-s*t+a*c)+beta*(s*v-b*u)=0,
    u/v=a*c/(b*t).                                   (11)

The genuine radial leaf has zero origin moment. Both other original
forces and their exact applications remain in (11).

The full physical support-span bounds give

    min_K y >= a-c,
    max_A y <= -b+u=a-1+u.

They hold whether or not either actual neighbor owns its normal and
include all original mean endpoints. Since K,A actually touch,

    c+u>=1.                                          (12)

If `c<=1/2`, then `t>=sqrt(3)/2>3/4`. From (9),(11) and
`v<=1` one gets

    u <= a*c/(b*t) <= c/(3*t) < 2/9,
    c+u < 1/2+2/9=13/18<1,

contradicting (12). Thus `c>1/2`, and

    h_H(n)=s*c+a*t > s/2 > 1/6 > epsilon.

This proves (8) on the whole original domain. In particular the
actual reversed normal at K has the strict support

    h_K(-n)=-h_H(n)<-epsilon.                         (13)

No assumption that H contains the entire epsilon disk was used in
the thin alternative (9).

## 4. Every original nonroot edge has nonzero moment

Call the actual graph after deleting H,L the private graph P. Every
one of its actual units avoids closed `D_epsilon`: physical boundary

(1), while `epsilon<1/60`.

Because U avoids O, one reflected own interval is `[a,a+1]` with
`a>0`. At the maximum-e point p its coordinate is exactly `a+1`:
that component of `e=p/t` is positive. Hence

    t>1, a<=t-1<=epsilon.

If the other own interval also missed zero, both absolute coordinates
of p would exceed one, giving `t>sqrt(2)>d`, impossible. Otherwise
U has the actual point `(a,0)`, whose norm is at most epsilon.
This contradicts the stated disk avoidance. Thus every original
positive private edge has a strictly nonzero origin moment, including
all actual variable-mean and owner alternatives.

## 6. Its actual source and sink force the unloaded-star contradiction

By hypothesis the private graph P is finite, connected, and has at
least two vertices A,K. An acyclic orientation of such a graph has
a distinct source and sink, each incident to at least one private
edge. At a source all original outward private moments are strictly
positive; at a sink all are strictly negative.

Every actual private vertex other than A,K has no original positive
edge to H or L. Its entire pair star is private, and its circle
atoms have zero origin moment. Its full torque equation therefore

At K all actual private pair moments have one strict sign. Its only

torque equation makes that edge's moment strictly nonzero of the
opposite sign. Its original circle total is zero by hypothesis;

By (1), compactness gives `dist(O,K)>1/4>sqrt(2)*epsilon`.
Its exceptional K-to-H support is strictly less than `-epsilon` by

premise of the explicitly proved full-star theorem in Section 2 now
holds at the real K, with all its original positive forces and means.
That theorem supplies the contradiction and proves Section 1.
