## 3. Why no positive bridge joins two loaded bodies

For clarity, this is the existing radius-free bridge argument, not a
new degree-two classification. Let a positive bridge have its unchanged
common application p, magnitude mu>0 and signed unit normal n. Sum all
original origin-moment equations on either component after removing
that bridge. Every internal pair cancels at its one actual common
application; circle forces are radial. The remaining equation is

\[
\det(p,n)=0.
\]

If p=0, both incident squares contain the origin and hence are
physically circle-clear for `R>sqrt(2)`, because their diameter is
`sqrt(2)`. Otherwise choose the direction so that `p=r n`, r>0,
with the inner square on `n.x<=r` and the outer square on `n.x>=r`.
The latter has projection width at least one, so containment gives
`r<=R-1`.

The actual inner square contains `p-t n` for every `0<=t<=1`.
This follows coordinate by coordinate in its own square frame, and
includes every original shared-side mean endpoint. Therefore the inner
square meets the centered disk of radius

\[
\delta=\max(R-2,0).
\]

If that square also met the outer circle, two of its actual points
would be at distance at least

\[
R-\delta=\min(R,2)>\sqrt2,
\]

contrary to its diameter. Thus an inner endpoint is physically clear.
Consequently a positive bridge cannot join two loaded bodies. This
holds throughout the present window, including its portion below two.
In particular the neighbor of a genuine positive leaf is physically
clear, since the leaf itself is loaded by its one nonzero pair force.
