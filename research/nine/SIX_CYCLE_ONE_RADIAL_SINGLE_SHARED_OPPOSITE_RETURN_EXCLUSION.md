## 1. The true shared-return chart

Normalize the actual return horizontally at height \(H>0\). At its
left unique endpoint which owns the normal, the physical circle corner
and original applications are
\[
 z=(-a,b),\quad a,b>\frac12,\quad a^2+b^2=R^2,\qquad
 T=(1-a,H),\quad Q=(1-a,b-1).
\tag{3}
\]
T is the genuine shared mean. The local strict-turn cover forces its
other port Q to be the opposite vertex. The actual mean interval and
proper concurrence give
\[
 0<H\le b,\qquad t=1-H/b\in[0,1),\qquad q=(1-t)z.
\tag{4}
\]
The mean coinciding with Q is excluded by the original distinct-port
theorem. The genuine endpoint \(H=b\) remains included.

In the inward own axes \((-1,0),(0,1)\), the actual normal from S
toward this unique unit is
\[
 G=(x,y)=\frac{(X,Y)}{\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}},\qquad
 X=1-ta,\quad Y=1-tb,\quad X,Y>0.
\tag{5}
\]
The strict signs follow from its original opposite-vertex feature:
its S-incident interface cannot also be shared. The unchanged common
moment has global sign \(\det(q,(1,0))=-H<0\), and its value on
the incoming S channel is proportional to \(-(1-t)(a-b)\).
Hence
\[
 a>b,\qquad a>R/\sqrt2,\qquad
             0<x\le1/\sqrt2,\quad y\ge1/\sqrt2.
\tag{6}
\]
The true reserve-one transmitter fixes the original radial port
\(P=Q-G\). In the same orthonormal axes through the original circle
origin,
\[
             P=(a-1-x,b-1-y),\qquad |P|^2=d^2+\frac14.
\tag{7}
\]
No statement that S's concurrence lies in its square is used.

## 2. Uniform normal ratios on the hypothetical high-channel domain

Suppose \(H\ge4/5\). Then
\[
                  4/5\le b<R/\sqrt2<3/2.
\tag{8}
\]
The radius window gives
\[
 a<77/40\quad(b\ge4/5),\qquad
 a<11/6\quad(b\ge1),\qquad
 a<17/10\quad(b\ge6/5).
\tag{9}
\]
The respective positive squared reserves are
\[
 (77/40)^2-(1105/256-16/25)=187/6400,
\]
\[
 (11/6)^2-(1105/256-1)=103/2304,\qquad
 (17/10)^2-(1105/256-36/25)=87/6400.
\tag{10}
\]

For \(\gamma<1\), \(X-\gamma Y\) increases with H, since its
coefficient of H is \(a/b-\gamma>0\). At \(H=4/5\),
\[
 b(X-\gamma Y)
   =\gamma b^2+(1-\tfrac95\gamma)b-a(b-\tfrac45).
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(b\ge4/5\), replacing a by an upper bound preserves a
lower bound in (11).

For \(4/5\le b\le1\), take \(\gamma=3/4\) and \(a<77/40\).
The lower polynomial
\[
                   \frac34b^2-\frac{91}{40}b+\frac{77}{50}
\]
decreases on this whole interval and has value \(3/200>0\) at 1.
Consequently
\[
                X>\frac34Y,\qquad x>\frac35,\qquad y<\frac45.
\tag{12}
\]

For \(1\le b\le6/5\), take \(\gamma=3/5\), \(a<11/6\).
The lower polynomial
\[
                   \frac35b^2-\frac{287}{150}b+\frac{22}{15}
\]
decreases on the whole interval, with endpoint value \(13/375>0\).

For \(6/5\le b<3/2\), use \(\gamma=3/5\), \(a<17/10\).
The lower polynomial
\[
                    \frac35b^2-\frac{89}{50}b+\frac{34}{25}
\]
has completed-square minimum \(239/6000>0\) on the whole real
line. These last two complete range estimates imply
\[
 X>\frac35Y,\qquad x>\frac3{\sqrt{34}}>\frac12,\qquad
                         y<\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\quad(b\ge1).
\tag{13}
\]
The inequalities are not inferred from signs at sampled points.

## 3. Every high-channel norm contradicts the actual radial square

First let \(4/5\le b\le1\). The lower radius bound gives
\[
 a>17/10,\qquad K_0^2-1-(17/10)^2=17/1600>0.
\]
Using \(x\le1/\sqrt2<5/7\), (9), and (12), we obtain
\[
                         |a-1-x|<13/40.
\tag{14}
\]
For the lower side, \(a-1-x>7/10-5/7=-1/70>-13/40\).

If \(17/20\le b\le1\), then
\[
 |b-1-y|=1-b+y<3/20+4/5=19/20.
\]
If \(4/5\le b\le17/20\), then \(t\le1/17\), so
\[
 X>1-(77/40)(1/17)=603/680,\qquad Y\le1.
\]
The exact reserve
\[
                         (603/680)^2-7/9=35681/4161600>0
\]
gives \(y<3/4\), and again
\[
                         |b-1-y|<1/5+3/4=19/20.
\tag{15}
\]
Therefore throughout this whole b range,
\[
 |P|^2<(13/40)^2+(19/20)^2=1613/1600<53/50.
\tag{16}
\]
The final reserve is \(83/1600>0\).

Now let \(b\ge1\). We have \(a>R/\sqrt2>139/100\), because
\(K_0^2-2(139/100)^2=1457/40000>0\). Equations (6), (9), and
(13) give
\[
                         -1/3<a-1-x<1/3.
\]
For the lower side, use
\(a-1-x>39/100-5/7=-227/700>-1/3\).
Also \(b<3/2\), \(y\ge1/\sqrt2>1/2\), and (13) imply
\[
                         -\sqrt3/2<b-1-y<0.
\]
Consequently
\[
                         |P|^2<1/9+3/4=31/36<53/50.
\tag{17}
\]

But \(d>9/10\) on the original radius window, so the actual radial
norm in (7) satisfies
\[
                              |P|^2=d^2+1/4>53/50.
\tag{18}
\]
Both cases contradict it. This proves the complete local height
theorem \(H<4/5\), including every original endpoint mean.

## 4. A nonshared opposite endpoint forces the same return to be high

Suppose both S-incident applications are opposite vertices and exactly
one endpoint owns the return. At the other endpoint use the unchanged
opposite-port chart
\[
 c=\cos\eta,\quad s=\sin\eta,\quad0<\eta<\pi/4,\qquad
 Z=H+s,\quad A=\sqrt{R^2-Z^2}>0,
\]
\[
 z=(-A,Z),\quad T=(-A+c,H),\quad
 Q=(-A+c-s,H-c),\qquad
                    G\ \hbox{parallel to}\ (sA/Z+s-c,c).
\tag{19}
\]
Section 1 of
[SIX_CYCLE_ONE_RADIAL_TWO_OPPOSITE_PORTS_SINGLETON_CHANNEL_EXCLUSION.md](SIX_CYCLE_ONE_RADIAL_TWO_OPPOSITE_PORTS_SINGLETON_CHANNEL_EXCLUSION.md)
establishes this actual local chart and its signs without assuming
a circle branch in advance. Only that local chart is used; no
two-nonshared theorem is imported.

The strict unique turn gives \(G_x<0\). Put
\(w=(c-s)^2+s^2\). Both sides of \((c-s)Z>sA\) are positive,
so squaring with \(A^2+Z^2=R^2\) gives
\[
                Z>\frac{sR}{\sqrt w},\qquad
                H>s\left(\frac R{\sqrt w}-1\right).
\tag{20}
\]

We first show that \(H\le4/5\) forces \(s<2/5\).
For \(2/5\le s\le1/2\), the function
\(w(s)=1+s^2-2s\sqrt{1-s^2}\) decreases. Indeed
\[
 w'(s)=2(s-c+s^2/c);
\]
the expression in parentheses increases in s and equals
\(1/2-1/\sqrt3<0\) at \(s=1/2\). Therefore
\[
 w\le w(2/5)=\frac{29-4\sqrt{21}}{25}<3/7.
\tag{21}
\]
The last comparison is \(32<7\sqrt{21}\), with squared reserve
\(1029-1024=5>0\). Also \(K_0^2-27/7=487/11200>0\).
Thus \(R/\sqrt w>3\), and (20) gives \(H>2s\ge4/5\).

For \(1/2\le s\le1/\sqrt2\), we have \(w\le1/2\).
The required squared comparison has the exact factorization
\[
 4s^2(1-s^2)-(s^2+1/2)^2
       =5(1/2-s^2)(s^2-1/10)\ge0.
\]
It gives \(s^2+1/2\le2sc\), hence the bound on w. Equation (20)
then yields
\[
 H>\frac12(K_0\sqrt2-1)>353/400>4/5
\tag{22}
\]
using \(\sqrt2>7/5\). These whole angle ranges establish
\[
                       H\le4/5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s<2/5.
\tag{23}
\]

Under (23),
\[
 A-c\ge\sqrt{R^2-(4/5+s)^2}-\sqrt{1-s^2}.
\]
The right side decreases strictly for \(0\le s\le2/5\).
After squaring positive sides, its derivative comparison is exactly
\(4/5>s(R-1)\), which follows from \(R<133/64\) and \(s\le2/5\).
Consequently
\[
                       A-c>\sqrt{R^2-36/25}-\sqrt{21}/5.
\tag{24}
\]
The exact bounds
\[
 \sqrt{R^2-36/25}>25/16,\quad R/\sqrt2>139/100,\quad
 \sqrt{21}/5<23/25
\]
have positive squared reserves \(123/6400,\ 1457/40000,\ 4/625\).
They give
\[
                         A-c+R/\sqrt2>2+13/400.
\tag{25}
\]

At the actual shared endpoint, let \(a_T\) be its horizontal
circle coordinate. Section 1 gives \(a_T>R/\sqrt2\).
The original reserve-one return relation is
\[
                   A+a_T=2+c,\qquad A-c=2-a_T<2-R/\sqrt2.
\tag{26}
\]
This contradicts (25) if \(H\le4/5\). Thus the two-opposite
one-owner family requires \(H>4/5\), in contradiction to the local
height theorem. The whole original family is excluded.
