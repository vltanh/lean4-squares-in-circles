## 3. A whole-domain projection bound: Delta.c_hat>1

**Lemma 3.1.** Throughout the entire original A-only owner, m>u and
Delta.c_hat>1, without any perpendicularity assumption on b_hat,c_hat.

*Proof.* Treat H,k,s,S as their fixed original geometric values and
write

    m-u=(1-omega*s/H)/(H*(H-k))+k/H-1+omega*S/H.

This is an affine function of omega. The original 0<t,z<1 puts
omega strictly between 0 and

    omega_max=min(H/s,H/S).

It suffices to prove positivity at both closed comparison endpoints.
At omega=0, use H^2+k^2=1 to obtain exactly

    m-u=2*k/(H-k)>0.

If S>=s, at omega_max=H/S one has u=0, t>=0 and
m=t/[H*(H-k)]+k/H>0. This also covers S=s.

If s>S, at omega_max=H/s one has t=0 and

    m-u=k/H-1+S/s.

The sign of this expression is the sign of HS-(H-k)s. The original
rotation identities give s=H*C+k*S. Since C<S and H(H-k)>0,

    HS-(H-k)s
       =HS-H*(H-k)*C-k*(H-k)*S
       >S*(H-H^2+k^2)
       =S*(1-H)*(2*H+1)>0.

Here H<1 follows from k>0. Both affine endpoint values are positive,
so m>u on the whole original interval.

Now c_hat is the normalization of nD-r*h, which in D's own frame
equals

    (1+r*k)*nD+r*H*tD.

Both coefficients are strictly positive. Since W-Q_C=nD+tD,
the full D diagonal has the strict width

    (W-Q_C).c_hat
       =(1+r*k+r*H)/sqrt((1+r*k)^2+(r*H)^2)>1.

Indeed the squared numerator exceeds the squared denominator by
2*(1+r*k)*r*H>0. The remaining displacement satisfies

    (Q_B-W).c_hat=(m-u)/sqrt(1+m^2)>0.

Adding proves Delta.c_hat>1. All original force ratios and V equations
were retained; the proof simply did not need to strengthen them. QED.

## 4. Every original side has d.b_hat>=0 and d.c_hat>0

**Theorem 4.1.** For the whole side return (3),

    d.b_hat>=0, d.c_hat>0.                      (10)

*Proof of the first sign.* Suppose d.b_hat<0. By the full cone cover,
d.w_B>0. The actual minimum-d support of the owned B in (6) is
then its far-b_hat, minimum-w_B vertex

    T=Q_B+b_hat-beta_B*w_B.

Every original U in C satisfies

    U.h>=Q_C.h-(1-chi_C)*cY.

Consequently the unchanged side displacement has

    (T-U).(-h)
       >=1+q+a0-beta_B*b0-(1-chi_C)*cY
       >1+q+a0-b0-cY>1.

The last inequality uses q>1, a0>b0, cY<1. It contradicts
|T-U|=1. Therefore d.b_hat>=0. This argument includes the entire
C-parallel endpoint and does not assume the sign of d.c_hat.

*Proof of the second sign.* Suppose d.c_hat<=0. A nonnegative cone
representation d=A*b_hat+B*c_hat gives

    1=A*(d.b_hat)+B*(d.c_hat).

Thus d.b_hat>0. Its actual B minimum-d support lies on the same
minimum-b_hat side as Q_B. By (9), its actual transverse coordinate
is at least -beta_B, including the full tied side if d=b_hat.
Using (5) and 0<beta_B<1 gives

    T.c_hat>=Q_B.c_hat-beta_B*(w_B.c_hat)
             >Q_B.c_hat-1.

The unchanged C maximum-c_hat support gives U.c_hat<=Q_C.c_hat.
Therefore

    d.c_hat=(T-U).c_hat>Delta.c_hat-1>0,

contradicting the supposition. This proves (10). QED.

The possible equality d.b_hat=0 has deliberately not been removed.
At that endpoint B can have a whole minimum-d side, with its original
mean using any b_hat coordinate in [0,1]. No replacement mean is
needed anywhere in the following argument.

## 6. One coupled budget for every original side coordinate

Define the original positive corner offsets

    x_B=1-beta_B in (0,1),
    x_C=chi_C in (0,1),
    E=b0+cY-(H+k).                              (14)

Every point T in the actual B has

    T.h<=Q_B.h+beta_B*b0,

because its b_hat coordinate is nonnegative and a0>0. Every point
U in C has U.h>=Q_C.h-(1-chi_C)cY as used above. Thus (3),
without any further support-uniqueness premise, gives

    1>=d.(-h)
      >=1+H+k-beta_B*b0-(1-chi_C)*cY.

Equivalently the whole original side satisfies

    b0*x_B+cY*x_C<=E.                          (15)

Both left terms are strictly positive. In particular

    E>0, x_B<E/b0, x_C<E/cY.                  (16)

Therefore E<=0 excludes every original side return directly. This is
a continuous whole-geometry consequence, not a finite pose test.

Define two explicit auxiliary comparison points using only the original
V/A/D data, their original force ratios, and the original circle origin:

    Y_B=Q_B+b_hat+(E/b0)*w_B,
    Y_C=Q_C-c_hat-(E/cY)*w_C.                   (17)

Let delta_B=E/b0-x_B and delta_C=E/cY-x_C. By (16) both are
strictly positive. Comparing with (13),

    Y_B=Z_B+delta_B*w_B,
    Y_C=Z_C-delta_C*w_C.

The actual inward own edge at Z_B is -w_B and at Z_C is +w_C.
The general adjacent-vertex outward comparison therefore gives

    |Y_B-O|^2>=R^2+delta_B+delta_B^2>R^2,
    |Y_C-O|^2>=R^2+delta_C+delta_C^2>R^2.        (18)

Both strict inequalities are necessary for any whole side return.
The strict excess does not discard an allowed mean endpoint: it comes
from the positive original side-interior coordinate of the other
genuinely owned exterior contact in (15).

Thus either E<=0, |Y_B-O|<=R or |Y_C-O|<=R excludes the full
original A-only side row at that original geometric datum. In particular
a later whole-domain proof of the disjunction would close the entire
row without splitting its physical masks or selecting side means.
No such universal disjunction is asserted here.

## 7. Exact elimination of both side coordinates

The two named physical corners in (13) also give a single exact
necessary inequality with no unknown B or C side coordinate. Use all
point coordinates below relative to O, and write

    X_B=(Q_B-O).b_hat+1,
    U_B=(Q_B-O).w_B,
    X_C=(Q_C-O).c_hat-1,
    U_C=(Q_C-O).w_C.

The actual adjacent vertices at the circle corners force

    X_B>=1/2, U_B+x_B>=1/2,
    X_C<=-1/2, U_C-x_C<=-1/2.

For example |Z_B-b_hat-O|<=R=|Z_B-O| gives X_B>=1/2;
the other three inequalities follow identically. Hence the positive
square roots are unambiguous, their radicands are at least 1/4, and
the original offsets are exactly

    x_B=sqrt(R^2-X_B^2)-U_B,
    x_C=sqrt(R^2-X_C^2)+U_C.                    (19)

Substitution into (15) gives the whole necessary condition

    b0*(sqrt(R^2-X_B^2)-U_B)
       +cY*(sqrt(R^2-X_C^2)+U_C)<=E,            (20)

with each parenthesized quantity in (0,1). Equation (19) preserves
every actual physical two-circle tie and zero individual coefficient;
it follows from the actual physical corners, not from an assumed
single-atom circle force.
