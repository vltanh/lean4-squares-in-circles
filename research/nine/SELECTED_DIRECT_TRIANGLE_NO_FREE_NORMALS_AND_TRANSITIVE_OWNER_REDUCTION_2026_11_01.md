## 4. A free internal edge forces two physically shared exits

Suppose the original internal edge \(AB\) is free. Its physical intersection
is a singleton, as is \(AC\). If \(A\)'s outside contact were also a physical
singleton, all three positive contacts would be singleton sets at distinct
points by Section 3. Property (S) would forbid the free \(AB\) normal.
Therefore \(A\)'s actual outside contact has positive shared length. Its
original normal is an own side axis of \(A\), with its unchanged full segment
mean, including either endpoint. The identical argument applies at \(B\).

Here sharedness is a conclusion about the actual intersection set. It is not
deduced from a point-valued mean, nor is a mean moved to obtain it.

We use the selection-free own-axis theorem
[`UNLOADED_TRIANGLE_OWNED_RETURN_AND_OWNED_EXTERIOR_FORCE_SAME_AXIS_2026_10_30.md`](UNLOADED_TRIANGLE_OWNED_RETURN_AND_OWNED_EXTERIOR_FORCE_SAME_AXIS_2026_10_30.md),
Section 1. Its exact local proof is short. If \(h\) is an own internal axis
of an unloaded true-three-force hub, and \(g\) is its other internal normal,
the physical touching-return theorem gives \(h\cdot g\ge0\). The complete
exit vector \(E=-\mu h-\gamma g\) has strictly negative \(h\)-projection.
If \(E/|E|\) is also an own axis, it must be \(-h\). The transverse component
then gives \(g=h\). Every original positive force is retained.

Apply this at \(A\). If its other internal normal \(AC\) were \(A\)-owned,
its shared own-axis exit would force the free \(AB\) normal onto that same
own axis, contrary to strict vertex exposure. Hence **neither internal
normal at \(A\) is \(A\)-owned**. Both actual applications are its strictly
exposed vertices. They are distinct, and cannot be opposite: Section 2.3 of
the touching-return source proves that neighbors at opposite strict vertices
cannot physically touch. Here those two neighbors are \(B,C\), which do
touch. Therefore the two original \(A\)-applications are adjacent vertices.
The same statement holds at \(B\).

## 5. Two or more free internal edges cannot survive

Suppose two internal edges are free, say \(AB\) and \(AC\). Every core is

contradicting freeness. The same argument rules out \(C\)-ownership.

The remaining internal contact is a physical singleton. Its only alternatives
are side ownership by one incident core or a free vertex/vertex normal. Thus

and three-free hypotheses without an unclassified force ratio.

## 6. Exactly one free internal edge has two complete owned-return exits

It remains to suppose exactly \(AB\) is free. The singleton contacts \(AC\)
and \(BC\) are nonfree. Section 4 rules out ownership by \(A\) and \(B\),
respectively. Hence **\(C\) owns both internal returns**. Their applications
are in the relative interiors of their actual \(C\)-sides, and are the
adjacent original vertices already identified on \(A\) and \(B\).

The two \(C\)-owned directions have nonnegative dot product because their
actual neighbors \(A,B\) touch. Two directed own axes with that property are
either equal or perpendicular; opposite supports are also directly separated
by the square's width. These give two complete geometric branches.

### 6.2 Perpendicular \(C\)-side directions: proof of the exact chart interface

Put the actual \(C\) at \([0,1]^2\), with its \(A\)-return East and its
\(B\)-return North. Write their original contacts as

\[
 p_A=(1,s),\quad p_B=(t,1),\qquad 0<s,t<1.
\]

Because \(A\) is nonparallel to \(C\), \(p_A\) is its unique leftmost vertex.
Its two inward edge directions can be written

\[
 u_A=(\cos\beta,\sin\beta),\quad
 v_A=(\sin\beta,-\cos\beta),\quad0<\beta<\pi/2.
\]

Its other internal application \(P=AB\) is adjacent to \(p_A\). The lower
choice \(p_A+v_A\) has height less than one, whereas the whole actual \(B\)
lies in \(y\ge1\). Thus \(P=p_A+u_A\).

Similarly \(p_B\) is \(B\)'s unique bottom vertex. Write

\[
 u_B=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha),\quad
 v_B=(-\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha),\quad0<\alpha<\pi/2.
\]

The left adjacent choice \(p_B+v_B\) has first coordinate less than one,
whereas the whole actual \(A\) lies in \(x\ge1\). Hence
\(P=p_B+u_B\). Equality of the two expressions gives

\[
 t=1-\cos\alpha+\cos\beta,\qquad
 s=1+\sin\alpha-\sin\beta.
\]

Since \(t<1\), \(\beta>\alpha\). Therefore
\(0<\alpha<\beta<\pi/2\), with precisely the two actual squares and contact
positions in the reviewed perpendicular theorem.

At \(A\), both internal applications have \(v_A\)-coordinate zero; their
outward normals have nonpositive \(v_A\)-component, and the \(A\)-to-\(C\)
force has strictly negative component \(-\sin\beta\) times its positive
magnitude. Full translation makes the own-axis exit point along \(+v_A\).
The identical argument gives \(+v_B\) at \(B\). Thus the two **original**
exit directions, not newly chosen ones, are exactly those required by
[`computer_assisted/PERPENDICULAR_OWNER_SINGLE_FREE_TRIANGLE_EXACT_EXCLUSION_2026_11_01.md`](computer_assisted/PERPENDICULAR_OWNER_SINGLE_FREE_TRIANGLE_EXACT_EXCLUSION_2026_11_01.md).

Its physical elimination, original means, actual outside points, exhaustive
roots, and exact certificate have been independently reviewed and replayed in
[`reviews/2026-11-01/REVIEW_PERPENDICULAR_OWNER_TRIANGLE_CERTIFICATE_2026_11_01.md`](reviews/2026-11-01/REVIEW_PERPENDICULAR_OWNER_TRIANGLE_CERTIFICATE_2026_11_01.md).
It gives \(R>25/12\), again contradicting \(R^2\le Q\).

Both branches are excluded. **No original internal normal is free.**

## 7. Unique owners form either a cycle or a transitive tournament

Every remaining internal physical singleton is a genuine vertex/side-interior
contact. Its side owner is unique: a common own axis on both cores would make
their frames parallel, already excluded. A side-normal application at an
owner's vertex would instead be a forbidden nonstrict vertex/vertex singleton,
or would share a positive segment with a side-interior neighbor; neither is
available in this domain.

Orient each of the three edges from its actual side owner toward its vertex
neighbor. A three-vertex tournament is either cyclic, with outdegrees
\((1,1,1)\), or transitive, with outdegrees \((2,1,0)\). This finite alternative
is exhaustive and does not identify different force directions or weights.

The complete cyclic theorem
[`CYCLIC_SIDE_OWNER_UNLOADED_TRIANGLE_INCENTER_EXCLUSION_2026_11_01.md`](CYCLIC_SIDE_OWNER_UNLOADED_TRIANGLE_INCENTER_EXCLUSION_2026_11_01.md)

applications, no common point, full unloaded original stars. It proves
\(R^2>9/2\), as checked in its root review. Thus only the transitive pattern
remains, which can be labeled

\[
 C\longrightarrow A,\qquad C\longrightarrow B,\qquad
 A\longrightarrow B.
\]

The same touching-return side-axis argument makes the two directions owned
by \(C\) equal or perpendicular. This leaves precisely the two full
transitive physical branches; their original side positions, weights, means,
outside equations, and completing squares remain to be handled.
