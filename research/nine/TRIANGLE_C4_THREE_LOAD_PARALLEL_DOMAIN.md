## 2. The whole opposite-normal alternative is impossible

Suppose \(n=e\), \(m=-e\), with \(e\) unit. Use \((e,Je)\)
coordinates and write \(y_A,y_C\) for the transverse coordinates
of the actual root applications. Equation (3) gives
\[
                              \mu y_A=\nu y_C.
\tag{7}
\]
If either is nonzero, both have the same strict sign. Equation (6)
then gives that same sign to the transverse components of both
\(F_A,F_C\), because (6) does not change the transverse coordinate
and each radial proportionality is positive. Equation (5) gives the
same sign to \(F_B\). Their total cannot be parallel to e, contrary
to (3).

If instead \(y_A=y_C=0\), both nonzero endpoint circle forces lie
on the e-axis. They would be either parallel or antiparallel, contrary
to their complete strict force-cone order in Section 1. This covers
the zero-height limit without assuming a contact normal is nonradial.

Thus the entire opposite-normal case is excluded, with every actual
root and shared-side endpoint retained.

## 4. Both wall endpoints are physically unique on the whole domain

\tag{14}
\]
The last comparison follows from \(R<21/10\) and

 \begin{split}
 |P|^2
 &\le s^2+\left(1-\sqrt{r^2-s^2}\right)^2\\

\tag{18}

\tag{20}
