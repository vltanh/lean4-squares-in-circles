### 3.1. A physical contact-triangle barrier

For any three actual interior-disjoint unit squares, a triangle
whose three vertices are one actual contact for each pair has
each of its three edges contained in one of the actual squares.
Every edge has length at most sqrt(2). Its area is therefore at
most sqrt(3)/2<1: if its longest side is b<=sqrt(2), its altitude
is at most sqrt(3)b/2, since the other two sides are at most b.

No fourth actual unit Z can meet the open interior of this contact
triangle. If it did, some point of int Z would be in that interior,
since int Z is dense in Z. The connected open set int Z cannot
cross any of the triangle's three edges: each edge is in a closed
occupied square, and int Z is disjoint from every other closed
square. Thus all of int Z would lie inside a region of area less
than one, contradicting its unit area.

This argument retains weak pair junctions and points on the
boundary of Z. A closed point of Z in the open triangle already
has nearby interior points there. It needs no force equation,
orientation choice or embedding assertion about Z.

Apply this barrier to the actual contact triangle P,Q,T of A,H,B.
Equation (7) shows that the whole H boundary arc from Q down to S
and then right to T, with Q and T omitted, lies strictly inside
triangle P,Q,T. For example, for 0<=y<sigma the point (0,y) is a
strict positive barycentric combination of

    (-c,-LV), (0,sigma), (j,0):

its first coefficient is

    (1-y/sigma)/(1+LV/sigma+c/j)>0,

the other two are respectively (y+LV times that coefficient)/sigma
and c/j times that coefficient, both strictly positive. The bottom
arc has the same direct calculation, with the two coordinates
interchanged. S itself also has all three coefficients positive.

It follows that no fourth actual unit can touch this entire open
arc. In particular no private unit of the terminal four-block can
