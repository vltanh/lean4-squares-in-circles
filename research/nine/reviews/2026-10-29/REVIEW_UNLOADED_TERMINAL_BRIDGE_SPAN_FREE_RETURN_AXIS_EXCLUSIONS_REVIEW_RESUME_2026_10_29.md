## 3. The sharp support reach covers the entire support feature

Let Q be an actual minimum-m support point of a unit K, with unit vectors m and r satisfying m·r<0. The claimed complete inequality is

\[
h_K(r)-r\cdot Q\le\sqrt{1-(m\cdot r)^2}<1.
\tag{R}
\]

At a vertex Q write K=Q+[0,1]e+[0,1]f in perpendicular inward unit axes. Minimum support gives m·e,m·f≥0. If both r-components in those axes were nonnegative, expanding m·r in the orthonormal frame would make it nonnegative. Thus at most one inward edge contributes a positive r-span. This is a full sign cover, including a zero component and the possibility that neither contributes positively.

Put a=−m·r>0. For either inward unit edge e, decompose e and r along m and its perpendicular. Since m·e≥0,

\[
r\cdot e
\le-a(m\cdot e)+\sqrt{1-a^2}\sqrt{1-(m\cdot e)^2}
\le\sqrt{1-a^2}.
\]

The positive part of at most one component gives (R). The case a=1 is valid: r=−m and every allowed inward projection is nonpositive, so the maximum relative r-span is zero.

If Q is in the relative interior of a support side, the inward perpendicular own axis is m. Its r projection is negative. The only positive reach can come from the tangential interval of total length one containing the original Q, and is at most |r·Jm|=sqrt(1−(m·r)^2). This treats every actual side mean, including means approaching either endpoint; the endpoints themselves are covered by the vertex argument.

The strict final inequality uses exactly m·r<0. No uniform angle gap, closed interval away from zero, sampled orientation, or compactness assertion is needed. In particular it remains strict arbitrarily close to a perpendicular pair and does not silently assume a fixed reserve.

This sharper bound implies the older strict span below one. Its derivation is independent of the owner of the physical contact supplying Q and independent of any circle data.

## 6. Theorem A: complete sole-terminal-axis owner and frame cover

### 6.1. The two return vertices cannot be opposite

At opposite exposed vertices P and Q, the corresponding normals lie in strictly opposite quadrants, so n·m<0. Since P maximizes n,

\[
n\cdot(P-Q)=|n_x|+|n_y|>1.
\]

Here strictness follows from both components being nonzero and n being unit: the square of the sum is 1+2|n_xn_y|>1. The actual Q is a minimum-m support point of W. By (R), h_W(n)<n·Q+1<n·P=ell_V(n), contradicting actual VW contact. No V/W force, mask, or owner was used.

Opposite vertical signs leave only opposite corners or corners on one vertical side. The opposite case is now excluded. The two return corners cannot both be on the left: both n and m would have negative horizontal components, as would the terminal force, contradicting the complete U force. Hence both are on the right.

Reflect vertically, without changing any labels, so that V is the upper return and the fixed bridge-end W is lower. This gives the full chart

n=(a,b),\quad m=(c,-d),

### 6.2. The entire V lies above Q, whatever owns UV

### 7.1. Every positive shift is impossible

If 0<s<1, Q lies in the relative interior of V's bottom side. Locally V occupies the open upper half-plane and U occupies the open Southwest quadrant. Every interior tangent direction of W at Q must avoid both sets. Such an interior direction lies strictly below the horizontal axis and cannot point left. Taking the closure of those interior directions forces W's complete inward tangent cone into the closed Southeast quadrant. An isolated permitted West boundary ray cannot enlarge this closure: it is not the limit of an open sector avoiding both forbidden interiors.

A square side-interior tangent cone is a half-plane and cannot fit. A square vertex cone has angle pi/2 and can fit only with inward axes exactly East and South. But m·South=−a0<0 contradicts minimum-m support at Q. Thus every positive shift is excluded. This exit needs neither the free-owner condition nor the path or bridge, so its use in the stronger common theorem is valid.

### 7.2. Every negative shift forces W above Q

If s<0, all V points have x≤L+s<L. Freeness gives a strict minimum-m vertex Q of W with both inward-edge m projections positive.

If W is parallel to U, only East and North have positive projection on the strict Northeast vector m. The resulting W is wholly in x≥L and cannot touch V. This includes all axis-frame endpoints.

Otherwise each coordinate of each inward edge is nonzero. No edge can be Southwest, because m has both components positive. Orthogonality then forces one inward edge Northeast and the other Northwest or Southeast. If the other were Southeast, both horizontal components would be positive and W would again lie wholly in x≥L, contrary to VW contact. Thus it is Northwest. Both vertical components are positive, so Q is the unique bottom vertex of W.

Consequently every z in W satisfies z_y≥H, and every z≠Q satisfies m·(z−Q)>0. This includes every nonparallel orientation that could have touched V. It does not assume an owner or mean for VW.

### 7.3. The unchanged U force gives the bridge gap

\[

\[

The strict inequality is uniform over the open quadrant: (alpha+beta)²=1+2alpha beta>1. It does not require a fixed positive angular margin. The complete path and real bridge now give

\[
