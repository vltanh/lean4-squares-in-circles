## 1. Full original scope and ray direction

The cited physical triangle dependencies supply nonzero external

## 2. Parallel K

With K normals both m, the other bodies are nonparallel. Their
zero-moment force lines have genuine concurrence O. The actual
proper KU application as viewed from U is P=-a m with a>=0.
As viewed from K it is on the unchanged maximum-m support.
Closed O containment therefore forces h_K(m)=-a=0. The whole
K lies in m.x<=0, whereas its actual pair sum is a positive
multiple of m. This proves the complete parallel alternative,
including side-mean endpoints.

## 3. Nonparallel K with three nonparallel bodies

At a shared edge of two nonparallel zero-moment bodies, proper
ports give P=a n=-b n with a,b>=0, hence P=O. If both other
bodies are nonparallel, both K supports pass through O. The
identity |N|^2=sum mu_i(N.n_i)>0 guarantees one strictly
positive N projection onto an original supporting normal.
That original half-plane excludes the whole positive N ray.
The argument includes coincident applications and zero proper
lengths and never imposes distinctness without proof.

## 4. The remaining parallel neighbor and actual shared means

When V is parallel and K,U are nonparallel, both normals from
K,U toward V equal n. Their original ports are a n and b n,
with a,b>=0. Equality of their coordinates on V's same actual
support forces a=b. This equality is derived from the physical
support; no real mean is changed to its midpoint or another
representative.

If a=0, both K ports are O and the previous support-cone
argument applies. If a>0, K and U both contain the distinct

contains the connecting segment. Its actual outward normal
m therefore has h_K(m)=0 and m.n=0. The unchanged positive
pair sum is mu m+nu n, whose m projection is exactly mu>0.
The conclusion follows from that actual support. This exhausts
the only case omitted by the all-nonparallel argument.
