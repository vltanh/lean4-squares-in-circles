## 2. A nonzero-moment triangle cannot have a tangent external ray

When O is in a root side interior and the external resultant is
parallel to that side, both horizontal normals would have the
same physical sign. Their actual nonnegative heights and zero
total moment force both individual moments to vanish.

Otherwise the two vertical components are of opposite signs.
The true concurrence lies on y=0. The downward port's proper
length is zero, so it is the concurrence itself. A downward
owned axis contradicts the other upward normal by the physical
owned-normal nonobtuse lemma. Otherwise it is a strict bottom
vertex. The upward port is either the opposite top vertex
(forbidden in an actual unit triangle) or the directly aligned
top point, whose proper line is the owned upward axis and
contradicts the same nonobtuse lemma. An interior top-side mean
would also require that vertical axis and line. This is a full
feature cover, including zero proper length and original endpoints.

At a physically clear hub with exactly two triangular force
systems, their complete external resultants are opposite.

put O on a segment interior, excluding a root vertex. A root
side would force both rays tangent, just excluded. Hence O
belongs to the root's open interior. The two triangle moments
are kept separate and need not have equal signs or magnitudes.
