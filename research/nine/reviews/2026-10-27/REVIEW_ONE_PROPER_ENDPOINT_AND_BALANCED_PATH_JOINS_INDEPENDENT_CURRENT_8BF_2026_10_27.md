## 3. The actual diagonal chart retains every allowed application

A genuine unloaded degree-two diagonal transmitter has its two actual
applications at opposite vertices, separated by sqrt(2) times its
original unit normal n. Compatible diagonal avoidance excludes a
surviving point-only vertex/vertex contact with this diagonal normal.
Consequently each endpoint application lies in the relative interior of
a side of its endpoint unit, and both endpoint frames own n. This is the
exact physical chart of the current force-domain input; it is not a
post-extraction choice of normal.

After taking n=e_x the centers have horizontal separation
D=1+sqrt(2). Every contained unit center has norm less than M=91/60,
since M^2+M+1/2-Q=31/57600>0. Thus the endpoint centers can be written

    c_X=(-p,y_X), c_Y=(q,y_Y), p,q>0, p+q=D,

and indeed p,q>D-M>53/60>1/2. Their original applications are

    p_X=(-p+1/2,h), p_Y=(q-1/2,h),
    |h-y_X|<1/2, |h-y_Y|<1/2.

Both strict inequalities are justified by the actual side-interior
premise. They do not shrink or specialize an allowed interval. A
vertex/vertex endpoint with the forbidden diagonal normal is not part
of the selected domain and cannot be reintroduced as a weighted limit.

Only Y is assumed to have a proper radial port:

## 6. The true loaded endpoint: every parallel and two-corner case

The endpoint instantiation requires a genuinely loaded Y with exactly
two actual positive pair forces, one toward U and the other toward a
physical boundary neighbor Z. This second-neighbor condition is
essential and is now explicit in the generic theorem.

If the two pair normals are parallel, full force balance makes their
axis parallel to the nonzero circle resultant. An outward force cannot
occur. For a unique physical corner it exposes an unshareable circle
point. For a physical two-corner square and a horizontal resultant it
exposes the outer radial face, whose support exceeds R-1, leaving less
than the minimum width one for a genuine unit neighbor. Nonhorizontal
resultants expose an unshareable outer corner. Thus both pair forces
point inward.

For a unique circle corner z=(a0,b0), a0,b0>1/2 in the actual square
frame, both inward contacts are at p=(a0-1,b0-1). Their full sum is a
nonzero negative multiple of z. Full torque gives det(p,z)=a0-b0=0.
The actual Y--Z contact is therefore radial, contrary to the complete
boundary-boundary radial-contact Lemma 6.1. This argument does not
assume U is boundary or invoke a three-boundary-body shared-point rule.

For two physical corners use Y=[K-1,K]x[-1/2,1/2] and its original
normalized resultant (K,eta), |eta|<=1/2. If eta!=0, both inward
contacts are at (K-1,-sign(eta)/2), whose determinant with the resultant
is (K-1)eta+sign(eta)K/2, nonzero with sign eta. Full torque is
impossible. If eta=0, both actual means may lie anywhere on the inner
side, but the complete shallow-inner lemma excludes the physical
boundary neighbor Z at every such mean.

This includes eta=+/-1/2, namely a zero individual circle weight in the
physical two-corner mask. It also includes side endpoints and all
intermediate horizontal means in the eta=0 case. The full current
shallow-inner proof has a convexity endpoint reduction for the other
unit's side contacts, so it is not restricted to an arbitrary chosen
vertex application.

The pair lines are consequently nonparallel. The complete current
rectangle concurrence lemma then supplies q_Y=tF_Y with t>0 and its
proper forward port. That local lemma needs no boundary neighbor and
no degree equation at X. For physical two-corner Y its radial point
need not belong to Y; no part of the new angle proof assumes otherwise.
