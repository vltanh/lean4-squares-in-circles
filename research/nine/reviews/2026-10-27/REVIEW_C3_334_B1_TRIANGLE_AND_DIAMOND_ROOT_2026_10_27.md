## 1. Actual triangle externals and positive rays

The triangle argument keeps the entire external system at each vertex,
including all original circle atoms and every external pair force. Its
zero origin moment is established before any concurrence is used. At a
nonparallel vertex, properness gives q=tF with t>0 and the two genuine
ports q+a_i n_i. The noncollinearity input is the full original theorem;
it is not obtained by splitting an articulation's circle load between
its blocks.

The strict positive-ray lemma includes the delicate case where the
weighted mean lies on the body's supporting face. There both actual
ports lie on that face; the normal component of their positive sum is
strictly positive. Thus the negative resultant points strictly inward.
If the positive external ray missed the body, an own coordinate interval
[a,a+1] with a>=0 would put q on its opposite side. Both proper ports
would lie on the far wall, with positive normal components. The actual
weighted moment puts zero between their transverse heights. The support
inequality h_i>=A/(n_i)_x, A=a+1>=1, then gives the stated common center
cap, of diameter strictly less than one. Distinct unit squares cannot
have centers less than one apart. The parallel case uses its actual two
wall means straddling zero and the separate same-cap bound; it is not
assigned a fictitious finite concurrence.

For a genuine leaf at a triangle tip, zero common cycle moment would
make the other loaded tip parallel radial S or D. The two remaining
proper origin lines force their mutual application to be O. The common
segment to the actual radial application fixes the two unit frames.
The leaf direction and actual corner then exclude both D and S, including
the low and critical radius endpoints. This establishes the nonzero
moment premise used in R2 and R4.

Two decorated petals with a clear common hub supply opposite positive
ray points in that actual hub. Convexity puts O in the hub. The
boundary-O alternative is excluded by the complete own-axis argument:
the nonnegative heights and zero moment force both ports onto the
boundary axis and hence zero individual moments, contrary to the
already proved nonzero cycle moment. The conclusion is O in the open
interior, not merely closed membership.

## 2. Hub clearance precedes removal of its circle force

I checked the complete D, aligned-S and tilted-S feature cover. In the
D case the other near-origin leaf root forces a small own-side margin;
one actual port is too central for the loaded tip and therefore belongs
to the hub. In the aligned case the two original wall means are on
opposite sides of zero. The hub port has squared norm below 1053/5000,
with the full allowed shared-side means retained.

For a tilted S root, the actual force and moment equations give the
positive concurrence and the complete feature alternatives. The small
P or +u port is below the physical boundary radius either because
da>=1 or, if da<1, because the other actual near-origin root forces
1-da<=epsilon. The latter bound is 4 epsilon+epsilon^2<r^2.
This establishes physical hub clearance while its full possible load
is still present. Only afterward may that circle resultant be set to
zero. No premise assumes the sought clearance.

## 3. Outside origin unit and actual winding

With a distinct actual E containing O openly, every closed triangle
body avoids O. A positive external-ray point in each body supplies a
strict separating halfplane containing that body and its proper radial
node. Contact chords can therefore be homotoped to the radial-node
polygon without passing O. In the unique parallel-vertex alternative,
the actual positive ray point and both unchanged wall means give the
same winding through two correction triangles; no parallel mean is
replaced by a midpoint.

The three external forces sum to zero, so the auxiliary triangle has
positive barycentric coordinates for O and winding one. Consequently
the actual contact triangle encloses the full open interior of E.
Each of its sides is a chord of a unit square, of length at most sqrt(2),
so its area is at most sqrt(3)/2<1. This contradicts the unit area of E.
The R2 application uses true degrees X3 and Y2 and the full external
branch sum at A. It imposes no false degree restriction on the adjacent
four-block root.

The geometric directed-moment variant assigns auxiliary positive
coefficients only to the three already chosen physical supporting
lines. It proves its own proper-node and cap statements with those
coefficients. It never asserts that the auxiliary coefficients are the
original stress, and never adds a positive edge at a zero-stress physical
diagonal. All homotopies remain in the same actual bodies.

## 4. Radial contacts and the diamond join

The radial-contact input starts from an already radial physical contact;
it does not assume every contact is a bridge. If the inner body avoids
O, its actual own interval gives radial support t>1. The resulting
fixed-axis neighbor cap has diameter less than one. Thus an origin-free
body cannot have two outward radial neighbors. A genuine leaf root,
which also meets the closed epsilon disk, cannot have another radial
neighbor in either direction.

The three-body radial-triangle proof retains all forces at the higher
degree loaded vertex. In both the nonparallel and parallel leaf-root
cases, the full port equations force the other radial node to have
negative parameter. Its actual far-wall port either contradicts the
small aligned hub-port bound or places two distinct contained neighbor
centers in the same diameter-less-than-one cap. The tilted coincident
vertex and parallel-mean endpoint cases are explicitly included.

For the diamond, the two directed outer moments are a,a and b,b, and
the real chord moment is b-a. If a differs from b, an actual triangular
face has three same-sign nonzero directed moments, so the geometric
triangle theorem applies. If both vanish, the genuine leaf root has
a forbidden additional radial contact. If a=b is nonzero, the real
chord is radial. The hub-leaf placement is excluded directly; the
tip-leaf placements use either two actual loaded private hubs or the
full radial-triangle theorem. The old no-unloaded-diamond-hub source
allows the arbitrary forces at neighboring vertices required here.
Every ordered hub/tip role is accounted for.
