## 2. The complete radius-free support cover

The first theorem assumes an actual unit square K, nonzero directions
m,j, and actual points P,Q with

    m.j>0, j.(P-Q)>0.

positive m component along j forces every minimum-m point onto

This treats every original Q mean and either side endpoint; Q is

Consequently j has a unique minimum vertex Q. In the actual two
orthonormal inward unit edges u,w there, both j components are
strictly positive. The full component cover for nonzero m is:

* If both m components are nonpositive, at least one is negative,
  so m.j<0, impossible.
* If both are positive, the sole minimum-m point is Q, impossible
  by the strict j separation.
* If one is negative and the other positive, the sole minimum is
  the far endpoint of the edge in the negative direction.
* If one is zero and the other positive, the entire minimum side
  is that corresponding edge from Q. The strict separation removes
  only Q and keeps every positive edge parameter up to one.

These exhaust the nonzero vector, including both m-axis cases;
zero/negative combinations were already in the first case. The
opposite vertex and both opposite sides therefore cannot occur.
Thus P lies at distance in (0,1] on one actual edge issuing from
the unique vertex Q. No circle, contact graph, load or degree was
used. The statement is valid for arbitrary nonzero scaling of the
two directions, not only for unit normals.
