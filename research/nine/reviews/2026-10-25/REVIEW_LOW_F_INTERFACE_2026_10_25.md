## 2. The projection proof retains the complete original f-interface

### 2.1. Original chart and scalar enlargements

In the physical fan, the f-owned row has d<0 and the genuinely shared row
has d=0. Setting p=-d gives exactly

    f=E n+p j, g=-p n+E j, delta=c-rp,
    s=Eu-pv>0, c=Ev+pu>0.

Since E^2+p^2=1 and u^2+v^2=1, s>0 implies p<u, not merely p<=1.
Consequently every actual parameter is included in the enlarged closed
scalar domain 0<=p<=u. The geometric offset delta stays the actual offset
of K. It is not confused with the distinct comparison displacement
deltaRot.

The full-window normal-ratio theorem u/v>5/9 is available before the
high-radius contradiction in its source. Its premises here are the true
A2 equation, negative A pressure, actual HAK contact, and K's physical
circle contact. It neither assumes cn>0 nor uses the new return proof.
There is therefore no circular dependency in this use of that theorem.

The positive f center of K follows directly from the original chart:

    L+1/2-delta > 1/2-(2/5)c > 1/10.

Thus the physical far f endpoint is always F=L+1-delta, and the other
f endpoint has strictly smaller squared norm at either fixed g endpoint.
This is valid before deciding whether the far g endpoint is h or h+1.

Both original far-f corner norms increase weakly with r on [0,1], because
F(r)>0 and F'(r)=p>=0. The comparison r=1 is therefore an upper bound on
the unchanged original corner norm. It does not need the original H at
r=1 to be physical, and it changes no shared application mean.

### 2.2. Upper-g comparison

I independently expanded the enlarged upper-g corner

    T+(1-c+p)f+g

in the original (n,j) frame. Its coordinates are exactly

    (C-deltaRot,G+Z),
    C=1+N-(u+v), G=1+v-D,
    deltaRot=(1-E)(1+p)+ps,
    Z=p(1-c)+E(1-E).

The inverse identities v=Ec-ps and p^2+E^2=1 account for both coordinate
signs. In particular deltaRot>=0 and Z>=0 also at p=0, where both vanish.

Under cn<=0, the bounds C>1 and G<7/5 are valid throughout the original
window. The proof of C>1 uses a>1, b>3/5 and the full-window strict ratio
u/v>5/9. The proof of the upper bound on G uses N<=u+v+1/2, D>0 and the

The comparison of squared norms uses only

    -2C deltaRot <= -2deltaRot,  2GZ <= (14/5)Z.

Thus a possible negative G does not reverse an inequality. Section 4 of
the separate rotation manuscript states a corollary with the stronger

the joined proof.

For the aligned bound C^2+G^2<q, A=u+v-1 and B=1+v are positive. Increasing
D is therefore in the stated direction. The reduced function of N is
concave on the entire enlarged interval [0,u+v+1/2]; its square root stays

## 3. The UUU return assembly covers every original KB/HB feature

### 3.1. True force signs and the petal

### 3.2. HB owners and the negative-B alternative

When p>0, the triangle side-normal theorem permits only the H axes n,j
as H-owned HB directions; both have positive pressure. At p=0, the extra
permitted axis -j is handled separately: it would put all of B strictly
below K, because H0-1<h. This correctly retains the zero dot-product
endpoint of the triangle theorem. Thus every H-owned HB, including
every shared HB, is excluded.

If B had negative pressure, its B-owned HB would be excluded by the
whole tip-owned triangle theorem. A free HB is excluded by the joined
fixed-singleton/high-radius theorem and the complete low shared-neighbor
diamond theorem. Swapping A and B preserves this diamond's true degrees,
the physical HKB triangle, K's physical circle contact, the intact petal,
and every original force. The low/high split covers q=17/4 exactly once.
The fixed-point exception is not silently discarded in those sources.

This establishes only c_H dot F_B>=0, including equality. That weak sign
is sufficient: B2 and c_H dot ell<0 force c_H dot k>0 strictly. At K, -f
is an actual owned KH axis, so the physical triangle gives k_f<=0.
Together with cf,cg>0, this forces k_g>0. Consequently the complete KB
fan is precisely the top side k=g or the upper-left vertex with
k_f<0<k_g. No alternative normal has been chosen.

### 3.3. Top-side KB: tilted and aligned B

For tilted B I checked the whole edges, not only their corners. Its
bottom vertex Q0 lies on K's top side, hence q_x>=X and q_y=W. The edge
from Q0 in direction (C,S1) misses H because all its points are on or to

At each remaining B feature the incoming normal ell has ell_f>0. If
ell_g<=0, then ell dot n>0, so H's complete maximum-ell support face is
contained in U-[0,1]j and has height at most H0<W. It cannot touch B.
Thus ell_g>0, giving positive HB pressure and the required contradiction.
The free-normal cone at the surviving B vertex is included.

For aligned B, its top and right edges are excluded separately, including
the equality x=X on the right edge. The remaining bottom/left incoming
normal cone has nonnegative f,g coordinates, at least one positive.
This again contradicts the strict negative HB pressure and retains
every side mean. At p=0 the whole top-side branch is already impossible
by the strict height gap H0<W. This top-side exclusion does not need
B to be physically unique.

### 3.4. Upper-left KB: legitimate fixed-tip use and support contradiction

Here the actual KB point is Q=(X,W), while the actual HB point P differs
from Q because Q is not in H. Both intersections are singletons before
the generic selection theorem is invoked: KB has a unique K support
vertex, and shared HB was already excluded. Thus the existing selected
certificate's fixed-point theorem has its exact hypotheses. Its
coincident radial-D exception is absent because P!=Q. No post-stress
normal change is made.

The original KB normal is not K-owned and HB is not H-owned; after the
fixed-tip exclusion both are B-owned axes. At B their actual outward

The pressure signs remove ell=k and ell=-Jk; the nonobtuse condition
removes ell=-k. The only remaining axis is ell=Jk, with both components

Writing the original B support coordinates as

    Q=I+theta ell, P=I+sigma k, 0<=theta,sigma<=1

therefore gives P_g>=Q_g=W. The incoming-normal convention in this formula
is correct: k and ell point into B from its two minimum supporting faces.
Neither application is replaced by I.

On H, ell dot j<0 strictly, even at p=0. Its maximum-ell support set must
therefore contain the full displacement -j and lies on the segment
joining U-j to U-n-j. Every point there has g coordinate at most
H0+p-E<=H0<W. The same original point P would have to lie below W,
contradicting the preceding B support inequality. Endpoint values of
theta and sigma only enlarge the argument and cause no sign loss.

This exhausts the complete actual KB fan and proves the whole UUU
f-owned/shared HK exclusion.
