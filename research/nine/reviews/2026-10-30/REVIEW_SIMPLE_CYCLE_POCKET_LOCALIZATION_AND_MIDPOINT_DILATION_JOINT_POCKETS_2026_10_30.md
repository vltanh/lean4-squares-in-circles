## 5. The midpoint dilation controls whole squares and all checked pairs

For the actual width-one channel let

    p_A=(ell,h), p_B=(ell+1,h), p=(p_A+p_B)/2.

The original two-port equations give p_A in A, p_B in B and the
whole segment p_A p_B in U. In particular p belongs to U. This

For a moving square Ei write H_i(n) for its support halfwidth.
Keeping its orientation fixed gives H_i(n)>=1/2 for every unit

    n.(c_i-p) >= H_i(n)+n.(p_A-p)
               >= H_i(n)-1/2 >= 0.

Under c_i(s)=p+s(c_i-p), s>=1, its center support against that
same fixed A separator cannot decrease. The identical estimate
with p_B treats B. This includes arbitrary frames and every
equality in the original physical separation. A/B separation

For U, the actual p in U gives

    n.(c_i-p)>=H_i(n)>=1/2.

Its gap therefore increases strictly for s>1. For two moving
squares an original separator gives

    n.(c_j-c_i)>=H_i(n)+H_j(n)>0.

The left side is multiplied by s and the support halfwidths do
not change. Their separation becomes strict as well. These are
full support inequalities for the actual units, not conditions
