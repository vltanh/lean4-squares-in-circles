## 2. Same-side transitive feature: complete physical reduction

Normalize the original same-side \(C\)-contacts to \((0,0)\), \((d,0)\),
\(0<d<1\), with \(C\) below the line. The nonparallel \(A\)-frame has its
bottom vertex at the first point. Nonobtuseness with its \(C\)-return leaves
only the two lower own-axis possibilities for AB. The \(-u\) choice would
put the actual second \(C\)-contact on the wrong side of its separating line,
so AB has normal \(-v=(s,-c)\) and application \(P=\ell u\), \(0<\ell<1\).

Both original \(B\)-applications are strict vertices. Opposite vertices
cannot have touching returns \(A,C\); hence they are adjacent. The exact
unit-distance equation gives
\(S=\ell s\), \(C_0=\sqrt{1-S^2}\), \(d=\ell c+C_0\).
The other quadratic root would make \(d<0\), because \(C_0>\ell c\) follows
from \(\ell<1\). The actual condition \(d<1\) gives \(\ell>2c\), and hence
\(0<c<1/2\). This proves coverage of the whole stated physical feature,
not only sufficiency of a chosen chart.

Dividing the complete local equations by the positive AB magnitude is an
algebraic normalization, not a modification of an outside equilibrium.
The full \(A\)-force has positive own components \(as\), \(1+ac\), forcing
its actual exit to \(u+v\). Its original moment gives
\(a=(1-\ell)/(s-c)>0\).

At \(B\), the frame has determinant \(-1\). With
\(K=sC_0+cS\), \(L=cC_0-sS<0\), the complete moment at its original bottom
contact is

\[
 -L+(tS-K)-\epsilon(tC_0-L)=0.
\]

The second own component is strictly positive. The first component therefore
gives exactly three support branches. Independent expansion reproduces

\[
 t=\frac{K+L}{S},\qquad
 t=\frac K{S-C_0},\qquad
 t=\frac KS,\quad\epsilon=-\ell L,
\]

with the stated positive-force predicates. In the third branch,
\(KC_0-LS=s\) gives the exact original mean; it is not an endpoint choice.
The complete \(C\)-moment gives \(m=dt/(a+t)\in(0,d)\).

All three \(W\)-points are actual outside-square points by the unit-step
lemma. Relaxing the last mean to every \(m\in[0,d]\) only enlarges the
possible point triples. Homogeneous clearing of \(t=N/D\) in the B unit
direction is valid because every original branch has \(D>0\); its boundary
extensions do not set a positive original force to zero. The exact horizontal
variance identity retains the dependence on the same mean \(m\), and the
resulting bound applies uniformly to its complete original range.

## 3. Perpendicular transitive feature: complete chart and every original moment

Put \(C=[0,1]^2\), with contacts \((1,s_0)\), \((t_0,1)\) in side interiors.
The \(A\)-owned AB normal must be one of its two leftward axes. The \(-u\)
side has height below one, whereas the whole actual \(B\) lies at height at
least one; it is impossible. Thus the original AB point is
\(P=p_A+\ell u\) with normal \(-v=(-s,c)\).

The two strict \(B\)-vertices are adjacent by the touching-return argument.
Since \(P_x>1>t_0\), the AB vertex is the right adjacent one, giving
\(s_0=1+s_1-\ell s\), \(t_0=1-c_1+\ell c\),
\(0<s_1<\ell s\). The complete \(A\)-moment is
\(1-\ell=a(c-s)\); hence \(c>s>0\), and both angles are strictly below
\(\pi/4\). This proves the claimed chart restriction from the original
equations, without an imposed angle cutoff.

At \(B\), its frame has determinant \(+1\). The exact complete moment is

\[
 -L+\epsilon(t c_1+L)-(t s_1-K)=0,
 \quad K=s c_1-cs_1>0,\quad L=cc_1+ss_1>0.
\]

The two strict corner support choices yield respectively
\(t(c_1-s_1)+K=0\) and \(ts_1=K-L<0\), both impossible.
The remaining original exit is its own side direction, with

\[
 t=K/s_1,\qquad \epsilon=L s_1/s,\qquad E_B=(s/s_1)V.
\]

The original \(C\)-exit is strict Southwest, so its original application
is \((0,0)\). Its full moment \(a s_0=t t_0\) gives exactly the displayed
negative-quadrant unit direction. The later omission of the residual scalar
compatibility relation is a safe enlargement **after** this original
implication is used. It does not assert that arbitrary enlarged parameters
are new physical certificates.

## 4. The A-exit endpoint argument bounds variance, not physical contact positions

The original actual point is \(W_A=q_A+e_A\), where the force parameter
places \(e_A\) on the Southeast unit arc from \((1,0)\) to \((s,-c)\).
The other two actual outside points remain fixed during this comparison.
Writing \(v_0=q_A-(W_B+W_C)/2\), the equal-weight variance depends on this
unit direction only through \((4/9)v_0\cdot e_A\).

The closed enlarged chart gives \(q_{A,x}\ge2\), \(q_{A,y}\le1\),
\(W_{B,x}\le2\), \(W_{B,y}\ge3\), \(W_{C,x}\le0\),
\(W_{C,y}\ge-1\). The nontrivial \(W_{B,y}\) bound was independently checked
using the exact positive squared difference
\(s_1^3(4-3s_1-2s_1^3)\); the zero endpoint gives equality three.
Thus \(v_{0,x}\ge1\), \(v_{0,y}\le0\).

For \(e=(\cos\delta,-\sin\delta)\), the scalar
\(v_{0,x}\cos\delta-v_{0,y}\sin\delta\) is nonnegative and concave on
the entire permitted arc. Its minimum is attained at an endpoint.
Consequently the **actual** variance is bounded below by the smaller of
the two auxiliary endpoint variances. Both endpoints are checked by the
certificate; neither auxiliary point is claimed to be in an original outside
square or to represent a newly selected support direction. This is the
essential valid bridge from the two roots to the full positive-force arc.
