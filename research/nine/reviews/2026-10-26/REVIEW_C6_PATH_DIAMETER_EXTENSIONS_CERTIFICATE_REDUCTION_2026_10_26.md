## 2. Two actual middle circle contacts suffice

The easy radius range is valid for every contained unit: from
\(\rho^2+\rho+1/2\le R^2<17/4\) one obtains \(\rho<3/2\).
At the remaining radii, a supposed endpoint distance at least three
forces both actual endpoint radii to exceed \(m=89/60\), since every
contained center has norm strictly below \(M=91/60\).

I checked the claimed weakening of the inherited sector theorem
line by line. The actual endpoint's circle equality is unnecessary:

* Its real corner-containment inequality gives the full
  \(1/2\le h<11/20,\ 11/25<k\le1/2\) domain.
* At fixed actual frame tilt, increasing only the scalar radius to
  \(\bar r^2+2\bar r h+1/2=M^2+M+1/2\) is justified by that inequality.
  The whole interpolation remains in \(m\le r\le M\).
* With \(D=(r-k)^2+h^2\), the independent derivative is
  \[
  E_r=2(r-x_0\cos(w+t))
       -2x_0r\sin(w+t)\,\frac hD>\frac{167}{80}.
  \]
* On the auxiliary curve,
  \[
  r'=-\frac{rk}{r+h},\qquad
  t'=\frac{rk-1/2-hr'}D,
  \]
  and the complete same-domain estimates give
  \(dE/d\psi<-272/675\).
* Decreasing the tilt to zero stays in that domain and reaches
  \((r,\psi)=(M,0)\), where \(E=y_0^2\).
  The exact fixed reserve makes \(y_0<3/2\).

This is an analytic comparison of a function, not a physical
motion or a reassignment of circle contact.

Only the two middle squares need the separate boundary-shadow bound
\(\omega<w=\arctan(23/16)\). Their actual physical circle contacts
supply its radius-equality premise. Those middle units avoid the
origin, so all three actual contacts have nonzero radial directions.

The new angular argument is valid without any cyclic ordering:
ordinary minor angular distance satisfies the triangle inequality,
so the actual contact path gives
\[
d_{\mathbb S^1}(c_0,c_3)<t_0+2w+t_3<\pi.
\]
A nonnegative split of this distance into
\(\delta_0+\delta_3\), with
\(\delta_i\le w+t_i\), exists because the sum of the two capacities
exceeds the distance. Placing the common reference direction between
the two actual endpoint directions puts both centers in the same
sector disk of radius \(y_0<3/2\). Their distance is then below three.

The complete proof retains arbitrary endpoint frames and physical

## 4. Complete support-owner and mean cover

The downward supporting feature of the axis-aligned \(B\) is exactly
one of the following:

1. Its bottom-side normal, with the entire original application mean.
2. Its strict bottom-right vertex normal.
3. Its strict bottom-left vertex normal.

A side-mean endpoint is retained in the first alternative. The first
two alternatives put the actual common application at first coordinate
greater than \(-2/3\), contradicting the whole-body inclusion for \(A\).

In the third alternative, the actual outward normal of \(A\) is
proportional to
\[
v=q_B-P=((1-\tau)a,\ 1-(1-\tau)b),
\qquad P=(-a,b-1).
\]
Both entries are positive by this strict support class; the first is
also bounded away from zero by the displayed parameters. I checked
\[
v_1>\frac{334}{435}>\frac34,\qquad
0<v_2<\frac15,\qquad v_2/v_1<4/15.
\]
The actual coordinate differences satisfy
\[
P_x-(c_A)_x<1/8,\qquad
P_y-(c_A)_y<73/60<5/4.
\]
These are upper bounds, so no missing lower-sign assumption is needed
when taking the positive linear combination. They imply
\[
\frac{v\cdot(P-c_A)}{v_1}
<\frac18+\frac4{15}\frac54
=\frac{11}{24}<\frac12.
\]
The actual support equality at the same unchanged application gives
\(v\cdot(P-c_A)=H_A(v)\ge|v|/2\ge v_1/2\), a contradiction.

This exhaustive support argument uses the genuine proper
intermediate concurrence. It does not justify the corresponding
claim for arbitrary force-free geometry, for an intermediate body
with additional positive forces, or for a side-width channel.
