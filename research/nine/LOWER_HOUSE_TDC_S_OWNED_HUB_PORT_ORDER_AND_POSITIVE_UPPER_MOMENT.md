normal owners, physical circle masks, individual zero weights and all

Use the complete tilted high-root reduction at its literal scope:

    sqrt(17)/2<R<=sqrt(1105)/16,
    d=sqrt(R^2-1/4)-1=1+epsilon, 0<epsilon<1/60,
    B=[U-1,U]x[V-1,V], U^2+V^2=d^2,
    1-epsilon<=U<=d, 0<V<31/120, U>V.

Its entire remaining feature and concurrence conclusion is

    n_BA is West or strict Northwest,
    n_BC is South or strict Southwest,
    q_B=sigma*(U,V), 0<sigma<=1, q_B in B.

The real BA application is (U-1,sigma*V) for West, or (U-1,V)
for strict Northwest. The real BC application is (sigma*U,V-1)
for South, or (U-1,V-1) for strict Southwest. These are the actual
original points or side means; no endpoint is selected anew.

Assume in this manuscript that A owns the original AB normal.
No ownership is assumed at B or either returned boundary unit.

Write n_BA=(-u,v), u>0, v>=0. Rotate properly to make the original

    k=P.e, y=P.Je, a=1-k.

    2-d<=a<=d, 59/60<a<61/60.                         (1)

The actual concurrence at B proves the strictly positive transverse

      =sigma*(U*v+V*u)>0.                            (2)

Root's uniform estimate is

    |P|^2<=(U-1)^2+V^2=d^2-2*U+1

In A's actual own frame there is therefore a real number c such that

## 2. The complete parallel-return exit

If A's two return normals are parallel, their actual touching neighbors
cannot be on opposite support sides. Thus their signs agree. Full A3
force balance with its positive East force makes both normals West.

Both actual returned units lie in x<=-a. Their centers consequently
satisfy

    x<=-a-1/2<-89/60, |center|<91/60.

The second estimate is the usual farthest-corner center bound under
the unchanged radius cap. Thus both centers lie in a rectangle with
horizontal width less than 1/30 and vertical width less than
2*sqrt(1/10). Its squared diameter is less than

    (1/30)^2+4/10=361/900<1.

Distinct actual units have center distance at least one, by their open
radius-1/2 incircles. This excludes the entire parallel-return case,
including all original left-side means and their endpoints. No load
equation at either return was used.

## 3. The complete nonparallel feature cover and actual concurrence

The two return normals now have nonzero vertical components of opposite
sign. A horizontal return would force the other to be horizontal by full
vertical balance, contradicting nonparallelness. A vertical owned return
would have negative dot product with the other's opposite vertical
component, contrary to the actual owned-axis physical triangle rule.

Thus both return normals strictly expose A vertices. They cannot both
have positive horizontal component because their positive sum balances
East. If precisely one had positive horizontal component, the opposite
vertical signs would put the two contacts at strictly opposite A
vertices; two such returned units cannot physically touch. Consequently
both horizontal components are negative.

The two actual applications are exactly the upper-left and lower-left
A vertices. Write

    P_+=(-a,c), P_-=(-a,c-1), h=c-y.

Full A force and torque make their nonparallel original lines concur
on the horizontal line through the original third mean P. Their strict
Northwest and Southwest rays force, respectively,

    0<h<1, q_A=(-a+t,y), t>0,
    n_+=(-t,h)/sqrt(t^2+h^2),
    n_-=(-t,h-1)/sqrt(t^2+(h-1)^2).                  (5)

For completeness, if h=0 the upper line would intersect y=c only at
P_+, while the strictly Southwest lower line meets that height strictly
to the right of x=-a. They cannot have a common intersection. The h=1
endpoint is the reflected contradiction. In the strict interval, each
line meets y=y below the upper port and above the lower one, so its
intersection is strictly to the right of their common x coordinate.
This proves t>0. It does not compare t with a.

The original AB point is now in the relative interior of A's right side,
and all three actual A ports are distinct. No assertion about a newly
chosen mean has been made.

## 4. The actual house embedding identifies T as the upper return

Use one sufficiently close compatible physical plane embedding of the
positive graph, as supplied by the standing uncrossing theorem. At A
and B the relevant three ports are distinct, so their center-to-port
strands have strict cyclic order. Small junction routing neighborhoods
elsewhere preserve that order and the physical boundary access of T,L.

Consider the actual positive four-cycle

    A-B-C-D-A.

At B, in its unchanged own frame, the leaf port relative to the center
is (1/2,1/2). The A port is on the left side strictly above the center:
for a West port its relative height is

    1/2-V+sigma*V>1/2-V>0,

and for Northwest it is exactly the upper-left vertex. The C port is
on the bottom side or is the lower-left vertex. Hence the three
distinct port rays have counterclockwise order

    L, A, C.

The genuine physical boundary L is outside the four-cycle. Its actual
edge BL cannot cross the embedded cycle. Therefore the cycle's bounded
local sector at B is the sector from BA to BC through the left side;
the complementary sector contains BL. Equivalently, the directed cycle
A to B to C to D to A has clockwise orientation. The opposite choice
would put BL, and consequently the boundary leaf itself, inside it.

At A, the B port is in the relative interior of the right side and
the two return ports are the upper-left and lower-left vertices. If
D were the upper return and T the lower one, the clockwise interior
sector from the outgoing AB strand to the incoming AD strand would
contain the AT strand. Its endpoint T would then be inside the
four-cycle, since this positive edge cannot cross the cycle elsewhere.

But T is an actual physical boundary square. Its center has protected
access through its own interior to the containing circle and the
unbounded face; it cannot lie inside this cycle of other actual units.
This is the same boundary-access property used at L, and does not
require either physical circle-corner coefficient to be positive.
The contradiction proves

    P_AT=P_+, n_AT=n_+,
    P_AD=P_-, n_AD=n_-.                              (6)

This labels the actual original T2 and D3; they are not exchanged on
the basis of an unproved symmetry. No mean on CD, BC or TD is restricted.

## 5. A complete thin-triangle argument makes the upper moment positive

Let M_+ and M_- denote the original positive-magnitude-weighted origin
moments of A-to-T and A-to-D. If gamma>0 is the original AB magnitude,
full A torque gives

    M_++M_-=gamma*y>0.                              (7)

The real T has exactly two pair forces. Its individually radial circle
forces contribute zero origin moment, so the original T-to-D moment
is exactly M_+. Since T,D are both physical boundary units, their
original positive contact is nonradial. Thus M_+ is nonzero.

Suppose M_+<0. Equation (7) gives M_->0. The unchanged triangle then
has positive moments in the orientation

    A to D to T to A.

At A, its incoming contact is P_+ and its outgoing contact is P_-.
The actual vertical chord from P_+ to P_- has strictly positive polar
advance about O: it runs downward on x=-a<0 and avoids O. At each
of D,T, the complete far-body port theorem gives nonnegative advance
from the negative incoming port to the positive outgoing port. Its
distance premise holds because every point of either boundary unit
has norm at least R-sqrt(2)>1/2>sqrt(2)*epsilon.

All three unchanged chords avoid O. Their positive total angular
advance is therefore the positive winding of the actual contact
triangle

    P_+ to P_- to Z to P_+,

where Z is the original TD application or shared-side mean. In
particular it is a nondegenerate triangle containing O in its interior.

The all-normal unloaded-three-force separator at A puts the whole
T,D pair strictly behind its East third support. Thus

    Z_x<1-a<=epsilon<1/60.                           (8)

Since both other contact vertices have x=-a<0, containing O forces
Z_x>0. Barycentric coordinates in this actual triangle give

    |Z_y| <= (Z_x/a)*max(|c|,|c-1|).

By (3),(5), 0<c=y+h<151/120 and |c-1|<1. Therefore

    |Z_y|<(1/59)*(151/120)<1/40,
    |Z|^2<(1/60)^2+(1/40)^2=13/14400<1/400.

This is impossible: Z belongs to the actual physical boundary units
T and D, so |Z|>1/2. Hence M_+ cannot be negative. It was already
nonzero, and therefore

    M_+>0,
    (t-a)*h+t*y>0.                                  (9)

Every actual mean and zero circle coefficient was retained. This sign
proof works whether O belongs to A, belongs to B, or belongs to neither.
It invokes no port-order theorem at the near unit A: its one vertical
chord was checked directly.
