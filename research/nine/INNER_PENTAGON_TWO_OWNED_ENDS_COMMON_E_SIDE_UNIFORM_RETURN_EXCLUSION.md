# A common owned return side excludes both unloaded pentagon ends at once

Date: October 28, 2026 UTC. Author: pentagon continuation.

**Status: complete proposed radius-free hand theorem, frozen for full
independent and root review.** Either original end return may be
perpendicular or nonaxis. Every such combination is treated together.
The complete original H4 and both A3/D3 stay; the actual degrees and
loads at B,C,E are unrestricted. No E2 equation or circle bound is
used. No whole original fine family is declared closed.

The proof extends the common-side part of the two-nonaxis-end result
to perpendicular ends, including every shared return mean. The new
perpendicular C estimate is a whole-unit bound with a uniform exact
reserve; it does not require E2 properness or a boundary neighbor.

## 1. Complete actual theorem

Keep actual interior-disjoint closed unit squares with positive edges

    HA, HB, HC, HD, AB, BC, CD, DE, EA.

H is unloaded with its full positive neighborhood A,B,C,D.
A and D are unloaded with their full neighborhoods H/B/E
and H/C/E. Retain the original compatible physical certificate,
its strict point-normal convention, its already common unloaded
three-distinct-singleton rule, every actual mean and every
other actual unit. The actual H is inside the occupied rim
A-B-C-D-E-A.

Assume the following original owner conditions:

1. A owns AH and D owns DH.
2. AB is not the same A axis as AH, and DC is not the same
   D axis as DH.
3. E owns the original AE and DE normals, and these two
   inward normals of E are identical.

Then this entire physical configuration is impossible.

The owned-axis triangle implication already excludes a return
axis opposite AH or DH. Thus each return in condition 2 is
either perpendicular or nonaxis in its own end. The common
E side is a premise about the original normals, not merely
about two points lying on one physical E side.

The complete shared-unloaded-end theorem excludes positive-
length AH and DH. Their original applications are strict H
vertices and relative interior points of the A and D sides.
In each of the perpendicular and nonaxis return features,
full end force balance makes its E force strictly expose
an end vertex. Therefore AE and DE are physical singletons,
at relative interior points of the actual E side.

## 2. Physical tools and the complete four-corner entry

We use the degree-free owned-axis triangle implication, and
the following sharp support span. If an actual unit K has
minimum-n support at Q and v is unit with n.v<0, then

    max_(Z in K) v.(Z-Q)<=sqrt(1-(n.v)^2)<1.            (1)

At a vertex the two inward orthonormal edges have nonnegative
n components; at most one has positive v component, bounded
by the displayed semicircle maximum. At a side its normal
displacement contributes nonpositively and its full tangent
interval of length one gives the same bound. This includes
every actual side mean and both endpoints.

Opposite strict vertices of a central unit cannot support
touching neighbors. For example, at the southeast and
northwest vertices of [0,1]^2, (1) confines the neighbors
respectively to x>0,y<1 and x<1,y>0; a common point would
be in the central open square. Also, a side-interior/strict-
vertex contact admits no third unit: its local half-plane
and strict right-angle sector leave two gaps smaller than
pi/2.

We give the complete local hub entry for the two possible
return types. In the own D chart put

    D=[0,1]^2, P_HD=(0,t), F_DH=(-gamma,0), 0<t<1.

The whole vertical reflection makes the C return point

    F_DC=(-c,c+gamma*t) at (0,1),
    F_DE=(gamma+c,-c-gamma*t) at (1,0), c>0.           (2)

If DC is perpendicular, they give

    F_DC=(0,mu) at (s,1), 0<=s<1,
    F_DE=(gamma,-mu) at (1,0),
    mu*(1-s)=gamma*t, mu>0.                          (3)

In either case the original E inward normal is strictly
southeast and (1) gives min_E x>0. H has unique maximum-x
point P=(0,t) and actual frame

    e_0=(-a_0,-b_0), f_0=(-b_0,a_0),
    a_0,b_0>0, a_0^2+b_0^2=1.

For (2), the H-to-C normal N satisfies N_y>0: support at

For tilted C its minimum-y point is (s,1), with

This again leaves only -e_0 or strict f_0 on H.

Since A owns AH, it strictly exposes an H vertex.
It is not P, which admits no third unit. At the
opposite vertex e_0+f_0 its negative x normal would,

## 3. The actual occupied rim fixes both end orientations

Rotate the common original E inward normal to East.
The whole E side has equation x=d; name its two
actual contacts Q_D=(d,h_D), Q_A=(d,h_A).

The complete occupied-rim homotopy moves each rim
center to its original contact-chord midpoint inside

winding one. An end chord has open segment in its

winding-one side is its left. At the off-chord H

H approaches in that same left-side region. Thus
the actual end orientation is fixed, including all
other original weak contact junctions.

In (2) or (3), H is on the left of the E-to-C
chord in D. Hence the positive rim traversal is

    E -> D -> C -> B -> A -> E.

At A the hub must be on the left of its B-to-E
chord. Since the E contact is a strict end vertex
and both its inward end axes point strictly left
in the East chart, these two signs give exactly

    u_D=(-p_D,-q_D), v_D=(-q_D,p_D),
    D=Q_D+[0,1]u_D+[0,1]v_D,
    P_D=Q_D+u_D+t_D*v_D,

    u_A=(-p_A,q_A), v_A=(-q_A,-p_A),
    A=Q_A+[0,1]u_A+[0,1]v_A,
    P_A=Q_A+u_A+t_A*v_A,                             (5)

where p_i,q_i>0, p_i^2+q_i^2=1, and 0<t_i<1.
The H ports are on the displayed opposite sides,
not sides incident with Q_i: this follows from
the full end equations and the strictly exposed
E vertex, as in (2)--(3).

For a perpendicular return at D, its actual C
application is Q_D+l_D*u_D+v_D, 0<l_D<=1.
For a nonaxis return it is Q_D+u_D+v_D.
At A the identical reflected descriptions hold
with its original B application. Their directed

1-l_D*t_D>0 and -(1-l_A*t_A)<0. In the nonaxis

The two E applications are distinct. Otherwise

## 4. Full end stars give a uniform small hub tilt

We record exactly what full force and torque give
in (5). At D let the H magnitude be gamma and
the E magnitude lambda. A perpendicular return
has vector Y*v_D at Q_D+l_D*u_D+v_D. Then

    gamma=lambda*p_D, Y=lambda*q_D,
    gamma*t_D=l_D*Y,
    t_D*p_D=l_D*q_D.                                (6)

A nonaxis return has X*u_D+Y*v_D at Q_D+u_D+v_D,
where X,Y>0. Full equations give

    gamma+X=lambda*p_D, Y=lambda*q_D,
    gamma*t_D+X-Y=0.
 
Thus r_D=X/Y is in (0,1), p_D>q_D, and

    t_D=q_D*(1-r_D)/(p_D-r_D*q_D),
    1-t_D=(p_D-q_D)/(p_D-r_D*q_D).                   (7)

These are the original entire end equations. At
A the reflected calculations are identical.

Define

    X_i=p_i+t_i*q_i,
    g_i=q_i-t_i*p_i.

In the perpendicular case g_i=q_i*(1-l_i)>=0.
In the nonaxis case (7) gives
g_i=r_i*q_i*(1-t_i)>0. Consequently, for both types,

    0<X_i<sqrt(2),
    g_i>=0,
    X_i^2+g_i^2=1+t_i^2>1,
    P_D=(d-X_D,h_D-g_D),
    P_A=(d-X_A,h_A+g_A).                             (8)

By (4) these H applications are adjacent actual
vertices, with P_A below P_D. Thus h_D>h_A, and

    0<h_D-h_A<1.

Name the rightmost H application D. If necessary
interchange A,D and B,C and reflect the whole
configuration across a horizontal line. Both end
return types are allowed in the theorem, so this
operation preserves its entire domain and gives
the same mirrored chart (5). Translate P_D to zero.
Then the actual H is

    H=[0,1]e+[0,1]f,
    e=(-a,-b), f=(-b,a),
    a>=0, b>0, a^2+b^2=1,
    P_A=e, P_HC=f, P_HB=e+f,
    a=X_A-X_D,
    b+g_D+g_A=h_D-h_A<1.                            (9)

Hence a>0. The strict inward D-to-H normal u_D
at the actual H vertex gives

    u_D.f=b*p_D-a*q_D>0,
    p_D>a.

Together with X_D>p_D and X_A=X_D+a<sqrt(2),
this yields a<1/sqrt(2).

We improve this to the uniform bounds

    0<a<3/7, b>9/10, g_D<1/10.                      (10)

If 3/7<=a<1/sqrt(2), (8)--(9) give

    0<X_D=X_A-a<sqrt(2)-a<1,
    g_D>sqrt(1-X_D^2)
        >sqrt(1-(sqrt(2)-a)^2).

The function

is concave on [3/7,1/sqrt(2)]. At the right
endpoint it is sqrt(2)>1. At the left its first

exceeds 1/10 because sqrt(2)<99/70 and
1-(69/70)^2>1/100. Hence F>1 on that interval.
But (9) would give 1>b+g_D>F(a)>1. This proves
a<3/7 and b>sqrt(40)/7>9/10. The final estimate
in (10) is immediate from (9).

## 5. Whole-C height when the rightmost return is nonaxis

Suppose the chosen D has a nonaxis return. Abbreviate
p=p_D,q=q_D,t=t_D,r=r_D,L=1-t. The common selector
makes C own CD, since the full D3 has three distinct
physical singleton ports and the return is not a D
axis. Relative to P_D=0 the entire original C is

    Q_C=L*(-q,p),
    L=(p-q)/(p-r*q) in (0,1),
    n_C=(-u,v)=(-(p*r+q),p-q*r)/sqrt(1+r^2),
    j_C=(-v,-u),
    C=Q_C+[0,1]n_C+[-rho,1-rho]j_C, 0<rho<1.        (11)

Let N be the original H-to-C normal at f. Triangle
CDH gives N.n_C>=0. Also N.j_C<=0. Indeed, if
both were positive, the minimum C point would be
Q_C-rho*j_C, whose u_D coordinate is strictly
negative, while H lies in the nonnegative-u_D
half-plane at zero. If N.n_C=0 and N.j_C>0,
then N=j_C and

    (0-Q_C).j_C=L*r/sqrt(1+r^2)>0,

contradicting the H-to-C support at zero, because
the minimum C j_C coordinate is Q_C.j_C-rho.

These two signs leave exactly three C features.
At the strict bottom C vertex, min_C y=f_y=a>0.
If N=-j_C=(v,u), write
f=Q_C+(1-rho)j_C+xi*n_C, 0<=xi<=1.
Strict H-f support gives a*u-b*v>0, hence u>b,
v<a, and min_C y=a-xi*v>0.

If N=n_C, write f=Q_C+eta*j_C. Put

    U=a*p+b*q, V=b*p-a*q>0.

The unchanged coordinate equations give

    L=U+r*V,
    eta=V*sqrt(1+r^2)>0.                             (12)

Write a=sin(phi),b=cos(phi),p=cos(theta),q=sin(theta).
Strict D-to-H support gives
theta<phi+theta<pi/2, and theta in (0,pi/4).
The positive function sin(alpha)+r*cos(alpha)
is strictly concave on [theta,pi/2]. Since L<1,
(12) implies

    L>q+p*r.

After substituting (11), this is exactly

    Delta(r)=p*(1-q)-q-(p^2-q^2)*r+p*q*r^2>0.

Delta is convex on [0,1], and
Delta(1)=(p-q)*(1-p-q)<0.
Its convex chord bound therefore forces Delta(0)>0,
or q<p/(1+p). This implies p>7/8: otherwise
q>=sqrt(15)/8>7/15>=p/(1+p).
Also u=(p*r+q)/sqrt(1+r^2)<L. Consequently

    min_C y=L*p-(1-rho)*u
           >L*p-u
           >-(1-p)*L
           >-(1-p)>-1/8.                             (13)

These three cases include every vertex, edge,
closed normal endpoint and the original HC mean.
Thus the whole C has height greater than -1/8
in the nonaxis case.

## 6. Whole-C height for every perpendicular return and mean

Now the chosen D return is perpendicular. Write
p=p_D,q=q_D,t=t_D,l=l_D,s=1-l,L=1-t. The exact
original point and equation are

    Q_C=-s*u_D+L*v_D
        =(s*p-L*q,s*q+L*p),
    t*p=l*q,
    g_D=q-t*p>=0.                                    (14)

In particular (Q_C)_y>=0. The complete local C
feature cover from Section 2 is retained after
the rotation. For tilted C, Q_C is its original
minimum-v_D vertex, and its two inward edges are
the rotation of an original northeast edge w
and northwest edge z. Thus w_y>0 and z_x<0.
The actual W=f lies on the latter edge:

    f=Q_C+lambda*z, 0<lambda<=1.

For aligned C, Q_C and f lie on its original
bottom side. Take z=u_D, directed left along that
side, and lambda=|f-Q_C| in (0,1]. Its other inward
edge is v_D and has positive y component. The
distance from f to the lower endpoint in direction
z is at most 1-lambda. This includes an arbitrary
original CD side mean and either endpoint.

If z_y>=0 in the tilted case, min_C y>=0. In all
remaining cases z_y<0. Only this edge can decrease
height, and both frames give the complete bounds

    min_C y >= a-(1-lambda)*|z_y|,
    lambda*|z_x|=b+(Q_C)_x,
    |z_y|<=q.                                       (15)

For the last inequality, minimum-v_D support at
Q_C gives v_D.z>=0. Since z_x,z_y<0, this says
q*|z_x|>=p*|z_y|; the unit identity yields
|z_y|<=q. It is an equality for aligned C.

If (Q_C)_x>=0, (15) gives lambda>=b, and hence

    min_C y>=a-q*(1-b)>0,

because a+b>1 and q<1. It remains that (Q_C)_x<0.
Eliminating t by (14) yields the exact expression

    (Q_C)_x
      =(p-q)*[-q/p+(p+q)*g_D/(p*q)].                 (16)

This forces p>q. Equality p=q gives zero. If p<q,
then q^2>=1/2 and p+q<=sqrt(2), so
q^2/(p+q)>=1/(2*sqrt(2))>1/10>g_D by (10).
The bracket in (16) would be negative and p-q
negative, giving a positive x coordinate instead.

For p>q, (16) and g_D>=0 give

    (Q_C)_x>=-(p-q)*q/p,
    1-lambda<=1-b+(p-q)*q/p.

Consequently (15) implies

    min_C y
      >=a-q*(1-b)-(p-q)*q^2/p
      >-(p-q)*q^2/p.                                (17)

The first two terms have strictly positive sum
because a>1-b and q<1. Finally put zeta=q/p in
(0,1). Then

    (p-q)*q^2/p
      =(1-zeta)*zeta^2/(1+zeta^2)
      <zeta*(1-zeta)/2
      <=1/8.                                        (18)

The strict inequality is
zeta^2/(1+zeta^2)<zeta/2, equivalent to
zeta*(1-zeta)^2>0. The last is the elementary
completed-square bound zeta*(1-zeta)<=1/4.
Equations (17)--(18) prove min_C y>-1/8.

Thus every perpendicular C frame, every aligned
shared side and its entire original mean interval
satisfies the same whole-C bound as (13):

    min_(X in C) X_y>-1/8.                            (19)

No C force or circle equation was used in either
height proof.

## 7. A perpendicular A return cannot reach that height

In the final East chart, the inward H-to-A normal
and its actual tangent are

    n=(c,-d_0), j=(d_0,c),
    c,d_0>0, c^2+d_0^2=1.

The entire A pose at V=e is

    A=e+[0,1]n+[-kappa,1-kappa]j,
    0<kappa<1,
    F_AH=-Gamma*n.

Suppose its B return is perpendicular. The actual
mirrored orientation (5) makes that return -j,
so its complete original force and application
table is

    F_AB=-sigma*j at R=e+tau*n-kappa*j,
    F_AE=Gamma*n+sigma*j,
    0<=tau<1,
    sigma*(1-tau)=(1-kappa)*Gamma.                   (20)

The last identity is full A3 torque at e, with
the entire original AB mean retained. Since AE
points East, its y component gives sigma*c=Gamma*d_0.
Thus

    (1-tau)*d_0=(1-kappa)*c.

The sharp upward span from R under its original
inward B normal -j is at most d_0. Therefore both
bounds hold:

    max_B y<=-b+(1-tau)*d_0-kappa*c
            <-b+d_0,
    max_B y<=-b+(1-2*kappa)*c
            <-b+c.

Hence

    max_B y<-b+min(c,d_0)
            <=-b+1/sqrt(2)
            <-1/8,                                  (21)

using b>9/10 and 1/sqrt(2)<31/40.
This contradicts the actual BC contact and (19).
Every physical shared-AB interval and mean endpoint
was kept in (20)--(21).

## 8. A nonaxis A return gives the same contradiction

Suppose instead that AB is nonaxis in A. Its
actual mirrored feature gives

    F_AB=-rho*n-sigma*j at R=e-kappa*j,
    F_AE=(Gamma+rho)*n+sigma*j,
    rho,sigma>0,
    sigma=(1-kappa)*Gamma+rho<Gamma+rho.              (22)

Full A3 force and torque give exactly this table.
Since AE is East,
sigma*c=(Gamma+rho)*d_0, so d_0<c and c>1/sqrt(2).

Write the unchanged strict H-to-A normal as

    n=delta*e-eta*f,
    delta=b*d_0-a*c>0,
    eta=b*c+a*d_0>0.

The actual HB normal at Z=e+f is x*e+y*f, with
x,y>0 and x^2+y^2=1. The sharp support span (1)
at the actual AB points, applied in directions
f and -f to A and B, gives delta+x>=1.
On the other hand,

    delta+a=b*d_0+a*(1-c)
            <=sqrt(d_0^2+(1-c)^2)
            =sqrt(2-2*c)<1.

Thus x>a,y<b, and the original HB direction is
strictly southwest in the East chart.

The original A3 has three pairwise distinct
physical singleton ports. The common selector
makes B own AB: the normal is nonaxis in A and
cannot be free. By (22) its actual inward unit
normal at R is

    n_AB=(-k,-h), k,h>0, k^2+h^2=1.

Its y component is negative because
rho*d_0-sigma*c<rho*(d_0-c)<0.
Both it and n_HB are southwest, so their dot
product is positive. In the actual B own frame,
minimum-n_HB support must have the minimum
n_AB coordinate. At Z this gives the exact
original equality

    n_AB.(Z-R)=0.                                    (23)

BC and (19), together with B's upward span from
R under -k,-h, give

    k>b-1/8+kappa*c>31/40,
    h<13/20,
    kappa*c<9/40,
    kappa*d_0<9/40.

Here 1-(31/40)^2=639/1600<169/400 and d_0<c.
The actual vector Z-R=f+kappa*j therefore gives

    n_AB.(Z-R)
      =k*(b-kappa*d_0)-h*(a+kappa*c)
      >(31/40)*(27/40)-(13/20)*(183/280)
      =1101/11200>0,                                (24)

using a<3/7,b>9/10. Every scalar factor compared
is strictly positive. This contradicts (23).

Sections 7 and 8 cover both possible A return
types. Sections 5 and 6 covered both possible
rightmost D return types, without assuming that
the relabeling preserved one chosen type. Thus
the entire common-owned-E-side theorem is proved.

## 9. Complete scope and exact inputs

This excludes all four combinations of perpendicular
and nonaxis original end returns whenever both end
hub contacts are externally owned and both original
E normals are the same E axis. All original shared
returns, arbitrary B/C/E degrees and loads, actual
circle masks and all other packing units remain.

The theorem does not replace the force-axis premise
by the weaker fact that two actual E applications
lie on one side. A nonaxis original E normal at a
side endpoint remains a separate interface unless
another complete proof supplies the stated premise.
No whole L124 flag or coarse parent changes here.
