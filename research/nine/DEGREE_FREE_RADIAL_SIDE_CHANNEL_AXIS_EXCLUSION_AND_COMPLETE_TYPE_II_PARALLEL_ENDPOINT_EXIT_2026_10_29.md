**Theorem1.1.** The configuration(1)--(3) is impossible.

*Proof.* Since Y is an actual unit square, its diameter is sqrt(2).
Choose any one of its actual physical circle corners Z_Y. For every
original point U in Y,

    R=|Z_Y|<=|U|+|Z_Y-U|<=|U|+sqrt(2),

coefficient and independently of every other contact or degree at Y.

On the other hand, the whole unchanged mean interval in(3) gives

    |U|^2<=(A_R-2)^2+1/4.

    (A_R-2)^2+1/4 < (R-sqrt(2))^2.              (5)

To prove it exactly, their difference, with left minus right, is

    2*(1+sqrt(2)*R-2*A_R).

Both 2*A_R and 1+sqrt(2)*R are positive. Therefore

    2*A_R>1+sqrt(2)*R

is equivalent, after positive squaring, to

    4*(R^2-1/4)>(1+sqrt(2)*R)^2,

or equivalently

    R^2-sqrt(2)*R-1>0.

The positive root of that quadratic is exactly R_0 in(1).
This proves(5). Also R-sqrt(2)>0 under(1), so square roots give
|U|<R-sqrt(2), contradicting(4). QED.

No upper bound on R is required by Theorem1.1. Its strict radius
