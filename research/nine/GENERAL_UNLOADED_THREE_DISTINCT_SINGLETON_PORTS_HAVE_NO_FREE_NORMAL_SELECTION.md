## 2. The complete off-vertex radial-line classification

Designate one free physical contact of U, at P0. Since its original
allowed normal must strictly expose the U vertex, use a local
rigid coordinate chart

\[
                    U=[0,1]^2,\qquad P_0=(0,0).                 \tag{1}
\]

Every allowed designated unit normal \(n_0\) is strict Southwest.
This is only a coordinate calculation; the physical circle and
packing are not moved.

Let P be any other actual singleton contact point of U, with
its permitted outward unit normal n. Assume \(P\ne P_0\) and
the normal line at P passes P0. Then

\[
                           P\times n=0.
\]

Write \(P=t n\). The coefficient t is positive. Indeed t cannot
vanish, and if it were negative then \(n\cdot P=t<0\), whereas
\(P_0\in U\) has n projection zero; P would not maximize the
original n support of U.

Thus n points radially outward from P0 to P. The complete square
feature classification gives exactly these possibilities:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
P&n\\ \hline
(1,0)&\text{East}\\
(0,1)&\text{North}\\
(1,1)&(1,1)/\sqrt2.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{2}
\]

To see there is no omitted side-interior case, a positive radial
coordinate forces its corresponding coordinate of P to be the
far interval endpoint one, by maximality. If both coordinates
are positive this gives (1,1); if exactly one is positive it
gives the corresponding adjacent vertex and axis.

The first two rows of (2) cannot be actual physical singletons
under the retained strict convention. If the external unit also
has a vertex there, that convention forbids a U-axis normal at
the point-only vertex/vertex contact. If the external application
is side-interior, its supporting side is collinear with the U
side ending at P. It extends in both tangent directions through
P, so its intersection with that U side contains a positive
segment, again contradicting physical singleton status.
An external side endpoint is its vertex and is already covered.

Consequently the **only** other singleton normal line that can
pass through P0 is the diagonal line at the unique opposite
vertex (1,1). In particular, at two distinct other physical
singleton ports P1,P2,

\[
          \text{their two normal lines cannot both pass P0}.   \tag{3}
\]

This holds for every allowed choice of the other normals,
including their fixed-owner or free alternatives. No positive
degree or force magnitude was used.

## 3. The entire parallel-return alternative is impossible by full positive force

Continue with (1) and three pairwise distinct singleton ports.
Suppose an unloaded full three-force equilibrium has positive
magnitudes \(\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2\), and the two normals n1,n2 are
parallel, allowing either sign. Its full force equation is

\[
                      \mu_0n_0+\mu_1n_1+\mu_2n_2=0.             \tag{4}
\]

Because \(\mu_0>0\), this forces n0 to be parallel to them.
All three unit normals therefore belong to \(\{n_0,-n_0\}\).

Any other normal equal to n0 has the unique U support P0,
because n0 is strict Southwest. That would repeat the
designated port, contrary to the hypothesis. Both other
normals must therefore equal -n0. But -n0 is strict Northeast
and has the unique U support (1,1), forcing P1=P2. This is
the other forbidden coincidence.

Thus the parallel-return case is impossible for every actual
positive equilibrium in this entire physical pattern. It
does not need a determinant exclusion or a newly selected
normal. In particular a prelisted pattern whose other two
normals are geometrically fixed and parallel can safely be
handled by this complete force argument.

## 4. A nonparallel pair makes the designated free determinant nontrivial

Compute torque about the fixed physical point P0, without
changing any application. Write the original three force-and-
moment columns as

\[
       w_i=(n_{i,x},n_{i,y},P_i\times n_i),\qquad i=0,1,2.       \tag{5}
\]

Here \(w_0=(n_{0,x},n_{0,y},0)\). Full force and torque at an
unloaded true U3 give

\[
                  \mu_0w_0+\mu_1w_1+\mu_2w_2=0,
                         \quad \mu_i>0,                       \tag{6}
\]

and hence \(\det(w_0,w_1,w_2)=0\). Torque about P0 is
equivalent to the original torque equation because the
unchanged full force sum is zero. No circle coefficient
or positive edge is deleted.

Suppose n1,n2 are nonparallel and hold them fixed. Their
columns w1,w2 are independent, already by their first two
coordinates. They span a two-dimensional plane. By (3),
at least one of their third coordinates is nonzero.
Their span therefore cannot equal the plane

\[
                  W_0=\{(v_x,v_y,0):v\in\mathbb R^2\}.          \tag{7}
\]

It follows that the linear functional
\(w_0\mapsto\det(w_0,w_1,w_2)\) is nonzero on W0.
As the designated free unit normal varies on its actual
open strict interval, the determinant is

\[
                            A\cos\theta+B\sin\theta,
                           \quad (A,B)\ne(0,0).                \tag{8}
\]

It has at most two zeros on the full unit circle and
cannot vanish throughout any allowed open strict interval.

If both other normals are geometrically fixed and
nonparallel, this already proves the required nonidentity.
If at least one of them is free, its parameter belongs
to its own distinct actual contact point. Choose its
allowed value, and any other independent free value,
so n1,n2 are nonparallel; an open interval cannot consist
only of directions parallel to a fixed unit vector.
Now apply (8). Thus the determinant is not identically
zero on the full allowed product of free normal intervals.

The only omitted parameter pattern is when both other
normals are geometrically fixed and parallel. Section 3
already excludes every positive equilibrium of that
pattern. No zero determinant in that case is incorrectly
declared a nontrivial generic condition.
