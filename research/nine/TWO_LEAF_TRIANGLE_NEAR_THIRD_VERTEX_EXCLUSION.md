## 3. The complete zero-cycle-moment alternative is impossible

Suppose the common directed triangle cycle moment is zero. Choose
two nonparallel triangle bodies, including one genuine leaf root,
named A. Their actual intersections are both O, and the proper-port
relations at their common edge force its actual application to be O.
Thus A and the chosen second unit contain O closed.

A cannot contain O openly because the second actual unit contains
it closed. Nor can O be its vertex: a vertex-origin unit has only
the nonzero radial support radii one and sqrt(2), neither equal to
d in (2). Thus O lies in a relative side interior of the actual A.

The third triangle unit Z cannot contain O. If the second nonparallel
unit was B, then B is another genuine S root at a relative side
interior, and the two complementary half-planes leave no room for H.
If the second unit was H and Z=B contained O, the same genuine-S
argument would make A and B complementary side-origin units, leaving
no room for the already closed-origin H. Both alternatives contradict
the existence of the third actual unit. Hence A's actual application
p at its edge to Z is nonzero.

In an own frame write

    A=[0,1] times [-a,1-a], 0<a<1.

Its genuine radial S support of radius d>1 must be a far vertex.
Consequently

    a=c or a=1-c, c=sqrt(d^2-1),
    epsilon<c<11/60,
    min(a,1-a)>epsilon.                               (4)

Every nonzero radial support of this actual unit has norm at least
min(a,1-a,1)>epsilon. This is the complete face/vertex cover: its
two near-side endpoints have radii a,1-a, its opposite-side foot
has radius one, and the far vertices are longer; only O is a radial
support in the near side's relative interior.

Properness at the genuine nonparallel q_A=O, however, gives
p=ell n with ell>0. Thus h_A(n)=ell=|p|>epsilon, contradicting
(3) because Z is near. This covers both choices of the second
nonparallel body and the possible parallel third body.

The common cycle moment is therefore nonzero. At each nonparallel
genuine root X=A or B, its actual concurrence and proper ports obey

    q_X=tau_X e_X, tau_X>0,
    P=q_X+ell n_X, ell>=0.                            (5)

These are the original positive radial-concurrence conclusions for
the full actual triangle. No radial identity is imposed on H.

## 4. Every tilted frame of either true private S root is excluded

This is the full local feature argument of
TRIANGLE_THREE_DISTINCT_LEAVES_EXCLUSION.md, Section 6. It uses only
the named true root's own three-force equation and the two actual
near neighbors; it does not use an isolated component force sum or
the other neighbors' degrees. For clarity the complete cover follows.

For a tilted named root X=A or B, use its own axes toward its actual
leaf corner and write

    e=(a,b), a>=b>0, a^2+b^2=1,
    alpha=da, beta=db,
    X=[alpha-1,alpha] times [beta-1,beta].              (6)

X cannot contain all of closed D_epsilon in its open interior,
because another actual near unit would overlap it. Therefore

    alpha>=1-epsilon, 0<beta<=2sqrt(epsilon)<13/50.     (7)

Indeed alpha<1-epsilon would give beta^2>4epsilon and hence
epsilon<beta<=alpha<1-epsilon, placing all four side distances
strictly above epsilon. The upper beta bound follows by subtracting
squares from alpha>=1-epsilon. All weak endpoints of (7) remain.

If the root pair is parallel, both normals point -e and apply at
the unique opposite vertex P=(alpha-1,beta-1). Its original torque
requires det(P,e)=(alpha-beta)/d=0, contrary to (7). Thus a tilted
root would have to be nonparallel.

Use (5). If tau>d, every proper port vector P-q has both own
components negative, forcing both actual ports to be the same
opposite vertex. Their two nonparallel lines would then intersect
at that vertex, contrary to q outside X. Hence 0<tau<=d.

The maximum-first-coordinate and minimum-second-coordinate own
sides have support heights alpha and 1-beta, both greater than
epsilon, so (3) excludes them. The genuine leaf vertex (alpha,beta)
cannot be shared with another unit: the actual S leaf occupies
its half-plane there, X occupies a right-angle sector, and the
two remaining sectors each have angle strictly less than pi/2.
The vertex (alpha,beta-1), including its axis endpoints, has
support at least min(alpha,1-beta)>epsilon.

At the opposite vertex P=(alpha-1,beta-1), put eta=d-tau>=0.
Its strict proper normal is in direction

    P-q=(eta a-1,eta b-1).

Both entries are negative at this feature, each has absolute value
at most one, and the second has absolute value at least
h=1-beta>37/50. The normalized negative second component therefore
has magnitude at least h/sqrt(2). Using alpha-1<=epsilon gives

    h_X(n)>=-epsilon+h^2/sqrt(2)>871/2500>1/3>epsilon.

Here sqrt(2)<3/2 and epsilon<1/60 give the displayed rational
bound. Axis endpoints were already included in the side cover.

The only remaining possible support features are the minimum-first
side, maximum-second side and their common upper-left vertex.
Every one of those outward normals has nonnegative second

This excludes every tilted frame with all genuine mean endpoints.

Thus both actual private roots A and B are aligned S roots.

## 5. Every nonparallel private root has a far concurrence and two inner vertices

For either aligned true root X, its actual own leaf frame is

    X=[epsilon,d] e_X + [l_X,l_X+1] Je_X,
    l_X<=0<=l_X+1.                                    (8)

If its pair is nonparallel, both transverse normal components are
nonzero with opposite signs. A longitudinal-axis normal would make
both normals longitudinal by the full force equation, and a
transverse-axis normal would make the other's transverse component
have the same weak sign by the physical triangle owned-normal lemma.
Thus neither axis alternative occurs.

Both normals strictly expose vertices. They cannot use opposite
vertices, by the physical triangle support-span theorem, and the
transverse signs forbid the same horizontal side. They also exclude
coincident ports: two strict normals exposing one vertex have the
same transverse sign, whereas these signs are opposite.
The two outer-face vertices have outward longitudinal components
of the wrong sign to balance the positive genuine leaf force.

Consequently the two actual ports are exactly the inner-face
endpoints

    epsilon e_X+y_+ Je_X,
    epsilon e_X+y_- Je_X,
    y_+-y_-=1, y_-<0<y_+, |y_+|,|y_-|<1.             (9)

Properness in (5) gives k_X=tau_X-epsilon>0 and normals in the
directions -k_X e_X+y Je_X. Equality k_X=0 would give the already
excluded transverse-axis alternative.

Choose an endpoint in (9) with |y|>=1/2. Its actual support bound
(3) says

    (-epsilon k_X+y^2)/sqrt(k_X^2+y^2)<=epsilon,
    y^2<=epsilon[k_X+sqrt(k_X^2+y^2)].                 (10)

If k_X<=4, the right side would be less than
(4+sqrt(17))/60<9/60<1/4, a contradiction. Thus

    k_X>4, tau_X>4.                                   (11)

Every actual port in (9) has norm less than sqrt(1+epsilon^2)
and therefore less than61/60. Moreover its reversed outward
normal at the other body differs in angle from e_X by less than

    arctan(1/4)<pi/12.                                (12)

The latter inequality follows from tan(pi/12)=2-sqrt(3)>1/4.

## 6. Both private pairs nonparallel force two distinct H vertices less than one apart

Suppose first that A and B are both nonparallel. Let P be their
actual common application and let theta in (0,pi) be the minor
angle between their genuine S directions. The noncollinear full
triangle externals in Section 2 exclude both equal and opposite
directions. Properness at both endpoints puts P on the segment
joining q_A=tau_A e_A and q_B=tau_B e_B.

If theta<=5pi/6, their projections on the unit angular bisector
would both be greater than4cos(5pi/12)>61/60 by (11). Every point
of their joining segment would then have norm greater than61/60,
contrary to the actual port bound in Section 5. The exact positive
comparison used here is

    [4cos(5pi/12)]^2=8-4sqrt(3)>16/15>(61/60)^2,

using sqrt(3)<26/15; the final rational reserve is119/3600.
Therefore

    theta>5pi/6.                                      (13)

Let P_A and P_B be the actual H applications with A and B.
The two outward H normals m_A,m_B differ from e_A,e_B by less
than pi/12 each, by (12). Their minor angular separation is
therefore greater than theta-pi/6>2pi/3, so

    m_A.m_B<0.                                        (14)

If either normal were an own side axis of H, the physical
triangle owned-normal theorem would give a nonnegative dot
product with the other. Thus both strictly expose H vertices.

By the actual inner-face endpoint statement (9),

    P_A=P+u_A, P_B=P+u_B,
    |u_A|=|u_B|=1,
    u_A perpendicular to e_A, u_B perpendicular to e_B.

Their dot product is either cos(theta) or -cos(theta). Since
both ports belong to H, their distance is at most sqrt(2),
which requires u_A.u_B>=0. Equation (13) makes cos(theta)<0,
so u_A.u_B=-cos(theta)>sqrt(3)/2. Hence

    |P_A-P_B|^2=2+2cos(theta)<1.                       (15)

The two ports are distinct: equality u_A=u_B would make
e_A=e_B or e_A=-e_B, both excluded in Section 2. They are
therefore two distinct vertices of the same actual unit H,
whose distance must be one or sqrt(2). This contradicts (15).

No full H force cancellation or H frame assumption was used.

## 7. A parallel private pair also forces two H vertices less than one apart

At most one triangle body is parallel. If either private root is
parallel, name it A; B is nonparallel and obeys (9)--(11).
Rotate e_A east. A's two actual cycle normals both point west,
and its original applications are

    P_AB=(epsilon,y), P_AH=(epsilon,z).

The nonzero common moment and the actual A torque make y,z
nonzero with opposite signs. They remain arbitrary original
means in the same transverse interval of length one. Thus

    t=|y|+|z| in (0,1].                               (16)

At B the actual normal toward A is east. Its proper-port
identity gives

    q_B=(epsilon-ell,y), ell>=0, |q_B|=tau_B>4.

The first coordinate is negative: if it were nonnegative, it
would be at most epsilon and, since |y|<=1, the norm would be
less than61/60. Write

    e_B=(-c,sigma s), sigma=sign(y),
    0<s<1/4, c^2+s^2=1, c>sqrt(15)/4>1/2.             (17)

Here s=|y|/tau_B is positive because y!=0. The other B port
P_BH is its other inner-face vertex. Since P_AB is the vertex
exposed toward the east, the actual unit edge to that other
inner vertex has direction (-s,-sigma c). Consequently

    P_BH-P_AH=(-s,sigma(t-c)),
    |P_BH-P_AH|^2=s^2+(t-c)^2
                      =1+t(t-2c)<1.                  (18)

The strict last inequality uses all of the original mean domain
0<t<=1<2c. It does not fix either mean at a vertex of A.

The outward H normal toward A is east. Toward B its direction
is k_B e_B-y_BH Je_B, where k_B>4 and |y_BH|<1. Its east
component is at most -k_B c+s<0. The two outward H normals
therefore have negative scalar product. As in Section 6, neither
can be H-owned, so both actual applications are strict vertices
of H. They are distinct because their first-coordinate difference
in (18) is -s<0. Their distance must be at least one, contrary
to (18).

This covers either genuine private root being parallel. If only
H is parallel, A and B are both nonparallel and Section 6
already applies; (14) itself excludes that possibility. These
are all triangle pair alternatives.
