## 1. An auxiliary owned separator preserves the original H4 equation

At the actual physical HK contact, choose a common separating unit
normal u which is an owned side axis of one of the two hubs, exchanging
H,K if necessary so the owner is H and u points H to K.

Such a choice always exists. A shared side or a vertex/side contact
already supplies it. At a point-only vertex/vertex contact, the two
square normal cones have a nonempty common strict angular interval
by the retained selection. An endpoint of their closed intersection
is a side axis of at least one square. Orient it H to K, exchanging
the hubs if that owner is K. Keep the actual positive HK normal n.
It lies in the common strict interval and each square cone has width
pi/2, so

    n.u>0.                                             (1)

For an actual owned singleton or shared normal one can take u=n,
and (1) holds there too.

The purely physical `UNIT_TRIANGLE_SIDE_NORMAL_OBSTRUCTION.md`
permits any actual geometric separator. Apply it in triangles HKB
and HKC using u and each unchanged actual H-to-tip normal m_i:

    m_B.u>=0,       m_C.u>=0.

The genuine full H4 equation, including its actual S-leaf force
lambda e_H, is

    lambda e_H+mu n+nu_B m_B+nu_C m_C=0,
    lambda,mu,nu_B,nu_C>0.

Its dot product with u and (1) give e_H.u<0. This is not an
equation with a reselected HK force.

Use u=e_x and H's other own axis as e_y. Since both actual leaf
roots meet D_epsilon, their real separating support h satisfies

    H=[h-1,h] times [a-1,a],
    K subset {x>=h},
    |h|<=epsilon.                                     (2)

The actual radial leaf support d e_H has first coordinate h-1.
Thus |h-1|<=d. If d<1, epsilon=0 and h=0, giving the immediate
contradiction 1<=d. This excludes every contact type at low radius.

For the rest d>=1, so d=1+epsilon and R^2>=17/4. The standard
exact bound gives r>129/200. If e_H is the west owned axis, its
actual support point has height zero and H's vertical interval
contains zero. Reflect vertically to make 0<=a<=1/2. Otherwise
the actual radial leaf support is a strict left corner. Its height

    y^2=d^2-(1-h)^2<=4 epsilon.

Reflect to make it the upper-left corner, so a=y>0 and a<1/3.
In both complete alternatives we therefore have

    0<=a<=1/2,       |h|<=epsilon.                     (3)

The actual H and all its forces retain these coordinates; only the

## 2. A near unit against this wall has height at most 1+3 epsilon

The actual K in (2) attains its minimum x at h and contains an
actual point q with |q|<=epsilon. We claim

    every point of K has |y|<=1+3 epsilon.             (4)

If its minimum-x feature is a side, K is aligned with these axes.
Its vertical interval has length one and contains q_y, proving the
stronger bound 1+epsilon.

Otherwise its actual minimum-x point is a vertex P=(h,p), with
the complete chart

    K=P+[0,1](c,s)+[0,1](s,-c),
    c,s>0, c^2+s^2=1.

Write q=P+xi(c,s)+eta(s,-c), 0<=xi,eta<=1. Then

    c xi+s eta=q_x-h<=2 epsilon.

Its maximum height is p+s, and consequently

    p+s=q_y+s(1-xi)+c eta
         <=epsilon+s+c min(1,2 epsilon/s).

For delta>=0 and 0<s<=1, c^2+s^2=1, the elementary bound

    s+c min(1,delta/s)<=1+delta

holds. For s<=delta use c<=1. For s>=delta and c<=s, use
s+delta c/s<=s+delta. For s>=delta and c>s, the exact comparison
delta(c-s)<=s(c-s)<=s(1-s) gives the same conclusion. Taking
delta=2 epsilon proves the upper bound in (4). Reflection and
interchange of c,s prove the lower bound. No K degree or circle
equation was used in (4).

## 3. A complete upper common-neighbor cap up to height one half

> A unit T lying in y>=a, touching the H top at some (p,a) with
> p<=h, and physically touching K, is strictly inside D_R.

Otherwise its actual minimum-y point is the unique vertex (p,a).
Write its unchanged full chart as

    T=(h-z,a)+[0,1](c,s)+[0,1](-s,c),
    c,s>0, c^2+s^2=1,
    0<=z<=c.                                          (5)

contact has x>=h and y<=1+3 epsilon. For any T point with x>=h,
its height is at least a+z s/c: in (5), c xi-s eta>=z implies
s xi+c eta>=z s/c. Therefore

    z<=min(c,c L/s),       L=1+3 epsilon-a.            (6)

This uses the original physical contact, not a sampled placement.

Let W denote the corner (h-z+c-s,a+c+s). The other three corners
are bounded as follows. The initial vertex has squared norm at
most (61/60)^2+1/4, and the corner in direction (c,s) has squared
norm at most (61/60)^2+9/4. For the corner in direction (-s,c),

    |z_corner|^2 <=(epsilon+c+s)^2+(1/2+c)^2
       <31/8+19/400=1569/400<17/4.                    (7)

Indeed its epsilon-free part is 5/4+c+c^2+2cs, at most
5/4+1+phi<31/8 by the exact golden-ratio identity from Section 1
of `TWO_LEAF_SS_DIAMOND_SHARED_HUB_INTERFACE_EXCLUSION.md`.
The remaining increment is at most
2 epsilon sqrt(2)+epsilon^2<19/400, using sqrt(2)<17/12.

The squared norm of W is convex in z, so it suffices to check
the two endpoints in (6), retaining all slider values between.

At z=0,

    |W|^2 <=(|c-s|+epsilon)^2+(1/2+c+s)^2
       <=9/4+sqrt(2)+2 epsilon+epsilon^2
       <11/3+121/3600<17/4.                           (8)

At z=c, this endpoint is available only when a+s<=1+3 epsilon.

    |W|^2 <=(s+epsilon)^2+(q_0+c)^2
       <=1+q_0^2+epsilon^2+2 epsilon+2q_0

The strict reserve in the last comparison is 1/72.

For the other endpoint z=cL/s<c, we have s>L>=1/2. Put

    B_0=s-c+cL/s>0.

    |W|^2 <=(B_0+epsilon)^2+(a+c+s)^2.                (10)

For fixed c,s,epsilon this expression increases with a wherever

    a+s+c^2(s-L)/s^2-epsilon c/s>0,

because s>1/2 and epsilon<1/60. Hence it is bounded by the value

    epsilon=1/60,       L=11/20,       s>=11/20.

    (s-c+(11/20)c/s)^2+(1/2+c+s)^2

## 5. A tilted K puts every east-axis boundary port below its wall vertex

Suppose K is not parallel to H. Its minimum-x feature is the
unique vertex P=(h,p), and this is the actual HK application.
Thus a-1<=p<=a. Keep its unchanged chart from Section 2.

is Q=(h,t). Then T also lies in x>=h and contains the actual

If p<t<max_y K=p+s, the K side from P to its highest vertex
has, at height t, a point with first coordinate strictly between
h and h+1. A nearby K interior point at that same height still
has first coordinate in (h,h+1). It belongs to T's real inward
segment, giving interior overlap by interior approximation.
Consequently t>p would force t>=max_y K. Since K meets D_epsilon,
max_y K>=-epsilon; and Q belongs to H, so t<=a<=1/2. This
would again give |Q|^2<=epsilon^2+1/4<r^2, impossible for T.

The case t=p is also impossible. At the common wall point the

    every east-axis boundary-tip mean has t<p.         (15)

All other actual tip applications are on H's bottom by (13).
Every genuine tip port is therefore below P in the clockwise

occur only at the bottom-right corner and is retained next.

## 6. Both actual triangle pages would have the same orientation

First take a tip T with its actual H port Q different from P.
The physical center/contact part of the triangle from P through
K and T to Q lies in E: K lies in x>=h and T satisfies (14).
The region E is star-shaped about J=(h,a-1) and avoids int H.
That external path is therefore homotopic, fixing P,Q and avoiding
int H, to the path P--J--Q along H's right and bottom boundary.
If Q is on the right side, its retraced subsegment reduces to the
downward path P--Q by (15). If Q is on the bottom side, the path
runs down the right side and left along the bottom.

Together with the unchanged H-center-to-port strands, this is a
clockwise sector of H with nonzero area. Its winding about a point
strictly inside that sector is -1. The homotopy in E preserves that
winding. A sufficiently close compatible physical embedding of the
actual three-cycle preserves it as well, because that test point
is in H's interior away from the strands. Thus the actual simple
triangle H--K--T--H has clockwise orientation. This argument does
not require any generic separation of actual means.

For completeness retain Q=P=J, the common bottom-right endpoint.
Translate this point temporarily to zero. H's inward tangent sector
is [pi/2,pi]. Since K has a unique minimum-x vertex, its inward
sector is [-theta,pi/2-theta] for some 0<theta<pi/2. A third
unit through this point cannot fit in the remaining upper gap,
whose angle is theta<pi/2. Its local sector must lie in the lower

since the gap has angle less than pi. Its genuine vertex sector
therefore follows H and precedes K in the clockwise cyclic order
H,K,T. The original actual HK normal is strictly between -theta
and zero, while an actual H-to-T normal belongs at or clockwise
of -theta. This is precisely the sector-order endpoint rule in
`TRIANGLE_DISTINCT_LEAVES_PORT_ORIENTATION.md`, Section 3.

The compatible physical untangling preserves that actual cyclic
sector and port order. Hence the triangle has the same clockwise
orientation in this coincident-port case too, including a shared
KT side or an actual KT mean at either of its endpoints. No
point normal or contact mean is replaced to make the order strict.

Apply this complete conclusion separately to T=B and T=C. Both
actual triangle cycles H--K--B--H and H--K--C--H would run
clockwise with the same directed HK edge. They would therefore

In a plane diamond this makes the HK edge incident to the
