## 1. The full local theorem

Let A,V,D,B be actual interior-disjoint unit squares. The original positive
edges AV,AD,DV make an actual physical triangle. A is unloaded of true
positive degree three, with exactly V,D,B as its positive neighbors.
Keep all original applications and complete shared-side means. Write the
outward A-to-V and A-to-D unit normals as h,g, with positive magnitudes
mu,gamma. The entire original exterior force is

    F_AB=-mu*h-gamma*g.                            (1)

Assume h is an actual own side axis of A. The pure physical triangle
theorem `UNIT_TRIANGLE_SIDE_NORMAL_OBSTRUCTION.md` applies with no
load, degree or radius premise at the other two triangle units. It gives

    h.g>=0.                                       (2)

**Theorem 1.** If the original exterior direction F_AB/|F_AB| is also
an actual own axis of A, then

    g=h, F_AB=-(mu+gamma)*h.                       (3)

Indeed (1),(2) give

    F_AB.h=-mu-gamma*(g.h)<0.

Among A's four directed own axes, only -h has negative h projection.
Thus F_AB=-p*h for some p>0. Taking its transverse component in (1)
gives g perpendicular-component zero. Since g is unit and g.h>=0,
g=h. Substitution gives (3).

In particular the theorem applies whenever AB has positive physical
shared length: its actual original normal must then be a common own
side axis. A shared contact whose original mean is an endpoint is still
shared and is included. The same conclusion applies to any other actual
A-owned exterior support, whether its physical intersection is a
singleton or a segment.

The theorem needs the **whole** original unloaded true3 force equation.
It does not remove further forces at a higher-degree A. The actual
degrees, loads, masks and all further forces of V,D,B are unrestricted,
apart from their retained physical triangle contacts. Every other actual
unit remains present. No circle or endpoint-torque theorem is used.

## 2. A strict external vertex when the other return is not that axis

Fix an own transverse unit axis e of A. If g is not h, (2) and the
unit-length condition give either g=+e, g=-e, or a direction with a
nonzero e component and g.h>0. In every case g.e is nonzero. Formula
(1) therefore has strictly negative h component and a nonzero e
component. Its actual exterior support on A is one uniquely exposed
vertex. Thus AB is a physical singleton in that case; no neighboring
unit can share a segment of A's strictly exposed support vertex.

This statement does not assign the exterior normal to B. Its owner
can be concluded only from the already adopted common-selector theorem
when all of that theorem's physical premises have been established.

## 4. A symmetric useful restriction on a free roof

with the two triangle returns exchanged. A physically shared exterior
AB would force AV onto the same hub axis, contradicting freeness.
Consequently that free-roof/owned-AD combination cannot have shared AB.
If AD is also a physical singleton, Section 2 and the original distinct
applications make all three contacts singleton, contradicting the same
initial common selector. Thus any surviving free-roof/hub-owned-AD case
must have a **physically shared AD**. Endpoint-valued shared means are
not changed into singleton contacts in making this conclusion.

These conclusions are about the existing common certificate only at
the stated selector premises. Theorem 1 itself is selection-free.
