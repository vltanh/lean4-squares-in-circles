## 2. At positive epsilon the two original roots occupy opposite side neighborhoods

Suppose epsilon>0. A unit containing O as a vertex has no positive
radial outward support of radius strictly below one: in its own quadrant
coordinates, every nonzero outward radial support uses an opposite side
at coordinate one. The two sides through O point outward from the
quadrant and cannot contain t n with t>0. Thus neither A nor C can
have an origin vertex in view of their actual positive support radius
epsilon<1 in (1).

Neither contains O openly, because the other closed root contains it.
Both therefore have O in a relative side interior. Their local inward
half-planes must be opposite; otherwise their open sectors at O overlap.
Their frames are consequently parallel, and their common origin side
line can be taken horizontal, with A above and C below it.

The small positive radial supports in (1) must be parallel to that
line. To check the whole feature cover, a far horizontal face has
distance one from O, whereas an origin-side interior normal has height
zero. A point of radius epsilon on an origin-side endpoint has horizontal
radial direction, and any other eligible side is vertical with horizontal
normal. A far horizontal vertex has norm at least one. These cover every
original side, vertex and endpoint. Thus both arm directions are
horizontal.

They cannot agree: their original genuine S leaves would be the same
physical unit S(e), contradicting distinct bodies and disjoint interiors.
Reverse the horizontal axis so the first direction is east. The exact
physical roots are therefore

    A=[epsilon-1,epsilon] times [0,1],
    C=[-epsilon,1-epsilon] times [-1,0].                  (3)

Both tangent sides contain O in their relative interiors, so A union C
contains a full neighborhood of O. No third actual unit can contain O.

## 3. A uniform two-point cone bound for every physical boundary unit

Let T be a unit square physically touching the radius-R circle and let
z be any one of its physical circle corners. Every point p in T obeys
|z-p|<=sqrt(2). Since R>sqrt(2), p is nonzero and z.p>0. The perpendicular
distance from z to the line through p gives

    angle(z,p)<=arcsin(sqrt(2)/R).

For any two actual points p,q in T, the ordinary angular triangle
inequality and monotonicity of cosine on [0,pi] consequently give

    (p.q)/(|p||q|) >= cos(2 arcsin(sqrt(2)/R))
                  = 1-4/R^2.                           (4)

This is a physical diameter bound independent of every force, degree,
mask and individual circle weight. It applies to any physical corner,
including one with zero selected weight. In the present domain d>=1,
R^2>=17/4, so the lower bound is at least 1/17.

## 4. The hub cannot be a physical boundary unit

Assume epsilon>0 and suppose H physically touches the circle. Let p be
its actual HA contact and q its actual HC contact. Both have norm at
least r, and (3) gives

    p_y>=0,  p_x<=epsilon,
    q_y<=0,  q_x>=-epsilon.

Put p/|p|=(a,b), q/|q|=(c,f), and t=epsilon/r. Thus b>=0, f<=0,
a<=t and c>=-t. If a,c have opposite signs, ac+bf<=0. If both are
nonnegative, ac+bf<=a<=t. If both are nonpositive, ac+bf<=-c<=t.
These exhaust the actual signs, so

    (p.q)/(|p||q|)<=epsilon/r
                  < (1/60)/(129/200)=10/387<1/17.       (5)

Here r>129/200 follows from d>1, R>103/50 and sqrt(2)<283/200.
Equations (4)--(5) contradict each other. Therefore H is physically
circle-clear. In particular its actual circle system vanishes only
after this original-geometry proof, with all its masks retained until
then.

H has exactly its two original triangle-pair systems. Its full equation
makes their pair sums opposite. The full external moments at all three
vertices of each triangle are zero by the actual branch sums. Applying
TRIANGLE_EXTERNAL_RAY_INTERSECTS_BODY.md, Section 5, therefore gives
O in the closed H. This contradicts the neighborhood cover from (3).
No nonzero-cycle-moment hypothesis is needed in this argument.

## 5. The critical radius forces four complete origin quadrants

applications are p_AU=p_CV=O. Thus all four distinct actual units

    A,C,U,V

contain O. A square's tangent sector at a contained point has angular

and can fit only by equality throughout. Each is a right-angle sector,
and the four sectors partition the full angle with common successive
side rays. Thus all four actual squares have O at a vertex, their

Every other physical unit T has open interior disjoint from the interior

    min_T n.z >= h_{[-1,1]^2}(n)=|n_x|+|n_y|>=1.

Every unit has projection half-width at least 1/2. For its center c_T,

    c_T.n>=3/2.
