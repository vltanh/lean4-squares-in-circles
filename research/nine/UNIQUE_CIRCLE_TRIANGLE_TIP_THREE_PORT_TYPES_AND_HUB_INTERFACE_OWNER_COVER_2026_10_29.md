    V=[a-1,a] x [b-1,b], Z=(a,b),

**Classification theorem.** Up to exchanging V's two actual own

## 2. Complete original support-feature cover

A physical circle point cannot belong to two contained units.
At any contained corner Z its inward unit axes u,v satisfy
Z.u,Z.v<=-1/2, so a short radial inward segment lies strictly
inside it. Two units containing Z would overlap open interiors.
Hence Z is not an original pair application.

The original normals at V cannot be parallel. Full force would
make them parallel to Z. A normal along Z supports the forbidden
circle vertex, while every allowed opposite normal strictly
supports the same inner vertex. Two such applications would
coincide, contrary to the actual distinctness hypothesis.

If one V normal is its own outward East axis, the physical triangle
side-normal theorem makes the other normal have nonnegative East
component. The full force equation dotted with East then has
strictly positive circle term kappa*a and nonnegative pair terms,
which is impossible. The same argument excludes a V-owned North
normal. No actual neighbor radius or normal is relaxed here.

The complete remaining support features at V are therefore

    West, South,
    strict Northwest at (a-1,b),
    strict Southwest at (a-1,b-1),
    strict Southeast at (a,b-1).                 (2)

If both normals are V axes, nonparallelism leaves West and South.
This is Type I. If one is West and the other strict, the triangle
axis inequality excludes the Southeast vertex. Full positive
vertical circle force excludes the Northwest vertex, since West
has zero vertical component. Thus the other normal is strict
Southwest at the inner vertex. This is Type II. A South axis
with a strict other normal is the coordinate-exchanged case.

If both normals are strict, actual distinctness puts them at two
different noncircle vertices. The Northwest/Southeast vertex pair
is opposite. Such a pair cannot occur in a physical unit-square
triangle, by the complete radius-free opposite-vertex support-span
argument in Section 2 of END_TRIANGLE_RESULTANT_SUPPORT_BOUND.md.
The two remaining adjacent pairs are exchanged by the own-axis
reflection. They give Type III, at the two left vertices.

This exhausts all original normal owners as well as all physical
singleton/shared choices. A strict support at a V vertex need
not yet have a hub side owner in the physical classification;
that later conclusion uses its explicitly stated selector input.

## 3. Type I: both original V normals are inner side axes

Use incoming normals East at the left-side application R and
North at the bottom-side application P. Their full original
data have the form

    R=(a-1,b-1+w), 0<=w<=1,
    P=(a-1+h,b-1), 0<=h<=1,
    (h,w)!=(0,0).

The last condition is exactly actual application distinctness.
Either displayed point may be an endpoint of its genuine shared
overlap; the physical contact is not relabeled a singleton.

The full force and torque equations are exactly

    mu=kappa*a, nu=kappa*b,
    a*(1-w)=b*(1-h).                             (3)

Their original normal lines meet at

    q=(a-1+h,b-1+w)=lambda*(a,b),
    lambda=1-(1-h)/a=1-(1-w)/b.                 (4)

Here 0<lambda<=1. At least one of a,b exceeds one because
R>sqrt(2); its corresponding coordinate in (4) is positive.
The common proportionality therefore has positive sign.
Both coordinates lie in V's actual intervals by h,w in [0,1].

No equality a=b, h=0 or w=0 is discarded unless it forces
the explicitly excluded common application (h,w)=(0,0).
Both entire original side-mean intervals remain.

## 4. Type II: a West mean and the opposite inner vertex

Use the original incoming East normal at

    R=(a-1,b-1+w), 0<w<=1,

and original incoming normal (C,S) at the inner vertex

    P=(a-1,b-1),
    C,S>0, C^2+S^2=1, q0:=S/C>0.

The strict w>0 is exactly original application distinctness.
Let mu,nu>0 be the East and corner magnitudes. The complete
V2 equations give

    nu*S=kappa*b,
    mu=kappa*(a-b/q0),
    mu*w=kappa*(a-b).                            (5)

The moment identity can be read directly about P: the circle
moment is kappa*(b-a), and the outgoing West force at R has
moment mu*w. Both are original terms.

Thus the entire positive domain obeys

    a>b, q0>=1,
    w=(a-b)/(a-b/q0).                            (6)

The denominator is strictly positive. The weak endpoint q0=1
is exactly w=1 and is retained. It is not an instruction to
replace an original shared mean by the upper endpoint.

The actual concurrence is

    q=(a-1+w/q0,b-1+w)
       =lambda*(a,b),
    lambda=1-(1-w)/b in (0,1].                  (7)

Its membership in V follows directly from 0<w<=1 and q0>=1.
Its positive radial sign follows from a>b, R>sqrt(2), hence
a>1, and its strictly positive first coordinate. No physical-
two-corner radial-membership inference is involved.

## 5. Type III: the two different left vertices

Use the original applications and outward V normals

    R=(a-1,b), n_A=(-c,s),
    P=(a-1,b-1), n_D=(-C,-S),
    c,s,C,S>0, c^2+s^2=C^2+S^2=1,
    p=s/c>0, q0=S/C>0.

Let mu,nu>0 be their original magnitudes and put
X=mu*c>0, Y=nu*C>0. The full force and moment equations are

    X+Y=kappa*a,
    -X*p+Y*q0=kappa*b,
    X*((a-1)*p+b)=Y*((a-1)*q0-(b-1)).            (8)

Use the two force identities in the last equation. It gives
Y=kappa*b, and therefore X=kappa*(a-b). Hence

    a>b,
    mu*c=kappa*(a-b), nu*C=kappa*b,
    q0=1+((a-b)/b)*p>1.                         (9)

All original positive magnitudes remain; their horizontal
components have been derived from the full equations. Neither
is assigned as a selected force ratio.

The actual normal-line intersection is

    q=(a-1+1/(p+q0), b-p/(p+q0))
      =lambda*(a,b),
    lambda=1-p/(b+a*p) in (0,1).                (10)

The equality uses p+q0=1+(a/b)*p. Both proper port lengths
are positive and the point lies in V. These are direct
identities in the genuine unique mask.

There is also a useful necessary actual-neighbor inequality.
At R the upper neighbor A contains R+n_A=(a-1-c,b+s).
Indeed R minimizes n_A on A; at a vertex the two inward
own components of the unit vector n_A lie in [0,1],
and at side-interior n_A is the inward own axis. Thus
the whole segment R+[0,1]*n_A belongs to the actual A,
including either side endpoint. Containment and (1) give

    b*s <= (a-1)*(1+c).                          (11)

It includes equality and all actual owners. In particular a>1,
also already following from a>b and R>sqrt(2). Formula (11)
is an actual original-square restriction, not a substituted

## 6. A nonfree physical singleton belongs to only two units

The following radius-free physical fact will be used literally.
Suppose two actual squares meet at a singleton P, one at a
relative side-interior point and the other at a vertex, with
the original normal strict at that vertex. The first square's
tangent sector is a half-plane. The second square's quadrant
is strictly inside its complementary half-plane along both
boundary directions. The two unused angular gaps each have
width strictly below pi/2.

Any third unit containing P has a connected tangent sector
of width at least pi/2. It cannot fit in either remaining gap
or cross an occupied open sector. Hence no third unit contains
P. No degree, load or selected-normal perturbation is used.

Under the conditional unique-roof owner assembly, every strict
corner port in Types II and III is nonfree. Since it strictly
exposes a V vertex, it cannot be owned by V. Its actual hub
therefore owns the normal at a relative side-interior point,
and its physical intersection is a singleton. Section 6's
fact applies to that exact original point.

## 8. A hub-owned singleton triangle port removes free AD

The following argument has a broader radius-free scope.
Let H be an unloaded true3 triangle hub with one original
triangle contact that is H-owned and physically singleton.
The third-unit fact and the complete touching-return separator
give three pairwise distinct actual applications at H.

Suppose its other original triangle contact were free. It is
a physical singleton. If the exterior contact were shared,
its normal would also be an H axis. The first triangle normal
n is an H axis, and the physical triangle gives n.m>=0 for
the other triangle normal m. Full positive force with exterior
axis t gives n.t<0, so t=-n. Projection onto Jn then forces
m=n, contrary to a free normal. This is the full radius-free
axis-propagation argument, with no term deleted.

If the exterior contact is singleton, all three physical
intersections are singletons at the just-proved distinct
points. The original common
GENERAL_UNLOADED_THREE_DISTINCT_SINGLETON_PORTS_HAVE_NO_FREE_
NORMAL_SELECTION.md applies literally and excludes the
assumed free triangle normal. No shared mean was fixed in
order to invoke it.

Apply this statement at the hub of the strict corner in
Type II, or at either hub in Type III. In both types the
original AD normal is therefore nonfree. It is owned by
A or D, and by both if AD has positive shared length.
This imposes no chosen AD application or mean endpoint.

There is a further exact owner consequence. At such a hub H,
if AD is not H-owned, it strictly exposes an H vertex and
is a physical singleton. Its exterior normal cannot be an
H axis, by the same axis-propagation argument; hence that
contact also strictly exposes an H vertex and is physically
singleton. The three original applications are distinct,
so the same original singleton selector removes a free
exterior normal. The actual exterior neighbor therefore
owns it, at its relative side-interior point. No true
degree or physical mask of that neighbor is changed.
