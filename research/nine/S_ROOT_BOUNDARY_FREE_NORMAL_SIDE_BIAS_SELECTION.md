# A compatible side-biased selection restricts free boundary contacts at S roots

Date: October 26, 2026.

**Status: complete reusable selected-certificate hand candidate, pending fresh full independent review.** The new selection is an explicitly nonempty open restriction of the original strict physical normal intervals, made before extraction. It retains the complete actual mean of an owned or shared side.

## 1. Exact theorem

Work in the original nine-unit physical packing and radius interval
\[
 \frac{79}{40}\le R\le\frac{\sqrt{1105}}{16},\quad
 d=\sqrt{R^2-\tfrac14}-1,\quad
 \epsilon=\max(d-1,0)<\frac1{60},\quad
 r=R-\sqrt2>\frac{11}{20}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Let U be the true degree-three root of a genuine radial S leaf L, with its other two original positive neighbors V,W. Assume:

* V meets the closed disk \(D_\epsilon\);
* W physically touches the containing circle;
* U,V,W have all three physical pair contacts;
* the actual U-to-V normal n is a side axis of U;
* UW is a physical free vertex/vertex singleton.

The degrees and circle weights of V,W are unrestricted. A shared UV side may have any actual application mean, including endpoints. The original S leaf remains present with its actual radial support point.

At the free UW vertex P, let a be the other U outward side axis beside n. Thus n,a are perpendicular, and each strict U-to-W normal has two positive components on these axes.

**Theorem 1.1.** There is one compatible pre-extraction selection for which every eventual positive tuple above has the following physical normal-cone restriction:
\[
 \boxed{\quad m\cdot n\le m\cdot a
       \quad\hbox{for every admissible strict common normal }m
       \hbox{ of the physical pair }U,W.\quad}
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, if W lies wholly beyond U's n-side, so that n belongs to the closure of this strict common-normal interval, no such positive tuple survives.

This is a condition on the entire fixed physical normal interval in the surviving feature, not a reselection of its eventual positive normal. A side-owned singleton UV is included as well as a positive-length UV intersection.

## 2. The actual three-force equations impose the diagonal inequality

The physical triangle and U's side ownership give \(n\cdot m\ge0\) for every genuine separating normal m from U to W. Since UW is free and strictly exposes its U vertex, this dot product is strict. The actual vertex P is therefore on the n-side of U.

Rotate so \(n=e_y\), and reflect horizontally so the actual free normal is
\[
                         m=(-u,v),\qquad u,v>0.
\]
The true U3 force equation, with the radial leaf force retained, makes the leaf direction strictly rightward and downward. Write its actual radial application as
\[
                     p_L=(A,-b),\qquad A,b>0,\qquad
                     A^2+b^2=d^2.
\]
This is U's lower-right vertex, and the complete physical chart is
\[
           U=[A-1,A]\times[-b,1-b],\qquad
           P=(A-1,1-b).
 \tag{3}
\]
The actual UV application is \(Q=(x,1-b)\), where \(A-1\le x\le A\). It is not fixed in advance.

Use positive original scalars \(\lambda,\mu,\nu\), with the real leaf force written \(\lambda(A,-b)\). The unchanged force equations are
\[
                    \lambda A=\nu u,\qquad
                    \mu=\lambda b-\nu v.
 \tag{4}
\]
The unchanged origin-moment equation is
\[
        \mu x+\nu\bigl((A-1)v+(1-b)u\bigr)=0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Subtract (5) from the product of A with the second equation in (4), and use the first one. This gives exactly
\[
                \mu(A-x)=\lambda A\left(1-\frac vu\right).
 \tag{6}
\]
Every coefficient on the right except the parenthesis is positive, and the full actual side interval gives \(A-x\ge0\). Consequently the actual normal in any positive tuple must satisfy
\[
                              \frac vu\le1.
 \tag{7}
\]
The mean endpoint \(x=A\) gives the equality case and is retained. No force magnitude, mean or physical circle weight was discarded.

This derivation uses the whole true root equation. It is not valid merely from a higher-degree root having some three chosen neighbors, and the theorem does not assert that extension.

## 3. The physical small-side request is unique

The genuine root U meets \(D_\epsilon\) by its real inward unit segment. The U/V supporting half-planes, with the near-origin point of V, give
\[
                              |h_U(n)|\le\epsilon.
 \tag{8}
\]
At the actual free vertex P, the two incident outward U side axes are n,a. If both had absolute support height at most epsilon, their orthogonal coordinates at P would give
\[
                       |P|\le\sqrt2\,\epsilon<r.
\]
But P belongs to the physical boundary unit W, every point of which has radius at least r. Therefore at most one of these two incident axes has small absolute support height. In an eventual tuple, it is precisely n.

This is a purely physical uniqueness statement. It permits a simultaneous choice without guessing the eventual genuine leaf, the identity of V, any root degree, or any unknown mean.

A physical unit meeting \(D_\epsilon\) cannot itself touch the containing circle: two points in that unit would have distance at least
\[
                  R-\epsilon>\frac{47}{24}>\sqrt2,
\]
exceeding the unit diameter. Thus a free pair with one near-origin endpoint and one boundary endpoint cannot request contradictory rules from its two ends.

## 4. The nonempty open selection before extraction

Prelist every physical free pair U,W with U meeting \(D_\epsilon\) and W physically boundary. At their fixed vertex P, inspect the two incident U side axes. If neither has absolute support height at most epsilon, impose no new restriction. If one does, denote this unique axis by n and the other by a.

Let I be the genuine open interval of strict common normals for this physical pair. These normals have positive n and a components. If
\[
                       I_+=\{m\in I:m\cdot n>m\cdot a\}
 \tag{9}
\]
is nonempty, restrict this pair's choice to \(I_+\). It is a nonempty open subinterval. If \(I_+\) is empty, retain I. All tests in this rule use fixed physical positions, frames and circle contact only.

For an eventual positive tuple, the actual owned n is the unique physical axis identified by Section 3. If its physical interval had \(I_+\ne\varnothing\), the selected actual normal would have \(v/u>1\), contradicting (7). Thus every surviving tuple has \(I_+=\varnothing\), which is exactly (2).

If W lies beyond the n-side, then n is a common supporting normal at P. The strict common interval approaches n from inside U's vertex cone, and hence contains directions with arbitrarily large \(m\cdot n/(m\cdot a)\). Its \(I_+\) is nonempty. This gives the stated no-survival corollary.

The restriction is open but need not be dense. Its simultaneous compatibility is checked next; it is not justified by falsely calling an arbitrary open half-interval dense.

## 5. Matched junctions and all earlier choices remain compatible

At a four-unit junction, the four physical tangent sectors fill the plane, so all four unit frames agree. Use its common axes \(u,v\) and its common point P. The two opposite-pair strict normals have matched positive component magnitudes a,b as in PHYSICAL_BODY_GRAPH_UNCROSSING.md.

Every request just described concerns a boundary unit at P, so \(|P|\ge r>\sqrt2\,\epsilon\). At most one of the two common coordinates \(|P\cdot u|,|P\cdot v|\) can be at most epsilon. Thus every request at this junction designates the same coordinate axis, irrespective of quadrant signs. If it designates u, all restrictions say \(a>b\); if v, all say \(b>a\). These are consistent nonempty open subintervals of the original matched quarter-circle. If only one opposite pair requests a restriction, the unused matched normal changes with it as prescribed. No new independent parameter is invented.

At smaller junctions there is no matched opposite-pair constraint. Distinct physical junctions have independent parameters. The complete product of the new permitted intervals is therefore nonempty and open.

The earlier nongeneric radial-block restrictions concern pairs of two physical boundary units, as spelled out in C7_TWO_CIRCLE_CORNER_BLOCKS.md, Sections 5, 7 and 8. They do not constrain the present near-origin/boundary pairs. Nor can an all-boundary opposite pair be coupled to a present pair at a four-unit junction: that would put three distinct physical boundary units at the same point, making them pairwise touching. Such a triple is impossible. Indeed distinct boundary-center directions are separated by more than \(2\pi/9\), while a touching pair has minor center separation less than \(2\pi/5\). Three pairwise-touching centers cannot meet these inequalities: if all three successive circular gaps are below \(2\pi/5\), their sum is below \(6\pi/5\), and if one gap exceeds \(8\pi/5\), its complementary two gaps would each exceed \(2\pi/9\) but have sum below \(2\pi/5\). These exhaust the three pair-distance conditions.

Thus those earlier open boundary-pair restrictions and the present restrictions use disjoint parameters, including the matching. All remaining earlier strict nonradial, diagonal-avoidance and finite determinant conditions are open dense on their genuine parameter domains. They can be imposed inside the resulting nonempty open product. In particular this does not replace an already chosen positive normal after extracting a stress.

Use this one common physical branch for the multiplier certificate. Uncrossing preserves surviving singleton normals and creates only genuine shared-side atoms; thinning deletes atoms; shared aggregation changes neither the axis nor the validity of (6) at its actual mean. Every possible eventual physical pair and small-side request was prelisted. The contradiction in Section 4 therefore survives all later operations. This proves Theorem 1.1.

## 6. Scope and fixed original inputs

The result applies simultaneously to every original true S-root triangle with an owned near-origin interface and a free physical boundary neighbor. The circle weight and actual degree at the boundary neighbor do not matter. It is especially useful when geometry makes that boundary unit lie beyond the same root side as its near-origin neighbor: the whole variable-mean free family is then excluded.

A boundary unit on the other side of the root corner can survive the local selection only when its entire admissible normal interval obeys (2). This remaining normal-cone geometry must still be analyzed; no whole original S/S gate is closed here.
