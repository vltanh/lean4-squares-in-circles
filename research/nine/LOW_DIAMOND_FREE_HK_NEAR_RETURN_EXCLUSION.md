Keep the original nine physical squares, true H5/K3/A2/B2, the intact
HXY petal, every original positive force and application mean. A and K

## 1. The actual free junction and its two retained K forces

Use the notation of

    E=su+cv, d=sv-cu>0,

In positively oriented (f,g) coordinates,

    n=(E,d), j=(-d,E),
    X=L-delta, F=X+1, V=(X,h), T=(L,h),
    H=V+[-1,0]n+[-1,0]j,
    K=[X,F] x [h,h+1].

The actual free HK normal m lies strictly between n and g and retains
its original magnitude eta>0. In particular m_f,m_g>0. Assume the actual
KB normal k is in K's strict near-vertex cone, so k_f,k_g<0 and the
physical KB application is V. Let alpha>0 and gamma>0 be the original
AK and KB magnitudes.

We first justify the actual K circle corner without using a K2 equation.
The same elementary L>1/2 calculation used in the parallel-HK source

    L>(39/100)(4/5)+(321/250)(1/3)=37/50>1/2.

Thus K's f center L+1/2-delta is positive and its actual far f coordinate

    kappa zK=alpha g+eta m-gamma k, kappa>0.             (1)

Its g component is strictly positive. The lower-g physical far-corner

    zK=(F,W), W=h+1>0, F^2+W^2=q,
    F^2-L^2=2(x-h)>0.                                  (2)

The last identity compares the original A and K circle equations.

## 2. F>1 and the original near-junction norm bound

Suppose F<=1. Equation (2) gives h>7/10, since
q0-1>(17/10)^2. Also h<x and h<b. Thus

    L^2=x^2+b^2-h^2>max(x^2,b^2),
    7/10<x,b<L<F<=1,
    a-b=x+1-b>x>7/10.

If c>=1/2 then s<9/10 and h=bs-xc<9/10-7/20=11/20, a contradiction.
Therefore c<1/2 and s>4/5. The unchanged proper A2 concurrence gives

    u/v=bc/[(a-b)s+bc]<25/53<1/2.

But E/v=s(u/v)+c<1, contrary to E>v. Hence F>1.

Now take K's actual moment equation about V. Both the HK and KB forces
are applied at V and have zero moment about that point. They remain in
the force equation (1); their zero moments do not delete either force.
The actual AK application is T=V+delta f. Thus

    kappa(W-F)=alpha delta>0.                           (3)

Consequently W>F>1 and h>X>0. The actual vertex satisfies

    |V|<1.                                             (4)

Indeed if |V|^2>=1, then X+h>=|V|>=1, and (2) would imply

    q=|V|^2+2(X+h)+2>=5>1105/256.

## 3. Every possible shared HB at V is excluded before using coincidence

Because V belongs to H and B, their physical intersection contains V.
It may initially be a segment; no singleton assumption is made here.
The strict vertex/vertex HK geometry also forces V to be a B vertex.
In detail, the tangent sectors of H and K at V each have aperture pi/2,
are disjoint except at zero, and leave two complementary sectors with
apertures pi/2+dAngle and pi/2-dAngle, where 0<dAngle<pi/2 is the
angle from f to n. Neither has aperture pi. A B side-interior point
would require a half-plane tangent sector of aperture pi in this
complement, impossible. A B interior point is impossible as well.

If HB is shared, its line through the common vertex V must be one of
H's two front-side lines. Since V is also a B vertex, positive-length
overlap forces the full one-unit aligned placement on that side.

Sharing H's n face would give

    B=V+[0,1]n+[-1,0]j.

This overlaps the interior of K: for sufficiently small positive
epsilon and 0<epsilon'/epsilon<d/E, the point V+epsilon n-epsilon' j
belongs to both interiors, since its f and g increments are positive.

The only other shared possibility is

    B=V+[-1,0]n+[0,1]j.                                (5)

Every one of its four original corners is strictly inside the circle.
For V this follows from (4). For V-n,

    |V-n|^2=|V|^2+1-2(EX+dh)<2<q.

For the two remaining corners, use (2) to obtain the exact gaps

    q-|V+j|^2=1+2[(1+d)X+(1-E)h]>0,
    q-|V-n+j|^2=2[(1+E+d)X+(1+d-E)h]>0.                (6)

All coefficients used are nonnegative and X,h>0; the first coefficient
of X in each gap is strictly positive. Thus (5) cannot be physically
circle-touching, with either mask. This excludes its entire original
shared HB interval and all possible means, without changing a mean.

HB must therefore be a singleton, and its original application is V.
Both B2 applications now coincide at the same physical point V.

## 4. Physical radial B cannot have this vertex

For a contained unit with two physical circle corners, put
S0=sqrt(q-1/4). Its two noncircle vertices have squared norm

    rho^2=(S0-1)^2+1/4.

The radius window gives S0>15/8, because

    q0-1/4-(15/8)^2=27/200>0.

Consequently rho^2>65/64>1. The other two vertices lie on the circle.
Equation (4) makes V none of these vertices, contradicting the vertex
conclusion of Section 3. Every individual radial circle-weight endpoint
is retained; no equality of those weights is used.

## 5. The unique-B coincident star has no boundary-neighbor exception

Suppose B has one physical circle corner zB and positive circle weight.
Since both original pair applications are V, true B2 torque about V gives

    det(V,zB)=0.                                       (7)

In B's actual ordered side frame write zB=(aB,bB), with aB,bB>1/2.
The adjacent noncircle vertices (aB-1,bB) and (aB,bB-1) have nonzero
determinants with zB. At the opposite vertex, (7) forces aB=bB.
Thus the only possible coincident star is exactly the retained radial-D
inner-vertex exception:

    aB=bB=R/sqrt(2),
    V=(1-sqrt(2)/R)zB, |V|=R-sqrt(2).                  (8)

This is also the coincident exception in
GENERAL_FIXED_TIP_NORMAL_SELECTION.md, but no new generic normal is
needed to derive it here. The circle vertex itself is ruled out by (4).

The actual boundary neighbor K rules out (8). Let zK be its physical
circle corner. Since V and zK both lie in one unit square,

    R=|zK|<=|V|+|zK-V|<=R-sqrt(2)+sqrt(2)=R.

All these inequalities are equalities. Hence zK is the same radial
circle point as zB, and |zK-V|=sqrt(2). The latter makes V,zK opposite
vertices of K. They are already opposite vertices of B by (8). A pair
of opposite vertices determines a unit square uniquely, so K and B
would coincide, contradicting their disjoint interiors.

Thus the unique-B exception is physically impossible as well.
