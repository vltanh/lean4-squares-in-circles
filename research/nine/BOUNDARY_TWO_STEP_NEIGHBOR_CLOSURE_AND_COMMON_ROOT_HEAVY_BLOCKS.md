## 1. The actual common interval for boundary neighbors

Let H be any one of the nine actual squares, with center c_H. The
central-disk theorem and complete Section 3 calculation in
NINE_SQUARE_CENTRAL_DISK_AND_SIX_BOUNDARY_NEIGHBORS_EXCLUSION.md prove
that c_H is nonzero and every physical boundary neighbor B of H has

    angle(c_B,c_H)<gamma:=arccos(-1/6)<5pi/9.          (1)

This is the exact interval estimate proved before that source counts
six neighbors. It applies simultaneously to every actual boundary
neighbor of the same H, independent of all forces and of H's circle
status. Denote that single open interval of center directions by

    I=(arg(c_H)-gamma,arg(c_H)+gamma) modulo 2pi.      (2)

Its length is 2gamma<10pi/9. Its closed complementary arc has length

    2pi-2gamma>8pi/9.                                (3)

The full force-free CENTERED_INNER_ARC.md theorem also gives, for
every two distinct actual physical boundary units,

    their minor center angular separation is >2pi/9. (4)

These statements concern the unchanged actual circle and centers.
They do not assign a favorable coordinate frame to separate neighbors.

## 2. A physical boundary path of length two preserves the interval

Let B,J,C be three distinct actual physical boundary squares with
physical contacts B--J and J--C. Suppose B and C physically touch
H; no H--J contact is assumed or created.

The actual contact-center theorem CIRCLE_CONTACT_ANGULAR_ORDER.md,
Section 3, says that each of the two contact edges uses an empty
minor center arc of length strictly less than 2pi/5. The two edges
must leave the direction of c_J in opposite circular directions.
Indeed, if both left it in the same direction, the nearer of c_B,c_C
would lie strictly inside the contact minor arc to the farther one,
contradicting that arc's emptiness. The three center directions are
distinct by (4), so there is no coincident-direction exception.

Consequently the actual center path B--J--C has a monotone angular
lift: c_J lies strictly between its endpoint directions on a single
arc A of length

    length(A)<4pi/5.                                 (5)

In particular A is shorter than pi. Both endpoints of A lie in the
same open interval I by (1). If A left I and reentered it, it would
traverse the entire complementary arc in (3), and thus have length
greater than 8pi/9. This contradicts (5), since 4pi/5<8pi/9.
Therefore

    arg(c_J) belongs to I.                           (6)

This includes every physical contact feature and corner tangency.
It uses center order, not an assumed order of selected circle-force
directions or an artificial contact between B and C.

## 3. Complete force-free two-step boundary-neighbor closure

Define the following set of actual physical boundary units, each
different from H:

* every physical boundary neighbor of H;
* every further physical boundary unit J which physically touches
  two distinct physical boundary neighbors of H.

Each member's center direction belongs to the same interval I by
Sections 1--2. Thus this set contains at most five units. For if it
had six, order their six lifted directions in I. Each of the five
consecutive positive differences is strictly greater than 2pi/9 by
(4). Their total span would then exceed 10pi/9, whereas I has length
strictly less than 10pi/9 by (2).

This is a physical closure theorem. It does not assert that a newly
included J physically touches H, and it does not add an edge, force,
circle weight, or occupied square. A single unit which occurs in
more than one described path is counted once. All nine original
squares are used in the inherited central-disk argument.

terminal four-block means one of these two unchanged-degree types:

* a full-three-loaded C4 H-U-J-W-H, with true private U2/J2/W2;
* a hub-rooted diamond HJ,HU,HW,UJ,JW, with true private U2/J3/W2.

degree. Each block's private bodies have precisely the displayed
degrees. In the diamond, the tips are loaded by the actual

NO_UNLOADED_DIAMOND_HUB.md. Thus all three private bodies in either

For a full-three C4, U and W are physical boundary neighbors of H,
and J physically touches both. All three therefore belong to the
set in Section 3. For a hub diamond, all three are already physical
boundary neighbors of H. Two terminal blocks sharing only H have
six distinct private bodies, so they would give six distinct members
of that set, a contradiction.
