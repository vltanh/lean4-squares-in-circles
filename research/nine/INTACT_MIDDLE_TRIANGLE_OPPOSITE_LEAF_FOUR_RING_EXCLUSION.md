**Gap lemma.** The stated configuration is impossible.

### 1.1. The entire positive or zero-length gap

Parameterize [O,k] by t k, 0<=t<=1. Convexity and O in int A
give an initial interval belonging to A, with last point
a=alpha k and alpha>0. The first point of K on the segment
is b=beta k, with beta<=1. Its intersection with the segment
may continue along an actual K side; no interior endpoint
at K is presumed.

The two closed units have disjoint open interiors. Since every
point before A's last point lies in int A, K cannot meet that
initial part. Hence

    0<alpha<=beta<=1.

The open segment (a,b) is outside both A and K and, by the
stated premise, outside every other actual unit. If alpha=beta,
the single point a=b is an actual physical A/K contact and
belongs to no other unit. This is a complete zero-gap case,
not a positive-distance assumption.

Draw the auxiliary edge gamma from the actual center of A
to a, along [a,b], and then from b to the actual center of K.
The open first and last segments lie in their actual square
interiors. In the zero-gap case they join through the original
two-square contact. The curve is simple and meets every other
