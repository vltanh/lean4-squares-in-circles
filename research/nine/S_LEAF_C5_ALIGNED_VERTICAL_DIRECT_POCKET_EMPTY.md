## 2. An occupied lower-right crosscut is the actual L-A-B exterior side

Choose the actual lower physical circle corner of L,
\[
                             z_L=(d+1,-1/2).
\]
Consider the occupied broken line
\[
 z_L\;-\;(d,-1/2)\;-\;(d,-b)\;-\;(q,-b)\;-\;z_B.
\tag{9}
\]
The first segment lies in L. The two middle segments lie
in A, because \(b>1/2,\ 0<q\le d\) and the actual p_AB
belongs to A. The final segment lies in B.
Every point of (9) has positive x coordinate and height
at most \(-1/2\).

The circle corner \(z_B\) is strictly below its top support
line \(y=-b\). An aligned top corner has a bottom corner
of larger norm; in the nonaligned case the unique top vertex
is (3), whose norm is less than R. Thus the final segment
of (9), apart from p_AB, lies strictly below that line.
The earlier segments lie at or above it and are separated
by their horizontal and vertical coordinates. Consequently
(9) is a simple disk crosscut, with any zero-length middle
segment simply omitted.

Join (9) to the outer-circle arc from \(z_B\) to \(z_L\)
lying in the lower-right quadrant. The bounded region
\(\Omega\) satisfies
\[
                         \Omega\subseteq\{x\ge0,\ y\le-1/2\}.
\tag{10}
\]
This is a containing exterior side for the original L-A-B free
region. For completeness,
the usual center/contact drawing of that path is homotopic
to (9) inside the closed original L,A,B units. In particular
the replacement of the drawn passage through the L-A contact
does not change its original force or mean.

Neither physical circle corner of C lies in the open
lower-right quadrant. With \(K=d+1\), its corners are
\[
 K n+\tfrac12 f=(-Kc-s/2,\ Ks-c/2)
\]
and
\[
 K n-\tfrac12 f=(-Kc+s/2,\ Ks+c/2).
\]
The first has negative x coordinate and the second positive
y coordinate. Thus no third loaded boundary spoke is inserted
on the chosen outer arc. The positive planar contact embedding
therefore places the actual L-A-B free region inside \(\Omega\),
with the other cycle path and its C spoke on the other side.
If B has a second physical circle corner, that additional boundary
contact may cut this containing side into smaller free regions;
it cannot enlarge it. Equality of the free region with all of
\(\Omega\) is not needed. This is the same occupied-crosscut and
boundary-spoke argument used in the accepted BC-pocket source.

At original side means and degenerate shared endpoints, the
compact-interior homotopy argument applies unchanged: a compact
path in an extra's open interior has positive distance from
the original closed occupied union. Additional zero-force
physical contacts may subdivide \(\Omega\), but cannot enlarge
it or alter this allocation label.

## 3. The common blocked point leaves less than forty-five degrees

Suppose a whole extra E lies in \(\Omega\). By (10) and its
vertical halfwidth at least \(1/2\), its center has norm at
least one. ANNULAR_CENTER_ARC_BUDGET.md then supplies an open
arc of the circle \(|z|=r=5/4\), lying in \(E^\circ\), with
angular length strictly greater than \(\pi/4\).

Put
\[
                             \beta_0=\arcsin(2/5).
\]
The exact closed intersection of L with that circle is the
radial arc \([-\beta_0,\beta_0]\). All its points, including
the endpoints, are blocked for another square's open interior.
The point in (8) has angle
\[
                             \theta_p=-\pi/2+\beta_0.
\tag{11}
\]
The entire closed intersection of B with this circle is one
closed angular interval, containing p. This follows from the
complete intersection formulas in
ANNULAR_CENTER_ARC_AT_23_25.md: a physical boundary unit has
center norm at least \(R-1/\sqrt2>1\), so its hypotheses
hold. Here B lies in the strict lower half-plane, and that
interval can be read unambiguously on \((-\pi,0)\).

Every inner-circle point of \(E^\circ\) has angle in
\((-\pi/2,0)\), by (10), and avoiding L forces its angle to
be below \(-\beta_0\). We claim that its angle must also be
strictly above \(\theta_p\).

The angle \(\theta_p\) itself is blocked by (8). If an unblocked
point had angle below \(\theta_p\), it would be below the
lower endpoint of B's closed interval. Follow the inner circle
from that point in decreasing angle to \(-\pi/2\).
This path avoids B. It also avoids every L/A part of (9):
those segments have height at least \(-b>-1\), whereas the
chosen circle path has height at most
\(-\sqrt{21}/4<-1\). The final B segment of (9) is avoided
because it lies in B.
The outer-circle arc is avoided because \(r<R\).
The downward ray from \((0,-r)\) then reaches infinity
without meeting the Jordan curve: its crosscut and chosen
outer arc both have strictly positive x coordinate.
This escape contradicts membership in its bounded side
\(\Omega\), proving the claim.

Thus the entire open inner arc supplied by E lies in the
single free interval
\[
                         (\theta_p,-\beta_0),
\]
whose length is
\[
                         \frac{\pi}{2}-2\beta_0<\frac{\pi}{4}.
\tag{12}
\]
The strict comparison is the accepted exact
\(\arcsin(2/5)>\pi/8\); equivalently
\(\sin(\pi/8)<2/5\).
This contradicts the strictly longer-than-\(\pi/4\) arc
inside E. The whole L-A-B pocket is empty.
