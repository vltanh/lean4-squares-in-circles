## 2. The actual contact triangle is equilateral

P and W are distinct actual A vertices strictly exposed by their free
normals. They cannot be opposite A vertices because V and D touch.
This is the pure physical triangle argument in Section 2 of
`END_TRIANGLE_RESULTANT_SUPPORT_BOUND.md`: if two actual strictly
exposed ports are opposite vertices of a unit square, the strict
support-span bound makes the two corresponding actual neighbors
closed-disjoint. It uses no degree-two equation or circle load at A.
The same argument at D applies to Q,W. Hence

    |P-W|=|Q-W|=|P-Q|=1.

Set k=sqrt(3)/2. Solving these three actual distance equations gives
W=(d-k,0) or W=(d+k,0). The second is impossible already by A's
unchanged roof support: if its original A-to-V normal is h=(c,-s),
c,s>0, then

    h.(W-P)=k*c+s/2>0,

whereas P maximizes h on A. Therefore

    W=(d-k,0).                                    (1)

Put

    v=(-k,-1/2), b=(-1/2,k),
    vD=(-k,1/2), c=(1/2,k).

The actual unit edge P-to-W is v. Its other inward A edge cannot be
-b=(1/2,-k), since h.(-b)=c/2+k*s>0 at the maximum-h vertex P.
Consequently its actual whole square is

    A=P+[0,1]*v+[0,1]*b.

The identical reflected support argument at D gives

    D=Q+[0,1]*vD+[0,1]*(-c).                      (2)

All four displayed edge vectors are unit. In particular the two
exterior own-axis directions b,c satisfy

    b.c=1/2.                                      (3)

This fixes the relative actual frames; no rotation of one square
independently of its original contacts has been made.

## 3. Full hub force fixes the exterior sides and keeps every mean

The complete closed mean intervals in (4),(5) are retained. A mean

    min_B b.x = min_C (-c).x = H0,
