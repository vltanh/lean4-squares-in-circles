## 1. A full-star near-unit theorem with weak return moments

Let U be unloaded. Its entire positive pair star consists of X and
at least two distinct other actual neighbors V_j. Retain original
normals n,g_j, applications p,q_j, and positive magnitudes lambda,mu_j.
Assume the full force and torque equations, and

    h_U(n)=p.n<0,
    det(p,n)>0,
    det(q_j,g_j)<=0 for every j,
    dist(O,V_j)>sqrt(2)*epsilon for every j.             (1)

The last inequality concerns the whole closed units. No corresponding
premise is made about X. The return neighbors need not touch.

**Weak full-star theorem.** In a positively oriented own frame,

    U=[a,a+1] x [b,c],
    0<a<=epsilon, b<=0<=c, c=b+1.                       (2)

The exceptional actual feature is West at a positive-height mean,
or strict Northwest at (a,c), with n=(-u,v), u,v>0 and cv<au.
In particular U is physically clear and epsilon>0. Reflection gives
the reversed-sign statement.

### 1.2. Both own intervals missing zero

Negative exceptional support puts O outside U. In the two-missing
case a proper quarter-turn gives U=[a,a+1]x[b,c], a,b>0,c=b+1>1.
The entire Northeast corner is excluded by the first cap. The
exceptional positive moment and negative support leave West,
strict Southwest, or strict Northwest.

For a Southwest exceptional normal every nonpositive-moment return
has nonpositive vertical component; the exceptional component is
strictly negative. Full vertical balance fails. For West, vertical
balance makes every return horizontal. West has strictly positive
moment in this chart, so every return is East. Two distinct return
neighbors contradict the fixed East cap. A zero return moment does
not add a new horizontal feature: all actual heights are positive.

For Northwest write n=(-u,v), u,v>0, kappa=v/u<a/c. Every return is
East, South, Southwest, or Southeast. For the full star let E be
the East magnitude, with actual aggregate height y and delta=c-y
when E>0; F,T the positive x and negative y sums of Southeast
forces; S,W the negative x and negative y sums of Southwest
forces; N the South sum; and N_x its same magnitude sum weighted
by the actual x-a in [0,1]. The full equations about P=(a,c) are

    lambda*u=E+F-S,
    lambda*v=T+W+N,
    E*delta+F-T-S-N_x=0, 0<=N_x<=N.                   (4)

These identities also hold for Southwest returns of zero origin
moment. If E=0, they give lambda*u=T+N_x<=lambda*v. Thus a>c>1,
and the far U corner has squared norm greater than five, impossible.

If E>0, its actual support yields a<=epsilon and 0<kappa<1. At a
Southwest application (a,b), the far-return premise forces b>a:
otherwise its norm is at most sqrt(2)*epsilon. The weak return
inequality -a*w+b*s<=0 consequently gives w> s for each strict
Southwest normal (-s,-w). Hence W>=S, with strictness whenever
such a force occurs. The full identities (4) give

    E*(delta-kappa)
      =(1-kappa)*S-W-(1-kappa)*F+N_x-N<=0.             (5)

Thus y>c-a/c>1-a. The actual East application (a+1,y) has norm
squared greater than two, contradicting the contact cap. This
exhausts the two-missing case, including every zero-moment return.

## 2. A radial return is impossible in an actual three-force triangle star

Assume exactly two returns V,W in Section 1, and assume these two
actual units physically touch. Then **neither return can have zero
origin moment**. The additional contact need not carry positive force.
All degrees and loads at X,V,W remain arbitrary.

Suppose V is radial. The other return W has strictly negative moment
by full U torque. Use the actual strip (2), and put A=a+1>1.

### 2.1. The actual outer radial unit has a narrow side rectangle

The radial contact is nonzero since U and V avoid O. U must be the
inner endpoint: if V were inner, its origin avoidance would force
radial distance t>1, whereas outer U has distance a<=epsilon.
Consequently its actual common point is P=t*e, with

    1<t<=d,
    U subset {z:e.z<=t}, V subset {z:e.z>=t}.

P cannot be a vertex of V. If its two orthonormal inward axes are
v_1,v_2, minimum-e support gives e.v_i>=0. The opposite corner has
squared norm

    |t*e+v_1+v_2|^2
      =t^2+2+2*t*(e.v_1+e.v_2)>=t^2+2+2*t>5,

contrary to R^2<=1105/256<5. This includes a radial side endpoint.
Thus P is in the relative interior of an actual V side and e is
its actual own axis. For its original transverse interval,

    V=P+[0,1]*e+[ell,ell+1]*Je,
    -9/16<ell<-7/16.                                  (6)

Indeed the two far-side corners give
ell^2,(ell+1)^2<=R^2-(t+1)^2<1105/256-4=81/256.
In particular every transverse V coordinate lies between -9/16
and 9/16. No circle mask or zero-weight convention is replaced.

The radial maximum on U has one of exactly three forms:

    P=(A,0), e=East;
    P=(A,c), c>0;
    P=(A,b), b<0.                                    (7)

In the last two forms the nonzero transverse coordinate has absolute
value less than 11/60<1/5, since t<=d<61/60 and A>1.

The actual radial V already occupies the wider cap (3): e_x=A/t>
60/61, |e_y|<11/61, and radial support t>1. Hence W cannot have
an East normal, nor a negative-moment Northeast normal at (A,c).
For the latter feature, A*v<c*u gives support h>=A/u>1/u, so its
actual normal and center would occupy that same cap. These are
distinct physical neighbors; both forces are retained.

We use two elementary physical triangle facts. An U-owned normal
has nonnegative dot product with the other U triangle normal. Also
strict normals at opposite U vertices cannot have touching outer
neighbors: the support span of the opposite-vertex unit in the
other normal's direction is strictly below one, while the two U
vertices are separated in that direction by more than one. Both
facts retain side means and are the full radius-free support-span
arguments of `UNIT_TRIANGLE_SIDE_NORMAL_OBSTRUCTION.md`.

### 2.2. Radial lower-right return

### 2.3. Radial upper-right return

Now P=(A,c), 0<c<1/5, b=c-1<-4/5. West and South W normals are

A strict Southwest W at (a,b) is an opposite-vertex contact to P,

    A*w>(1-c)*s,
    h=A*s+(1-c)*w<=d.                                 (8)

Put z=s/w. First z<1. Otherwise (8), A<61/60 and 1-c>4/5 give
1<=z<61/48<4/3. On angles alpha=atan(z) in [pi/4,atan(4/3)],

Thus w>1/sqrt(2). The W normal has negative Je projection on V.

    -w+max(0,(A*s-c*w)/t)+(9/16)*(c*s+A*w)/t.           (9)

    z >= (t+c-(9/16)*A)/(A+(9/16)*c) > 7/16,           (10)

### 2.4. Radial East-side return, including both original side endpoints

Let P=(A,0), e=East. Since U owns this axis, the triangle fact

    lambda*u=mu_V, lambda*v=mu_W,
    mu_V*c=mu_W*(x-a), 0<=x-a<=1.

    lambda*u=mu_V+mu_W*s, lambda*v=mu_W*w,

    c^2<=c*kappa<a<=epsilon<1/60,
    0<=c<2/15, b<-13/15.                              (11)

    Z_x-A<=1, Z_y-b>=7/16-c,

The three forms (7) are exhaustive. This proves the entire radial
