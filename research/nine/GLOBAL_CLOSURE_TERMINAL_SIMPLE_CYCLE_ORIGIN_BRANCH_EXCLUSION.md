## 1. Entire original graph and load scope

Let a terminal block of a connected selected positive component be the
actual simple cycle

    H,T_1,...,T_m,H,                    m>=2.             (2)

Every private T_i has its true positive degree two. H may have arbitrary
other positive edges and its original circle load. Suppose one component
of the graph after deleting H contains an actual body E outside (2)
with

    O in int E.                                           (3)

**Theorem.** The entire configuration is impossible.

No private load count, boundary-neighbor premise, width choice, or root
clearance is assumed. H's complete force system is never replaced by
its two cycle forces. The theorem is not a statement about a cycle
whose private vertices have additional positive forces.

By (3), every closed body of (2) avoids O. The outside component
containing E also contains a genuinely circle-loaded body B. Otherwise
it is a nonempty unloaded set whose only positive neighbors outside
that set belong to H, contradicting CY.LOAD.1 in
`CYCLIC_BLOCK_INVENTORY.md`. There is an actual positive E-to-B path
avoiding H and every private cycle body.

The proof will use this unchanged outside path only in the topological
argument of Section 2. All local cycle equations use the original
private degree-two bodies and the true two cycle forces at H.

## 2. A real cycle surrounding O contradicts the outside branch

Choose the common physical embedding from
`PHYSICAL_BODY_GRAPH_UNCROSSING.md`. Its body vertices are their actual
centers. Its selected positive edges retain their actual physical
cyclic port orders. Every physically circle-touching body vertex is
incident to the unbounded face.

The cycle (2) is a simple embedded Jordan cycle. Its actual
center/contact representative has the same winding about O as an
arbitrarily close such embedding: its finitely many closed cycle
bodies avoid O, so the routing disks and tubes for this cycle can be
chosen to avoid O. Compact paths inside int E from O to c_E have
positive distance from those bodies and their contact junctions.
Choose the embedding neighborhoods smaller than these clearances.
Then the representative and embedded cycle have the same winding at
both O and c_E, and these two winding numbers agree.

If the physical cycle had nonzero winding about O, c_E would lie
strictly on its bounded side. The actual positive E-to-B graph path
from Section 1 is disjoint from the embedded cycle: its vertices and
edges belong to the other component after deleting H. Planarity
therefore keeps c_B on the same bounded side. A vertex strictly
inside an embedded cycle cannot be incident to the unbounded face
of the full graph. This contradicts the actual circle contact of B.

Consequently the original physical cycle has winding zero about O.
This conclusion uses neither an area bound for its hole nor any
capacity theorem for a force polygon. Every later nonzero winding
contradiction refers to this same actual embedded cycle.

## 3. The original common cycle moment is nonzero

Orient (2), and let f_i be its directed positive force vector, applied
at the original common point P_i on that edge. Each genuine private
circle resultant G_i is radial in the origin-moment sense, so the
private torque equations give one common value

    det(P_i,f_i)=k                                       (4)

on every cycle edge. In particular the two outward root forces u,v,
with original applications P_1,P_2 at H, obey

    det(P_1,u)=k,       det(P_2,v)=-k.                    (5)

These are consequences of the true private equations. They do not
delete H's own circle force or any other original root force.

First k cannot be zero. For a genuine unloaded private transmitter,
its two applications have displacement w e, with w in {1,sqrt(2)},
by `PARALLEL_TRANSMISSION_AND_FOUR_CYCLES.md`, Lemma N.1. If k=0,
their common line passes through O. Their entire joining segment
belongs to the actual origin-free transmitter, so, after orienting e,
its two applications are t e and (t+w)e with t>0.

At the near contact its actual inner neighbor also avoids O. The
purely geometric radial-contact fact in
`ROOTED_ORIGIN_FREE_RADIAL_CONTACT_EXCLUSIONS.md`, Section 1, gives
t>1. At the far contact the contained outer neighbor requires the
support height t+w<=d. Since w>=1 and d<61/60, this is impossible.
Thus every private body would have to be loaded if k=0. But m>=2
then supplies two adjacent physically circle-touching private bodies
with an actual radial contact. The same radial-contact geometry makes
the inner endpoint physically circle-clear, another contradiction.

Reverse the cycle if needed and henceforth take

    k>0.                                                  (6)

There is at least one private load, independently, by CY.LOAD.1
applied to the whole private set attached only through H.

## 4. All true loaded private stars, including every parallel case

### 4.1. A nonparallel private star

The complete conditional loaded-degree-two theorem in
`FIVE_CYCLE_FORCE_POLYGON_DOMAIN.md`, Lemma 1.1, gives a genuine
concurrence

    q_i=s_i G_i,       s_i>0,
    p-q_i=ell n,       ell>=0                           (7)

at each original outward port (p,n). That conditional theorem requires
no boundary neighbor. It includes coincident applications, unique
circle corners, physical two-corner masks, and zero individual weights.
A two-corner concurrence need not belong to its actual square or disk;
no such containment is used here.

Every actual x in that square satisfies G_i.x>0. For each positively
weighted actual circle corner z,

    z.x >= R^2-R sqrt(2)>0,

by the square diameter. The concurrence in (7) has the same strict
positive projection. Thus conv(T_i,q_i) avoids O. Replacing the
center vertex of the representative path through T_i by q_i is an
O-avoiding homotopy with both actual contacts fixed.

### 4.2. A parallel private star

Opposite signs of the two outward normals are impossible. Their full
circle resultant would be parallel to them, and one actual normal
would point in its direction. A unique positive corner or an unequal
two-corner resultant strictly exposes an actual unshareable circle
corner in that direction. An equal two-corner resultant instead
exposes the outer radial side at height sqrt(R^2-1/4)>R-1; a contained
unit neighbor of width at least one cannot lie beyond it. This is
the complete opposite-sign cover, including individual weight zero.

For the same-sign alternative, the genuine original weighted port
mean gives the exact radial S/D geometries of Lemma N.4 in
`PARALLEL_TRANSMISSION_AND_FOUR_CYCLES.md`. A radial D has just one
inner port, whose line is radial, contrary to (6). The remaining
body is the actual radial S

    T=[d,d+1]e+[-1/2,1/2]Je.

Its circle resultant points along e. Write its directed incoming and
outgoing cycle forces as a e and -b e with a,b>0. Its two unchanged
applications are

    q_- = d e+y_- Je,       q_+ = d e+y_+ Je,
    -a y_-=k=b y_+,         y_-<0<y_+.                    (8)

For an auxiliary vector-path calculation only, retain these two actual
port points as two nodes. Assign the positive radial vectors

    G_-=(a/d)q_-,       G_+=(b/d)q_+.

Their sum is exactly the full original circle resultant:

    G_-+G_+=(a+b)e=G_T.                                  (9)

Put f_*=(k/d)Je. Direct expansion of the same original equations gives

    G_-=a e-f_*,       G_+=f_*-(-b e),
    q_+-q_-=(d/a+d/b)f_*,       det(q_-,f_*)=k.          (10)

The added segment is the actual inner-side chord of T. Equations
(9)--(10) are algebraic identities on its two real port points. They
do not split physical circle atoms, add a square, or claim a new
physical stress edge along that chord. The original load and both
actual applications are unchanged.

## 5. The exact positive radial path and its possible zero steps

Replace every genuine unloaded private body in the representative
by its actual straight transmission segment, keeping all reserves
and transverse means. Between consecutive loaded representatives,
properness (7), the actual channel lengths, and (8)--(10) give nodes
q_1,...,q_s, positive radial vectors K_j q_j with K_j>0, and directed
vectors f between them satisfying

    q_(j+1)-q_j=L_j f_j,       L_j>=0,
    det(q_j,f_j)=k,
    K_j q_j=f_(j-1)-f_j.                              (11)

A true channel contributes strictly positive length. An internal S
step in (10) is also strictly positive. The first and last directed
vectors are u and -v. In particular the original private force sum
telescopes exactly to

    M:=sum_i G_i=sum_j K_j q_j=u+v.                     (12)

An intervening zero step identifies two consecutive nonzero nodes;
combine their positive radial coefficients and cancel their common
intermediate vector in (11). This is only deletion of a zero segment
from the representative and an algebraic telescoping. Repeat finitely
often. Every remaining internal step now has L_j>0.

There are at least two remaining nodes. If a parallel S occurs, its
two distinct ports in (8) cannot be merged across their strictly
positive step. If all loaded private stars are nonparallel and at
least two are present, their positive circle-resultant directions
are distinct. Indeed the actual boundary-center directions differ
by more than pi/5 by `CIRCLE_CONTACT_ANGULAR_ORDER.md`, while every
nonzero circle resultant is within pi/12 of its own center direction
by the complete single-body calculation in
`FOUR_BOUNDARY_PATH_ENDPOINT_SEPARATION.md`, Section 1. Those open
intervals are disjoint. Thus no consecutive loaded-node step is zero.

The sole remaining possibility would be one nonparallel private load
Q, with all other private bodies unloaded. Then there is only one
node q. The force directions u,v are nonparallel because Q's two
actual star directions are nonparallel and unchanged along its two
channels. Private properness and the real channels give

    q=P_1+c_1 u=P_2+c_2 v,       c_1,c_2>=0.

At least one coefficient is strictly positive, since m>=2. If the
other is zero, q belongs to H and simultaneously lies strictly on
an outward normal ray from H, impossible. If both are positive,
P_1 and P_2 are both the unique nearest point in the convex H to q.
They therefore coincide, and two nonparallel rays from that point
cannot meet again at q. This excludes the one-node possibility.

By (6),(11), each remaining internal segment has a strictly positive
angular increment less than pi. Write eta>0 for the total angular
advance of the path q_1,...,q_s. Endpoint properness and every actual
end channel give

    q_1=P_1+c_1 u,       q_s=P_2+c_2 v,
    c_1,c_2>=0.                                         (13)

The endpoint segments P_1 to q_1 and q_s to P_2 also have nonnegative
angular advance, strictly positive when nonzero, because their
directed lines have moment k. The resulting private path represents
the unchanged original cycle under the O-free homotopies of Section 4
and the actual transmitter chords.

## 6. Private angular advance at least pi is impossible

Close the representative path through any actual point D of H. The
closed convex H avoids O, so all its points lie in one open angular
interval of length less than pi. Its closing path from P_2 through
D to P_1 has net angular advance strictly between -pi and pi.

If eta>=pi, the complete representative loop consequently has strictly
positive total argument. Its winding is the same as the actual simple
embedded cycle. That winding must then be one, contrary to Section 2.
Only the entire remaining domain

    0<eta<pi                                             (14)

requires further analysis.

## 7. Nonparallel root pair: negative concurrence or a real winding

Suppose u,v are nonparallel, and let q be the genuine intersection
of their actual root lines. Equation (5) gives q=tM for a real t,
where M=u+v is nonzero. The positive radial path in (14) lies in the
strict endpoint cone. Its sum (12) therefore has an expression

    M=A q_1+B q_s,       A,B>0.                          (15)

The endpoint vectors are independent. Write

    q_1=q+a u,       q_s=q+b v

with initially arbitrary real a,b. Comparing the two coefficients
in the independent u,v basis in (15) gives

    A a=B b=1-t(A+B).                                   (16)

Neither a nor b is zero: otherwise both vanish, identifying the two
independent endpoint vectors. They have the same sign. If both were
negative, (13) would put q strictly on both outward normal rays of
H. The same unique-nearest-point argument as in Section 5 excludes
that possibility. Thus a,b>0.

Set Delta=det(u,v). From (5) and the endpoint determinant,

    k=-t Delta>0,
    det(q_1,q_s)=[t(a+b)+ab]Delta>0.                    (17)

The second strict sign follows from (14). If t>=0, the first equation
either contradicts k>0 or makes Delta<0 while the bracket in the
second equation is positive. Hence

    q=-sigma M=-sigma u-sigma v,       sigma>0.         (18)

The auxiliary polygon q,q_1,...,q_s,q has positive angular increment
on every edge. Its internal path has increment eta<pi; each of its
two added root-line edges has increment less than pi. Its total is
a positive multiple of 2pi strictly below 3pi, so its winding is one.

The actual cycle representative is

    D,P_1,q_1,...,q_s,P_2,D.

As oriented chains avoiding O, its difference from the auxiliary
polygon is D,P_1,q,P_2,D. This correction is the sum of the triangles
D,P_1,P_2,D and P_1,q,P_2,P_1. The first lies in H and has winding
zero. The latter has no edge through O: its two root-line edges
have nonzero moment, and its third edge lies in H.

If O is outside that latter triangle, including every degenerate
triangle, the correction has winding zero. The actual cycle then
has winding one, contrary to Section 2.

If O is strictly inside it, write the original ports as

    P_1=q+b_1 u,       P_2=q+b_2 v.

In the u,v basis with origin q, the circle origin has coordinates
(sigma,sigma), both positive, while the triangle vertices are
(0,0),(b_1,0),(0,b_2). Strict interiority therefore forces b_1,b_2>0.
Both actual root ports are proper in this alternative.

Choose an own coordinate of H whose closed interval misses zero,
and reflect it so H lies in x>=h>0. Both ports have positive x;
strict triangle interiority forces q_x<0. Equation (18) gives M_x>0,
so H avoids the entire nonpositive M ray. All hypotheses of
`ROOTED_NONPOSITIVE_RAY_PROPER_PORT_CAP_EXCLUSION.md` now hold for
the original two root neighbors, actual forces u,v, unchanged ports,
negative concurrence (18), and proper coefficients b_1,b_2.
That purely geometric theorem places the two real neighbor centers
at distance less than one, a contradiction. It imposes no degree
or circle-load premise on either neighbor and no equation on H's
other forces. Thus every nonparallel root case is excluded.

## 8. Both parallel root cases, with the real side means

First suppose u=a e and v=-b e, a,b>0. By (5), both actual root
ports have strictly negative Je coordinate:

    (P_1).Je=-k/a,       (P_2).Je=-k/b.

The endpoint representatives (13) preserve these coordinates. Under
(14), all radial nodes lie in their positive endpoint cone, hence
also have strictly negative Je coordinate. Their positive radial
sum (12) cannot equal the axial vector u+v. This excludes the whole
opposite-direction case.

Now suppose u=a e and v=b e, a,b>0. Equation (5) gives strictly
opposite root port heights, with P_1 below and P_2 above the e axis.
They expose the same supporting face of H and are distinct, so e
is an actual side axis and

    H=[h-1,h]e+[b_0,b_0+1]Je,       b_0<0<b_0+1.

Since the closed H avoids O, either h>1 or h<0. If h>1, both actual
root neighbors beyond that support have center e-projection greater
than 3/2 and norm less than 91/60. Their common cap rectangle has
squared diameter

    (91/60-3/2)^2+4((91/60)^2-9/4)=29/144<1,

contradicting their disjoint open radius-1/2 incircles.

If h<0, P_1 is strictly in the lower-left quadrant and P_2 in the
upper-left quadrant of the e,Je frame. Every nonzero segment of
the private representative has positive angular advance. From
P_1 to P_2 its total positive advance therefore exceeds pi. The
closing path inside H may be taken through D=(h-1/2)e. It remains
in the negative-x half-plane and has the small strictly positive
angular advance from P_2 to P_1 through the negative axis. The
complete representative consequently has positive winding, and
so does the original physical cycle. Section 2 again contradicts it.

This argument retains arbitrary endpoints and weighted means on the
common root side; it never substitutes their midpoint. It completes
every parallel alternative and the proof of the theorem.

## 9. Exact terminal-five-cycle application and wider permitted scope

In the original C3.335.b0 chain with an intact terminal triangle at A,
middle triangle A-H-C, and a terminal simple five-cycle at H, the
accepted intact-end theorem gives O in int A. Every private vertex
of that five-cycle retains its true positive degree two. Deleting H
leaves the component containing A outside the five-cycle. Thus all
hypotheses of Sections 1--8 hold, with E=A. Every original circle
mask at H and at its four private cycle vertices is retained. This
excludes the entire terminal-C5 chain, without any load-orbit split.

The same theorem applies to any other original terminal simple-cycle
block when its unchanged outside component contains an actual
origin-interior unit. Its proof does not depend on five cycle vertices.
The outside loaded vertex is supplied by CY.LOAD.1, not assumed by
shortening a branch or changing any true degree.

A bouquet whose origin-interior root H itself belongs to the cycle
does not satisfy (3), and is not excluded here. A terminal house or
fan has private higher-degree vertices and is not assigned the
degree-two concurrence equations of this source. Those original
five-block families remain separate obligations. No complete C3.335
coarse-cell claim or gate update is made by this source.
