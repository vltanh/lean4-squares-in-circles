# Two genuine short arms cannot share a root of any cyclic block

Date: October 29, 2026 UTC. Author: pentagon_global continuation.

**Status: complete proposed hand whole-attachment theorem, frozen for
full root and separate independent review.** The argument has no
restriction on the cyclic block's size, chord set, private degrees or
private circle loads. Its new step uses one actual bridge path and a
force-free empty ray to put the entire root-deleted block on one side
of a disk crosscut. That one side cannot supply the root's full
required two-coordinate resultant.

## 1. Exact original scope

Retain all nine actual physical unit squares, one common compatible
selected positive certificate and physical embedding, and the window

    79/40<=R<=sqrt(1105)/16,
    d=sqrt(R^2-1/4)-1,
    epsilon=max(d-1,0)<1/60,
    r=R-sqrt(2), 1/2<r<2/3.

Let Q be an actual cyclic block and H a vertex of Q. The only positive
edges from H outside Q are the two genuine original short arms

    H--X--L,       H--Y--M.

The four outside bodies are distinct and lie outside Q. X and Y have
their true positive degree two, and L and M are genuine positive
leaves. All other positive edges at H belong to Q. Retain the true
degrees and all other forces at every Q body. In particular H has
its actual degree `deg_Q(H)+2`, not a two- or three-force equation.

Every original application mean, selected normal, physical circle
corner and individual zero circle coefficient remains. The block
may contain further positive contacts or loaded bodies of any actual
degree. Additional physical contacts outside the positive graph
remain occupied. All original completion units remain present.

**Theorem.** This entire configuration is impossible.

The proof uses only that `Q-H` is connected and contains all the
remaining positive neighbors of H. Biconnectivity supplies this
property for every actual cyclic block. Thus the conclusion applies
simultaneously to every original same-root two-short-arm attachment
of the five-body-block/four-bridge profile C1.5.b4, as well as the
corresponding attachments at smaller or larger block sizes whenever
the full displayed hypotheses hold.

## 2. Physical bounds and the complete actual arm cover

A contained unit with center c and orthonormal own axes u,v has a
farthest corner of squared norm

    |c|^2+1/2+|c.u|+|c.v|>=|c|^2+|c|+1/2.

Consequently every actual center satisfies

    |c|<=sqrt(R^2-1/4)-1/2<91/60.                    (1)

The strict final reserve is
`(121/60)^2-1041/256=31/57600>0`.
If a unit is physically circle-touching, each of its actual points
P satisfies `|P|>=r`, by its diameter sqrt(2).

The real roots X and Y of the genuine leaves are physically clear.
Their full true degree-two equations make them actual unloaded
normal transmitters. For either arm, let e be its actual radial
leaf direction. The unchanged applications and H support are

    p_XL=r_L e, p_HX=(r_L-w)e,
    r_L=r for D, r_L=d for S, w in {1,sqrt(2)},
    h_H(e)=r_L-w.                                    (2)

These are the genuine radial-leaf and true-transmitter conclusions,
with the entire actual side means retained. The complete arm-only
proof is Sections 1--2 of e023 below; its argument is reproduced
here to make clear which parts are independent of the private block.

A D arm root contains O in its open interior. For its side case,
the longitudinal interval `[r-1,r]` straddles zero, and a transverse
endpoint at zero would be a forbidden own-axis normal at a
point-only vertex/vertex contact. For its diagonal case the two own
coordinates of O are `r/sqrt(2)` in (0,1). An S/diagonal arm root
likewise contains O openly, since `d/sqrt(2)` belongs to (0,1).

Suppose one arm has such an origin-interior root. A second D or
S/diagonal root would overlap it. An S/side root at d<=1 contains
O closed and would also overlap it. For an S/side arm at d>1, its
actual H application is `epsilon e`. At any actual supporting point
P of a unit square with outward unit normal n, the entire segment
`P-[0,1]n` belongs to that square: in own coordinates each nonzero
component points from its supporting endpoint into the unit interval
and has absolute value at most one. Hence

    [epsilon-1,epsilon]e is a subset of H.

Thus H contains O closed and again overlaps the distinct
origin-interior root. Both arms must therefore be S/side.
Their real leaf directions are distinct, since equal directions
would give the same actual radial S leaf.

If d<1, each true side root X,Y contains O closed. Neither may
contain it openly, so each has O in a relative side interior; the
longitudinal interval straddles zero strictly. Their occupied local
half-planes must be opposite, making their radial axes parallel.
Equal leaf directions are impossible, so they are opposite. But
(2) would give

    h_H(e)+h_H(-e)=2(d-1)<0,

contradicting the positive width of H. This excludes the entire
strict-low radius domain, for every actual Q.

## 3. The full critical and high root charts

### 3.1. Critical radius

Let d=1. Both real arm applications on H are O. Since their normals
are distinct, O cannot be in a relative side interior of H, whose
outward unit normal there would be unique. It is an H vertex.
Rotate and reflect the complete own frame so that

    H=[0,1]^2.                                      (3)

Both outward arm normals belong to the closed southwest normal cone
at O and are distinct. Their positive weighted sum W has both
coordinates strictly negative. A zero coordinate would force both
unit normals to be the same cone boundary ray.

H is physically clear because it contains O and r>0. Its full
original force equation, with both arm magnitudes retained, is

    N=-W=(alpha,beta), alpha,beta>0,                  (4)

where N is the sum of every original H-to-Q pair force.
The critical argument keeps the complete possible arm-normal cone;
it does not assume that either normal is an H own axis.

### 3.2. Strict high radius

Let d>1, so epsilon=d-1. The actual H arm points are `epsilon e_i`.
If one e_i strictly exposes a vertex, write its own coordinates
as `(a,b)` with a,b>0. The exposed H vertex is
`(epsilon a,epsilon b)`. Its adjacent outward own-side heights are
strictly between zero and epsilon, and its other side heights are
greater than `1-epsilon>epsilon`. Thus no own-axis normal can
supply a second radial support of height epsilon. A second strict
vertex cannot have norm epsilon, since distinct H vertices are at
least one apart whereas the proposed points are at most 2epsilon
apart. The same vertex would give the same leaf direction.

Both e_i are consequently H own side axes. They cannot be opposite,
which would give width `2epsilon<1`, and cannot be equal. They are
adjacent. Rotate and reflect the whole actual chart to obtain

    H=[-epsilon,A]^2, A=1-epsilon>59/60,
    e_X=(-1,0), e_Y=(0,-1),
    p_HX=(-epsilon,0), p_HY=(0,-epsilon).             (5)

H is physically clear because its actual arm points have norm
epsilon<r. Its full unchanged force equation is again (4), where
alpha,beta are now precisely the actual two positive arm magnitudes.

In both (3) and (5), H contains the actual smaller square
`[0,59/60]^2`. The root-deleted block must supply a full resultant
with both coordinates positive. No application point, physical
leaf, or contact normal is assigned to this aggregate.

## 4. A force-free empty ray from the far root corner

We reproduce the complete geometric inequality needed here from
Section 7 of e023. This avoids using any of that source's C4
private-load or concurrence arguments.

**Geometric lemma.** Put

    A0=59/60, t0=189/200, M0=91/60.

No unit square T with `|c_T|<M0`, disjoint open interior from
`[0,A0]^2`, can have both `max_T x>t0` and `max_T y>t0`.

Let w in `[1,sqrt(2)]` be its common horizontal and vertical width.
The two maxima would give

    c_x,c_y>t0-w/2>7/30,                             (6)

using `sqrt(2)<17/12` and `t0-17/24=71/300>7/30`.
The separating-axis theorem supplies an own axis of either square.

For a coordinate separator the negative side is impossible because
the corresponding maximum is positive. On the right side,

    c_x>=A0+w/2, c_y>t0-w/2.

The squared lower sum increases for w>=1 since A0>t0, yielding

    |c_T|^2>(89/60)^2+(89/200)^2>M0^2,

with exact reserve `3921/40000`. The upper side is symmetric.

For an own-axis separator of T, choose its unit normal n toward T.
Its minimum own-axis support gives

    c_T.n>=A0(n_x^++n_y^+)+1/2.                     (7)

Two nonpositive components contradict (6). For a mixed axis
`n=(a,-b)`, a>0,b>=0, equation (7) gives

    c_x>=A0+1/(2a)+(b/a)c_y
        >=89/60+(b/a)(7/30).

Since c_x<M0, one has `b/a<1/7` and hence `w=a+b<8/7`.
Therefore `c_y>t0-4/7=523/1400`, and

    (89/60)^2+(523/1400)^2-M0^2=77529/1960000>0.

Exchange coordinates for the other mixed case.

Finally suppose `n=(a,b)`, a>=b>=0, exchanging coordinates if
needed. Equations (1),(7) give

    w=a+b<61/59,
    ab=(w^2-1)/2<120/3481.

Because `a>=1/sqrt(2)>7/10`, this implies
`b<1200/24367<1/20` and `a>99/100`. Thus

    c_y>t0-61/118=5051/11800>107/250,

where the last reserve is `3/59000`. Using c_y<M0 in (7),

    c_x>=A0+1/(2a)+(b/a)(A0-c_y)
        >89/60-(5/99)(8/15)=8651/5940>131/90.

Now

    (131/90)^2+(107/250)^2-M0^2
      =31351/20250000>0.

All separating axes, including tangencies and own-axis endpoints,
are excluded. This proves the geometric lemma.

Apply it to every actual unit other than H. Each satisfies (1)
and is disjoint from the smaller square contained in H. Since
`A>59/60>189/200` in the high case and A=1 in the critical case,
no such unit can contain any point of the closed diagonal segment

    E={ (s,s): A<=s<=R/sqrt(2) }.                    (8)

Its first endpoint is the actual far corner F=(A,A) of H; its last
endpoint lies on the containing circle. The segment exists since
`sqrt(2)A<=sqrt(2)<R`. Its open part is outside H. The whole E is
disjoint from every other actual closed unit, with positive compact
clearance because there are finitely many units. In particular no
other unit physically touches F, even at a zero-stress contact.

Thus (8) is a genuine empty path from H to the containing circle.
It is used only for planar separation, never as a positive edge,
force, motion, or supporting application.

## 5. The actual arm and empty ray give a separating crosscut

Choose the actual H--X--L path, and a physical circle corner of L.
Its original positive edges and its physical boundary access are
already part of the common compatible physical embedding. All
circle-corner force coefficients may remain as they are; the selected
physical corner need not carry a positive individual coefficient.

Extend the embedding at H by a purely topological marker from the
H body vertex through the interior of H to F, then along (8) to
the containing circle. The absence of any physical contact at F,
and the positive clearance of (8) from all other closed units,
permit this marker to be drawn disjoint from every other body and
edge except at its common H body vertex. Inside the convex H use
the cyclic order of the actual boundary ports, inserting F between
the top and right faces. This is an ordinary drawing extension;
the selected certificate and every contact normal remain unchanged.

The concatenation of the original boundary-access path

    circle -- L -- X -- H

with this marker is a simple disk crosscut. Its two circle endpoints
are distinct, since the one on (8) belongs to no actual L point.
The standard physical embedding can be chosen with arbitrarily
small compatible routing neighborhoods, as in bc98 below, so this
extension preserves every original positive path and boundary access.

The embedded graph `Q-H` is connected. It is disjoint from X,L,H
and from the marker away from H. It therefore lies in one of the
two open components of the disk cut by this crosscut. Every actual
H-to-Q edge, with H removed from that edge, lies on this same side:
it cannot cross the crosscut and ends in `Q-H`.

We translate that topological side into the actual root support
features. At the critical radius the arm port is O, the southwest
vertex of (3). The two boundary arcs from O to F are

* the left face, northwest vertex and top face;
* the bottom face, southeast vertex and right face.

Every outward support normal on the first arc has x component
nonpositive; every outward support normal on the second has y
component nonpositive. At the common near endpoint O the entire
closed outward cone has both components nonpositive. The far
endpoint F has no physical neighbor by Section 4.

At high radius the selected real arm port is `P_X=(-epsilon,0)`
on the left face in (5). One boundary arc from P_X to F runs along
the upper part of the left face, the northwest vertex, and the top
face. Every normal there has x<=0. The other arc runs along the
lower part of the left face, the southwest vertex, bottom face,
southeast vertex and right face. Every normal there has y<=0.
At P_X the sole outward unit normal is West, which satisfies both
weak inequalities. Again F has no physical neighbor.

These are complete actual support-feature covers, including own-axis
endpoints and every original shared-side mean. They are not a choice
of geometric routing points in place of those means. If another
original application coincides with the near cut port, its normal
satisfies both coordinate bounds just established. If a contact
segment meets that port, the same weak bound holds for its entire
actual mean interval. Such a coincidence therefore causes no loss
when assigning root contacts to the two sides.

For precision, the cyclic order used above is the physical order of
the convex H boundary. The compatible embedding at a shared contact
respects the original incident sectors. A port away from the two
cut ends can be approached from the exterior of H only on the side
corresponding to its boundary arc. Perturbations at coincident ports
can only change the assignment at the near endpoint, where both
bounds hold. The empty far endpoint is separated from all other
units. Thus the entire original H-to-Q star on the chosen side has
either every x component nonpositive or every y component
nonpositive. Additional zero-stress physical contacts do not open a
crossing through an occupied arm or through the empty marker.

## 6. The full root resultant is impossible

Every H-to-Q magnitude is positive. Section 5 proves that either

    N_x=sum_{K in N_Q(H)} mu_HK (n_HK)_x<=0,

or

    N_y=sum_{K in N_Q(H)} mu_HK (n_HK)_y<=0.

Both alternatives contradict the full original equation (4), which
has `N_x=alpha>0` and `N_y=beta>0`.

This is the complete contradiction. It needs no equation at any
private Q vertex, no private loaded-set classification, no root
concurrence, and no deletion of a chord or higher-degree force.
Every private force is retained in the actual connected block;
every root force is retained in N or in the two real arm terms.
