## 1. The complete wall domain and a necessary westward endpoint

\[
 \begin{gathered}

 P_A=(-s,y),\quad P_C=(-s,-w),\qquad
 \frac14<s<\frac12,\quad y,w>0,\quad y+w\le1.
 \end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
These are the actual applications on H's original side, not chosen
midpoints. Both A and C have exactly one physical circle corner;
B may have one or two. The exact radius/support coupling, the
intact HXY petal and all completion units remain.

Let \(g_A,g_C\) be the actual unit normals pointing from A,C
toward B, with positive magnitudes \(\gamma_A,\gamma_C\). Write
\(F_B\) for B's actual outward radial circle resultant, with its
original nonnegative individual corner weights. The genuine B

\[
                       F_B=\gamma_Ag_A+\gamma_Cg_C.
\tag{4}
\]

## 2. Every westward endpoint has a B-owned singleton contact

Use the complete tilted upper-endpoint frame
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_A=(-s,y),\qquad
 u_A=(-a_A,b_A),\quad v_A=(-b_A,-a_A),\\
 0<a_A\le b_A,\qquad a_A^2+b_A^2=1,\\
 A=P_A+[0,1]u_A+[0,1]v_A,\qquad
 z_A=P_A+u_A+v_A.
 \end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
The order \(a_A\le b_A\) follows from genuine proper concurrence
in the cited wall-domain theorem. The actual HA point is its
rightmost vertex \(P_A\), so that pair intersection is a singleton.

Write a westward outgoing normal as
\[
                       g_A=(-c,-d),\qquad
                 c,d>0,\quad c^2+d^2=1.
\tag{7}
\]
Then
\[
                    g_A\cdot v_A=cb_A+da_A>0,\qquad
                    g_A\cdot u_A=ca_A-db_A.
\tag{8}
\]
If the second expression is positive, the exposed A vertex is its
circle corner \(z_A\), which cannot be shared with another contained
square. If it is zero, the exposed feature is the entire outer
lower face \(P_A+v_A+[0,1]u_A\); the normal is \(v_A\).
TRIANGLE_C4_TILTED_WALL_OUTER_FACE_EXCLUSION.md excludes this
whole face alternative, with every actual mean and both endpoints.

Consequently
\[
                ca_A-db_A<0,\qquad
                T_A=P_A+v_A=(-s-b_A,y-a_A)
\tag{9}
\]
is the actual AB application. It is a singleton and differs from
\(P_A\). Both applications of the unique-circle-corner degree-two
body A are therefore fixed physical points, and B is a physical
boundary neighbor. BOUNDARY_TIP_FIXED_APPLICATION_SELECTION.md
removes the possibility of a free strict vertex/vertex AB normal
in the selected certificate. This uses the finite selection made
before the force weights; no normal is changed afterward.

The normal in (7) is not an A side-axis normal: both projections
in (8) are nonzero. Hence it is an actual side-axis normal of B.
This follows from the strict physical normal convention: a
point-only vertex/vertex intersection retains a strict free normal,
whereas an axis-owned nonfree normal comes from an actual side
support. Thus **B owns every westward endpoint contact**.

The weak inequality from (9) gives \(a_A\le d\). For example,
squaring \(ca_A\le db_A\) and using the two unit identities gives
\(a_A^2\le d^2\). Define
\[
                              f_A=cb_A+da_A.
\tag{10}
\]
The entire owner cone has
\[
 f_A-c=c(b_A-1)+da_A
      =a_A\left(d-\frac{ca_A}{1+b_A}\right)\ge0.
\tag{11}
\]
The same statement holds at a reflected lower westward endpoint.
These inequalities retain all weak cone limits, although the
outer-face equality itself has already been excluded.

This reduction is made only at westward endpoints. In particular,
it makes no fixed-application assertion at an endpoint whose
outgoing first coordinate is nonnegative. Its coincident
radial-D application or genuine side mean remains available below.

## 3. Both outgoing normals westward

Suppose \((g_A)_x,(g_C)_x<0\). Both normals are axes of the same
actual square B. By (5), they are distinct and cannot be opposite.
They are therefore perpendicular. With the notation (7),
\[
                              g_C=(-d,c).
\tag{12}
\]
The lower tilted endpoint has
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u_C=(-a_C,-b_C),\quad v_C=(-b_C,a_C),\\
 0<a_C\le b_C,\quad a_C^2+b_C^2=1,\qquad
 T_C=(-s-b_C,-w+a_C).
 \end{gathered}
\tag{13}
\]
Its owner cone gives \(a_C\le c\). Set
\[
                           f_C=db_C+ca_C\ge d.
\tag{14}
\]
The original minimum B supports in the orthogonal coordinates
\((g_A,g_C)\) are
\[
 \begin{split}
 \alpha&=g_A\cdot T_A=c(s+b_A)-d(y-a_A),\\
 \beta&=g_C\cdot T_C=d(s+b_C)-c(w-a_C).
 \end{split}
\tag{15}
\]
Thus the opposite physical B corner is
\((\alpha+1)g_A+(\beta+1)g_C\). Its first coordinate is exactly
\[
                -s-(c+d)-cf_A-df_C+cd(y+w).
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (3),(11),(14) bound (16) above by
\[
                            -s-1-(c+d)+cd.
\tag{17}
\]
Put \(p=c+d\in(1,\sqrt2]\). Since \(cd=(p^2-1)/2\),
the function \(p-cd=p-(p^2-1)/2\) decreases on that interval, and
\[
                            c+d-cd\ge\sqrt2-\frac12.
\]
Therefore the actual B corner has first coordinate at most
\[
                     -s-\frac12-\sqrt2
                         <-\frac34-\sqrt2<-R.
\tag{18}
\]
The last inequality follows already from
\(\sqrt2>7/5\) and \(R<21/10\).
This contradicts physical containment.

The argument uses an actual corner of B. It does not prescribe
B's physical circle mask, its individual circle weights, its
contact means, or the distinctness of its two applications.

## 4. Exactly one outgoing normal westward

Reflect if necessary so that A is westward and
\[
                           g_C=(\xi,\eta),\qquad
                             \xi\ge0,\quad\eta>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Keep the complete actual BC application \(T_C\). We first prove
the uniform coordinate bounds
\[
                          (T_C)_x\ge-s-1,\qquad
                          (T_C)_y\ge-w.
\tag{20}
\]

If C is tilted, its inward axes at \(P_C=(-s,-w)\) are those of
(13). Since
\[
                             g_C\cdot u_C<0,
\]
every exposed point lies on the segment
\(P_C+[0,1]v_C\), or at one of its endpoints. More explicitly,
the sign of \(g_C\cdot v_C\) selects the upper endpoint, the
whole segment, or \(P_C\) itself. Every one of these actual
applications satisfies (20). This includes \(T_C=P_C\);
no coincident radial-D case is discarded.

If C is aligned, the full wall-domain theorem gives
\[
             C=[-s-1,-s]\times[-Y,-Y+1],
                 \qquad Y=\sqrt{R^2-(s+1)^2}.
\tag{21}
\]
The actual root mean \(-w\) lies in the vertical interval, hence
\(1-Y\ge-w\). The signs in (19) place \(T_C\) on the upper side
(or its upper-right vertex), proving (20) for every genuine
mean in the aligned case as well.

By Section 2, \(g_A\) is an axis of B, whose minimum \(g_A\)
support is \(g_A\cdot T_A\). At BC, the outward normal from B
is \(-g_C\), and
\[
                            (-g_C)\cdot g_A
                              =c\xi+d\eta>0.
\]
The support feature of B for \(-g_C\) is therefore entirely
on its maximum \(g_A\) face, regardless of whether the other
coordinate is fixed or a shared-side mean. Its width is one:
\[
                           g_A\cdot T_C=g_A\cdot T_A+1.
\tag{22}
\]
From (20) and \(c,d>0\),
\[
                 g_A\cdot T_C\le c(s+1)+dw.
\]
Using the actual \(T_A\) from (9), equation (22) yields
\[
                   f_A+1-c\le d(y+w)\le d<1.
\tag{23}
\]
But (11) makes the left side at least one. This is the required
contradiction.

Only AB was assigned to an actual B axis. BC can still have a
free strict vertex normal, an owned normal or a genuine shared
mean. No concurrence or distinct-application hypothesis at B
was used. A physical two-corner B with one zero individual
weight remains covered.
