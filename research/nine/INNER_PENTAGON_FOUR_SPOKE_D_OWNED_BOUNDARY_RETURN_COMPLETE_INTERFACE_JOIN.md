## 2. Full D3 and the actual H--C contact force the exact nonparallel chart

Normalize the physical D frame so

touching returns of an owned-axis triangle. A West normal would make
the other return East, putting their entire actual units beyond
opposite D supports and preventing their contact. An East normal
makes the other return horizontal; West is excluded by the same
support separation, leaving East. Thus this entire case is parallel.

For clarity, the triangle fact is degree-free: if the first neighbor
lies beyond a North D side and the other normal has negative vertical
component, the latter whole unit has maximum y strictly less than one,
while the first has minimum y at least one. The strict bound follows
from the actual unit support span, including any external side mean.
No neighbor equation is used.

components must have opposite signs, since DE is West. Opposite
strict D vertices cannot give touching returned units: at NE and SW
vertices the full unit-support bounds put any putative common point
in int(D), and the other opposite pair is a reflection. Therefore
the two vertices lie on the same vertical D side. They cannot both
be on the left, because every horizontal force component would be
negative. Reflect vertically if necessary so

    P_H=(1,0), F_H=(f,-t),
    P_C=(1,1), F_Cpair=(s,t), f,s,t>0.                 (1)

Both strict mean endpoints are therefore genuinely impossible in the
nonparallel branch. This is a consequence of full equilibrium, not
