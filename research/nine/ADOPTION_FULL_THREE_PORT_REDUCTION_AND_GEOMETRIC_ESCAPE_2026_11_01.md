### 3.1. One radius-free theorem excludes the last graph

Let L be any entirely unloaded region with its FULL positive cut
partitioned into two nonempty groups. Every outside terminal in the
first group contains an actual point p; every terminal in the second
contains an actual point q. The groups may have arbitrary multiplicities.
Let F be the second group's full vector resultant, C the sum of ALL
cut magnitudes, and I the complete internal support work. Summing the
unchanged translations and using the actual terminal support planes gives

    I + C/2 <= F dot (q-p),       C >= 2|F|.

The case F=0 is impossible because C>0. If the two groups have disjoint
inside host sets, summing over all first-group hosts gives an internal
cut of magnitude at least |F|. Each original internal pair contributes
at least its magnitude to I. Thus I>=|F| and

    2|F| <= I+C/2 <= |F| |q-p|,
    |q-p| >= 2.

More generally, if the two inside host sets have original internal
graph distance ell, complete summation across ell edge-disjoint distance
layers gives `|q-p|>=ell+1`. No radius, planarity, normal selection,
terminal load or body-count assumption is used in this local theorem.

Apply it to the exact graph G with `L={X,Y,T}`. The FULL cut is
`AX,BX,AT,CY`; the A/B hosts are `{X,T}` and the C host is `{Y}`.
Take the original contact applications `p=p_AB` and `q=q_BC`.
Both points belong to the SAME actual square B, so `|q-p|<=sqrt(2)`.
The disjoint-host lower bound gives `|q-p|>=2`, a contradiction.
The original weak BC edge is retained and supplies the actual contact.

This excludes the whole last graph at arbitrary outside degrees, loads,
leaf types and terminal identifications. Its join to the complete census
