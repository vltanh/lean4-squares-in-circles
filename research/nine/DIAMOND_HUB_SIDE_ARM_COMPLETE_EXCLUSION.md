## 2. Every tilted H is excluded by the endpoint signs

Suppose e is not an own side axis of H. Choose its two inward
own directions at the arm vertex so that

    e=(-p,-q),       p,q>0,       p^2+q^2=1,
    H=[-epsilon p,A] times [-epsilon q,B_0],
    A=1-epsilon p,       B_0=1-epsilon q.              (10)

If all three root forces have the same direction, (2) makes it
-e. That strictly exposes the single far H vertex (A,B_0).
Both outer ports are therefore the same point with the same
normal, contradicting their strictly opposite moment signs (7).

We are left with (6). Write theta=angle(-e), so 0<theta<pi/2.
The outer normal angles have lifts

    theta-pi<angle(n_B)<theta<angle(n_D)<theta+pi.      (11)

The first normal must have positive x-component. Otherwise (11)
puts it strictly southwest, where its unique port is the small
arm vertex, or exactly south. In the south case (7) forces its
port x<0 on y=-epsilon q, again of norm at most epsilon.
Both alternatives are impossible for boundary B. Similarly the
last normal has positive y-component: a remaining strictly
southwest port is the same small point, and an exactly west
normal has negative moment only at a port y<0, again small.

Thus write n_B=(c,s), c>0, and n_D=(v,t), t>0. There are only
the following possibilities. We use the geometric cap theorem
FAR_FACE_RADIAL_PORT_TWO_NEIGHBOR_CAP.md, Sections 1--2: two

with n_x>0, or the rotated counterpart with n_y>0. That theorem
needs no shared concurrence or force equation at either neighbor.

    g_B s=B_0+c(A s-B_0 c)>B_0,

neighbors violate the top-face cap.

If v>0, the symmetric argument has s<=0 and both actual

g_B c=A+w(epsilon q c-A w)>A; the axis case gives g_B=A.
For the last northeast vertex,
g_D v=A+t(B_0 v-A t)>A. The right-face cap excludes them.

The entire remaining feature domain is therefore

    n_B=(c,-s), c>0,s>=0,
    n_D=(-v,t), t>0,v>=0.                             (13)
