## 1. A force-free corner-bypass theorem

Let four actual closed unit squares A,X,H,B have pairwise disjoint
open interiors. Suppose three of them have the exact common-frame
positions

    X=[h,h+1] x [Y-1,Y],
    A=[h,h+1] x [Y,Y+1],
    H=[h-1,h] x [g-1,g],
    g-1<Y<=g.                                      (1)

Let C be any actual set satisfying

    every z in C has z_x<=h-1 and z_y<Y.             (2)

Assume B physically touches both A and C, with actual points or
support means V in A intersection B and P in B intersection C.
Retain any given common supporting normal G from A to B, so V
maximizes G on A and minimizes G on B. Then these hypotheses are
impossible.

The points V,P belong to the same actual unit B. Therefore

    |V-P|<=sqrt(2).                                 (3)

### 1.1. Two entire supporting faces are excluded by the actual diameter

If G_x>0, its actual support has V_x=h+1. Condition (2) gives
V_x-P_x>=2, contradicting (3). If G_y>0, its actual support
has V_y=Y+1 and V_x>=h. Then

    V_x-P_x>=1, V_y-P_y>1,
    |V-P|^2>2,

again impossible. Thus G_x<=0 and G_y<=0.

### 1.2. Every downward supporting feature is excluded, including its means

Suppose G_y<0. If G_x<0, the original support is V=(h,Y).
If G_x=0, retain its complete actual bottom-side mean
V=(xi,Y), h<=xi<=h+1. For h<xi<h+1, the union of the actual
A and X contains a full neighborhood of V, so a third unit
cannot contain it while remaining interior-disjoint from both.
For xi=h+1, its distance to P is already at least two. Thus
only V=(h,Y) could remain in either case.

If g>Y, this point is in the relative interior of H's right
side: g-1<Y<g. The local inward sectors of H, X and A cover
the whole plane, leaving no nonempty inward sector for B.
If g=Y, their local sectors are respectively the lower-left,
lower-right and upper-right quadrants. B's inward sector must
therefore fit in the remaining upper-left quadrant. A unit square
has an inward sector of angle at least a right angle at every
boundary point. It follows that V is a B vertex and its inward
axes are exactly West and North. But a minimum-G supporting
point requires nonnegative dot product of G with both actual
inward axes, whereas G.North=G_y<0. This is impossible.

Thus no original downward feature or bottom-side mean survives.
The complete original normal has consequently become

    G_x<0, G_y=0,
    max_B x=h,
    V=(h,y_V), Y<=y_V<=Y+1.                        (4)

No normal was reselected, and no original mean was moved to a
convenient endpoint.

### 1.3. The full B lies above the actual H, in every B frame

We prove explicitly that (1),(4) and interior disjointness force

    min_B y>=g.                                    (5)

First suppose B is parallel to the common frame. Its whole actual
horizontal interval is [h-1,h]. Write its vertical interval as
[z,z+1]. Since H has the same horizontal interval, disjointness of
their open interiors forces either z>=g or z+1<=g-1. The second
alternative is impossible because the unchanged actual V belongs
to B and has y_V>=Y>g-1. Therefore z>=g, proving (5). This
argument retains every original mean on a physical shared AB side.

If B is not parallel to the common frame, its rightmost support is
a unique vertex, necessarily the unchanged V. Its two actual inward
axes there may be written

    (-c,s), (-s,-c),
    c,s>0, c^2+s^2=1.

Thus the actual lowest B corner is

    Z=(h-s,y_V-c),
    h-1<Z_x<h.                                     (6)

Indeed the other three heights are y_V, y_V+s and y_V+s-c,
all strictly greater than y_V-c. If y_V<g, the actual V has
g-1<y_V<g, and interior B points sufficiently near V lie inside
H, a contradiction. Hence y_V>=g. If Z_y<g, the segment from
V to the actual corner Z crosses the top of H and then enters
its interior: along that segment the x coordinate is strictly
between h-1 and h, and just after the crossing its height is
strictly between g-1 and g. This also contradicts disjointness.
It follows that Z_y>=g, proving (5) in every nonparallel frame.

Finally (5), Y<=g and (2) give

    min_B y>=g>=Y>every height in C.

This contradicts the actual BC contact. The force-free theorem is
proved for all B orientations and every original contact mean.
