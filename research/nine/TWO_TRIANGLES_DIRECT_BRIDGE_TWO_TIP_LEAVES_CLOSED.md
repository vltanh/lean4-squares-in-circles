## 1. A general bridge consequence of the complete triangle ray theorem

Suppose a positive bridge J--K has each endpoint in exactly one
actual positive triangle, with no other positive edge there, and
both endpoints are physically circle-clear. Every full external
triangle origin moment is assumed zero, as given by complete
private branch sums in the block tree.

Let n be the actual J-to-K unit normal and beta>0 its original
magnitude. Summing the actual private branch origin torques gives
the unchanged common application

    p=rho n,       rho real.

The real separating supports are

    J subset {x:n.x<=rho},       K subset {x:n.x>=rho}.
                                                               (1)

Their full triangle externals are beta n and -beta n respectively.
TRIANGLE_EXTERNAL_RAY_INTERSECTS_BODY.md therefore gives

    a n in J,       -b n in K,       a,b>=0.

Equation (1) forces

    a<=rho<=-b,

and hence rho=a=b=0. In particular

    O belongs to both closed J and closed K.           (2)

Thus this entire situation is impossible if either endpoint avoids
O. It is also impossible if either actual incident triangle has
nonzero common cycle moment: the strictly positive-parameter
theorem TRIANGLE_NONZERO_MOMENT_EXTERNAL_RAY.md would make the
corresponding a or b strictly positive, contrary to the same
support inequalities.

The zero-moment/origin-boundary alternative is deliberately retained
in (2). A possible own circle load or an additional positive edge
would change the full external force, so the lemma is not applied
until those hypotheses have been proved for the real endpoints.
