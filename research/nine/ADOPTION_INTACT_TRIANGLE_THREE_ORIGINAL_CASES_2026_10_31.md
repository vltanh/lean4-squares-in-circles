## The proof that closes all three cases together

An intact positive triangle HBC has actual positive contacts HB, BC, CH
and FULL original positive degrees two at B and C. The root H may have
any degree. If a tip were unloaded, its two forces would be opposite,
placing the other two squares beyond opposite support lines with a
positive gap; they could not touch. Thus both tips are loaded.

The same-nine analytic lower-window theorem gives R>2. The original
intact-root reduction and one common initial certificate then give the
complete root/private-block datum. The parallel, free, and owned root
normal alternatives exhaust this SAME datum. The preserved complete
theorems exclude them as follows:

1. Parallel: the actual two-tip obstacle excludes every additional
   boundary neighbor at H, contradicting the two required loaded private
   root neighbors.
2. A free normal: the physical-two tip alternative is excluded in its
   full mask; the unique-tip alternative has a common positive-pressure
   circle arc. The complete private moment DAG and protected actual tip
   access propagate that arc to all private circle corners, contradicting
   the full component force sum.
3. Both normals owned: the complete owner split gives the strict high
   support needed by the high-owned theorem. Its actual finite-edge
   geometry puts every additional boundary root neighbor in one cap;
   the same full private path/circle-order argument controls distant
   loads, and a fixed linear functional contradicts the complete circle
   resultant. Shared means and zero individual corner weights remain.

For each displayed coarse row the two maximal blocks have no bridges
and share one articulation H. The triangle's other two vertices have no
outside positive edge; otherwise there would be another block/bridge or
the asserted blocks would merge. They therefore have their literal full
degrees two. The intact theorem applies with the entire larger block,
every chord and load, and all 2/1/0 actual completing squares retained.
No separate enumeration of the larger block is required.

The theorem also excludes a terminal triangular block and any bridge-free
positive component containing a triangle and at most one nontriangular
block. It does not shorten a decorated triangle's full star.
