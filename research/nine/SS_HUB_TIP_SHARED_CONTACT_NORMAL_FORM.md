**Theorem 1.1.** The whole original SS hub-tip domain satisfies:

1. The actual normals on HB and BK each have a genuine side owner.

## 3. Coframed point contacts are free in the actual certificate

We record the exact physical implication used in the join.

**Lemma 3.1.** If two distinct coframed units physically touch
and their actual positive normal is side-owned, their physical
intersection has positive length.

Indeed a physical singleton of two coframed squares must be a
vertex of both. In their common frame, their center differences
have absolute values one on both axes. If one tangential
difference had absolute value below one, the actual intersection
would have positive shared length instead.

At a singleton vertex/vertex contact, the construction in
PHYSICAL_CONTACT_STRESS.md, Section 2.2, chooses the normal
strictly inside both normal cones, exposing the vertex uniquely
in both units. For coframed squares this strict normal has two
nonzero common-frame components and is not a side axis of either
unit. It is free. Thus a positive coframed side-owned singleton
is impossible.

This is a property of the original current certificate, not a
new avoidance choice. Its later generic restrictions retain
strictness. The accepted uncrossing preserves surviving point

thinning does not change a surviving normal. No endpoint mean

## 4. Each page has at most one shared perimeter contact

side frame. Their actual HK contact is either shared or a
vertex/vertex singleton.

HB and BK cannot both be shared.

No geometric C4 or smaller force graph is substituted for either

## 5. A shared central edge excludes both shared root-page edges

Now suppose HK is shared. If HB were shared as well, H,K,B
would be coframed. The whole-domain BK owner conclusion of
Section 2 and Lemma 3.1 would make BK shared. This contradicts
the first page exclusion in Section 4.

The same argument with HB and BK exchanged uses the whole HB
owner conclusion to exclude a shared BK. Hence

\[
          HK\text{ shared}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
             HB,BK\text{ are both singletons}.
 \tag{2}
\]

Their actual normals remain side-owned. If B had the common
H/K frame, either singleton would contradict Lemma 3.1.
Thus B is tilted relative to that common frame.

Together with the second page exclusion, (2) permits only one
additional shared edge when HK is shared. If HK is not shared,
Section 4 permits at most one shared edge on each page.
The full at-most-two conclusion follows.
