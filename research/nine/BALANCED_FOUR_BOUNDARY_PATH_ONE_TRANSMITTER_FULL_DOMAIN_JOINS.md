## 1. A uniform two-exit theorem

Let S1--S2--S3--S4 be a physical contact path of four distinct actual
positively loaded boundary units. Assume the complete actual circle
resultants satisfy

    F1+F2+F3+F4=0.                                      (1)

Let U be an actual unloaded degree-two body with exactly the positive
neighbors S1,S4. The configuration is impossible under either of the
following independently sufficient additional premises:

1. Another actual unit V, distinct from U and the two endpoints,
   physically touches both S1 and S4. V may be either of the two
   internal path bodies or another actual packing body. Its genuine
   degree, every force and every application mean remain arbitrary.
2. One path endpoint has true total positive pair degree two and its
   real other positive neighbor is its boundary-path neighbor. More
   generally it suffices that this endpoint has the actual positive
   proper radial port of ONE_PROPER_ENDPOINT_DIAGONAL_CHANNEL_ANGLE_BOUND.md.

For premise 1, UNIT_BYPASS_NORMAL_CHANNEL.md, Sections 1--3, forces
the actual U channel to have side reserve exactly one. Therefore its
original endpoint applications satisfy

    |p_4-p_1|=1,
    |c_4-c_1| <= |c_4-p_4|+|p_4-p_1|+|p_1-c_1|
               <=1+sqrt(2).                            (2)

The full FOUR_BOUNDARY_PATH_ENDPOINT_SEPARATION.md theorem contradicts
(2), since (1) and the actual boundary path give

    |c_4-c_1|>1+sqrt(2).                                (3)

The bypass theorem needs no force equation at V or either endpoint,
and its side channel includes every actual transverse mean. No endpoint
or bypass degree is reduced.

For premise 2, the side reserve is excluded by exactly (2)--(3).
For diagonal reserve sqrt(2), the new one-proper-endpoint theorem
gives minor endpoint center angle less than 5pi/6, whereas the same
balanced boundary-path theorem gives it strictly greater than 5pi/6.
The complete local parallel endpoint exclusions and all two-corner
masks are in that new source, Section 7. This proves the second exit.

The first exit is independent of the new diagonal-channel theorem.
Neither exit assumes that the other path bodies have pair degree two.
Equation (1) follows by summing the complete component force equations
when these four bodies carry every positive circle load of that component.
Every internal force, including any additional spoke or chord force,
cancels with its actual opposite. A subset of a larger loaded set does
not acquire (1) merely by being a path.

## 6. Rooted C5, house and fan: the full leaf force blocks a direct transfer

FIVE_BODY_BLOCK_LEAF_GRAPH_LOAD_COVER.md uses an actual loaded radial
leaf L attached to a physically clear root R of a five-body block.
The exact component load set is the displayed block loads together
with L; the leaf resultant F_L is nonzero. Summing the complete block
and leaf equations gives

    sum of all block circle resultants = -F_L != 0.      (5)

Consequently a four-load boundary path entirely in the block is not
balanced when it consists of all four block loads. In particular this
rules out an illicit direct use of Section 1 for the rooted C5 four-load
row, the rooted house-hub four-load row and the rooted fan-hub row.
The root retains its actual additional leaf force in every case.

With three block loads, the complete four-load set contains L. In
the specified positive graph L's sole positive neighbor is the clear
root R, so L has no positive edge to another loaded body. Hence that
graph supplies no four-boundary contact path through all four loads.
This applies to every three-block-load house orbit and to the fan
endpoint and inner-root rows, as well as the rooted C5 three-load rows.
No additional zero-weight physical boundary contact is assumed or
excluded to repair this missing hypothesis. Two block loads give only
three total loads and are outside the theorem.

Thus the complete rooted load cover, including both S/D leaf types
and every root degree, gives no additional unconditional fine join
from Section 1. This is a premise check, not a new obstruction to
physical contacts not forced by the load-cover graph.

The same warning applies to larger attached graphs: a path inside one
block need not carry the whole component circle balance. External
positive branch forces cannot be erased to create that premise.
For any larger exact component in which the four specified path bodies
do carry every circle load and the real transmitter hypotheses hold,
Section 1 remains directly applicable with all additional unloaded
forces retained. No complete seven-, eight- or nine-body coarse graph
family is asserted to satisfy those hypotheses merely from its size.
