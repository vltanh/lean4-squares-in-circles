## 2. An orientation-complete physical vertical-clearance function

For a folded original slope t in [0,1] put

    a=1/sqrt(1+t^2), b=t*a, h=(a+b)/2, k=(a-b)/2.

For two such frames F=(a,b,h,k), G=(A,B,H,K), define

    sigma(F,G)=max(1+|h-H|,
                       1/2+max(k*A+h*B,h*A-k*B)).  (3)

This is a lower bound for their true physical side-axis support sum for
either relative chirality. The second expression is exactly the support
at the folded-angle difference. To check the other chirality explicitly,
write the folded angles as theta,phi in[0,pi/4] and d=|theta-phi|.
Their sum lies in[d,pi/2-d]. The function cos+sin has its minimum on
that interval at its equal-valued endpoints, so the opposite-chirality
support is at least the difference support. The first expression in(3)
is the original width-increment bound. The independent integer checker
also reconstructs the identical relative cosine/sine formula before
intersecting their interval bounds.

For a horizontal gap bounded by d<1, put

    K(F,G,d)=max(sqrt(max(0,sigma^2-d^2)),
       min((sigma-a*d)/max(b,1/40),(sigma-b*d)/a,
           (sigma-A*d)/max(B,1/40),(sigma-B*d)/A)). (4)

If the two original squares are vertically ordered, their vertical center
gap is at least K. Their actual center distance is at least sigma, giving
the radical. Every original owner axis gives one of the four facet lower
bounds. All numerators are positive because sigma>=1>d. A zero vertical
component cannot separate; replacing a small positive denominator by the
larger1/40 only weakens the required lower gap. The finite formula thus
includes all aligned frames and both signs of every normal.
