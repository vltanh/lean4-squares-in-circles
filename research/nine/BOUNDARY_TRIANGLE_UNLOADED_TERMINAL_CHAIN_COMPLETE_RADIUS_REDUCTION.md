## 3. The original degree-two equations transmit the exact physical width

\[

\]

\[
                         \nu=\mu,\qquad q=n.
\tag{10}
\]

The original incoming and outgoing applications `P_-,P_+` belong to the actual common intersections. They realize the minimum and maximum supports of `T` in direction `n`. The full original torque equation is

\[
 -\mu\det(P_-,n)+\mu\det(P_+,n)=0.
\]

Hence `P_+-P_-` is parallel to `n`, and its scalar projection is the positive actual width. Therefore

\[
                   P_+-P_-=w_T(n)n.
\tag{11}
\]

There are exactly two possibilities, with no angular interval omitted.

* If `n` is an own side normal of `T`, its opposite support sides are distance one apart. Equation (11) gives `w_T(n)=1` and `P_+=P_-+n`. The two original means have exactly the same transverse coordinate. Both closed side endpoints are allowed when they occur as original shared means.
* If `n` is not an own side normal, both supports are unique opposite vertices. In the actual own frame their difference is one of `+/-e+/-f`, of length `sqrt(2)`. Its being parallel to `n` forces `n` to be the corresponding diagonal direction. Thus `w_T(n)=sqrt(2)` and `P_+=P_-+sqrt(2)n`.

The alternatives exhaust the entire circle of directions, including the axis endpoints. This also rederives the exact discrete transmitter theorem of `PHYSICAL_CONTACT_STRESS.md`, Section 5, directly from the current original equations.

Apply (10)--(11) successively to every actual `T_i`. Every original path normal points forward along the same `n`; every positive pair magnitude equals the first one. Let `P_i` be the actual original application between `T_i` and its successor. Then

\[

\tag{12}
\]

The last actual contact is the minimum support of `C`. Consequently

All intermediate physical squares and all original means remain in (12). In particular (13) is not a new direct `U-C` edge.
