## 5. Two actual cycles sharing only H cannot both surround O

Two Jordan curves meeting at only one point have either
disjoint bounded interiors or one bounded interior inside
the other. To see the relevant alternative directly,
remove the common point H from one curve. The resulting
connected open arc avoids the other Jordan curve, so it
lies wholly in one component of its complement. The same
is true with the curves exchanged. If their bounded
interiors overlap, these two arc placements force nesting;

require a second intersection. There is no interlacing
crossing at a lone shared vertex in a plane embedding.

nested. Every vertex of the inner cycle except H lies
