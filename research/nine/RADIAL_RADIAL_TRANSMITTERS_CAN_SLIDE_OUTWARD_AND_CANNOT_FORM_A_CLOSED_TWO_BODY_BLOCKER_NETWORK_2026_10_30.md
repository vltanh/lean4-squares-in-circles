# Outward freedom and a complete two-transmitter collective motion

Date: October 30, 2026. Author: common-certificate continuation.

**Status: complete proposed hand theorem, awaiting independent full review.**
Every original radial/radial side transmitter can slide a little outward
while every other actual square stays fixed. Actual core contacts are
retained: an outward-blocking zero chord would cut a loaded body off from
its circle spoke. Actual completion contacts are retained: an outward
blocker would occupy the empty radial/radial pocket.

This outward freedom gives a complete collective motion for two such
transmitters meeting the same radial loaded body. At an original
radius/Phi minimum they cannot have all their inward blockers inside
that two-body set. The proof covers every actual contact feature,
including an owned longitudinal side, an owned transverse side and a
multiple active-axis junction. No pair of physical blockers is assumed
distinct.

## 1. Exact scope and statements

Keep an entire original compatible simple positive cycle with at least
three loaded bodies and all its physical squares, original normals,
force and torque equations, application means, circle masks, compatible
physical embedding and actual circle spokes. A radial endpoint below
means a loaded square with two physical circle corners; either
individual coefficient may be zero.

An RR transmitter means one actual unloaded true-degree-two side
transmitter U in a channel X--U--Y whose two loaded endpoints are
radial. In its unchanged outward channel frame the accepted radial
geometry gives

\[
 P_X=(-1/2,d),\quad P_Y=(1/2,d),\quad
 U=[-1/2,1/2]\times[b-1/2,b+1/2],
 \quad d-1/2<b<d+1/2,
                                                               \tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
 d=\sqrt{R^2-1/4}-1,
 \qquad \phi=\arctan\frac1{2d},
 \qquad N=(0,1)
                                                               \tag{1.2}
\]

is its outward transverse direction. No mean has been centered.

**Theorem 1.1 (full outward freedom).** In
\(79/40\le R\le\sqrt{1105}/16\), every RR transmitter admits
all sufficiently small translations \(+\epsilon N\), epsilon>0,
with every other actual square fixed, as complete feasible packings
at the original radius. This statement does not use a Phi minimum.

**Theorem 1.2 (no closed two-body RR blocker network).** Suppose in
addition \(2<R\le\sqrt{1105}/16\), the original packing minimizes
Phi among radius minimizers, and two RR transmitters U_+,U_- are the
two original positive neighbors of the same radial loaded body X.
Then at least one of them has an actual inward blocker other than
the other one. Here inward blocker has the complete all-common-
separator meaning of [B], not merely a chosen normal at a contact.

By the complete boundary-clearance theorem [T] and central routing [B],
such an external blocker must be another physically circle-clear
unloaded core body or an actual central additional square. Theorem 1.2
is a complete exclusion of the closed two-body blocking alternative;
it does not exclude those external alternatives.

## 2. A genuine outward core blocker would be an exterior chord

First use any side transmitter whose original opposite side applications
are in side interiors at one common height h. Its top face lies strictly
above h. Orient every common separator n of a touching pair U,W from
U toward W. A genuine outward blocker means

\[
                       n\cdot N>0
 \quad\hbox{for every common separator.}                     \tag{2.1}
\]

As in the complete separator argument of [B], failure of (2.1)
provides a common separator with n.N<=0 and therefore preserves this
pair under \(+\epsilon N\). A pair satisfying (2.1) intersects U
only on its top face, because the maximum support of U in any such
normal is there.

Suppose W is a core body other than U's two positive neighbors X,Y.
Add the one actual U--W contact edge to the original positive cycle,
only as a geometric graph edge. No force or positive coefficient is
assigned to it.

This graph still has the compatible physical plane embedding with
all its original circle spokes. Here is the full local check. If
U,W share a segment, draw the added edge through a relative-interior
point of that segment away from the finitely many other contact
junctions. If they meet at one point and at most three squares meet
there, every local graph has a noncrossing drawing in the actual
sector order. If four squares meet, their frames agree and their
union is a full aligned 2x2 block by [P], Section 1. A point-only
U,W pair must then occupy opposite quadrants and admits a common
normal parallel to U's transmission axis. Its N component is zero,
contrary to (2.1). Thus the only new opposite-pair crossing that
could invalidate the construction cannot occur. All other junctions
are unchanged. The geometric construction in [P], Sections 5--6,
therefore applies to the cycle plus this one edge and preserves all
circle spokes simultaneously.

The new edge must lie in the interior face of the original cycle.
Indeed the two U--W arcs of that cycle contain X and Y respectively
as internal vertices, since W differs from both. Both X,Y physically
touch the containing circle. If the new chord lay in the exterior
face, one of those two cycle arcs would cease to be incident to the
unbounded face of the augmented graph. Its internal loaded vertex,
X or Y, would have no exterior access to its actual circle corner.
This contradicts its retained exterior spoke. This elementary chord
argument uses each actual circle access, not an abstractly planar
graph with forgotten boundary order.

On the other hand, the actual top-face contact forces the added edge
to leave U in its exterior sector. To see this without assigning an
artificial center location, homotope the U part of the cycle to its
actual horizontal application chord at height h, as in [C], Section 2.
Move the graph vertex within U to the midpoint of that chord. The
new third strand can run from there to the top-face contact with
interior above the chord. The three distinct boundary ports retain
their original sector order. The complete outward channel routing
identifies that upper sector as the exterior side of the cycle.
This contradicts the interior-chord conclusion.

Consequently no nonconsecutive core body is a genuine outward blocker.
The original endpoints X,Y have their unchanged transverse-invariant
separating planes and are not outward blockers either.

## 3. A genuine outward extra would occupy the own channel pocket

Now let W be additional to the core and satisfy (2.1). Its actual
intersection is on the top face of U, strictly above the application
chord.

The full local routing proof of [B], Section 3, applies with upper
and lower interchanged. For completeness, if the contact is a
segment, choose an interior point p, where no third square meets.
For a singleton involving a side interior, its two tangent sectors
leave room for at most one other square. For a vertex/vertex
singleton, the only four-square possibility is opposite aligned
quadrants, already excluded by its transverse-invariant separator.
Thus at some actual contact p no positive cycle edge between other
core bodies passes through p. The U chord is a positive distance
below p.

No actual circle spoke passes through p either. A spoke is inside
its own open core square except at its unshareable circle endpoint;
p is neither such an endpoint nor an interior point of another core
square. In the RR caller U itself is circle-clear by (1.1).

All sufficiently small compatible junction routings can therefore
miss a fixed disk about p. A point inside U above its application
chord has the label of its own exterior channel pocket by [C],
Section 2. Join such a nearby point to p inside U and then to a
nearby open-interior point of W. This small comparison path avoids
the cycle and its spokes, so that point of W has the same own-pocket
label. Compact paths in the connected open interior of W have
positive distance from the closed core union; the unchanged weak-
boundary argument then puts the whole additional square in that
same closed actual channel pocket.

For an RR channel, [R], Section 7, proves that this entire pocket
contains no additional unit. Hence no additional square is a
genuine outward blocker of an RR transmitter.

## 4. Complete full-packing outward motion

Sections 2--3 exclude every genuine outward blocker of an RR U.
Choose, for each actual neighbor, a common separator with n.N<=0.
The support of the translated U changes by epsilon(n.N)<=0, so
all those full-square separations remain valid. Original noncontacts
have positive compact clearance and remain disjoint for a common
sufficiently small epsilon.

Equation (1.1) gives b>0 and the strict corner bound

\[
                  1/4+(b+1/2)^2<R^2.
\]

It therefore permits a sufficiently small positive increase of b.
All other bodies and their containment constraints stay fixed. This
proves Theorem 1.1 with every original physical contact retained.

This is a feasibility theorem. It does not say that moving outward
alone lowers Phi: the purpose is to allow the outward compensation
in the genuine collective motion below.

## 5. The exact two-RR chart at their common radial load

Suppose X has the two RR transmitters U_+,U_- as its positive
neighbors. Orient the radial axis of X as the positive first axis.
The exact consecutive radial/radial center gap is 4phi, and the
original cycle order puts the other loaded endpoints on opposite
sides of X. Consequently the two outward channel axes are

\[
 N_+=(\cos2\phi,\sin2\phi),\qquad
 N_-=(\cos2\phi,-\sin2\phi).
                                                               \tag{5.1}
\]

Both actual application chords have height d in their own frames.
The actual centers and full square frames are therefore

\[
 c_+=b_+N_+,\qquad c_-=b_-N_-,\qquad
 d-1/2<b_+,b_-<d+1/2,
                                                               \tag{5.2}
\]

with own axes N_+,JN_+ and N_-,JN_-. No original body is moved or
reflected to obtain this description; it follows from the two actual
RR gaps around their common radial endpoint.

Put

\[
 a=-\cos4\phi>0,\qquad s=\sin4\phi>0,\qquad
 H=\frac{1+a+s}{2}.
                                                               \tag{5.3}
\]

Every one of the four side-normal separating-axis tests for this
fixed pair has the same sum of half-widths H. The absolute center
projections on those axes are exactly

\[
 b_++a b_-,\qquad a b_++b_-,\qquad s b_-,\qquad s b_+.
                                                               \tag{5.4}
\]

The separating-axis theorem for two convex rectangles thus says
that their open interiors are disjoint exactly when at least one
of the four quantities in (5.4) is at least H. If the squares
actually touch, every quantity is at most H and at least one is
equal to H. This formulation includes every side/side, vertex/side,
vertex/vertex and multiple-axis endpoint without choosing a new
original force application.

## 6. One exact inequality controls every collective transverse case

The same-nine lower window gives R>2. More generally assume this
explicit inequality in Theorem 1.2. Then

\[
              d>\frac{14}{15},\qquad
              b_+,b_->\frac{13}{30}.                    \tag{6.1}
\]

Indeed \(\sqrt{15}/2>29/15\), with squared reserve
\(15/4-841/225=11/900>0\).

The exact identity for cos(4 arctan t), with t=15/28, gives

\[
 -\cos\bigl(4\arctan(15/28)\bigr)
     =\frac{393119}{1018081}<\frac25,
                                                               \tag{6.2}
\]

whose cross-multiplied margin is
\(2\cdot1018081-5\cdot393119=70567>0\).
Since \(\tan\phi<15/28\) and \(0<4\phi<\pi\), it follows
that

\[
                             0<a<2/5.                    \tag{6.3}
\]

Using s<=1, and the positive derivative of
\((a+a^2/2)/(1+a^2)\) on \([0,2/5]\), gives

\[
 \frac{aH}{1+a^2}
 \le\frac{a(1+a/2)}{1+a^2}
 <\frac{12}{29}<\frac{13}{30}<b_+,b_-.
                                                               \tag{6.4}
\]

For the derivative, after removing the positive denominator the
numerator is \(1+a-a^2>0\). The rational reserve between the two
middle fractions is \(17/870>0\). Thus (6.4) holds uniformly over
all original radii and both full mean intervals in (5.2).

## 7. Every closed two-body blocker network has a Phi-decreasing motion

Suppose, contrary to Theorem 1.2, neither U_+ nor U_- has an
inward blocker outside this two-body set. At every actual contact
with a fixed external body, each inward-moving transmitter therefore
admits a common separator preserving its inward motion. Every
outward-moving transmitter is already safe against all fixed bodies
by Theorem 1.1. The original loaded endpoints remain fixed and use
their unchanged transverse-invariant supports. Both moving units
have strict circle clearance.

If U_+,U_- do not touch, move either one a little inward and keep
the other fixed. Pair clearance and the preceding external checks
give a complete feasible Phi decrease.

Suppose instead that they touch. Choose any equality in the complete
four-axis list (5.4).

If \(s b_-=H\), move U_+ inward and keep U_- fixed. This actual
separating-axis inequality is independent of b_+, so it is preserved
exactly. Phi decreases because b_+>0. The equality \(s b_+=H\)
is handled by interchanging the labels. These cases include contact
owned by a longitudinal side; no artificial force is inserted there.

If \(b_++a b_-=H\), make the actual simultaneous translations

\[
                  b_-\longmapsto b_--\epsilon,
 \qquad          b_+\longmapsto b_++a\epsilon.
                                                               \tag{7.1}
\]

The selected separating inequality remains exactly H. The change
in the sum of the two actual squared center norms is

\[
 \Delta\Phi
   =2\epsilon(a b_+-b_-)+(1+a^2)\epsilon^2
   =2\epsilon\bigl(aH-(1+a^2)b_-\bigr)
                         +(1+a^2)\epsilon^2.
                                                               \tag{7.2}
\]

The linear coefficient is strictly negative by (6.4), so the change
is negative for all sufficiently small positive epsilon. The other
transverse equality \(a b_++b_-=H\) gives the reflected motion.
If several equalities hold, preserving the one chosen actual
separator suffices for nonoverlap; no unchosen equality has to remain
a contact in the competing packing.

All other actual contacts have already been checked according to
the sign of their body's motion. All original noncontacts and both
circle constraints persist under one finite common smallness bound.
Every unchanged original square remains present. This is therefore
a complete original-packing Phi decrease, contradicting canonical
minimality and proving Theorem 1.2.

## 8. Literal short-C7 network and the remaining external terminals

The accepted occupied-side-pocket theorem [O] and the empty RR
pocket theorem [R] imply that every surviving no-long C7 has at
least one side channel with a physical-unique loaded endpoint.
Its numbers of isolated transmitters are three, two or one for
four, five or six positive loads respectively. Hence a surviving
C7 has at most two RR channels.

If it has two RR channels, the only possible case is four loads
and three side transmitters. A five-load cycle has only two
channels; if both were RR, no side pocket could be occupied. The
same argument excludes the six-load RR possibility. In the
four-load case the third channel cannot be diagonal, since the
only other side pockets would be the empty two RR pockets.

Among four cyclically ordered loads there is just one direct
loaded/loaded edge. Two RR channels which have disjoint loaded
endpoint sets would make all four loads radial; then every side
pocket would be empty. Thus the two RR channels share a radial
load X. Their other endpoints are radial, and the fourth load
is physical-unique at one end of the direct loaded/loaded edge.
The third side channel joins that unique body to a radial one.

This is exactly the two-body chart of Section 5. Its only possible
external core blocker is the third, radial/unique side transmitter.
The other external blocker allowed by [B] and [T] is the one
possible central completion square. The third channel has capacity
at most one by [R], so the two actual completion squares consist
of one central square and one extra in that third side pocket.
Theorem 1.2 proves that the RR pair must connect through an actual
inward-blocking contact to that central square or to the third
transmitter; the two RR bodies cannot supply all their own
constraints in isolation.

This retains the important remaining alternatives. A central square
may block both RR bodies, and the third transmitter may itself have
an occupied pocket and additional actual contacts. The present
collective motion does not ignore either terminal and does not
claim a whole mixed-mask C7 closure. Cases with only one or no RR
channel retain their own physical blockers as well.
