## 2. Loaded-endpoint cone theorem, including parallel and coincident cases

**Theorem 2.1.** The original channel direction d is in the closed
positive cone generated by b,c. More precisely, exactly one of these
types occurs:

1. B's two original normal lines are parallel. Then d=b and B is
   physical radial S, with equal positive circle weights.
2. C's two original normal lines are parallel. Then d=c and C is
   physical radial S, with equal positive circle weights.
3. Both endpoint normal pairs are nonparallel. Then d lies in the
   strict positive cone generated by b,c.

The first two types cannot occur simultaneously because b,c are
nonparallel. The conclusion is about the unchanged actual channel;
it does not choose a new d inside an allowable cone.

*Proof.* At B the original outward pair normals are -b,-d.
If their lines are parallel, the full parallel-loaded-star theorem

d=c and its physical S conclusion. These two cases retain all

distinct, apply the complete original nonparallel two-application

    q_B=Q_B+ell_B*b=T+alpha*d,

## 3. A full physical-mask circle-corner lemma

**Lemma 3.1.** Let E be any actual unit square contained in a disk
about O, with positive total circle weight. Fix its actual orthonormal
own axes f,w, and suppose its full original circle resultant G obeys

    G.f>=0, G.w<=0.

Then the actual +f/-w corner

    Z=center(E)+(f-w)/2

lies on the containing circle. It remains a physical circle corner
when an individual coefficient there or at another physical corner
is zero.

*Proof.* Work relative to the unchanged O, and put
x=(center(E)-O).f, y=(center(E)-O).w. Among the four actual corner
squared radii, maximization chooses the +f sign when x>0 and the
-f sign when x<0, with both signs tied when x=0. The identical
statement holds independently in the w coordinate.

If x<0, every physical circle corner must have f coordinate
x-1/2<0: a corner with the other sign has strictly smaller squared
radius and cannot be on the disk boundary when E is contained.
The positive weighted sum of actual circle corners would then give
G.f<0, contrary to the premise. Thus x>=0. Similarly G.w<=0
forces y<=0. The displayed +f/-w corner is therefore a farthest
actual corner, including either coordinate tie.

Positive total circle weight means at least one actual corner is on
the containing circle. Its maximal squared radius is R^2. Every
farthest tied corner, including Z, has the same radius. This argument
uses physical positions of all circle corners, independently of which
individual weights vanish. QED.

The lemma needs no radius window, degree or force equation at an
external neighbor. In a loaded two-force application, the needed signs
come from its entire vector (1), not from a partial circle mask.
