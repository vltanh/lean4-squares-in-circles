## 3. One continuous angular argument bounds the entire aperture

**Theorem 3.1.** Let Theta be the clockwise angle from b_ext to
c_ext. Then

    0<Theta<3*pi/4.                               (9)

Define the actual angles and positive ratio

    alpha=atan(s/c), psi=atan(b/a), m=D0/N0,
    phi=atan((s+q)/c).

From (2), u0=cos(alpha+psi), v_y=sin(alpha+psi).
Their strict positivity and E=u0-v_y>0 imply

    0<alpha+psi<pi/4.                             (10)

Thus alpha,psi are both in (0,pi/4), and phi is in (0,pi/2).
In the actual (h,e) coordinates, b_ext has argument
pi/2+atan(m) and c_ext has argument phi. Consequently

    Theta=pi/2+atan(m)-phi in (0,pi).              (11)

If m<=1, (11) immediately gives Theta<3*pi/4, since phi>0.
This includes the equality m=1.

Suppose m>1. For fixed a,b,c,s the exact ratio

    (1-a+H*s)/(1-H*c+b)

is strictly increasing in H, because its derivative has positive
numerator s*(1-H*c+b)+c*(1-a+H*s). Its denominator stays
positive up to H=1. Since H<=1,

    m<=m1=(1-a+s)/(1-c+b).                        (12)

The inequality m1>1 says c+s>a+b. The function cos(t)+sin(t)
is strictly increasing on (0,pi/4), so alpha>psi. Combined with
(10), this gives psi<pi/8 and hence a>9/10. For the last rational
comparison, cos(pi/8)^2=(2+sqrt(2))/4>17/20>81/100.

Together with the hand entry s<2/3, this gives

    2*a-s-1>2/15>0.

The exact positive identity

    a*(1-c+b)-b*(1-a+s)
       =a*(1-c)+b*(2*a-s-1)>0

therefore proves

    m<=m1<a/b=cot(psi),
    atan(m)<pi/2-psi.                             (13)

Independently, the same original return identity proves

    (s+q)/c-(a-b)/(a+b)
       =a*E/[b*c*(a+b)]>0.                       (14)

Every denominator is strictly positive. The second fraction on the
left is tan(pi/4-psi), whose angle lies in (0,pi/4). Strict
monotonicity of atan on the positive axis gives

    phi>pi/4-psi.                                 (15)

Substitute (13),(15) into (11):

    Theta<pi/2+(pi/2-psi)-(pi/4-psi)=3*pi/4.

This completes both exhaustive m cases and proves (9). The proof uses
no endpoint force, circle-mask distinction, numerical angle cover or
transverse support-gap bound.

## 6. The entire two-unique central pentagon is empty

opposite-return side feature contains no additional unit square.

Let A_pre denote the polygon area after subtracting this A cut only.

The exact determinant expansion, followed by the two projections of

    S_area=beta_B*cos(delta_B)+beta_C*cos(delta_C)
             +beta_B*beta_C*sin(Theta).          (22)

For completeness, the base determinant is

    det(K-W,L-W)=a*x+b,

and the unchanged unit bridge gives

    Delta.c_ext=cos(delta_C)+beta_B*sin(Theta).

The remaining determinant terms are therefore precisely S_area,
as in the exact central-pentagon expansion for the regular two-strict
chart. This calculation retains the actual A cut x/2.

Define the nonnegative quantity

    Q_gap=(beta_B-beta_C)^2/2
       +beta_B*beta_C*(1-cos(Theta)-sin(Theta))
       +beta_B*(sin(delta_B)-cos(delta_B))

    S_area=(|Delta|^2-1)/2-Q_gap.                 (24)

The actual displacements (21) and a^2+b^2=1 give

    |Delta|^2=x^2-2*b*x+2+2*a.

Substitution in (22)--(24) proves

    G(x)=x^2/2+(a-b-1)*x+a+b+1/2.                (25)

The quadratic G is convex on [0,1]. Its two endpoint values are

    G(0)=a+b+1/2<=sqrt(2)+1/2<2,
    G(1)=2*a<2,                                  (26)

where b>0 makes a<1. Thus G(x)<2 for the entire original interval
0<x<1. Equations (25)--(26) give A_pre<1, and the actual central
free area is smaller still. No further unit square can lie there.

This proves the entire two-unique central exclusion at once. It does
not use a finite parameter cover, a sampled pose, any asserted
