impossible. In particular it excludes every two-short-arms-at-

## 1. The full actual arm-type cover

Each genuine root X or Y is physically clear and is a true
unloaded transmitter. Its actual radial applications are

    p_XL=r_L e,
    p_HX=(r_L-w)e,
    r_L=r for D, r_L=d for S,
    w in {1,sqrt(2)}.                                  (1)

The real S/diagonal root contains O openly in its own coordinates
d/sqrt(2) in (0,1). A D root likewise contains O openly; its
full selected side-normal chart has both positive horizontal
origin margins and a transverse interval containing zero in
its relative interior. The original D/diagonal alternative is
already forbidden, and would also have origin-interior geometry.
These are precisely the degree-independent arm facts recorded
in Section 1 of FOUR_CYCLE_TWO_SHORT_ARMS_SAME_ROOT_CLOSED.md.

If one arm root is origin-interior in these ways, another such
root overlaps it. An S/side root of the other arm at d<=1
contains O closed, also giving overlap. At d>1 the other actual
S/side application on its cyclic root is epsilon e. The real
inward segment [epsilon-1,epsilon]e belongs to that cyclic root,
so it contains O closed and overlaps the distinct origin-interior
arm root. Thus both arms must be S/side.

Their two actual S directions are distinct, since equal directions
would give identical genuine radial S leaves. Each side root
has its unchanged radial interval [d-1,d] and an original
transverse unit interval containing zero.

## 3. Strict-low radii force two exact staggered rows

Let d<1. Both X,Y contain O closed. Neither can contain it
openly, so O is in a relative side interior of each. Their
local occupied half-planes must be opposite. Each actual radial
leaf axis is tangent to that common boundary line; the two
leaf directions are consequently equal or opposite. Equality
was excluded above. After rotating and reflecting the whole
physical picture, the actual units are therefore

    X=[d-1,d] times [0,1],
    Y=[-d,1-d] times [-1,0].                            (2)

Their genuine directions are respectively east and west. The
unchanged applications on the true roots are

    p_HX=(d-1,0),       p_KY=(1-d,0).                   (3)

Since 2d>1 and d<1, the first point is in the relative interior
of Y's top side and is X's lower-left vertex. The occupied
local sectors of these two actual squares leave exactly the
northwest right-angle sector for the distinct H through that
point. A unit-square interior at a boundary point has angle at
least pi/2. Thus H must occupy exactly that sector, making
the full original unit

    H=[d-2,d-1] times [0,1].                            (4)

This does not call the HX contact a singleton: its positive-
length shared side and original endpoint application in (3)
are retained. The reflected argument at p_KY gives

    K=[1-d,2-d] times [-1,0].                           (5)

The two actual rows in (2),(4),(5) contain the full central
rectangle

    [-d,d] times [-1,1]
       subset H union X union Y union K.               (6)

Indeed the upper row has x interval [d-2,d], and the lower
row has [-d,2-d]; both contain [-d,d] because d<1.

## 4. Neither a direct contact nor a physical bypass is possible

Every H point has x<=d-1<0, while every K point has
x>=1-d>0. Thus the two roots do not physically touch.

Suppose the distinct actual Z touched them both, at P in H
and Q in K. Its closed convex square contains the entire
segment PQ. Since P_x<0<Q_x, that segment has a point with
x=0 strictly between its endpoints. Moreover

    0<=P_y<=1,       -1<=Q_y<=0,

so the interpolated y is strictly between -1 and 1. This
point lies in the open central rectangle in (6).

A distinct physical unit cannot have even a closed point in
that open rectangle: a neighborhood of the point is covered
by the four actual squares in (6), and interior points of Z
approach every point of its closed square. A sufficiently
near interior Z point avoids their finitely many boundary
segments and consequently belongs to one of their interiors.
This violates pairwise interior disjointness. Thus Z cannot
physically touch both roots. All seam and endpoint limits
in (6) are included by this open-neighborhood argument.

Both alternatives in the local hypothesis have been excluded,
completing the strict-low domain and the theorem.
