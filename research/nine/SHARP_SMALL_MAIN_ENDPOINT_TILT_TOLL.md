## 1. Original physical statement

Retain the original pair, direction e, support notation, and constants
from `UNIVERSAL_MAIN_ENDPOINT_TILT_TOLL.md`:

\[
 a=46,\quad b=27/2,\quad k=207/16,\quad T=211/4,
 \qquad Q=1105/256.
\]

The folded main frame components satisfy
\(\alpha^2+\beta^2=1\), \(\alpha\ge\beta\ge0\). Define

\[
 \tau_*(\alpha,\beta)=k\beta-(a+k)(1-\alpha)
       =\frac{207}{16}\beta-\frac{943}{16}(1-\alpha).
 \tag{1}
\]

**Theorem.** Two original physical unit squares M,B, with disjoint open
interiors in a disk centered at zero of radius \(R\le\sqrt Q\), obey

\[
 \boxed{a[-e\cdot c_M-h_M(e)]
       +b[-e\cdot c_B-h_B(e)]+\tau_*(\alpha_M,\beta_M)\le T.}
 \tag{2}
\]

The inequality is strict when \(R<\sqrt Q\). Both original orientations,
both centers, all physical corners and both possible owners of the actual
pair separator are unrestricted. Together with the preceding theorem the
same original pair may use the maximum of the three main tolls

\[
 \max\{4\beta,\ 10\beta-a(1-\alpha),\ \tau_*(\alpha,\beta)\}.
 \tag{3}
\]

The first-order coefficient of (1), divided by the main weight, is
\(k/a=9/32\). This is the coefficient of this support inequality; no
claim that every physical equality condition is achievable at nonzero
tilt is needed.

## 2. Preserve the genuine owner and original main angle

The support reduction in the preceding manuscript gives

\[
 \operatorname{load}\le H(c)-P_M-P_B,
 \qquad c=e\cdot n\in[-1,1],\quad s=\sqrt{1-c^2},
\]

where n is an original owned physical separator, and

\[
 H(c)=\sqrt Q\,[L_a(c)+L_b(-c)],\qquad
 L_w(c)=\sqrt{w^2+k^2+2wkc},
\]

\[
 P_M=a h_M(e)+h_M(ae+kn)+k h_M(n),\qquad
 P_B=b h_B(e)+h_B(be-kn)+k h_B(n).
 \tag{4}
\]

Use the direct support functions E,N,D defined in (4) of the preceding
manuscript. If M owns n, its frame is fixed by n. The unmodified free B
frame is covered by its three triangle-edge candidates. Consequently the
three complete one-variable rows are

\[
 F_j(c)=T-H(c)+N_a(c)+J_j(-c)
                 -\tau_*(\max(|c|,s),\min(|c|,s)),
 \tag{5}
\]

with \((J_0,J_1,J_2)=(E_b,N_b,D_b)\).

If B owns n, then \(P_B=N_b(-c)\). Unlike the preceding weaker toll,
no concavity assertion is made for the modified free main frame. Keep its
entire original angle explicitly. Write

\[
 t=\beta/\alpha\in[0,1],\quad
 \alpha=(1+t^2)^{-1/2},\quad\beta=t\alpha,
\]

and let \(\epsilon=\pm1\) describe its two canonical chiralities, with
frame \((\alpha,\epsilon\beta),(-\epsilon\beta,\alpha)\). Thus its
true support in any vector (x,y) is

\[
 h_\epsilon(x,y)=
 \frac{|x\alpha+\epsilon y\beta|+|{-\epsilon x\beta+y\alpha}|}{2}.
\]

Define the two full-angle rows

\[
 G_\epsilon(c,t)=T-H(c)+\frac a2(\alpha+\beta)
       +h_\epsilon(a+kc,ks)+k h_\epsilon(c,s)
       +N_b(-c)-\tau_*(\alpha,\beta).
 \tag{6}
\]

These formulas retain every physical frame and both signs of c. Their
nonnegativity suffices for (2).

### Complete large-angle complement

For \(t\ge1/3\),

\[
 \frac{1-\alpha}{\beta}=\frac{t}{\sqrt{1+t^2}+1}
    \ge\sqrt{10}-3>\frac{143}{943}=\frac{k-4}{a+k}.
\]

The monotonicity follows by differentiation or by the increasing tangent
of half the folded angle. The last positive-root comparison is exact:

\[
                     10\cdot943^2-2972^2=59706>0.
\]

Hence \(\tau_*\le4\beta\), so the already accepted four-beta theorem
covers this entire main-angle complement, including t=1/3. It remains to
prove (6) only on \(0\le t\le1/3\).
