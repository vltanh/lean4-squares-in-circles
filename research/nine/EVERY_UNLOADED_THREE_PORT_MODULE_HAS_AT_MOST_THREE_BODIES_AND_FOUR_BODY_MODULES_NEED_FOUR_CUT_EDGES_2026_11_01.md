## 4. Common-point full-cut work

We print the elementary fact needed to check the smaller cut sizes.
Let L be a nonempty unloaded region with a nonempty FULL positive cut,
and suppose all its actual terminal squares contain the same point x.
Its internal graph need not be a tree. For each original outward cut
normal v at an inside body i, actual terminal membership gives

    v dot(c_i-x)<=-h_i(v)<=-1/2.

Dot the complete original translation at every i in L with c_i-x and
sum. Every original internal pair contributes minus its support work,
whose two halfwidths sum to at least one. Every positive cut term is
strictly negative. The resulting total cannot be zero. Thus such L
is impossible.

This argument uses no equilibrium at a terminal. In particular it
applies to a residual component whose full terminal set consists of
one unchanged actual body, or a subset of two physically touching
actual bodies.

## 5. The full cuts of size zero, one and two

### No cut

If p=0 and K has at least two bodies, connectedness supplies an internal
positive pair. Sum the complete translations dotted with c_i-O. The
total is minus the strictly positive internal support work, with no
cut contribution, which is impossible. A degree-zero isolated unloaded
singleton need not be excluded and is irrelevant to Theorem B.

### One cut

If p=1, sum the complete unloaded translations over K. Every internal
pair cancels and the one remaining original positive cut force is
nonzero. It cannot sum to zero. Thus p=1 is impossible at any body count.

### Two cuts

Let the two original cut edges have inside hosts x,y and outside actual
terminals P,Q. If P=Q, Section 4 already gives a contradiction. Hence
the actual terminals are distinct. Their cuts form the two groups of
the accepted radius-free two-anchor theorem printed in the multicycle
source used in Section 3. Its complete graph-distance strengthening
applies to this exact full two-edge cut.

Write ell for the shortest number of ORIGINAL internal positive edges
from x to y, with ell=0 when they coincide. For ANY actual a in P and
b in Q, the theorem gives

    |b-a|>=ell+1.

The host distance is finite because K is connected. Taking the minimum
over the compact actual terminal squares gives

    dist(P,Q)>=ell+1.                              (1)

The theorem's proof retains both original cut forces, every original
internal pair and all true degrees. Its ell is an actual edge distance,
not a distance in a suppressed drawing.

Every actual center has |c-O|<M=91/60, and each actual unit contains
its concentric closed disk of radius one half. Distinct unit centers

are disjoint. Therefore

    dist(P,Q)<=|c_P-c_Q|-1<2M-1=61/30<3.            (2)

Equations (1)--(2) force the integer ell to be zero or one.

If x=y, any nonempty positive component of K minus x has a nonempty
full cut only to x: both original outside cut edges were at x. That
single terminal contains a common point, so Section 4 excludes every
such component. Only x remains, and K has one actual body.

If ell=1, x and y have an actual original positive contact. Any nonempty
positive component of K minus {x,y} has nonempty full cut only to x,y.
Those two actual terminals physically touch, so again Section 4 excludes
the entire component. Only x,y remain, and K has two actual bodies.

Thus every connected unloaded full-two-cut region has at most two
actual bodies. All remaining stars retain their genuine full degree
