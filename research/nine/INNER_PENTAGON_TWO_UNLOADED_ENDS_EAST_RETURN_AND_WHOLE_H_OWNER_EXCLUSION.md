## 3. Complete C geometry, including the aligned endpoint

### 3.1. The strict negative Y_C alternative

Suppose Y_C<0. Define the unchanged C-owned CD axes by

    N_C=sqrt(X^2+Y_C^2),
    c=X/N_C, s=|Y_C|/N_C,
    n=(c,-s), j=(s,c), c,s>0, c^2+s^2=1.

Both actual inward axes have positive x components. Consequently
C's minimum-x point is its unique actual vertex. It must be the
original HC point S in (1), and

    C=S+[0,1]n+[0,1]j.                                 (11)

The original CD point R is strictly inside C's minimum-n side.
Therefore for its actual side position rho,

    R=S+rho*j, 0<rho<1.

Its two world coordinates give the exact identities

    q=a-rho*s,
    z=1+b-rho*c>b.                                     (12)

The last inequality uses rho<1 and c<=1. This is the actual HC
height; it is not an endpoint substituted into its torque.

Since Y_E>|Y_C|, we have c>c_E. Equation (7) then gives c>a,
and the positive unit pairs imply s<b. Thus the entire C has

    min_C y=z-s>b-s>0.                                 (13)

We also require a lower bound on the unchanged q. The exact
original D3 expressions give

    (a-b)*X-(a+b)*|Y_C|=gamma*b>0.

It follows that s/c<(a-b)/(a+b). Normalizing the two positive
pairs and using (a-b)^2+(a+b)^2=2 gives

    s<(a-b)/sqrt(2).

The first identity of (12) now yields

    q>a-s>a-(a-b)/sqrt(2)
      >(1-1/sqrt(2))*(a+b)
      >1-1/sqrt(2)>1/4.                               (14)

For the middle strict comparison, the difference is
(sqrt(2)-1)*b>0. Also a+b>1, and 1/sqrt(2)<3/4.
The entire original rho interval has been used, not shortened.
