## 1. Full original theorem

squares with pairwise disjoint open interiors in the same unchanged disk

K is genuinely loaded and has at least two distinct actual positive

its strict point-only vertex/vertex normal convention. For each actual
positive pair term keep its original unit outward normal n_i from K
toward neighbor L_i, positive magnitude mu_i, and actual application P_i.

    F+sum_i mu_i*n_i=0,
    sum_i mu_i*det(P_i,n_i)=0,
    F=2*sum_j lambda_j*Z_j,
    lambda_j>=0, sum_j lambda_j>0, |Z_j|=R.

Each Z_j is an actual physical circle corner of K. An individual
coefficient may be zero even when its physical corner remains present.

Suppose all original pair normals are parallel as unoriented lines:

    n_i in {e,-e}

for one unit direction e. The signs and positive magnitudes are not
prescribed.

**Theorem.** After one circle-centered orthonormal coordinate choice,
the entire actual star has exactly the following necessary form:

    a=sqrt(R^2-1/4), d=a-1>0,

Thus K is physically radial S, with both actual circle weights equal

Every actual mean remains unrestricted except by the unchanged displayed

There is no assumption that the P_i are distinct or that the star has

## 2. Complete physical corner masks and an actual neighbor reach

Choose a physical farthest corner Z of K and its two inward edge axes
to write, in own coordinates,

    Z=(a,b), K=[a-1,a] times [b-1,b], a>=b.

Containment of its two adjacent corners gives a,b>=1/2. A second
adjacent corner lies on the circle precisely when the corresponding
coordinate is 1/2. The opposite corner can have the same norm only if
a+b=1, hence a=b=1/2, which would give R=1/sqrt(2). Since R>sqrt(2),
at most one coordinate can be 1/2. Therefore there is either one
physical circle corner or exactly two adjacent physical circle corners.
For the latter rotate or reflect the actual own frame to obtain

    K=[a-1,a] times [-1/2,1/2],
    a=sqrt(R^2-1/4)>1.

This physical classification is independent of the individual weights.

No physical circle point can belong to two actual squares. Indeed a
point on the strictly convex containing circle must be a vertex of
each square containing it. If Z is such a vertex and u,v are either
square's two inward unit axes, containment of Z+u,Z+v gives

    Z.u<=-1/2, Z.v<=-1/2.

Hence -Z has strictly positive components on both inward axes. For a
sufficiently small positive t, Z-t*Z is in its open interior. Two
squares containing Z would therefore have intersecting open interiors.
In particular no original pair application is a physical circle point.

We also need the following real-neighbor fact at any original contact:

    P_i+n_i belongs to L_i.

At a vertex of L_i, write n_i in its two inward orthonormal axes.
Both components are nonnegative because P_i is a minimum-n_i support
point, and each component is at most one because |n_i|=1. Thus
P_i+n_i belongs to the actual unit. At a relative side-interior point,
n_i is its inward side axis and the entire length-one normal segment
belongs to that same unit. Side endpoints are already included in the
vertex case. This covers every original shared mean and normal owner.

## 3. A strict common support vertex cannot carry two same-normal neighbors

This elementary consequence of the retained contact convention is the
new ingredient needed when the original means coincide.

Suppose a fixed unchanged original normal n strictly exposes a vertex
P of K and two distinct actual positive neighbors L_1,L_2 use that
same n at P. Both actual physical intersections with K are singletons:
the maximum-n support of K is the single point P, so each intersection
is contained in {P}.

For either L_i there are exactly two possible physical features at P.

* If P is a vertex of L_i, the original point-only vertex/vertex
  convention makes -n strict in L_i's outward vertex cone. Equivalently
  n is strict in its inward quadrant. Thus P+t*n is in int L_i for
  every sufficiently small t>0.
* If P is in the relative interior of a side of L_i, n is the actual
  inward normal of that side, and again P+t*n is in int L_i for every
  sufficiently small t>0.

An interior point of L_i cannot be an original support application.
These cases therefore exhaust the actual contact feature. Choosing t
small enough for both real neighbors puts P+t*n in both open interiors,
a contradiction.

Consequently a normal which strictly exposes a K vertex can occur at
that vertex for at most one distinct actual positive neighbor. This
argument does not perturb the selected normal, pick a new mean, or
delete a term. It also explains why distinct actual neighbors, rather
than a count of multiplier atoms, are essential.

## 4. Full parallel-star proof, including every physical weight endpoint

The complete physical mask in Section 2 makes F nonzero. With one
physical corner, F is a positive multiple of that corner. With two
physical corners, the whole original force is

    F=k*(a,eta), k=2*(lambda_++lambda_-)>0,
    eta=(lambda_+-lambda_-)/(2*(lambda_++lambda_-)),
    -1/2<=eta<=1/2.

Its x component is positive. In particular either zero individual
coefficient is represented by eta=+/-1/2, not reclassified as a
one-physical-corner mask.

Since the original pair force sum is parallel to e, the full force
equation makes e parallel to this nonzero F. Each original n_i must
therefore point along F or opposite F.

### 4.1. One physical circle corner

In the own coordinates of Section 2, F is a positive multiple of
Z=(a,b), with a,b>0. An original normal along F strictly supports K
at Z, which is an unshareable physical circle point. Thus every
original normal must point opposite F. That direction strictly
supports the unique opposite K vertex, so all original applications
coincide at that vertex with the same selected normal.

There are at least two distinct actual positive neighbors. Section 3
rules out their two coincident same-normal singleton applications.
The whole one-physical-corner alternative is impossible.

### 4.2. Two physical corners with unequal weights

If eta!=0, a normal directed along F strictly supports the outer
circle corner (a,sign(eta)/2), and is again impossible. Every original
normal must point opposite F, which strictly exposes one unique
inner K corner. All actual applications then coincide there with
that same normal, again contradicting Section 3.

This argument includes eta=+/-1/2. Neither zero coefficient endpoint
of the full physical two-corner mask has been discarded.

### 4.3. Two physical corners with equal weights

The only surviving mask has eta=0. Thus the two original physical
weights are equal and strictly positive, and F points East in the
actual radial chart.

An original East normal would have an actual application P with
P_x=a. Its real neighbor contains P+East by Section 2. But

    a+1>R,

because R>sqrt(2)>1 and

    a^2-(R-1)^2=2*R-5/4>0.

That neighbor point would have x>R and be outside the original disk.
Consequently every original normal is West. Every unchanged mean is
on the actual inner face x=d=a-1. The full original torque is exactly

    sum_i mu_i*y_i=0,

since det((d,y_i),West)=y_i. The full force equation also retains the
original total magnitudes, with sum_i mu_i=2*(lambda_++lambda_-)*a.

This proves every conclusion of Section 1. The proof uses no upper
bound on R, no circle-boundary premise at any neighbor and no original
application-distinctness premise.
