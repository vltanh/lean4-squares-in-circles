## 5. One exact derivative identity for the original reconstructed port

For fixed R,H define the explicit scalar function

\[
                                F(\eta)=|P(\eta)|^2,
\tag{25}
\]

using (8)--(11). Its derivative on the whole preceding domain is

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 F'=rac{2b}{L^3}
      \left[
       \left(1-\frac CA\right)\mathcal C
              +t'(1-t)b
      \right],
 \qquad t'=\frac{HC}{Z^2}>0,
 \quad}
\tag{26}
\]
where

\[
                   \mathcal C=L^3+tL^2+(1-t)x>0,
                         \qquad x=1-ta.
\tag{27}
\]

Here is the full algebra behind (26). In the actual orthonormal
square frame, \(z=a v+b u\), \(Q=z-v\), and
\(V=v-tz\). Therefore

\[
 L^2=1-2ta+t^2R^2,\qquad
 J:=Q\cdot V=(1+t)a-tR^2-1,
\]
\[
                       F=R^2+2-2a-\frac{2J}{L}.
\tag{28}
\]

Differentiating (28), clearing the positive \(L^3\), and using
\(R^2-a^2=b^2\) gives

\[
 \frac{L^3}{2}F'
 =-a'\bigl[L^3+1-t(1+t)a+t^3R^2\bigr]
                      +t'(1-t)b^2.
\tag{29}
\]

The bracket equals (27), because
\(1-t(1+t)a+t^3R^2=tL^2+(1-t)(1-ta)\).
Substitution of (19) proves (26). This is an identity, not an
estimate of a sampled derivative.

## 6. The derivative is strictly positive on the entire interpolation interval

If \(A\ge C\), both terms in the bracket of (26) are
nonnegative and its second term is strictly positive. Thus \(F'>0\).
Consider the remaining possibility \(A<C\).

### 6.1. In this possibility \(c<1/2\)

Recall \(a>b>1/2\), \(a^2+b^2=R^2\), and
\(A=ca+sb\). For fixed \(c,s,R\), the function

\[
                 y\longmapsto c\sqrt{R^2-y^2}+sy
\]

is concave on \([1/2,R/\sqrt2]\). If \(c\ge1/2\),
then \(s/c\le\sqrt3\), since \(c\le1/\sqrt2\).
At the left endpoint its value minus C is

\[
 c(d+1)+s/2-(c+s)=cd-s/2>0,
\]

because \(d>9/10>\sqrt3/2\). At the right endpoint its
value is \((R/\sqrt2)C>C\). Concavity then gives \(A>C\)
at every allowed \(b\), a contradiction. Hence in the case
under consideration

\[
                              1/4\le c<1/2.
\tag{30}
\]

### 6.2. Uniform elementary estimates for the two derivative terms

On (30),

\[
 D\le(\sqrt{15}-1)/4<23/32,
 \qquad C\ge(1+\sqrt{15})/4>6/5.
\tag{31}

Use \(A<C\), \(C^2+D^2=2\), and \(R\ge K_0\):

\[

\tag{32}
\]

The last step uses the increasing function
\(y/(K_0^2-2+y)\) at \(y=(23/32)^2\); its exact
squared reserve is \(18848/4641075>0\). Thus \(1-t>8/15\).

Also, by (13) and (31), \(A>359/320\), and hence

\[
 Z^2<(133/64)^2-(359/320)^2=(7/4)^2-1/400.
\]

It follows that

\[
                             \frac CZ>\frac{24}{35}.
\tag{33}
\]

We next bound the positive coefficient \(\mathcal C\).
Put \(m=tA\) again. Since \(c<1/2\), we have
\(C^2=1+2cs\le1+\sqrt3/2<15/8\), so
\(K_0^2-C^2>3241/1600>2\). Therefore

\[

The last inequality follows from \(c^2\le1/4\).
Moreover \(D>(\sqrt3-1)/2>7/20\), \(A>59/64\),

\[
                       m>\frac7{20}\frac{59}{133}

Equations (34)--(35) imply

\[

Using \(0<x\le L\), \(0\le t<1\), and (36),

\[
 \mathcal C\le L^3+tL^2+(1-t)L
                 \le L^3+L<\frac3{2\sqrt2}<\frac{15}{14}.
\tag{37}
\]

Finally (13), \(C\ge1\), and \(A<C\) give

\[
                         1-\frac CA> -\frac5{59}.
\tag{38}

The negative part of the bracket in (26) is therefore greater

\[

Consequently the whole bracket is strictly greater than

\[
