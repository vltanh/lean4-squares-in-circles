## 2. A high-radius boundary-origin S root permits only one other closed-origin unit

This section is force-free except for the genuine-leaf classification.
Suppose d=1+epsilon>1 and X is a genuine S-leaf root with O on its
boundary. Let Y be a distinct actual genuine S-leaf root in the same
packing. The actual inward unit segments from their real leaf ports
give

    epsilon e_X in X, epsilon e_Y in Y.                  (1)

Both actual leaf directions are retained. No contact between X,Y is
assumed, and their true degrees and all remaining forces are arbitrary.

### 2.1. The exact boundary-root rectangle

An aligned X would have own interval [d-1,d] along e_X, which excludes
O because epsilon>0. Thus e_X has two strictly positive components
a,b in suitable inward own axes u,v. Its actual outward radial port
is the exposed vertex d e_X, and its two own intervals are

    [d a-1,d a], [d b-1,d b],
    a,b>0, a^2+b^2=1.

Closed origin containment and boundary occurrence imply that one of
d a,d b is exactly one. They cannot both equal one because d<61/60
whereas that would give d=sqrt(2). Exchange the axes so d a=1, and put

    beta=d b=sqrt(d^2-1).

The entire exact chart is

    X=[0,1]u+[beta-1,beta]v,
    e_X=(u+beta v)/d,
    0<epsilon<1/60,
    epsilon<beta<11/60,
    1-beta>49/60>epsilon.                              (2)

Indeed beta^2-epsilon^2=2epsilon>0, and
(61/60)^2-1=121/3600. Thus O is in the relative interior of one
actual X side, never at a vertex. These are strict conclusions at
d>1; they are not extended to d=1.

### 2.2. The full three-origin-unit tangent-cone cover

Suppose two further distinct actual units E,F both contain O closed.
X has the open right half-plane as its local interior cone in (2).
Every interior cone of E or F must therefore lie in the closed left
half-plane. A square's cone at an interior, side, or vertex point has
angle respectively 2pi, pi, or pi/2. The disjoint interiors of E,F
inside a half-plane of total angle pi force each to have its vertex
at O and angle exactly pi/2.

Their two open cones are disjoint and together exhaust that left
half-plane. Hence their own axes are the u,v axes and, after swapping
E,F, the actual units are exactly

    E=[-1,0]u+[0,1]v,
    F=[-1,0]u+[-1,0]v.                                (3)

There is no angle or side-length approximation in this step. A rotated
ninety-degree cone would leave a gap and force overlap with the other
ninety-degree cone inside the same half-plane.

By (2)--(3), X union E union F contains the closed disk D_rho for
every rho with

    epsilon<rho<min(beta,1-beta,1).

In particular the whole D_epsilon is contained in the open interior
of that union. Every point of that disk has an actual neighborhood
covered by these three closed squares.

Y cannot be E or F: a genuine S-leaf root containing O closed at
d>1 and not openly must have the side-interior geometry (2), whereas
E and F have an origin vertex. It cannot contain O openly because
it is distinct from X. Thus Y is a fourth actual unit, and (1) gives
a point of Y inside D_epsilon and hence inside the open union above.

This is impossible for an interior-disjoint actual Y. Its open
interior is dense in its closed square, so it has an interior point
in that open union. An open portion of int Y cannot be contained in
the finitely many boundary segments of X,E,F. It therefore overlaps
one of their open interiors.

This contradiction proves that at most two actual units contain O
closed whenever X has the geometry (2) and the second actual genuine
root Y is present.
