## 2. Every sufficiently small aligned cross covers the original origin

orthonormal frame about O. Reflect the two axes, and relabel the

    (x+1,y), (x,y+1), (x-1,y), (x,y-1).                 (3)

    0<=x<3/5,             0<=y<3/5.                    (4)

greater than 2^2+1^2=5>Q. This is impossible. Its reflection in the
diagonal excludes y>1/2 and x>=1/2.

It follows that O belongs to at least one of the five actual units:

- If x,y<=1/2, then O belongs to the closed central T.
- If x>1/2, then y<1/2 and 1/2<x<3/5<3/2. Therefore
  O lies in the open left ring unit, whose center is (x-1,y).
- If y>1/2, the same argument places O in the open bottom ring unit.

All equality cases x=1/2 or y=1/2 remain in the first case; no
boundary point of a square is discarded.

int H. Indeed its interior points approach O, and int H contains a
neighborhood of O. Thus any cross unit containing O must be the very

Consequently in every surviving contrary cross either

This uses identity of actual physical squares forced by nonoverlap,
