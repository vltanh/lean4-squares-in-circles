## 2. Two elementary complete support facts

### 2.1. A minimum support has backward span strictly below one

Let Y be an actual unit, P a minimum-n support point, and e a unit vector
with n.e<0. Then max_Y e.(Z-P)<1.

At a vertex P, use its two actual inward orthonormal axes i,j. Both have
nonnegative n projection, so at least one e projection is negative. The
only positive contribution is at most one, and equality would force e to
equal an inward axis, contradicting n.e<0. At a side-interior P the inward
normal segment has negative e projection; only the remaining part of the
actual tangent interval can contribute, with projection strictly below
one. Side endpoints are already covered. All original means remain.

Consequently the original strict NE support at P=(h+1,V) implies

    every actual Z in T has Z_u>h.              (3)

This is the existing sharp support-span fact, reproduced with its full
scope. The point P is not assumed a vertex of T.

### 2.2. A positive-quadrant support point gives a radial lower bound

Let an actual contained unit have a minimum-n support point (X,V), where
X>V>0 and n has both components positive. Then

    R^2 > X^2+(V+1)^2.                          (4)

At a support vertex, at least one of the two inward orthonormal axes w
has both coordinates nonnegative. If an inward axis is Northwest or
Southeast, its permitted perpendicular is Northeast; Southwest is forbidden
by n. Since w_u+w_v>=1, the actual point P+w has squared norm at least
X^2+(V+1)^2. The inequality is strict if w_u>0 since X>V. If w is North,
the other permitted inward axis is East and gives a still larger norm.
At a side-interior P the actual inward normal n/|n| is strictly Northeast;
P+n/|n| belongs to the opposite side and gives the same strict comparison.
This covers every actual T owner and side endpoint, without a circle-mask
or load premise.

## 6. Acute support directions expose one actual radial point of T

**Acute-support side lemma.** If two nonzero minimum-support directions
of an actual unit have positive dot product, any two distinct points in
their respective support faces lie on one actual side of the unit.

In actual own axes, two strict directions could expose opposite vertices
only if both coordinate signs were opposite, which gives a negative dot
product. Otherwise their exposed vertices are equal or adjacent. If one
direction is an own axis, positive dot product places the other support
at a vertex of that same support side, or on the same side if parallel.
If both are own axes, positive dot product makes them the same directed
axis. This proves the statement including every original side mean and
endpoint. It does not replace either selected support direction.

    F-P=(-Lx,Ly),
    Lx=1+p*lambda-r*A>0,
