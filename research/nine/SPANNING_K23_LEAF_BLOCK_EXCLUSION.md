## 1. A genuine leaf makes its root exterior to every retained subgraph

PHYSICAL_BODY_GRAPH_UNCROSSING.md, Sections 5--6, supplies arbitrarily
small physical plane embeddings with every physically circle-touching
vertex incident to the unbounded face. The construction preserves
center-to-circle-corner spokes while routing the actual contact
junctions. This holds regardless of a body's circle multiplier.

Let L be a genuine positive leaf with root H. Its sole positive
edge HL is a bridge and L is circle-loaded. The radial-leaf theorem
therefore makes L physically boundary. A vertex of degree one
has just one incident face: the two local sides of its only edge
belong to that face. Since L is exterior, that face is unbounded.
The whole edge HL, and therefore its endpoint H, is incident to
the same unbounded face.

Removing edges and vertices cannot take a surviving vertex off
the unbounded face. An access curve from an outside sector to
infinity in the original complement still avoids the smaller
graph. Thus H is exterior to any subgraph that retains H,
including the K2,3 in the theorem. The same inheritance holds
for every physically circle-touching vertex of that subgraph.

This is not an inference that an arbitrary neighbor of an
exterior vertex is exterior. The actual degree-one leaf edge
is essential to the argument. No force or physical obstacle
is removed; restriction to a subgraph is only a topological
proof step.

## 2. An enclosed unit forces the exact aligned four-ring cross

**Lemma 2.1.** If another physical unit square has its center on
the bounded side of an embedded positive four-cycle, all five
physical squares are an aligned cross. The enclosed unit is
the central unit, and the four ring units share its four full
sides in their cyclic order. This statement has no force-degree,
circle-contact or radius premise.

Here is the full equality and weak-junction argument. Keep the
original ring applications p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3, with their original
means, and write ell_i=|p_i-p_(i-1)|<=sqrt(2). The embedded edge
homotopies lie in arbitrarily small neighborhoods of the actual
ring squares. If the fifth unit T has its center inside the
embedded ring, these neighborhoods can be chosen to avoid a
smaller concentric square in T. The actual contact polygon has
winding one at T's center. Its winding is then constant on
T's connected open interior, which is disjoint from all closed
ring squares and hence from every contact-polygon edge.

If opposite applications coincide, the contact polygon is two
retraced segments and has zero winding off them. If consecutive
applications coincide, it is a triangle with side lengths at
most sqrt(2) and area at most sqrt(3)/2<1. Further coincidences
only degenerate these cases. None can contain the full open
unit square T with winding one. Thus the hypothetical enclosed
unit forces all four applications distinct.

The actual center/contact loop is now Jordan. Its homotopy to
the contact polygon has winding only zero or one, as in the
full ring-area source. If A_free denotes its free winding-one
area, the exact inequalities are

    1 <= A_free
       <= A(P) - (1/2) sum sqrt(max(ell_i^2-1,0))
       <= sum [ell_i^2/4 - (1/2)sqrt(max(ell_i^2-1,0))]
       <= 1.                                             (1)

The first inequality uses T's full physical unit area.
For 0<=x<=1 the scalar summand is x/4<=1/4, with equality
only at x=1. For 1<x<=2, set u=sqrt(x-1) in (0,1]; then

    x/4 - sqrt(x-1)/2 = 1/4 + u(u-2)/4 < 1/4.

Hence equality in (1) forces every ell_i=1 and A(P)=1.
The equality case of the exact four-vertex polygon inequality
forces P to be a positively oriented regular unit square:
in its finite Fourier proof only the first positive mode has
the maximal area-to-edge-energy ratio. This is the existing
exact equality statement, not a numerical shape approximation.

No positive area of the ring squares lies inside P. Each of
their unit-length contact chords therefore lies on its boundary

would leave a positive occupied piece inside P. Each ring square
occupies the exterior side of that side of P. They are precisely
its four exterior unit neighbors. T is contained in P, has
the same area and is closed; hence T=P. All five physical
squares have one common frame.

This proves Lemma 2.1 including every coincident-contact limit,

## 3. Two boundary ring units already give a sharp cross restriction

Write (x,y) for the actual central unit's center in the forced
common orthonormal frame about the unchanged circle origin.
The four ring centers are

    (x+1,y), (x,y+1), (x-1,y), (x,y-1).

For a unit centered at (a,b) in this frame, its maximal squared
corner norm is

    M(a,b)=(|a|+1/2)^2+(|b|+1/2)^2.                     (2)

Containment gives M<=R^2; physical circle contact gives equality.
No multiplier equation is involved.

### 3.1. An opposite boundary pair forces R^2=5/2

Suppose the right and left ring units are physically boundary.
Their equal maxima in (2) give |x+1|=|x-1|, hence x=0.
Their common squared radius is

    R^2=9/4+(|y|+1/2)^2.

The top or bottom ring unit farther in the sign of y has maximum

    1/4+(|y|+3/2)^2 = R^2+2|y|.

It is contained, so y=0. Therefore R^2=5/2. The same argument
after a quarter-turn covers the other opposite pair.

Since (79/40)^2-5/2=2241/1600>0, an opposite boundary pair is
impossible in the declared window. In particular three or four
boundary ring units are impossible, since they include an
opposite pair. This strengthens the older three-boundary cross
comparison using only containment of the other two units.

### 3.2. An adjacent boundary pair puts the other ring units far from zero

If at least two ring units are physically boundary and no
opposite pair is present, there are exactly two, and they are
adjacent. Rotate and reflect so they are the right and top units.

Compare the boundary right unit with the contained left unit
in (2). This gives |x+1|>=|x-1| and hence x>=0. Similarly
the top/bottom comparison gives y>=0. Equality of the right
and top maxima is now

    (x+3/2)^2+(y+1/2)^2
      =(x+1/2)^2+(y+3/2)^2,

so x=y=t>=0. The common radius is exactly

    R^2=2t^2+4t+5/2.                                   (3)

The right side increases for t>=0. Its value at t=2/5 is
221/50, and

    221/50-Q=663/6400>0.

Thus t<2/5 throughout the original upper radius bound.
The left ring unit has every point with first coordinate at
most t-1/2<-1/10. The bottom ring unit has every point with
second coordinate at most t-1/2<-1/10. Consequently each
of the two nonboundary ring units has every point at distance
strictly greater than 1/10 from the circle origin. All original
square orientations and contacts have already been included
by the forced common-frame conclusion of Lemma 2.1.
