## 2. A continuous two-near-unit half-plane lemma

**Lemma 2.1.** Let two actual interior-disjoint unit squares X,Y each
meet D_epsilon, where 0<epsilon<1/60. Suppose

    X subset {n_X.x<=epsilon},
    Y subset {n_Y.x<=epsilon},

for actual unit directions whose minor angle theta satisfies
0<=theta<=1/7. Then each square has an actual vertex in the closed
disk D_(5epsilon).

Put r=5epsilon and eta=asin(1/5). Since r+epsilon=6epsilon<1/10,
one may choose an actual near point z of either square and two signed
perpendicular own axes with at least 1/2 available length from z. The
rectangle `z+[0,1/2]u+[0,1/2]v` lies in that same actual square.
On |x|=r, the inequalities x.u>epsilon and x.v>epsilon therefore
give an open-interior arc of length

    pi/2-2eta.                                           (2.1)

Both actual coordinate increments from z are positive and less than
r+epsilon<1/2. No origin-location or corner assumption is used.

Suppose one of the squares had every vertex strictly outside D_r.
Its actual occupied angular measure on this circle is at least

    pi-2eta.                                             (2.2)

Here is the full one-square proof. If O is outside the square, its
nearest point cannot be a vertex. In an own frame it is
`[a,a+1] x [b,b+1]`, with 0<a<=epsilon and b<0<b+1.
Both near-face endpoints are outside D_r, so the full open arc x>a
lies in its open interior. Its length is
2acos(a/r)>=2acos(1/5)=pi-2eta. If O lies on a side, it is a
relative-interior point and the same proof gives an open semicircle.
An origin at a vertex is incompatible with the assumed absence of
vertices in D_r.

If O is interior, diameter 2r<1 allows at most two faces to cut the
circle, and they must be adjacent. With two such faces at distances
a,b, their common actual vertex has a^2+b^2>r^2. Hence
acos(a/r)+acos(b/r)<pi/2, so the removed outside arcs have total
measure below pi. A single cutting face also removes less than pi,
and with no cutting face the full circle remains. Tangencies remove
zero measure. Every case gives (2.2).

The allowed circle set of either printed half-plane is an arc of length
pi+2eta centered opposite its normal. Since theta<=1/7, the union
of these two allowed arcs has measure at most

    pi+2eta+theta.                                       (2.3)

For example their complementary forbidden arcs have length pi-2eta
and their intersection has length pi-2eta-theta>0, which gives
(2.3) without any choice of angular cut.

The actual open interiors are disjoint. Equations (2.1)--(2.2) would
therefore require occupied measure at least 3pi/2-4eta inside (2.3).
This is impossible, since

    eta<5/24,
    6eta+theta<5/4+1/7=39/28<3/2<pi/2.                   (2.4)

For the first strict inequality, the derivative of asin on [0,1/5]
is strictly below 25/24: its smallest denominator sqrt(24)/5 exceeds
24/25, with squared reserve 24/625. Integrating gives eta<5/24.
Thus (2.4) is an exact bound on the whole angle interval.

The assumption that all vertices of one square lie outside the closed
disk is contradicted. Interchanging X,Y proves the lemma for both.
Equality at the asserted radius 5epsilon is included; neither a pose
perturbation nor a strict-vertex-radius hypothesis is imposed.

In the original six-body-block/three-genuine-leaf parent, suppose the

genuine roots. The accepted full-network cover [N] gives exactly two
root terminals, renamed A,C. The remaining genuine root B has no

For clarity, the exact connected private graph is either a path x-y-z
or a triangle xyz. In the path, each endpoint has a nonempty attachment
set in {A,C}, the middle has any attachment set, and the total union is
{A,C}. In the triangle, at least two distinct private vertices have
nonempty attachment sets, and the union is {A,C}. These are exactly
the biconnectivity conditions: each path-end remnant must retain an
attachment after the middle is removed; a triangle cannot have every
attachment concentrated at one vertex. Conversely these conditions
keep the root path and the remaining private vertices connected after
any one vertex is deleted. No optional terminal edge is dropped.

**Complete all-private-boundary exclusion.** Suppose all three private
units physically touch the circle. Theorem 1 makes each terminal A,C
incident to at most one private body, since its own genuine leaf is
already its other boundary neighbor. Each terminal has at least one
private incidence by [N], so it has exactly one.

If AC is absent, the exact full root degrees are now A3,B3,C3. Their
two actual private neighbors are physical boundary returns, of whatever
full private degrees the original graph gives them. The complete chain
theorem [CH], Theorem 1, excludes the whole family without needing a
joining edge between these returns.

If AC is present, the actual roots form the full root triangle with
middle B3. Every actual external neighbor of A or C is physically
boundary. The complete all-boundary-return alternative of [RT] applies
at their full original degrees and excludes the entire family. No root
triangle equation is used in the absent-AC alternative.

Thus every remaining connected-private partition3 configuration contains
at least one physically circle-clear private body. This is a full
physical-boundary mask conclusion, not merely an assertion that a
selected circle coefficient vanishes.

There is a second literal join. In any such connected-private graph
where each terminal has at least one positive physical-boundary private
neighbor, Theorem 2 excludes the entire nonparallel-middle feature,
regardless of any additional clear private neighbors or optional AC.
The original parallel-middle cases with clear private bodies, and cases
