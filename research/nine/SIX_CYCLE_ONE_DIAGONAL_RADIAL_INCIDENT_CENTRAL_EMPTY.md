# The whole central region is empty when the sole radial load is incident to the diagonal

**Status: complete continuous-domain hand argument; root and separate independent full source, geometry, topology and exact audits passed.**

Let the actual alternating positive component be \(B-W-C-U_C-T-U_B-B\), in \(79/40\le R\le\sqrt{1105}/16\). W is the sole diagonal transmitter. B is physically radial; C,T are physically unique. Every original circle weight, true pair force, actual side mean and all three extras remain.

**Theorem.** The entire central free union contains no extra unit, across the complete C normal fan and every T interface type. No radial concurrence is asserted to belong to its square.

## 1. The complete incident-radial endpoint domain

The physical diagonal frame, avoidance convention and positive channel height give
\[
 B=[-K,1-K]\times[-1/2,1/2],\quad C=[c-1,c]\times[e-1,e],\quad K=\sqrt{R^2-1/4},\quad d=K-1,
\]
\[
 c=2+\sqrt2-K,\quad c^2+e^2=K^2+1/4,\quad0<h<1/2,\quad e-1<h<e. \tag{1}
\]
The C endpoint is physically unique. Its strict diagonal mean gives e<3/2. Since \(e^2=2(2+\sqrt2)K-(2+\sqrt2)^2+1/4\), increasing in K and equal to9/4 at K=2, one has
\[
 91/100<d<1,\quad \sqrt2<c<\sqrt2+9/100<31/20,\quad5/4<e<3/2. \tag{2}
\]
These are the same direct endpoint estimates proved in SIX_CYCLE_ONE_DIAGONAL_TWO_RADIAL_EXCLUSION.md, Section2; they do not require T radial.

The radial width-one support cover forces B's other application to a strict inner vertex. The normalized original circle resultant is \((-K,\omega)\), with \(|\omega|\le1/2\). Its proper concurrence is a positive multiple of this vector and lies on y=h, so \(\omega>0\). The exact B force equation then makes its other normal downward, fixing
\[
 p_B=(-d,-1/2),\quad n_B=(a,-s),\quad a=\cos\alpha,\ s=\sin\alpha,\quad0<\alpha<\pi/2,\quad P=p_B+n_B. \tag{3}
\]
Here P is the unchanged application on T.

The other C normal is downward as well. If its horizontal component were positive, C would use its lower-right vertex and the T application Q would have Q_x>c. But
\[
 Q_x-P_x>c+d-a=1+\sqrt2-a>\sqrt2,
\]
impossible for two points of a unit T. Thus the complete remaining C fan consists of an inner lower vertex with \(n_C=(-b,-r)\), \(b=\cos\beta,r=\sin\beta\), \(\pi/4<\beta<\pi/2\), and \(Q=(c-1-b,e-1-r)\); or its bottom-side axis \(n_C=(0,-1)\), with its original mean \(p_C=(c-1+\zeta,e-1)\), \(0\le\zeta\le1\), and \(Q=(c-1+\zeta,e-2)\).

In the first case the proper unique C concurrence and original positive W force give
\[
 Y_C=h+1-e=\frac{c-e}{c-e\cot\beta},\qquad c-e\cot\beta>0.
\]
Thus c>e and \(\beta>\pi/4\), directly, without a radial turn bound.

## 2. A uniform actual diagonal height and a true B-weight bound

The unique C concurrence lies in C, so in both fan cases \(h\ge g(c):=e(1-1/c)\). Put \(L=2+\sqrt2\); then \(e^2=L^2+1/4-2Lc\). The sign of g'(c) is the sign of \(L^2+1/4-Lc(c+1)\), which strictly decreases with c. Consequently g has no interior minimum on \([\sqrt2,\sqrt2+9/100]\).

At c=sqrt2, e=3/2 and \(g(c)=(3/2)(1-1/\sqrt2)>7/16>127/300\), using sqrt2/2<17/24. At \(c_0=\sqrt2+9/100>3/2\),
\[
 e_0^2=189/100-(9/50)\sqrt2>327/200>(127/100)^2,
\]
where the last margin is221/10000. Therefore \(g(c_0)>127/300\). The complete interval satisfies
\[
 h>127/300>21/50. \tag{4}
\]

Put k=cot(alpha). The actual B force lines meet at \(q_B=(-d-k(h+1/2),h)\). No containment of q_B in B is used. Its positive relation to the original resultant \((-K,\omega)\) gives
\[
 \omega=\frac{Kh}{d+k(h+1/2)}\le1/2,
\]
and hence
\[
 k\ge\frac{2Kh-d}{h+1/2}>\frac{4h-1}{h+1/2}>\frac{17}{23}. \tag{5}
\]
The middle comparison uses d<1 and h<1/2; the last uses(4). In particular
\[
 s<81/100,\qquad a>59/100. \tag{6}
\]
Both rational comparisons follow by squaring \(s<23/\sqrt{818}\) and \(a>17/\sqrt{818}\). Every original individual B circle weight remains unchanged.

In the C axis case the original vertical force line and unique concurrence give \(Q_x=ch/e<c/(2e)<31/50\). Thus
\[
 0\le\zeta=Q_x-(c-1)<81/50-\sqrt2<1-\sigma,\qquad\sigma=1/\sqrt2. \tag{7}
\]
## 3. A complete steep-normal extension of the one-sided sandwich

Use the one-sided sandwich lemma in the radial-opposite axis theorem: if E avoids \(W_0=\{|x|+|y|\le\sigma\}\) and \(E\subset[-1,\sigma]\times(-\infty,0]\), then for a unit \(N=(u,v)\), \(0\le u\le v\),
\[
 -\min_E N\cdot p\ge\min\bigl((1-\sigma)u+(1+\sigma)v,\ 1+\sigma v\bigr). \tag{8}
\]
For the complementary whole sector \(u\ge v\ge0\), one has
\[
 -\min_E N\cdot p\ge(1-\sigma)u+\sqrt2\,v. \tag{9}
\]

Here is a full proof, including endpoints. Write E's axes \((A,B),(-B,A)\), A,B>=0,A²+B²=1, and w=A+B. Let \(D_E(n)=-\min_E n\cdot p\), homogeneously for nonunit n. The horizontal strip alone gives \(D_E(1,0)\ge w-\sigma\). Equation (8) at the diagonal gives \(D_E(1,1)\ge2\).

For u>=v, the support-halfwidth identity gives
\[
 D_E(u,v)=(u-v)D_E(1,0)+vD_E(1,1)-m,
\]
where m=0 if A<=B, and otherwise \(m=\min((u-v)B,\ v(A-B))\). Consequently
\[
 D_E(u,v)\ge L_0+(u-v)(w-1)-m,\quad L_0=(1-\sigma)u+(1+\sigma)v.
\]

For A>=B,
\[
 m-(u-v)(w-1)\le(u-v)(1-u).
\]
If A>=u (equivalently B<=v), the left side is \((u-v)(1-A)\). If A<=u, write A=cos(theta),B=sin(theta), with theta in [atan(v/u),pi/4]. The left side is \(u-v+(2v-u)A-uB\), whose derivative is \(u(B-A)-2vB\le0\); its initial value is exactly \((u-v)(1-u)\). If A<=B the same bound follows from m=0 and w>=1. Finally
\[
 (u-v)(1-u)=v\frac{(u-v)v}{1+u}
 \le v\frac{uv}{1+u}\le\frac{v}{2(1+\sigma)}=(1-\sigma)v.
\]
Here u>=sigma and uv<=1/2; v=0 is immediate. Subtracting this from L_0 proves (9). There is no unexamined angle sector.

## 4. The actual contact chord bounds the whole central region

Translate the actual W center \((\sigma-d,h)\) to zero. Then
\[
 P=(a-\sigma,-h-1/2-s). \tag{10}
\]
In the nonaxis C case, \(Q=(\sigma-b,-Y_C-r)\); all six actual contact vertices lie in \([-\sigma,\sigma]\times(-\infty,0]\). In the C axis case, \(Q=(\sigma+\zeta,-Y_C-1)\); the C bottom mean is \((\sigma+\zeta,-Y_C)\). By (7), all six contact vertices lie in \([-\sigma,1]\times(-\infty,0]\).

The actual unique T concurrence obeys the proper rays
\[
 q_T=P+\lambda n_B=Q+\mu n_C,\qquad\lambda,\mu\ge0. \tag{11}
\]
Thus in the nonaxis case \(\Delta_x:=(Q-P)_x=\lambda a+\mu b\ge0\). If Delta_x=0 then P=Q. In that case all contact vertices are above the horizontal line through P, and necessarily alpha<pi/4 (the estimate below excludes a nonpositive vertical difference when alpha>=pi/4). The horizontal sandwich gives a contradiction immediately, because \(h+1/2+s<1+\sigma\). So suppose P!=Q; then Delta_x>0.

In the C axis case Delta_x=sqrt2+zeta-a>0 directly. Here the force turn at unique T is alpha+pi/2>pi/2. The complete unique-square feature cover in SIX_CYCLE_ONE_DIAGONAL_INNER_CAPACITY.md, Section 1, puts the two T applications on one inner side and gives alpha<=pi/4. Its interior is below the directed side P to Q: the inward normal has nonnegative scalar product with both n_B and the downward n_C; its vertical component must be negative because a horizontal inward normal would make this side vertical, contrary to Delta_x>0. The right application has outward normal (0,1), so this directed inner side cannot slope downward. Hence Delta_y>=0. This invokes only the unique T geometry, never a radial bound.

Let the unit normal above P-Q be \(N=(-\Delta_y,\Delta_x)/|Q-P|\). Both pre-transmitter endpoint applications are above this line: by (11) their signed cross products are respectively \(\mu\sin(\alpha+\beta)\) and \(\lambda\sin(\alpha+\beta)\), with beta=pi/2 in the axis case. The two diagonal applications have larger projections by their positive vertical offsets. For the C axis mean there is also a left displacement -zeta; when Delta_y>=0 it increases the N projection. Therefore the entire contact polygon lies above P-Q.

The full compact-interior winding argument of CONTACT_RING_HOLE_AREA.md and SIMPLE_CYCLE_CHANNEL_POCKET_CAPS.md puts every whole central extra in the displayed strip, below y=0, and above this actual chord line, while avoiding W_0. This remains true for the entire weak central free union, all shared junctions and all original means.

## 5. Every actual chord-normal sector contradicts the sandwich

First suppose Delta_y>=0 and write N=(-u,v), u>=0,v>0,u²+v²=1. The actual chord depth relative to W is
\[
 D=u(a-\sigma)+v(h+1/2+s). \tag{12}
\]
Reflect horizontally to use (8)--(9). If u>=v, then
\[
 \begin{split}
 D-[(1-\sigma)u+\sqrt2 v]
 &=u(a-1)+v(h+1/2+s-\sqrt2)\\
 &<u(a-1)+v(1+s-\sqrt2)\\
 &\le v(a+s-\sqrt2)\le0.
 \end{split}
\]
This contradicts (9), for every alpha.

It remains u<=v. If alpha<=pi/4, then a>=sigma,s<=sigma. One has
\[
 D-L_0=u(a-1)+v(h-1/2+s-\sigma)<0.
\]
Also
\[
 D-\sigma v<u(a-\sigma)+v(1+s-\sigma)\le1.
\]
The last inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz: the vector \((a-\sigma,1+s-\sigma)\) has norm at most 1. Its squared norm is increasing with alpha on [0,pi/4], and equals 1 at pi/4. Hence D<L_1 as well, contradicting (8).

In the remaining alpha>pi/4 case, C is nonaxis, and
\[
 \Delta_y=e-1/2+s-r>\sigma-1/4>9/20,\qquad
 \Delta_x=\sqrt2-a-b<17/12-59/100=62/75.
\]
Thus u/v>1/2 and v<2/sqrt5. Using (6), a<=sigma, s<81/100 and sigma>7/10 gives
\[
 \frac{D-L_0}{v}<\frac12(a-1)+s-\sigma
 \le s-\frac12-\frac{\sigma}{2}<-1/25<0.
\]
Furthermore
\[
 D-\sigma v<v(1+s-\sigma)<\frac2{\sqrt5}\frac{111}{100}<1,
\]
the last exact comparison being222²<5*100². Again D<L0,L1, contradicting (8).

Finally suppose Delta_y<0. Only the nonaxis C case remains. By (11), Q-P is a nonnegative combination of the directions n_B and -n_C, so its downward tilt has magnitude at most alpha. The preceding positive Delta_y estimate excludes alpha>=pi/4. Thus write N=(u,v), with 0<u<=v. Now
\[
 D=u(\sigma-a)+v(h+1/2+s).
\]
Since a>=sigma and s<=sigma,
\[
 D-L_0=u(2\sigma-1-a)+v(h-1/2+s-\sigma)<0,
\]
and
\[
 D-L_1<u(\sigma-a)+v(1+s-\sigma)-1\le0.
\]
This contradicts (8) in the last sector.

All actual T chord possibilities, including a coincident-application limit, have been covered. No central completion unit exists.

This is a complete central-region theorem for the sole-radial-incident family. It does not yet assert either exterior completion capacity or the whole family exclusion. The diagonal-pocket and C--T side-pocket obligations retain their original physical domains.
