    -nu*det(P_D,n_D)-gamma*det(W,g)+det(P_C,F_DC)=0.  (2)

Each circle atom at V has zero origin moment, which is why the V

**Theorem A.** The five original applications

    P_A, P_D, W, P_B, P_C

are pairwise distinct. In particular both full unloaded hub stars have

**Theorem B.** In the existing common certificate with the accepted

free positive normal at either hub forces at least one of its other

Theorem B concerns physical intersections, not the number of points
used to represent an aggregate mean. An endpoint-valued mean on a

## 5. Two strict V corners forbid either triangle-application coincidence

In the two-strict-corner row, A minimizes the original direction
n_A=(-c,s) at P_A. Every actual point z in A therefore satisfies
n_A.z>=n_A.P_A. But

    n_A.(P_D-P_A)=-s<0,

so P_D is not in A. Since the actual AD mean W belongs to A, W
cannot equal P_D. Similarly D minimizes n_D=(-C,-S) at P_D, while

    n_D.(P_A-P_D)=-S<0.

Hence P_A is not in D and W cannot equal P_A. This uses the original
support directions and actual point membership only. It does not
require either roof contact to be free or hub-owned, and does not
assume that AD is a singleton.

Both triangle coincidences are therefore impossible on the entire
row (6), including both circle-weight endpoints and any shared AD mean.

### 6.1. The actual West hub is forced to its aligned endpoint

D lies in x<=d by its original West contact with V. It contains both
P_D and P, which are distinct points on x=d. Therefore its East
support is a full actual side and its actual frame is aligned:

    D=[d-1,d] x [h-1,h],
    h>=1/2, h-1<=-t<0.

If h>1/2, P is in the relative interior of D's East side. Its only
minimum-support normal is then West, so the original A-to-D normal

    -n_A=(c,-s).

Their dot product is -c<0. At a unit-square support point any two
outward normals belong to a cone of angle at most pi/2, so their dot
product is nonnegative. These two actual A normals cannot coexist.
Consequently h=1/2, giving the whole actual unit

    D=[d-1,d] x [-1/2,1/2].                            (8)

All possible endpoint values of P_D remain in (8). In particular no
West mean was reset to a midpoint or made into a singleton.

### 6.2. The actual A corner must open into the upper half-plane

At P, the units D and V occupy respectively the Southwest and Southeast
right-angle tangent sectors. Together their open interiors occupy every
sufficiently near lower-half-plane point except their common dividing
line. The disjoint actual A can have no inward tangent direction with
negative vertical component: an open interior perturbation of any such
direction would enter D or V.

Since P is D's upper-right vertex, its minimum-support normal g obeys

    g_x<=0, g_y<=0.

It is linearly independent of (c,-s). A positive parallel multiple
would have positive x component, and a negative parallel multiple
would have positive y component. Both are impossible, including the
axis limits g=West or South. Hence the two original A triangle
normals at P are independent, so P must be an actual A vertex.

Write its actual inward orthonormal edge directions as u,v. Then

    A=P+[0,1]*u+[0,1]*v,
    u_y,v_y>=0, |u|=|v|=1, u.v=0.                    (9)

The two original positive triangle forces at P lie in A's outward
normal cone there. They are independent. Their positive sum is
therefore strictly inside that cone: in the basis -u,-v both summed
coefficients are strictly positive, since otherwise the two rays would
be parallel. Full A3 force balance in (2) makes F_AB strictly inside
the opposite cone spanned by u,v.

Thus A's actual third support is the unique opposite vertex

    P_B=P+u+v.

In particular the physical AB contact is a singleton here, regardless
of which unit owns its original normal. This is forced by actual strict
support, without any generic-normal rule.

### 6.3. Full torque forces a diagonal whose actual support is too large

Take the complete A torque about P. Both original triangle applications
are exactly P by (7), so their moments vanish. The original third
force remains and gives

    det(u+v,F_AB)=0.

Because F_AB has strictly positive u and v components, it is a positive
multiple of u+v. Its original unit normal is therefore

    f=(u+v)/sqrt(2).

The nonnegative vertical components in (9) satisfy
u_y^2+v_y^2=1 and u_y+v_y>=1. Consequently

    f_y>=1/sqrt(2), |f_x|<=1/sqrt(2).

Using d>0 and P=(d,1/2), the actual A support toward B obeys

    h_A(f)=P.f+sqrt(2)
          =d*f_x+(1/2)*f_y+sqrt(2)
          >=(5/2-d)/sqrt(2)>d.                       (10)

The last strict inequality is precisely the bound on d in (1). But
the actual contained B lies beyond this unchanged original support,
so (4) requires h_A(f)<=d. This contradiction excludes (7).

The proof retains the entire original AD physical contact. If W was
an endpoint-valued mean on a positive-length AD side, its equality
to P still gives exactly the torque used above. If instead the same
physical contact contains P while its original mean W is elsewhere,
no equality (7) has been assumed and that geometry is not excluded
by this argument. Distinct applications do not imply that all physical
intersection sets or all physical junctions are disjoint.

Sections 4--6 prove all five pairwise distinctions and Theorem A.
The reflected West/lower row is a whole-data reflection, so neither
individual-zero physical circle endpoint is omitted.
