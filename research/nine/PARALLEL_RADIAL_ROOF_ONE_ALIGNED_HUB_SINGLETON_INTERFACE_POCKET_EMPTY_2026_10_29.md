## 2. Every original A--D singleton is an A-owned transverse axis

Let n be the original A-to-D unit normal. A has horizontal extent
[a,d]. If n_x<0, its original application q lies on x=a. The strict
unit support-span lemma at the actual minimum-n support point q of D,
in direction East, would give

    max_D x<q_x+1=a+1=d,

contradicting D's actual roof contact and max_D x=d. Hence n_x>=0.

Suppose n_x>0. Then q lies on A's maximum-East face x=d. It is also a
maximum-East point of the actual D, since max_D x=d. The following
elementary rectangular support fact gives a contradiction.

If one point p of a unit square K minimizes n.z and maximizes e.z,
then n.e<=0. In K's own orthonormal coordinates, a positive e-coordinate
forces the corresponding p-coordinate to be its upper endpoint, so the
corresponding n-coordinate is nonpositive. A negative e-coordinate
forces the lower endpoint and a nonnegative n-coordinate. Zero
e-coordinates contribute zero. Every coordinate product n_i*e_i is
therefore nonpositive, proving the assertion for all faces and endpoints.

Apply this fact to K=D, p=q and e=East. It gives n_x<=0, contrary to
n_x>0. Therefore n_x=0 exactly.

Reflect the whole physical data in the horizontal axis if necessary so
n=North and D lies above A's top support line y=h. Since the actual
contact is a singleton and the retained normal is an A side axis, q is
in the relative interior of that A side. If q were an A vertex and a
D vertex, the strict singleton convention would forbid this A-axis
normal. If q were an A vertex and a relative D side-interior point,
both actual supporting sides would be horizontal and would share a
positive interval near q. That would contradict the physical singleton
hypothesis. Thus all endpoint alternatives are covered.

D cannot have an aligned horizontal minimum side: its frame would
then be the same as A's. Its maximum East support d would give the
same horizontal interval [a,d], and contact above A at y=h would be
their full positive shared side. Hence D is strictly tilted. Its
minimum-North support q is a unique vertex with inward axes

    w=(l,s), u=(-s,l), l,s>0, l^2+s^2=1.

Its unique maximum-East vertex is q+w. The actual roof support gives
q_x+l=d, proving the full D chart in (3). In particular 0<l<1 and
q_x=d-l lies in (a,d), as also required by the original relative-side
contact. No independent frame is rotated or selected in this argument.

## 4. Full opposite-hub torque forces the strict sign h<0

Assume h>=0. Equations (5),(7) imply

    0<s<1/2, l=sqrt(1-s^2)>s.                      (11)

    F_DC.u=nu*s+gamma*l>0.                          (12)

application is then the unique actual vertex q+w+u, regardless of

    -nu*s+det(w+u,(-nu,gamma))
       =nu*l+gamma*(l-s)>0.                         (13)

The original A--D force passes through q, so has zero moment there.
Equation (13) contradicts D's full unloaded torque. This contradiction
uses no force, degree, circle mask or equation at C.

Therefore every surviving original external-D feature, including its

    gamma*s<=nu*l.

Since l<1, this gives the strict whole-domain estimate

    gamma*(1-l)
       <=nu*l*(1-l)/s
        =nu*s*l/(1+l)<nu*s.                         (14)

The equality in the first comparison is retained; strictness comes
only from l/(1+l)<1. Thus no endpoint of the actual D side mean is

    mu*(1-h)<nu*(h+2s).                             (15)

    2(h+s)*(1-h)<h+2s,

(5). The case h=0 is included, since it would say 0<0. This proves
