## 1. A nonzero-moment physical triangle meets each positive external ray away from O

Let a physical positive triangle have complete zero-origin-moment
external resultants F_i, and nonzero common directed cycle moment.
The complete actual-external-resultant theorem makes those vectors
nonzero and noncollinear. Fix one triangle body K and write

    M=mu_1*n_1+mu_2*n_2=-F_K,

where mu_i>0 and n_i are its actual outward cycle normals.

**Ray lemma.** Some point of the actual closed K is s F_K with s>0.
The point is not claimed to be in K's open interior in this lemma.

### 1.1. Nonparallel cycle pair

The accepted nonzero-moment concurrence theorem gives

    q=t F_K=-t M, t>0,
    P_i=q+a_i n_i, a_i>=0,                             (1)

at the two unchanged proper ports. Suppose K contained no point
of the open positive F_K ray. Then q is not in K, so both a_i>0.
Put W=mu_1/a_1+mu_2/a_2>0. The actual convex combination

    P_bar=sum_i [(mu_i/a_i)/W] P_i
          =q+M/W=(1/W-t)M                             (2)

belongs to K with both weights strictly positive.

We record an elementary square-face fact used only at a boundary
equality in (2). If P_bar is on a side of K with outward own-axis
normal N, both actual P_i are on that side. An outward unit normal
of a square at any point of that side has nonnegative dot product
with N. If both dot products vanished, n_1,n_2 would both be
tangent to the side, hence parallel. Thus

    M.N>0 for every side containing P_bar.             (3)

If P_bar is a vertex, the same statement holds for both incident
sides. Therefore -M points strictly into K from P_bar.

The coefficient in (2) cannot be negative, since that would already
be a forbidden positive F_K-ray point. It cannot be zero either.
If P_bar=O is interior, the positive ray immediately meets int K.
If O is on the boundary, (3) makes O-sM lie in int K for small
s>0, again a positive F_K-ray point. Consequently

    P_bar=cM in K, c>0.                               (4)

Also O is not in int K under the assumed ray avoidance. Choose an
own coordinate whose interval does not straddle zero strictly, and
reflect it so K has that interval [a,a+1], a>=0. Equation (4)
gives M_x>=0. If M_x=0, then a=0 and P_bar is on the minimum-x
side; (3) with N=-e_x contradicts M_x=0. Hence M_x>0.

Since q_x=-t M_x<0, equations (1) force (n_i)_x>0. Both
actual ports are on the far side x=A, where A=a+1>=1. Their
vertical normal components cannot have one common strict sign:
that would put both ports at one vertex, making q that vertex,
contrary to q not in K. The same proper-port calculation as in
ROOTED_NONPOSITIVE_RAY_PROPER_PORT_CAP_EXCLUSION.md Sections 3--4
therefore gives q_y between the port heights. Together with (4),
the opposite weak signs of q_y and (P_bar)_y put zero between
the two transverse side heights.

Write P_i=(A,y_i). The true pair origin moment is zero, so

    A M_y=sum_i mu_i*y_i*(n_i)_x.

The ratio M_y/M_x is a positive weighted mean of y_i/A. Using
the unchanged proper-port slopes from (1), exactly as in the
cited proof, gives

    h_i=P_i.n_i>=A/(n_i)_x>=1/(n_i)_x.                 (5)

All original side means are still used. The only change from that
accepted cap argument is A>=1 rather than A>1. It is sufficient:
since h_i<=d<61/60,

    (n_i)_x>60/61, |(n_i)_y|<11/61, h_i>=1.

For each actual neighbor center c_i, its halfwidth is at least1/2,
so c_i.n_i>=3/2 while |c_i|<91/60. Its transverse component has
magnitude less than sqrt(181)/60<27/120. Consequently

    (c_i)_x> (3/2)(60/61)-(11/61)(27/120)
               =3501/2440=:L_*.

Both centers lie in the same open cap rectangle. Its squared diameter is

    (M_*-L_*)^2+4(M_*^2-L_*^2)
      =52124381/53582400<1.                           (6)

This contradicts the distance at least one between centers of distinct
interior-disjoint unit squares. The nonparallel ray lemma follows.

## 2. A genuine leaf root and a true loaded tip force nonzero triangle moment

Consider an actual triangle A,B,H in which A has exactly one additional
pair edge to a genuine leaf L, B has true degree two and positive
circle load, and H retains its complete other external system of zero
origin moment. Initially retain any actual H circle load.

**Moment lemma.** The common directed cycle moment is nonzero.

Suppose it were zero. At every nonparallel body its two actual cycle
lines then meet at O. B cannot be nonparallel: the full conditional
proper-loaded-tip theorem makes its concurrence a strictly positive
multiple of its nonzero actual circle resultant. Thus B is parallel.

Its two outward cycle normals have the same sign. The complete
parallel-load classification makes B physical radial S or D, with
inner radial application

    p=t_B e, t_B=d or r, t_B>0.

The two actual B means individually have zero origin moment, so each
is exactly p: the radial axis intersects the original inner support
face in that one point. No original mean was chosen anew.

The full external-resultant theorem says at most one triangle body
has a parallel pair. Hence A,H are nonparallel with concurrences O.
At their mutual edge the two physical proper lengths are nonnegative
and opposite, so both vanish and the actual AH application is O.
Both actual A,H also contain p. Their nondegenerate common segment
therefore lies along e, making their own frames parallel with axes
e,n, where n points A to H and is perpendicular to e.

The actual A square is exactly

    A=[t_B-1,t_B]e+[-1,0]n.                            (7)

Its genuine positive forces toward B and H are mu e and nu n,
with mu,nu>0. The leaf force has both own components strictly
negative. Thus its actual support on A is the unique vertex

    p_L=(t_B-1)e-n.                                   (8)

A genuine D leaf requires a relative-side-interior application on
its root by the selected nonradial point convention, so L cannot
be D in (8). It is S and |p_L|=d.

If B is S, t_B=d and (8) gives (d-1)^2+1=d^2, so d=1.
But then p_L=-n has zero e-component, contradicting the strictly
negative e-component of the true leaf force. If B is D, t_B=r<7/10,
and (8) gives

    |p_L|^2=(1-r)^2+1>109/100>(61/60)^2>d^2,

also impossible. This proves the moment lemma, with every S/D
mask, parallel weighted mean and critical d=1 endpoint retained.

## 4. The origin cannot lie on H's boundary

Suppose O were on a side of H with outward own normal N. Since
the two opposite nonzero ray points of Section 3 belong to H,
their directions are tangent to that side. Thus M_A, M_C are
parallel to H's other own axis.

We use the following complete physical triangle-axis fact. If two
actual positive outward forces on a square sum to a positive multiple
of an own axis e, both actual ports lie on the far e side, and
each outward normal has positive e-component. Indeed:

* An owned transverse axis conflicts with the triangle nonobtuse-
  normal lemma and zero total transverse component.
* An owned -e axis conflicts with the positive total e-component.
* An owned +e axis makes the other force parallel by transverse
  balance, with the same positive sign.
* If neither is an axis, both strictly expose vertices. Their
  transverse components have opposite signs, their vertices cannot
  be opposite, and the positive e sum puts both on the far e side.

This is the radius-free axis argument already used in
END_TRIANGLE_AXIS_RESULTANT_SUPPORT_BOUND.md and the parallel-wall
source; it does not require boundary neighbors or an intact petal.

Apply it to either H triangle pair, orienting e along its own pair
sum. Use transverse coordinate y>=0 into H from the origin side.
Both actual ports have x=A, and their normals satisfy (n_i)_x>0.
The pair resultant has zero transverse component. Its true total
origin moment is therefore

    0=A M_y-sum_i mu_i*y_i*(n_i)_x
      =-sum_i mu_i*y_i*(n_i)_x.

All summands have the same weak sign, so both actual heights y_i
are zero. The ports are the same far-side/zero-side vertex. At
that vertex both normal transverse components are nonpositive;
their positive sum is zero, so both are zero. Thus both forces
are parallel to e and both individual origin moments are zero.
This contradicts the nonzero triangle cycle moment from Section 2.

Every boundary face, including an origin vertex, is covered by
this argument. Therefore

    O belongs to the open interior of H.              (10)
