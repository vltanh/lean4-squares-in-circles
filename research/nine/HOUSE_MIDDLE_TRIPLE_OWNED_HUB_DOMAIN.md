## 1. Complete coordinates and the strict left location of B

 p_{AD}=0,\qquad n_{AD}=e_x,\qquad

\tag{2}
\]
\[
 D=[0,1]u+[0,1]v,\qquad

\tag{3}
\]
The application is in the relative interior of the A side: a point-only
vertex/vertex contact cannot use an axis endpoint under the retained strict

inward axes having positive x-components, as in (3).

Let alpha be the unit normal from A to T. The physical roof triangle and
the A-owned AD axis give alpha_x>=0. Write gamma>0 for AD's magnitude
and mu>0 for AT's. The actual outward AB vector at the unloaded A is

\tag{4}
\]

\tag{5}

This includes all shared AB means. Any actual C must still physically

## 2. The roof contact at D is a non-axis adjacent vertex

Its application would be u+v, its global x-component positive, and the
support-span lemma would give min_C x_x>a+b-1>0, contrary to (5) and BC.
This includes every shared DT side.

half-plane there, while the oblique D quadrant lies strictly inside the

The opposite D vertex u+v is also impossible as a roof application.
Its strict outward normal has positive global x-component, and a unit

Thus DT uses one of D's two adjacent vertices. Reflect across y=0 and

\tag{6}
\]
In particular beta_y=rb+sa>0. The normal can be T-owned or genuinely
free; no ownership at T is inferred here.

## 3. The entire roof contact at A is above the interface line

If alpha_x>0, the actual AT application is (0,h), including an arbitrary
vertical-side mean when alpha=e_x. Since T contains both (0,h) and u,
and u is its minimum-beta support point, (6) gives
\[
                    \beta\cdot((0,h)-u)=\beta_yh-r\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently h>0. A negative alpha_y would instead select A's bottom
vertex of height -t<0, so alpha_y>=0.

If alpha_x=0, alpha is a vertical axis. The downward choice would put
all of T at height at most -t while T contains u of height b>0. It is
impossible. Hence alpha=e_y and the whole actual application is on
A's upper side of height H>0.

The exhaustive alternative is therefore
\[
                 \alpha_x\ge0,\quad\alpha_y\ge0,
                 \quad p_{AT,y}>0.
\tag{8}
\]
Only alpha=e_x gives parallel A forces. Every other alternative has
alpha_y>0 and keeps its actual upper-side/upper-right application.

\tag{14}

The vertex case (11) has negative height as well. Thus every actual DC

## 5. The two exact A-star alternatives

 n_{AB}=-e_x,\qquad\mu_{AB}=\gamma+\mu,

### 5.2. The nonparallel upper-roof star

\[
 q_{A,x}=p_{AT,x}-H\frac{\alpha_x}{\alpha_y}\le0,
