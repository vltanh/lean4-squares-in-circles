## 1. The entire low-radius domain is impossible

Suppose E contains O closed. No other actual unit can contain O in
its open interior, since interior points of E approach O.

A D leaf puts O openly inside its actual root. Thus both genuine
leaves must be S. Both A and B contain O whenever d<=1, by their
actual inward radial unit segments.

If d<1, a tilted S root has own chart

    [da-1,da] times [db-1,db],
    a,b>0, a^2+b^2=1,

with all four strict inequalities placing O in its interior. This
has already been excluded. An aligned S root has radial interval
[d-1,d] containing zero strictly. To avoid containing O openly,
zero must be an endpoint of its transverse interval. O is then
in a relative side interior of that actual root.

Consequently both A and B have relative side-interior origins.
Their two open tangent half-planes must be complementary, or they
overlap near O. Their union contains a whole neighborhood of O
apart from their common boundary line. No third unit E containing
O can fit. This excludes every d<1 alternative without using a
boundary-tip equation.
