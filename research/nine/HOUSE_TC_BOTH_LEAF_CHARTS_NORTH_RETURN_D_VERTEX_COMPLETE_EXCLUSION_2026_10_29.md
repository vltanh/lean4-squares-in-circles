Date: October 29, 2026 UTC. Author: house continuation.

review and root adoption.** This excludes the whole original TC.S feature
with strict inner Northwest AD, North AT, and the actual AD point a vertex
of D. Both surviving genuine-S leaf charts, both original C-force rows, all
actual T frames and physical circle masks, and the entire original DT mean

## 3. The actual D tangent sector and the sign of G

We first prove that the two actual inward D axes at S have the form

    j=(-c,s), k_D=(-s,-c), c,s>0, c^2+s^2=1,
    D=S+[0,1]j+[0,1]k_D.                                (9)

Suppose F!=S. Its nonzero displacement from S belongs to the actual
right-angle inward sector and has nonnegative v component. The same
sector contains Q-S, which is strictly Southwest by (6),(7). These two
facts force its upper ray into the Northwest quadrant and its lower ray
into the Southwest quadrant, both with negative u components. To see
the endpoint issue explicitly, a sector meeting the strict Southwest
quadrant and a nonzero vector with v>=0 must cross the negative-u axis.
Its angle is pi/2. A nonnegative-u ray would force the lower ray South
and the upper ray West as its sole possible endpoint case. But the
inward South ray has positive dot product with DA=(rho,-omega), contrary
to strict support at S. This proves (9) in the F!=S case.

Suppose instead F=S. Both DA and G are outward support vectors at the
same D vertex. Its closed outward normal cone has angle pi/2, so their
dot product is nonnegative. If G_u<=0, (8) would give

    DA.G=rho*G_u-omega*G_v<0,

which is impossible. Thus G_u>0 in this case. For any hypothetical
inward D axis with u>=0, its dot product with G is positive if its
v component is nonnegative, and its dot product with DA is positive
if its v component is negative. Both contradict support. Hence both
inward axes have negative u components, proving (9) here too.

For F!=S, suppose for contradiction that G_u<=0. Equation (8) and
(9) give G.j>0, so actual D support forces

    F=S+j+lambda*k_D, 0<=lambda<=1.

Its u component is strictly less than h<=x, and F_v>=V. Since F is
a minimum-G support point of T, its other actual point P must satisfy

    G.(P-F)>=0.

Both coordinate terms on the left are nonpositive. It is strictly
negative if G_u<0. If G_u=0, equality would require F_v=V; but then
G.k_D=-c*G_v<0 forces lambda=0 and F_v=V+s>V, again impossible.
We have proved, uniformly in both cases,

    G_u>0, G_v>0.                                       (10)

No proper-concurrence assertion or sign of h was used to obtain this.

## 4. The complete DT support forces the opposite D diagonal

By (9),(10), G.k_D<0. Its actual maximum support on D therefore has

    F=S+tau*j, 0<=tau<=1.                               (11)

If G.j>0 then tau=1, if G.j<0 then tau=0, and if G.j=0 its entire
original side mean remains allowed.

We use the elementary acute-support fact proved in full in the strict
outer-return source: two minimum support directions with positive dot
product have their distinct applications on one actual side of a unit.
For completeness, strict vertex supports in opposite corners would have
nonpositive dot product. Adjacent vertex supports have their common
side. If either support is a side axis, every distinct acute support is
an endpoint of that very side. This also includes side-interior means
and the coincident application case.

Here the two incoming directions at T are North and G, whose dot product
is positive by (8). Hence the actual P,F have distance at most one.
If tau=1, their distance is exactly

    |F-P|^2=1+2*c*(x-h)+(x-h)^2.

Thus x=h and P=S. If T is tilted to the root frame, its minimum-North
point P is a vertex and A-T is a vertex/vertex singleton. Its original
North normal is an axis endpoint on A, contrary to the strict selected
point-only convention. If T is parallel to the root frame, its minimum
G point is its bottom-left vertex and has height V, whereas F_v=V+s.
Both alternatives are impossible. Consequently

    tau<1, G.j<=0.                                      (12)

The original strict DA support at S gives DA.j<0 and DA.k_D<0.
Combining these with G.j<=0 and G.k_D<0 in the full vector equation
(8) makes both projections of nu*g strictly positive. Therefore its
unique actual D maximum is the opposite vertex:

    Q=S+j+k_D,
    |Q-S|^2=2,
    K=c+s, W=c-s.                                       (13)

This is a physical diagonal conclusion about the original D. The
DT mean tau in (11) has not been replaced by a vertex.

## 7. Zero DT mean and both parallel T frames are excluded

    T=[h,h+1] times [V,V+1].

    0<tau<1, G.j=0,
    G=-alpha*k_D=alpha*(s,c), alpha>0.                  (19)

T cannot be root-parallel at tau>0 either: its unique minimum G

The frames of D and A are genuinely tilted by (9), and therefore

## 10. Both complete A4 leaf stars contradict that same bound

### 10.1. Owned genuine leaf

    t-4*a*q=a*(1/2-3*q)-(q+1/2)^2/2

    B0*(4*tau_A-1)<c.                                  (27)

This contradicts tau_A>1/2 from (22) and B0>c from (25).

### 10.2. Upper adjacent genuine-leaf vertex

Here the true radial leaf force in (3) is essential. By (18), X>b,
so (3) gives

    mu*a*b>tau_A*beta*X.

Also X>1, t>1/3, a<1 and b<121/1200 give

    X*t>3*a*b,

with coarse strict reserve 1/3-3*(121/1200)=37/1200>0. Hence
mu*t>3*tau_A*beta. On the other hand, the second and third full
equations of (3), together with (26), give

    mu*t=omega-rho+(1-tau_A)*beta
          <alpha*c+(1-tau_A)*beta.

Combining these again gives exactly (27), contradicted by the same
(22),(25). Thus the actual upper adjacent leaf star is excluded too.
