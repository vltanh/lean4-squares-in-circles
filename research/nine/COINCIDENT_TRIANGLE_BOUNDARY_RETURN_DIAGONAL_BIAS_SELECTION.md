## 1. The physical pair that requests a restriction

their actual intersection is a free vertex/vertex singleton {P}. Consider
the nonparallel-frame case in which, after the unique choice between
U's two incident inward axes, the full actual pair has the chart

    P=(0,0), U=[0,1]^2,
    a=(-sin u,cos u), b=(-cos u,-sin u),
    C=P+[0,1]a+[0,1]b, 0<u<pi/2.                       (1)

Here the C inward tangent sector begins at angle pi/2+u and ends at
pi+u. A diagonal reflection of the chart is used if C originally lies
on the other side of U's opposite diagonal. This is a description of
the fixed physical pose, not a change to the packing.

The complete strict common-normal interval from U into C is

    n(phi)=(-cos phi,-sin phi), 0<phi<u.                 (2)

Indeed its inward projections on C's actual axes are sin(u-phi) and
cos(u-phi), and strict exposure on U requires 0<phi<pi/2. These
conditions are exactly (2). The interval is not enlarged by permitting
a side-axis endpoint.

The excluded parallel opposite-quadrant case would be u=pi/2. It is
not assigned a new restriction. It cannot occur in the coincident-star
application of Section 3, as proved there. The endpoint u=0 would give
a shared UC side and is outside the physical singleton premise.

Write the actual circle origin in this chart as O=(xi,zeta). Prelist

    U avoids closed D_epsilon, or |xi|>epsilon,          (3)

where the disk is about the original O. This predicate, the pair's

If u>pi/4, restrict the actual UC normal to the nonempty strict interval

    pi/4<phi<u.                                         (4)

If u<=pi/4, impose no new restriction. The direction is unambiguous:
only the physically clear endpoint is U. A physical boundary square

## 3. Uniform full unloaded-three-force consequence at a coincident triangle

Suppose a final physically clear U is unloaded of true positive degree
three, with exactly the actual positive neighbors C,H,E. Assume

    U intersection C = U intersection H = {P},

both of these physical singletons are free, C is physically boundary,
and the physical pair UC satisfies predicate (3). The actual degrees,
loads and additional forces at H and E are arbitrary. C retains its
whole original star. The real H and C both contain P, so their physical
contact is present regardless of whether it carries positive force.

The two inward sectors of C,H and the U quadrant are disjoint. If C
were the aligned quadrant opposite U, the two remaining gaps would each
have angle pi/2. H, having a quadrant sector, would have to occupy one
of them exactly. It would then share a full side with U, contrary to
the free UH singleton. Thus UC has the oblique chart (1), after the
diagonal reflection fixed by that actual pair.

The gap between U's North side and C's first axis has angle u<pi/2.
It cannot contain H's quadrant. The entire H quadrant must lie in the
other remaining gap. Consequently its actual inward axes have angles

    pi+u+v, 3*pi/2+u+v,
    v>=0, u+v<pi/2.                                    (5)

The strict upper bound follows from the physical UH singleton: equality
would make H the Southeast quadrant sharing U's bottom side. The
complete strict U-to-H normal interval is therefore

    n_H=(-cos phi_H,-sin phi_H),
    u+v<phi_H<pi/2.                                    (6)

If v=0, H and C share their full side from P. If v>0, they have just
the singleton P in common. Both are included. No HC force or mean is
used in the following full U3 calculation.

All components of both original U-to-C and U-to-H forces are negative.
Full original U force balance therefore makes its E normal strictly
northeast. Its actual U application is the opposite vertex Q=(1,1).
Taking the full U torque about P gives

    det(Q,n_UE)=0,
    n_UE=(1,1)/sqrt(2).                                 (7)

Thus the two actual negative-component force vectors at P must sum to
a vector on U's southwest diagonal. If u>pi/4, the selected UC normal
has phi_C>pi/4 by (4), while every permitted UH normal has
phi_H>u+v>=u>pi/4. Each force's downward magnitude strictly exceeds
its leftward magnitude. Their positive sum cannot have equal absolute
components as required by (7).

We obtain the complete necessary conclusion

    0<u<=pi/4.                                          (8)

This keeps every positive original force in the true U3. It is a
selected-certificate theorem with an explicit compatible initial
selection, not an assertion about every differently selected stress.
It does not give a three-force equation to a hub of larger degree.
