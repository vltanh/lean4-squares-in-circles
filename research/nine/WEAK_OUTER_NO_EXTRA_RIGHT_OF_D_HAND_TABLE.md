## 3. A putative extra to the right must be above D

Suppose X is an original distinct from C,D,G and \(x_X\ge x_D\).
Equations (2)--(3) give

\[
 0\le x_X-x_D<\rho-4/3=11/60.
\]

\[
 1-(11/60)^2-(49/50)^2=539/90000>0
\]

and original center separation therefore imply

\[
                         |y_X-y_D|>49/50.
 \tag{6}
\]

If X were below D, the accepted C lower-region exclusion would force
\(y_X>y_C\). Hence
\(y_D-y_C>49/50\), and the retained C/D cone would give

\[
 x_X\ge x_D>x_C+49/50>439/300.

Equation (4) would simultaneously give

\[
 y_X<y_D-49/50<47/120-49/50=-353/600.
\]

\[
 |c_X|^2>(439/300)^2+(353/600)^2
        =\rho^2+67393/360000>\rho^2,
\]

contradicting (2). Thus the only possible sign in (6) is

\[
                         y_X-y_D>49/50.
 \tag{7}
\]

## 7. Stronger fixed strip: every extra has x less than 5/4

Suppose an original X distinct from C,D,G has \(x_X\ge5/4\).
Since \(4/3<x_D<\rho\) and \(5/4\le x_X<\rho\),

\[
 |x_X-x_D|<\rho-5/4=4/15.
\]

The exact reserve
\(1-(4/15)^2-(24/25)^2=41/5625>0\) and original center separation
force \(|y_X-y_D|>24/25\).

First suppose X is below D. The unchanged C lower-region theorem
gives \(y_X>y_C\). Hence, using the original C/D cone and its bound
\(x_D-x_C<31/30\),

\[
 0<y_X-y_C<(y_D-y_C)-24/25<31/30-24/25=11/150.
\]

We also have \(x_X>x_C\), since \(x_C<4/5<5/4\). The exact comparison

\[
 1-(11/150)^2-(49/50)^2=77/2250>0
\]

therefore gives \(x_X-x_C>49/50\), so \(x_X>439/300\).
At the same time \(y_X<47/120-24/25=-341/600\). Consequently

\[
 |c_X|^2>(439/300)^2+(341/600)^2
         =\rho^2+11813/72000>\rho^2,
\]

an impossibility. Thus X is above D by more than 24/25.

The center-radius bound and the exact reserve

\[
 (5/4)^2+(7/8)^2-\rho^2=401/14400>0
\]

give \(|y_X|<7/8\). The same original G horizontal lower bound as
in Section 4 gives \(|x_X-x_G|<1691/2160\). Now use the sharper
exact comparison

\[
 1-(1691/2160)^2-(31/50)^2=316559/116640000>0
\]

to obtain \(|y_X-y_G|>31/50\). X cannot be above G, because that
would imply

\[
 y_X>17/40+31/50=7/8+17/100>7/8.
\]

It follows that G is above X by more than 31/50. Thus

\[
 y_G-y_D>24/25+31/50=79/50,
\]

contradicting the same complete table (5). This proves

\[
                  \boxed{x_H<5/4,\qquad x_I<5/4.}
\]

No further finite table, angle assumption, or physical-owner omission
has been introduced. All constants in this stronger routing argument
are checked by the same exact arithmetic companion.


## 8. A height-routing barrier beside the short-chain middle square

The same original-center argument gives a useful all-angle routing
condition when F is low. For either unselected original X, the following
two implications hold, retaining both cases when the horizontal
coordinates are equal:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
x_X\le x_F:& |y_X-y_A|+|y_F-y_X|>3/5,\\[1mm]
x_X\ge x_F:& |y_X-y_F|+|y_D-y_X|>3/5.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{8}
\]

A numerical search over 361,201 two-gap samples first located the
extremal horizontal span 9/5 at vertical gaps 0 and 3/5. This candidate
also excludes the computer search's concrete necessary-relaxation
pose with F=(-0.202344,0.208145), D=(1.516667,0.165428), and the right
extra I=(0.678520,0.208145): its relevant total vertical variation is
less than 0.043. That sampled pose was never a physical packing or a
proof premise. The following argument proves the full continuous
statement without sampling or angle restrictions.

For any three original centers P,X,T in horizontal order, suppose

\[
 u=|y_X-y_P|,\qquad w=|y_T-y_X|,\qquad u+w\le3/5.
\]

Their genuine unit-center separations give

\[
 x_T-x_P\ge\sqrt{1-u^2}+\sqrt{1-w^2}.
\]

For fixed s=u+w, the function
\(u\mapsto\sqrt{1-u^2}+\sqrt{1-(s-u)^2}\) is concave on [0,s].
It therefore attains its minimum at an endpoint, yielding

\[
 x_T-x_P\ge1+\sqrt{1-s^2}\ge1+4/5=9/5.                 \tag{9}
\]

The center order established above gives A<X<D for either extra.
The accepted original F strip is
\(-115/432<x_F<17/64\). Consequently the two possible outside spans
are both strictly too short for (9):

\[
\begin{split}
x_F-x_A&<\rho+17/64=1711/960=9/5-17/960,\\
x_D-x_F&<\rho+115/432=3851/2160=9/5-37/2160.
\end{split}
\]

Apply (9) to A,X,F when \(x_X\le x_F\), and to F,X,D when
\(x_X\ge x_F\). These contradictions prove (8). This argument uses
only original physical center separation, so it retains every signed
orientation and every original separating-axis owner. No assertion
that either new link is an actual projection comparison is needed.

For a finite height routing, (8) may equivalently be expanded into the
union of its four signed linear differences. If the relevant endpoint
heights differ by at most 3/5, it says more explicitly that X lies
strictly outside the closed interval
\([(y_P+y_T-3/5)/2,(y_P+y_T+3/5)/2]\), with P,T=A,F or F,D as
appropriate. This conditional barrier helps rule out low-F placement
branches; it does not by itself complete the remaining original-nine
height routing or close either original mask.
