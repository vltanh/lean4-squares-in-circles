## 1. A universal physical support bound for a contained unit square

Let X be any actual closed unit square contained in the disk of radius
R about O=0, and let n be any unit vector. Write

    M=max_(x in X) n.x,
    L_R=sqrt(R^2-1/4).

Then

    M>=1-L_R.                                              (1)

This requires no boundary contact, load, selected normal or degree.
Choose its actual orthonormal outward own axes u,v at a maximum-n vertex
Q, and choose their signs and order so

    Q=X's maximum-n vertex,
    X=Q-[0,1]u-[0,1]v,
    c=n.u>=s=n.v>=0,
    c^2+s^2=1.

A zero component is permitted; Q can be either endpoint of the actual
maximum side in that case. The actual far edge parallel to v has
midpoint m=Q-u-v/2. Its two endpoints m+v/2 and m-v/2 are in the disk,
so the parallelogram identity gives

    |m|^2+1/4 <= R^2,
    |m|<=L_R.

Moreover

    n.m=M-c-s/2,
    (c+s/2)^2=1+cs-3s^2/4>=1+s^2/4>=1.

Thus `M=n.m+c+s/2>=-|m|+1>=1-L_R`, proving (1).
The equality cases, both original side endpoints and every interior
mean cause no exception: M is the support of the whole actual square.

In particular, if an actual contained unit C lies in

    n.x<=n.P-1,

then

    n.P>=2-sqrt(R^2-1/4).                                  (2)

Throughout the original upper-radius window `R^2<=1105/256`,

    (121/60)^2-1041/256=31/57600>0.

Consequently the same actual P satisfies
