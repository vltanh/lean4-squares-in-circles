# Exact articulation splitting and branch sparsification of physical stresses

Date: October 26, 2026.

**Status: complete hand argument; root full-source review passed.**
See REVIEW_ARTICULATION_SPLITTING_AND_CAP_ARCS_ROOT_2026_10_26.md.
The algebraic splitting theorem below applies to the original physical
force-and-torque equations. Its support-minimal corollary is a legitimate
selection of a new stress on the **same unchanged packing**. It is not a
claim that every original stress has minimal support, nor a closure of
an old coarse cell. The small-component consequence additionally inherits
the accepted exclusions of all positive components on at most five bodies.

No Lean compilation, CI, numerical search or new scalar certificate is used.

## 1. Branch forces and moments at an actual articulation

Use the physical certificate of PHYSICAL_CONTACT_STRESS.md after aggregation
of each shared-side pair to its original application mean. Retain the
compatible point-normal choices and planar body graph. Work at the same
selected physical minimizer as the existing proof; do not change its pose.

Let A be an articulation of a connected positive component G. Write the
components of G minus A as V_1,...,V_k, with k >= 2. For branch j let

    f_j = sum of the actual outward pair-force vectors at A
          on the edges from A into V_j,
    a_j = sum of the actual circle weights on the bodies of V_j.

Let A's positive circle atoms be (lambda_z,z), with |z|=R. Put

    F_A = 2 sum_z lambda_z z.

The actual A force equation is

    F_A + sum_j f_j = 0.                                      (1)

The origin moment of each branch's force at A vanishes separately:

    sum_{e from A to V_j} det(p_e, mu_e n_e) = 0.              (2)

Indeed, sum the origin-moment equations over V_j. All internal pair
moments cancel because the two incident bodies use the same actual
application point. Every circle force is radial and has zero origin
moment. The only surviving terms are the negatives of those in (2).
This uses every original force inside V_j, regardless of cycles there.

Moreover a_j > 0. Otherwise V_j would be a nonempty all-unloaded set
whose only positive neighbors outside it lie in A, contradicting the
center-dotted force identity CY.LOAD.1 of CYCLIC_BLOCK_INVENTORY.md.

Thus branch j can be multiplied by any nonnegative scalar t_j: multiply
every circle atom in V_j and every pair atom internal to V_j or joining
it to A by t_j. All its body force and moment equations remain true;
its force contribution at A becomes t_j f_j and its moment there remains
zero. Pair applications, normals and occupied physical squares do not move.

## 2. Exact bounded circle-weight splitting criterion

Choose a nonempty proper set I of the branch indices. Suppose there are
numbers eta_z with

    0 <= eta_z <= lambda_z,
    2 sum_z eta_z z = -sum_{j in I} f_j.                       (3)

Then the original certificate is the sum of two nonnegative physical
force-and-torque certificates:

* The first retains A and the branches in I, with A circle weights eta_z.
* The second retains A and the other branches, with A circle weights
  lambda_z-eta_z.

All pair weights and means in each retained branch are unchanged. At A,
the first force equation is exactly (3); the second follows by subtracting
it from (1). Equation (2) proves both A origin-moment equations. Combining
origin moment with the already established force equation gives the
original angular equation about c_A. Equations at every other retained
body are unchanged. Every omitted physical square stays in the packing
with zero stress in the chosen certificate.

Both resulting certificates have positive total circle weight because
each side contains at least one branch and every a_j is positive. They
can therefore each be normalized to total circle weight one. Their
positive body graphs are connected subgraphs, hence planar; their actual
point normals, contact types and shared means are inherited unchanged.

Condition (3) is exact: -sum_{j in I} f_j must belong to the compact
set {2 sum_z eta_z z : 0<=eta_z<=lambda_z}. For one positive circle
corner this is the segment [0,F_A]; for two it is the parallelogram
generated by the two original weighted corner forces. This statement
does not infer (3) merely from collinearity or from zero total torque.

**Parallel-resultant corollary.** If each incoming branch resultant
-f_j is a positive multiple theta_j F_A of the same nonzero F_A, then
sum_j theta_j=1. Taking eta_z=theta_j lambda_z proves (3) for every
single branch, including two physical circle corners and every original
individual zero weight. Internal branch cycles are allowed.

## 3. The all-bridge loaded articulation is exactly splittable

Suppose A is loaded and every incident positive edge is a bridge.
The local all-bridge argument in
TRIANGLE_THREE_BRIDGES_LONG_CHAIN_EXCLUSION.md, Section 1, applies
without any assertion that G is a tree:

* A is the outer endpoint at each bridge, because an inner endpoint
  meets the tiny central disk and is physically circle-clear.
* All incoming applications equal A's unique nearest point p to zero;
  all incoming pair forces have the same radial direction e=p/|p|.
* The combined radial force and torque give the actual radial S or D
  geometry of RADIAL_BRIDGE_AND_LEAF_REDUCTION.md, Section 6.
* In the selected point-normal convention D has degree one and S has
  degree at most two. A degree-two S has its two original equal positive
  circle weights and two incoming magnitudes mu_1,mu_2 > 0.

Thus the only articulation alternative is S of degree two. Its total
circle force is (mu_1+mu_2)e. Each branch can be retained separately
with both A circle weights multiplied by

    theta_j = mu_j/(mu_1+mu_2),  0<theta_j<1.                  (4)

This is precisely the exact split of Section 2, not a new application
of a degree-one equation to A while keeping two forces. The new stress
has A of degree one only after its complete new force and angular
equations have been proved. Both original physical sides of A and
both actual circle corners remain in the packing.

If a component of size m<=9 admits any split (3), the two resulting

    m_1+m_2 = m+1 <= 10.

At least one is therefore at most five. It is a complete nonnegative
normalized physical certificate on the same selected packing, with
every other one of the nine squares retained. The small-core theorem
excludes it. Hence **no such splittable articulation occurs**.

In particular, in the original selected nine-square certificate every
loaded body incident only to bridges is a genuine degree-one S/D leaf.
An all-bridge degree-two loaded S is excluded even when both sides
contain cycles. This is stronger than excluding only a pendant tree
side. The source of the strengthening is the exact size identity and
the full small-component theorem, not a presumption that either branch
was originally a separate component.

The scope inheritance is essential. The small-core sources prelist
their generic conditions for physical candidates before stress
extraction, so thinning or splitting retains those conditions.
Any geometric secondary-minimizer premise is a property of the same
unchanged pose. A conclusion proved only for a separately reselected
normal system could not be imported here; the above corollary uses
the common selected certificate stipulated by those assemblies.

## 5. A stronger general selection theorem at every articulation

There is also a useful support-minimal version that needs no small-core
exclusion. Fix the physical pose, all actual permissible selected normals,
and the compatible finite generic conditions. Among the available
normalized planar physical stresses choose one with the smallest number
of positive scalar atoms before shared-side aggregation. Such a minimum
exists because the number is a nonnegative integer and the feasible
family is nonempty. No compactness of the multiplier set is needed.

At any articulation A, let k be its number of branches and let ell be
its number of positive circle atoms (ell is 0,1 or2 in the radius window).
Then

    k + ell <= 3.                                            (5)

To prove this, assign to each branch and circle atom a vector in R^3:

    v_j = (f_j, a_j),
    v_z = (2 lambda_z z, lambda_z).

All last coordinates are strictly positive. The original force balance
and circle normalization give

    sum_j v_j + sum_z v_z = (0,0,1).                          (6)

If k+ell>3 these vectors are linearly dependent. Choose a nonzero
relation sum_i beta_i v_i=0. Since all last coordinates are positive,
the beta_i have both signs. Replace every original coefficient 1 by

    t_i = 1 - beta_i/max_h beta_h.

All t_i are nonnegative and at least one vanishes. They still satisfy
(6). Multiplying whole branches and A circle atoms by these t_i gives
a new nonnegative normalized physical stress by Sections 1--2's force
and origin-moment calculation. Its surviving support is connected
through A (or one can select a connected component if needed), planar,
and uses only the original normals and actual means. At least one
whole positive branch or positive circle atom has vanished, and no
new atom has appeared. This contradicts minimal support, proving (5).

Scaling a whole branch multiplies both endpoint atoms of each original
shared-side interaction by the same scalar. Its application mean is
therefore unchanged. Consequently atom-level minimality and the
aggregated physical formulation agree for this argument.

The resulting common selected certificate has these uniform restrictions:

| Positive circle atoms on an articulation | Maximum number of branches |
| --- | ---: |
| 0 | 3 |
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 1, so such a body is not an articulation |

The two-atom statement includes a physical radial square only when
both of its actual circle weights are positive. A physical second
circle corner with zero weight is counted as one positive atom, and
must not be removed from its geometry.

The maximum is on the components of G minus A, not on the number of
positive pair edges at A. An entire cyclic branch can contain several
such edges and keeps every one of its internal body equations.

## 6. Inheritance and remaining scope

These operations preserve the actual packing, radius, all physical
contacts and circle masks, all side means, all surviving point normals,
planarity and any pose-based canonical-minimizer property. A dropped
body remains one of the original nine physical squares and becomes
a completion square for the retained stress.

The normalization (6) ensures that the new objects are full stresses,
not arbitrary force fragments. A subsequent argument must nevertheless
use the genuine degrees and circle weights in that newly selected
certificate. One must not splice an equation from it into an old
certificate at a different root.

No statement here closes bare C6, an original larger coarse cell,
the sharp lower bound, or equality classification. The exact utility
is to remove splittable stress representations and to provide the
uniform articulation bound (5) before future graph classification.
