# Cone triples beside an actual four-chain: reusable hand geometry and a complete caller

5 November 2026. This is a complete hand argument for the stated caller.
Its component proofs have received independent mathematical reading.
No mathematical program, numerical search,
certificate replay, Lean build, or CI is used. The theorem concerns the
complete stated nine-original domain. It does not close the other
actual-four occupancies, the case with a lower second central original,
or the global no-actual-four domain.

Put

\[
 Q=\frac{1105}{256},\quad r=\sqrt Q,\quad
 \rho=\frac{91}{60},\quad l=\frac{29}{60},\quad u=\frac{13}{12},
 \quad G=\frac{15}{31}.
\]

## 1. Complete domain and mathematical inputs

Retain nine independently oriented original unit squares with pairwise
disjoint open interiors in the centered disk of radius \(R\le r\).
Four originals \(M_0,M_1,M_2,M_3\) form an actual horizontal projection
chain, and their consecutive center differences also lie in the positive
horizontal cone. Write their centers as \((x_i,y_i)\), their horizontal
and vertical support halfwidths as \(h_i\), and their contained aligned
square halfwidths as \(g_i=1/(4h_i)\). The actual endpoint span is

\[
 D=x_3-x_0-h_0-h_3\ge2.
 \tag{1}
\]

The five other originals have the following positions. There are exactly
two left-side outsiders \(S,H\), with

\[
 -u<x_S,x_H\le-l,\qquad y_S<y_1<y_H;
 \tag{2}
\]

exactly two central outsiders \(F_1,F_2\), with

\[
 |x_{F_j}|<l,\qquad y_{F_j}>\max(y_1,y_2)\quad(j=1,2);
 \tag{3}
\]

and one right-side outsider \(T\), with \(l\le x_T<u\).
The letters in (2)--(3) name the same physical originals throughout.
There is also a designated outsider horizontal cone three-chain
\(L<_cP<_cR\). Its links need not be actual projection separations.
All original pair and corner constraints are retained.

The conclusion proved below is that three distinct originals, other than
\(M_0,M_3\), satisfy the widened five-square cap hypotheses:

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 -u\le x_A\le0\le x_B\le u,\qquad |x_U|\le l,\\
 y_A,y_B\ge\frac35,\qquad y_U-h_U\ge\frac1{10}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{4}
\]

The widened cap consequently gives \(D\le2\), strictly if \(R<r\).
Thus this whole nine-original domain is impossible at a strict subcritical
radius. At \(R=r\), this note only claims the terminal and its inequality;
it does not classify equality.

## 2. A central original has a positive clearance from both inner mains

For either inner main \(M\), put \(e=h_M-1/2\). If \(F\) is any
central outsider, (5) gives

\[
                 |x_F-x_M|<1-e.
 \tag{8}
\]

Reduce the main frame to absolute components \(a\ge b\ge0\), with
\(a^2+b^2=1\) and \(e=(a+b-1)/2\). The near-alignment estimates
in the hand main-height source give \(a>99/100\), \(b<7/200\).
For \(b>0\), the identity \(1-a=b^2/(1+a)\) gives

\[
 \frac{ae}{b}=\frac a2-\frac{ab}{2(1+a)}
 >\frac{99}{200}-\frac7{400}=\frac{191}{400}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Suppose \(|y_F-y_M|\le23/50\). In the main's own orthonormal
frame, the absolute coordinate transverse to its near-horizontal side is
less than

\[
 b(1-e)+a\frac{23}{50}<\frac7{200}+\frac{23}{50}
                         =\frac{99}{200}<\frac12.
\]

Its other absolute coordinate, using (8)--(9), is less than

\[
 a(1-e)+b\frac{23}{50}
 <1-\left(\frac{191}{400}-\frac{184}{400}\right)b<1.
\]

When \(b=0\), the corresponding strict bound is simply
\(|x_F-x_M|<1\). Thus in every frame the distance of \(c_F\) from
the physical main square is strictly less than \(1/2\). The open
radius-\(1/2\) disk inside \(F\) meets the main's interior, a
contradiction. Therefore

\[
             |y_F-y_1|>\frac{23}{50},\qquad
             |y_F-y_2|>\frac{23}{50}.
 \tag{10}
\]

This also rederives general central routing in the current caller.
A central outsider between the inner main heights would have a gap
greater than \(59/60\) from its own-half main and greater than
\(23/50\) from the other, requiring a total greater than
\(433/300>31/30\), contrary to (5). Thus every central outsider is
above both or below both. For either upper original in (3), (6) and
(10) give

\[
                  y_F>\frac{11}{100}.
 \tag{11}
\]

If additionally \(x_F\le0\), own-half clearance gives the stronger

\[
                  y_F>\frac{19}{30}>\frac35.
 \tag{12}
\]

The original \(H\) in (2) likewise satisfies

\[
                  y_H>y_1+\frac{97}{100}>
                         \frac{31}{50}>\frac35.
 \tag{13}
\]

## 3. The high left original pushes every bad central floor rightward

Call an upper central original \(F\) *bad* if
\(y_F-h_F\le1/10\). We prove

\[
                         x_F>-\frac15.
 \tag{14}
\]

It suffices to exclude \(x_F\le-1/5\). Such an \(F\) is high by
(12), and its bad floor gives

\[
                  \frac35<y_F<\frac{97}{120},
\]

using \(h_F\le1/\sqrt2<17/24\). Reflect x and write
\(c_H=(s,z)\), so \(l\le s<u\), \(z>3/5\),
\(s^2+z^2<\rho^2\), while the reflected F center has
\(1/5\le x_F<l\).

If H is below F, their horizontal gap is below \(53/60\) and
their vertical gap is below \(5/24\). The sum of these squared
bounds is below one. If H is above F, their squared distance is bounded
by

\[
      (s-1/5)^2+\bigl(\sqrt{\rho^2-s^2}-3/5\bigr)^2.
 \tag{15}
\]

After expansion, the only nonlinear term is a negative multiple of
the concave square root; (15) is convex in \(s\in[l,u]\), so its
maximum is at an endpoint. The exact comparisons

\[
 \sqrt{\rho^2-l^2}<\frac{36}{25},\qquad
 \sqrt{\rho^2-u^2}<\frac{17}{16}
\]

give the endpoint bounds

\[
 \left(\frac{17}{60}\right)^2+
 \left(\frac{21}{25}\right)^2<1,
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{53}{60}\right)^2+
 \left(\frac{37}{80}\right)^2<1.
\]

Every vertical order therefore contradicts the disjoint radius-half
disks of the two originals. This proves (14), including the boundary
\(x_F=-1/5\). This is the general two-disk bad-floor cut, reproduced
here to make its hypotheses explicit.

## 4. At least one of the two upper central originals has a good floor

If both were bad, (11), (14), and \(h<17/24\) would put both centers
in the same rectangle

\[
         -\frac15<x<l,\qquad
         \frac{11}{100}<y<\frac{97}{120}.
\]

Its squared diameter is

\[
 \left(\frac{41}{60}\right)^2+
 \left(\frac{419}{600}\right)^2
       =\frac{343661}{360000}<1.
 \tag{16}
\]

Their radius-half disks would overlap. Hence at least one original has

\[
                         y_F-h_F>\frac1{10}.
 \tag{17}
\]

No side-height choice for the right original has been made in this
argument.

## 5. A negative bad upper central cannot have another upper central

We prove a stronger statement needed when the right-side original is low:

> If there are two upper central originals as in (3), every one with
> nonpositive x-coordinate has whole floor greater than \(1/10\).

Suppose \(F\) is bad and \(x_F\le0\), and call the other upper
central original \(V\). Equation (14) gives
\(-1/5<x_F\le0\). Equation (12) gives
\(y_F>19/30\), and the bad floor gives \(y_F<97/120\).

### 5.1. The other upper central must have positive x-coordinate

Suppose \(x_V\le0\). Then \(y_V>19/30\), again by (12).
If \(y_V\le y_F\), the horizontal and vertical gaps are respectively
below \(l\) and \(7/40\), whose squared sum is
\(3805/14400<1\).

If \(y_V>y_F\) and \(x_V>-1/5\), their horizontal gap is below
\(1/5\), while the vertical gap is below
\(\rho-19/30=53/60\). Their squared sum is
\(2953/3600<1\).

The only remaining possibility is \(y_V>y_F\), \(x_V\le-1/5\).
The center-radius bound gives

\[
 y_V<\sqrt{\rho^2-1/25}<\frac{301}{200},
\]

where the last squared reserve is \(1709/360000>0\).
The horizontal gap is below \(29/60\), and the vertical gap is below
\(301/200-19/30=523/600\). Their squared sum is

\[
 \left(\frac{29}{60}\right)^2+
 \left(\frac{523}{600}\right)^2
          =\frac{357629}{360000}<1.
\]

All three possibilities contradict their original radius-half disks.
Thus

\[
                            0<x_V<l.
 \tag{18}
\]

### 5.2. The other upper central lies above the bad one

If \(y_V\le y_F\), (11) gives a vertical gap below
\(97/120-11/100=419/600\), and (14), (18) give horizontal gap
below \(41/60\). Equation (16) is again a contradiction. Therefore

\[
 D_F:=x_V-x_F\in(0,41/60),\qquad
 V_F:=y_V-y_F\in(0,53/60).
 \tag{19}
\]

The vertical upper bound uses \(y_F>19/30\) and \(y_V<\rho\).

### 5.3. The bad floor forces one actual F-owned separating side

Fold the one original F frame to absolute components
\(a\ge b\ge0\), \(a^2+b^2=1\), and put

\[
 h=\frac{a+b}{2},\quad k=\frac{a-b}{2},\quad
 t=b/a,\quad c=G-\frac9{20}=\frac{21}{620}.
\]

For F relative to the aligned inset of \(M_1\), put
\(d=x_F-x_1>0\), \(v=y_F-y_1>0\), and \(g=g_1>G\).
The same originals satisfy

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \frac{17}{60}<d<\frac{31}{60},\qquad
 d-g<x_F+\frac1{60}\le\frac1{60},\qquad d+g>\frac12,\\
 v-g<h-c.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{20}
\]

The final inequality follows from the bad floor and \(y_1>-7/20\):
\(v<h+9/20\), then subtract \(g>G\).

A separating axis for this physical square and physical inset is either
a coordinate axis or a side normal of F. The horizontal coordinate
cannot separate, since \(d<31/60<g+h\). The vertical coordinate
cannot separate, since \(v-g<h\). A side normal with nonpositive
vertical component has projection at most \(d<31/60\), whereas the
two supports sum to \(1/2+g(a+b)>1/2+G\); it cannot separate either.

Every upward-left F side normal has one of the two component orders
\((-a,b)\), \((-b,a)\). The adjusted projections are respectively

\[
 -a(d+g)+b(v-g)
 < -a(d+g)+bh-bc
 =\frac12-a(d+g+k)-bc<\frac12,
\]

and

\[
 -b(d+g)+a(v-g)
 < -b(d+g)+ah-ac
 =\frac12+b(k-d-g)-ac<\frac12.
\]

Here \(bh=1/2-ak\), \(ah=1/2+bk\), and
\(k\le1/2<d+g\). Thus both orders, and both original chiralities,
have been included and excluded.

An upward-right near-horizontal normal \((a,b)\) would require

\[
 \frac12\le a(d-g)+b(v-g)
 <a(x_F+1/60)+b(h-c)
 =\frac12+a(x_F+1/60-k)-bc.
\]

Since \(x_F\le0\), it would require \(k+tc<1/60\).
But

\[
 k=\frac{1-t}{2\sqrt{1+t^2}}\ge\frac{1-t}{3},
 \qquad
 k+tc\ge\frac{1-t}{3}+tc\ge c>\frac1{60},
\]

because \(0<c<1/3\). This last owner is impossible.

Consequently, unless separation has already been contradicted, the actual
separating normal must be the upward-right near-vertical F side normal

\[
                 n=(b,a).
\]

The other F side direction can therefore be taken as \(m=(a,-b)\).
These are the axes of the actual original F, not an independently chosen
replacement orientation. Its separator from the original M1 inset gives

\[
 n\cdot c_F\ge\frac12+g(a+b)+b x_1+a y_1
 >\frac12+\frac{83}{620}a-\frac{61}{1860}b.
 \tag{21}
\]

Since \(b\le a\) and \(a\ge1/\sqrt2>7/10\),

\[
 \frac{83}{620}a-\frac{61}{1860}b
 \ge\frac{47}{465}a>
 \frac{329}{4650}>\frac7{100}.
\]

Thus

\[
                         n\cdot c_F>\frac{57}{100}.
 \tag{22}
\]

### 5.4. The second original's incircle meets the actual F

Write \(w=c_V-c_F=(D_F,V_F)\). By the original center-radius bound
and (22),

\[
 0<n\cdot w=n\cdot c_V-n\cdot c_F
       <\frac{91}{60}-\frac{57}{100}=\frac{71}{75}.
 \tag{23}
\]

Equation (19) also gives

\[
 m\cdot w=aD_F-bV_F<\frac{41}{60},
 \qquad
 -m\cdot w=bV_F-aD_F
 <\frac34\frac{53}{60}=\frac{53}{80}<\frac{41}{60}.
 \tag{24}
\]

The exact distance to a unit square, expressed in its own orthonormal
frame, is

\[
 \operatorname{dist}(c_V,F)^2
 =\bigl(|m\cdot w|-1/2\bigr)_+^2
       +\bigl(|n\cdot w|-1/2\bigr)_+^2.
\]

Using (23)--(24), this is strictly below

\[
 \left(\frac{11}{60}\right)^2+
 \left(\frac{67}{150}\right)^2
       =\frac{20981}{90000}
       =\frac14-\frac{1519}{90000}<\frac14.
 \tag{25}
\]

The open radius-half disk of the unchanged original V therefore meets
the physical square F in interior points. This contradicts original
nonoverlap and completes the claim of Section 5.

The proof has used the *same F frame* in its main separator and in the
final distance calculation. Forgetting that chirality would lose the
argument; replacing either square by an independent proxy would not be
valid.

## 6. Both selected endpoints are side originals: complete height split

Suppose the selected cone triple has its endpoints in the two side
columns. The common routing identifies \(L=H\), puts \(P\) among
the two original upper centrals, and identifies \(R=T\) with
\(y_T>y_2\). No further weak-link or actual-link assumption is used.

If \(y_T\ge3/5\), Section 4 gives one good central original U.
The three distinct originals \(H,U,T\) satisfy (4), including the
boundary \(y_T=3/5\).

Suppose instead \(y_T<3/5\). The low-side extraction makes every
nonnegative upper central good. Section 5 makes every nonpositive upper
central good. These alternatives include \(x=0\), so **both** original
upper centrals have whole floors greater than \(1/10\). Their centers
are therefore above \(3/5\).

At least one of these two centers is positive. If both were nonpositive,
their x-gap would be smaller than \(l\), contrary to upper-column
separation. Choose a positive one as B, and the other as U. Then the
three distinct originals \(H,U,B\) satisfy (4). This covers every
possible sign of the other central original, including zero. The widened
cap again supplies the terminal.

Thus the complete both-side-selected-endpoints, two-upper-centrals branch
uses no seven-original low-right or low-floor exclusion table.

## 7. A selected cone endpoint is central: complete unused-side split

It remains to cover the alternative in which the selected outsider cone
triple has a central endpoint. Its middle P is already central; since
there are only two central originals, these are exactly \(F_1,F_2\).
Call their left-to-right order F,U. Three selected central originals are
impossible by the horizontal cone span bound. Their cone comparison gives

\[
 x_U-x_F\ge g_F+g_U>\frac7{10},\qquad
 x_F<0<x_U,
\]

and

\[
 x_F+g_F\le x_U-g_U<l-\frac7{20}
                  =\frac2{15}.
\]

The coupled cone-gap input (7) applies to the actual negative original F,
using \(y_1>-7/20\), and makes it good. The original H is high by
(13). The sole right-side original T has exactly the following alternatives.

* If \(y_T>y_2\) and \(y_T\ge3/5\), choose B=T.
* If \(y_T>y_2\) and \(y_T<3/5\), low-side extraction makes the
  actual positive central U good, hence high; choose B=U.
* If \(y_T<y_2\), the one-sided hand main-height theorem gives
  \(y_2>-7/20\). The positive central U has its own-half clearance
  above M2, so \(y_U>19/30>3/5\); choose B=U.

Equality \(y_T=y_2\) is excluded by the physical same-half clearance.
These alternatives cover the unused right-side square as well as a
selected one. In the latter case common routing already excludes the
third alternative, but including it loses nothing.

In every case the distinct originals \(H,F,B\) satisfy (4): H is
negative and high, F is central with a good whole floor, and B is
nonnegative and high. This completes the remaining selected-triple
positions.

## 8. Coverage and exact scope

The selected middle P is central. There are exactly two central originals,
so its selected endpoints are either both side originals or exactly one
central original. Sections 6 and 7 cover these alternatives. Section 6
retains both right-side height ranges; Section 7 additionally retains the
upper/lower alternative of an unused right-side original. All sign-zero
faces, the shoulder height \(3/5\), and the bad-floor boundary \(1/10\)
are included.

The full left side is used through its actual lower original S to obtain
the hand bound \(y_1>-7/20\), and through its actual upper original H
in the bad-floor cut and final cap. The previously unused second central
original is essential to Sections 4 and 5. No original has been discarded
or reoriented separately. The selected cone links are never declared
actual by fiat.

The dependency order is: physical and actual-four bounds; the full-side
hand main-height theorem and common cone routing; the low-side and
coupled cone-gap hand inputs; the new two-central diameter and physical
frame trap; the widened five-square cap. None of those earlier inputs
assumes this (2,2,1) conclusion.

This proves the stated two-upper-central caller. It does not address the
same occupancy with one upper and one lower central original, nor the
other actual-four occupancies or the no-actual-four domain. The global
sharp lower bound consequently remains a separate open implication.

## Appendix A. Complete common routing used in this caller

For any horizontal center-cone edge \(A<_cB\), the aligned inset
argument gives

\[
 x_B-x_A\ge g_A+g_B\ge1/\sqrt2>7/10,
 \qquad |y_B-y_A|\le x_B-x_A.
 \tag{A1}
\]

Both inequalities are about the unchanged originals. From the outsider
strip \(|x|<u\) and the two selected edges, (A1) gives

\[
 |x_P|<\frac{23}{60}<l,\qquad
 x_L<-\frac{19}{60}<0<\frac{19}{60}<x_R.
 \tag{A2}
\]

The selected middle P is therefore one of the two upper centrals in (3).
Its horizontal center gap from each inner main is below \(9/10\).
The distance from its radius-half disk to the corresponding aligned
main inset, with \(g_i>12/25\), gives \(y_P-y_i>3/4\).
Indeed a contrary vertical gap at most \(3/4\) would make the squared
distance to that inset less than

\[
 (9/10-12/25)^2+(3/4-12/25)^2
                    =\frac{2493}{10000}<\frac14.
\]

If L is a side original and were below M1, its same-half gap would give
\(y_1-y_L>97/100\). Thus
\(y_P-y_L>3/4+97/100=43/25\). But its selected cone edge gives

\[
 y_P-y_L\le x_P-x_L<23/60+13/12=22/15<43/25,
\]

a contradiction. Hence a selected left-side L is H. The reflected
argument shows that a selected right-side R is above M2 and hence is
the sole right original T with the upper choice. Selected central
endpoints are already upper by (3).

The total selected cone span is at least \(\sqrt2>7/5\), whereas
three central centers would have span below \(2l=29/30\). Thus
there cannot be two selected central endpoints; Sections 6 and 7
exhaust all positions. This proof does not use any actual selected short
link or any hypothesis that those links are weak.

## Appendix B. A low right-side original forces a same-side central floor

This appendix reproduces the complete physical extraction used in
Sections 6--7. Let T be a right-side original with
\(y_2<y_T<3/5\), and let F be a distinct central original with
\(0\le x_F\le l\), \(y_F>y_2\). The intended conclusion is
\(y_F-h_F>1/10\).

Put

\[
 \zeta=\frac{2211}{4000},\quad J=\frac{23}{150},\quad
 A_0=\frac{18911}{59520},\quad
 c_0=A_0-\frac1{10}=\frac{12959}{59520}.
\]

The shared zero-side theorem in [the full-sides chapter](ACTUAL_FOUR_FULL_SIDES.md),
Sections 3.1--3.2, states the following without a full-side hypothesis:
for this same side original, if \(|y_T|<7/16\), then

\[
       x_T<\zeta,\qquad x_T-g_T<J,
                   \qquad y_T+g_T>A_0.
 \tag{B1}
\]

Its hypotheses are precisely actual-and-cone main four-chain, right-side
position, and T above its own main. The endpoint inset, also printed in
that chapter, contains

\[
 [503/480,937/480]\times[-221/600,221/600].
 \tag{B2}
\]

The main cone height bound in (5), and the physical same-half clearances,
give

\[
             y_T>-\frac{113}{600},\qquad
             y_F>-\frac7{40}.
 \tag{B3}
\]

### B.1. The low side center has height below 7/16

Suppose \(y_T<7/16\). Then (B3) implies \(|y_T|<7/16\), so
(B1) applies. The horizontal gap between F and T is nonnegative in
the direction from F to T, and is below \(\zeta\). Their original
radius-half disks give vertical gap greater than \(5/6\), since
\(\zeta^2<11/36\). If F were below T, this would give
\(y_F<7/16-5/6=-19/48<-7/40\), contradicting (B3).
Thus F is above T, with \(y_F>129/200\).

Suppose its whole floor is at most \(1/10\). Fold its one physical
frame to \(a\ge b\ge0\), \(a^2+b^2=1\), with
\(h=(a+b)/2\), \(k=(a-b)/2\). Put

\[
 d=x_T-x_F\ge0,\quad v=y_F-y_T>0,\quad g=g_T.
\]

The original inset and bad-floor bounds give

\[
                d-g<J<1/2,\qquad v-g<h-c_0.
 \tag{B4}
\]

Neither coordinate axis separates F from T's aligned inset. A
side normal with nonpositive vertical component has adjusted projection
at most \(|n_x|(d-g)<1/2\), so cannot separate either. Every
upward normal pointing to the right, away from the actual displacement
\((-d,v)\), is also impossible. If its vertical component is b,
the adjusted projection is less than \(bh\le1/2\). If its
vertical component is a, the adjusted projection is less than

\[
 \frac12+b(k-d-g)-ac_0<\frac12,
\]

since \(g>1/3\), \(k\le1/2\), and
\((b/a)(k-d-g)<1/6<c_0\).

The aligned frame has no remaining axis and is impossible. For
\(t=b/a\in(0,1]\), the two possible upward-left side normals
would require, respectively,

\[
                  d-g>k+tc_0,
             \qquad d-g>-k+c_0/t.
 \tag{B5}
\]

The first contradicts (B4), because
\(k\ge(1-t)/3\), \(c_0<1/3\), and consequently
\(k+tc_0\ge c_0>J\). The second would require
\(t(k+J)>c_0\), whereas \(k\le(1-t)/2\) gives

\[
 t(k+J)\le(J+1/2)t-t^2/2
           \le\frac{(J+1/2)^2}{2}
            =\frac{2401}{11250}<c_0.
\]

The final difference is \(96041/22320000>0\).
This excludes every physical owner, chirality, and separator sign, and
proves the floor claim in the first height range.

### B.2. The side center has height from 7/16 to 3/5

Now let \(7/16\le y_T<3/5\). Its radius-half disk avoids the
actual endpoint rectangle (B2). Since \(x_T<u<937/480\) and
\(y_T-221/600<1/2\), this first forces \(x_T<503/480\), and then

\[
 x_T\le\frac{503}{480}
       -\sqrt{\frac14-(y_T-221/600)^2}
 <\frac{503}{480}
       -\sqrt{\frac14-(3/5-221/600)^2}
 <\frac{49}{80}.
 \tag{B6}
\]

The final squared radical comparison has reserve
\(38839/5760000>0\). If F were below T, (B3) and (B6) would give

\[
 |c_F-c_T|^2<
     (49/80)^2+(31/40)^2=\frac{1249}{1280}<1.
\]

Hence F is above T. Their horizontal gap is below \(49/80\), so
their vertical gap is greater than \(3/4\), with squared reserve
\(399/6400>0\). Finally

\[
 y_F-h_F>\frac7{16}+\frac34-\frac{17}{24}
                              =\frac{23}{48}>\frac1{10}.
\]

The two height ranges cover the entire low-side premise, with the
boundary \(7/16\) in the second range. This completes the extraction.

## Appendix C. A coupled central cone gap, without an archived scalar table

This local theorem requires only an actual-and-cone main four-chain,
physical separation of F from its main, and the displayed conditions.
It does not require the (2,2,1) occupancy or the existence of a second
upper central original.

Let an upper central original F satisfy

\[
          x_F\le0,\qquad x_F+g_F\le\frac2{15},
                      \qquad y_1\ge-\frac7{20}.
 \tag{C1}
\]

We prove the contract (7) directly. Fold the actual F frame to
\(a\ge b\ge0\), put \(t=b/a\in[0,1]\), and write
\(h=(a+b)/2\), \(k=(a-b)/2\), \(g_F=1/(4h)\).
For its displacement from the actual M1 inset put

\[
 d=x_F-x_1>0,\qquad v=y_F-y_1>0,\qquad g=g_1>G,
                  \qquad z=d-g<x_F+\frac1{60}.
\]

We first give the complete physical loss inequality

\[
           y_F-h>y_1+G-\bigl[t(k+x_F+1/60)\bigr]_+.
 \tag{C2}
\]

The horizontal coordinate cannot separate, since \(d<31/60<g+h\).
A vertical separator immediately gives \(v-h\ge g\), which is
stronger than (C2). A side normal with nonpositive vertical component
cannot separate: its scalar product is at most \(d<31/60\), while
the physical support sum is \(1/2+g(a+b)>1/2+G\).

For \(b>0\), every remaining F-owned side normal is one of the
four upward absolute-component forms. Their separating inequalities give
the following lower bounds for \(v-h\):

| Upward side normal | Necessary lower bound for \(v-h\) |
| --- | --- |
| \((b,a)\) | \(g-t(k+z)\) |
| \((-b,a)\) | \(g-t(k-d-g)\) |
| \((a,b)\) | \(g-(z-k)/t\) |
| \((-a,b)\) | \(g+(d+g+k)/t\) |

These follow from \(ah=1/2+bk\), \(bh=1/2-ak\).
The second bound is no weaker than the first because \(d\ge0\);
the fourth is at least g. For the third, note that \(z<1/60<g_F\).
When \(t<1\), the exact identity

\[
 (1-t^2)g_F=(1+t^2)k
\]

shows \((z-k)/t\le t(k+z)\). At \(t=1\) these two expressions
are equal. Thus the third lower bound is also no weaker than
\(g-[t(k+z)]_+\). The same conclusion is immediate for \(b=0\),
when a vertical coordinate separator is the only possible one. Since
\(z<x_F+1/60\) and \(g>G\), (C2) follows, with all actual owners
and both chiralities covered.

The exact same-frame coupling identity is

\[
 k-g_F=-\frac{t^2}{\sqrt{1+t^2}(1+t)}.
\]

Using (C1), the loss in (C2) is at most

\[
 f(t):=t\left[\frac3{20}
        -\frac{t^2}{\sqrt{1+t^2}(1+t)}\right]_+.
 \tag{C3}
\]

Here is a complete two-interval bound \(f(t)<3/100\).

For \(0\le t\le1/2\),
\(\sqrt{1+t^2}<9/8\) and \(1+t\le3/2\), so

\[
 f(t)\le\left[\frac3{20}t-\frac{16}{27}t^3\right]_+.
\]

The cubic has its maximum on the nonnegative half-line at
\(t=3\sqrt{3/5}/8\); differentiation gives its value
\(3\sqrt{3/5}/80<3/100\), because \(\sqrt{3/5}<4/5\).

For \(1/2\le t\le1\), the function

\[
 q(t)=\frac{t^2}{\sqrt{1+t^2}(1+t)}
\]

is increasing, since

\[
 q'(t)=\frac{t(2+t+t^2)}{(1+t)^2(1+t^2)^{3/2}}>0.
\]

Its value at \(1/2\) is \(1/(3\sqrt5)>7/50\); the squared
comparison is \(2500>2205\). Thus (C3) is less than
\(3/20-7/50=1/100\) on this whole second interval.

Both intervals include their endpoints, including the aligned frame at
\(t=0\). Substituting this strict loss bound into (C2) gives

\[
 y_F-h_F>-\frac7{20}+\frac{15}{31}-\frac3{100}
           =\frac{161}{1550}
           =\frac1{10}+\frac3{775}.
\]

This proves (7) with no sampled parameter and no computational table.
