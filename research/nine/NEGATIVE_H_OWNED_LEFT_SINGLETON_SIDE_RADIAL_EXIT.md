## 2. A complete monotone comparison in x

Fix the actual q and c. For comparison only, allow x to run through the entire interval \([1/2,x_{\rm actual}]\), and define X,Y,\(\beta,\mathsf A,\mathsf B,\tau\) by the same formulas as (1)--(2). No physical square or actual mean is changed; this is a scalar inequality proof.

All needed constraints remain valid on this comparison interval. In particular

\[
Y>7/4,\quad \mathsf A\ge x+1\ge3/2,
\quad\mathsf B>0,\quad X>0.
\tag{6}
\]

For \(Y>7/4\), use \(x\le x_{\rm actual}<9/10\), \(Y\ge\sqrt{q-x^2}\), and \(q_0-81/100-49/16=9/320>0\). The original inequality \(\mathsf A\ge x+1\) persists as x decreases because

\[
\mathsf A_x=-\mathsf B/Y,
\qquad (\mathsf A-x-1)_x=-\mathsf B/Y-1<0.
\]

The useful uniform bound on this entire comparison interval is

\[
                              \mathsf B<5/4.
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed the inverse-coordinate identity is
\(x=\mathsf Bs-(\mathsf A-1)c\). By (6),

\[
\mathsf B\le(\mathsf A-1)(1+c)/s
            \le\sqrt3(\mathsf A-1).
\]

If \(\mathsf B\ge5/4\), then \(\sqrt3<7/4\) gives \(\mathsf A>12/7\), and

\[
q>144/49+25/16=3529/784>1105/256,

whose final reserve is \(2319/12544>0\). This proves (7).

\[

The expression in braces is strictly positive on the full comparison interval. From (6), \(\tau>3/7\), \(s>6/7\), \(Y^2<9/2\), and \(\mathsf A\ge3/2\), one has

Also \(\mathsf B=sX+cY<X+R/2<X+21/20\). Hence, using (7), the braces in (8) are greater than

Therefore M strictly decreases with x. It remains to bound \(M(1/2)\).

## 3. One uniform c comparison at x=1/2

In this section only, put

\[
X=1/2-c,\quad Y=\sqrt{q-X^2},\quad
p=\tau\mathsf A,
\qquad 0\le c\le1/2.
\]

This includes the closed comparison endpoints. We prove

\[
|1-\mathsf B|\le1/2-(4/5)c,
\qquad p\le d+(21/100)c.
\tag{10}
\]

First,

\[
\mathsf B-1/2=c(Y-H),\qquad
H=s+\frac{c}{2(1+s)}.
\]

The exact identity \(H-1=c(1-2c)/(2(1+s))\) gives \(H\ge1\). Since \(s\le1-c^2/2\) and \(1+s>7/4\),

\[
H\le1-c^2/2+2c/7\le51/49.
\]

Moreover \(Y\ge\sqrt{q-1/4}>191/100\) and \(Y\le R<21/10\). Thus, for c>0,

\[
(4/5)c<\mathsf B-1/2<(11/10)c.
\tag{11}
\]

The lower reserve is \(191/100-51/49-4/5=339/4900>0\). Because \(c\le1/2\), the interval in (11) implies the first bound in (10): both \(1-\mathsf B\) and \(\mathsf B-1\) are at most \(1/2-(4/5)c\). At c=0 the bound is an equality.

For the second bound, keep q fixed and differentiate with respect to c along \(X=1/2-c\). The exact identities are

\[
\mathsf A_c=-\beta(c/s+X/Y),\qquad
\tau_c=c/(sY)+sX/Y^3,
\]

\[
p_c=c(3-t-t^{-1})+X(2t-1)
       -\frac{c^2X}{sY}-\frac{csX^2}{Y^3},
\qquad t=s/Y.
\tag{12}
\]

Here \(0<t<1\) and \(X\ge0\). The first two terms in (12), considered as a function of t, have derivative \(c(t^{-2}-1)+2X>0\). Since \(Y>19/10\), replace t from above by \(10s/19\). Using \(s\le1-c^2/2\), and \((1-z)^{-1}\ge1+z+z^2\) for \(z=c^2/2\in[0,1/8]\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
3-10s/19-19/(10s)
 &\le109/190-(261/380)c^2-(19/40)c^4,\\
20s/19-1&\le1/19-(10/19)c^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

In the first line, the function of s on the left is increasing for \(0<s\le1\), which justifies substituting the upper bound on s before applying the reciprocal estimate. Dropping the nonpositive last two terms of (12), and then its nonpositive fifth-degree term, yields

\[

1/38+(99/190)c-(5/19)c^2-(61/380)c^3.

On the entire interval \([0,1/2]\), \(Q'(c)\ge11/80>0\). Therefore

\[

\tag{13}
\]

with exact reserve \(137/15200\). At c=0, \(p=\sqrt{q-1/4}-1=d\). Integration proves the second bound in (10).

\[
\begin{aligned}

&\le\{d+(21/100)c\}^2+\{1/2-(4/5)c\}^2\\

 +c\{(21/50)d-4/5+(6841/10000)c\}\\

The final coefficient uses only \(d<61/60\) and \(c\le1/2\). At c=0 equality is allowed at x=1/2, while the actual x>1/2 still makes the comparison in Section 2 strict. The original tilted singleton has c>0, so (14) already has a strict margin. Together with (8)--(9), this proves (5) for every original mean W.
