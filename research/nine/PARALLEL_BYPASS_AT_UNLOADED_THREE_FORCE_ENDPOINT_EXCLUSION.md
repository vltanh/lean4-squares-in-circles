## 4. A support-point observation retaining every neighbor mean

We use the following elementary, radius-free fact. If P belongs
to a unit square C and minimizes a direction n=(c_0,-s_0) with
c_0,s_0>0, then every point Z of the actual closed C satisfies

\[
                    Z_x>P_x-1,\qquad Z_y<P_y+1.
\tag{7}
\]

If P is a strict vertex in this direction, choose its two inward
orthogonal unit axes u,v. Both n dot u and n dot v are positive.
At most one inward axis can have negative x component, since n
is a positive combination of u,v and n_x>0. That one component
is strictly greater than -1: equality would be the direction
(-1,0), whose scalar product with n is negative. Thus the minimum
x coordinate on the whole unit is greater than P_x-1. The same
argument, using n_y<0, bounds its maximum y strictly below P_y+1.

If n is an owned C axis, set j=(s_0,c_0). For the full actual
mean parameter 0<=eta<=1 the unit is exactly

\[
               C=P-\eta j+[0,1]n+[0,1]j.
\]

Its smallest x coordinate is P_x-eta s_0>P_x-1 and its largest
y coordinate is P_y+(1-eta)c_0<P_y+1. This includes both side
endpoints, so (7) covers every actual C feature.

Applied to (6), this yields the two strict whole-square bounds

\[
                         C\subset\{x>1,\ y<t\}.
\tag{8}
\]

## 5. The actual second channel must be shallow and diagonal

Retain the two unchanged A--Y--C applications R,S and direction
m of (5). Since R_x<=0 and S_x>1 by (3),(8),

\[
                         w m_x>1.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus w cannot be one. It is sqrt(2), and m_x>1/sqrt(2).
If m_y>0, the actual maximum-m point of A is its upper-right
vertex R=(0,a), with a>=t. Then S_y=a+sqrt(2)m_y>t,
contrary to (8). Therefore m_y<=0.

Write

\[
 m=(c,-s),\qquad c^2+s^2=1,\qquad
 c>1/\sqrt2,\quad 0\le s<1/\sqrt2,\quad c>s.
\tag{10}
\]

If s>0, the actual A support is the lower-right vertex
R=(0,a-1), so (4) gives R_y=t-1+delta with 0<=delta<1.

\[
             R=(0,t-1+\delta),\qquad 0\le\delta<1.
\tag{11}

\[
 Y=R+[0,1]u+[0,1]v,

## 6. An actual open section overlaps the original V

At x=1 the two actual endpoints of that open section have heights

\[
\begin{split}
 y_-&=t-1+\delta-
              \frac{\sqrt2-c+s}{c+s},\\
 y_+&=t-1+\delta+
              \frac{\sqrt2-c-s}{c-s}.
\end{split}
\tag{13}
\]

For example, these follow from the lines through S with directions
-u and -v respectively; before simplification they are
R_y-sqrt(2)s minus or plus
(sqrt(2)c-1)(c-s)/(c+s) or
(sqrt(2)c-1)(c+s)/(c-s). The identities in (13) use only
c^2+s^2=1 and retain s=0.

Every denominator in (13) is positive. The two numerators are
strictly positive as well: c+s<sqrt(2), and sqrt(2)-c+s>0.
Using (11),

\[
                       y_+>t-1,
                \qquad y_-<t-1+\delta<t.
\tag{14}
\]

The section is nonempty and open. Equations (14) imply that its
height interval intersects (t-1,t) in a nonempty open interval.
Choose any actual point (1,z) in this intersection. It belongs
to the open interior of Y and to the closed left side of
