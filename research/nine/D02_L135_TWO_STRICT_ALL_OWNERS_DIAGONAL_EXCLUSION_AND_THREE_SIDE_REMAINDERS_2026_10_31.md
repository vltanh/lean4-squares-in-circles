### 2.1. Both roofs hub-owned

an owned triangle return together with an owned exterior force makes
the other triangle return the same own axis. If AD were free, the
original singleton selector would force that exterior contact shared,
giving exactly the contradiction in [G], Section 3.

A physically shared AD is excluded in full by [S]. For a genuine
singleton, its original normal must consequently be owned by one hub.
Exchange the two hubs if necessary so A owns it. Put e=(s,c). The
physical triangle support inequalities permit only

    A-to-D g=h or g=-e.

Indeed -h opposes the A roof, while +e has strictly positive dot
product with the D roof and so violates the D triangle inequality.
The same-axis g=h case is excluded by [A]. The perpendicular g=-e
case is precisely [R]. The shared-axis boundary cannot be a genuine
side-interior singleton by [J]. Thus the owner list has no missing
