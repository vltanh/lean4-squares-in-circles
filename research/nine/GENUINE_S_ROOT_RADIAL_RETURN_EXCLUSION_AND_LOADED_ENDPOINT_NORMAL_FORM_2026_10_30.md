## 2. A genuine S root cannot have a distinct radial S return

**Theorem 2.1.** Let A have its actual genuine positive degree-one S leaf
L_A, with actual radial direction e. Let another distinct actual square
B be epsilon-near. Suppose a further actual square P, distinct from
A,B,L_A, is the radial S square

\[
 P=[d,d+1]n+[-1/2,1/2]Jn
 \tag{2.1}
\]

and the original positive A--P normal from A toward P is n. Then the
configuration is impossible.

There is no restriction on A's other positive forces or its full degree.
P need not be a degree-one leaf. The theorem permits a shared A--P
segment and every value of its original mean.

### 2.1. The case where A owns the A--P normal

The unchanged application has the form

\[
 r=dn+yJn,\qquad |y|\le1/2.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

If A owns n, its actual n interval is [d-1,d]=[epsilon,d].
By [L], its genuine leaf has an actual application de in A and support
d in direction e. If e is an own side direction, the choices -n and
+/-Jn are incompatible with the positive n interval: their point de
has n-coordinate -d or zero. Thus e=n.

If instead the real leaf touches a strict vertex of A, the n coordinate
of de is positive, so e.n>0. That maximum-e vertex is consequently on
the n-maximum face, giving d(e.n)=d and e=n again, contrary to strictness.
Thus the only possibility is e=n and the real leaf is exactly (2.1).
This repeats the same actual square as L_A and P, a contradiction.

### 2.2. A strict A vertex and a strict real-leaf vertex

Suppose A does not own n. Then r is its unique maximum-n vertex.
If |y|=1/2, it is also a vertex of P. The intersection would be a
singleton of two vertices, whose selected normal must strictly expose
both. But n owns P's full inner side. Therefore

\[
 |y|<1/2.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Suppose first that A's real S leaf is at a strict A vertex. Choose A's
own outward axes f,g directed toward that real-leaf vertex. Its exact
chart is

\[
 A=[U-1,U]f+[V-1,V]g,\qquad
 U,V>0,\qquad U^2+V^2=d^2.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

The real application is z=Uf+Vg=de. If r=z, its norm d and (2.2)
give y=0, n=e and L_A=P. At the opposite vertex,

\[
 |z-f-g|^2=d^2+2-2(U+V)<d^2,
 \tag{2.5}
\]

because U+V>d>1, whereas |r|^2=d^2+y^2>=d^2.

For either remaining adjacent vertex, exchange f,g if needed to write
r=z-f. Its norm gives

\[
 U=\frac{1-y^2}{2},\qquad \frac38<U\le\frac12.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

Consequently

\[
 V^2=d^2-U^2<d^2-\frac9{64}<(1-\epsilon)^2,
 \tag{2.7}
\]

since 4epsilon<1/15<9/64. Also V>epsilon, for
V^2>=d^2-1/4>3/4. Thus all four quantities
U,1-U,V,1-V exceed epsilon. The entire closed D_epsilon lies in
the open interior of A. The other actual near square B has a point
there; interior points of B approach that point, giving open overlap.
This excludes every strict-real-leaf alternative.

### 2.3. A strict A vertex and an owned real leaf

If A owns its real S direction e, [L] gives the whole actual chart

\[
 A=[\epsilon,d]e+[\beta,\beta+1]Je,\qquad
 0\in[\beta,\beta+1].
 \tag{2.8}
\]

Since r is an A vertex, its e coordinate is epsilon or d. At epsilon
its transverse coordinate would have squared magnitude

\[
 |r|^2-\epsilon^2=1+2\epsilon+y^2>1,
 \tag{2.9}
\]

impossible for a unit interval containing zero. At d its transverse
coordinate has magnitude |y|<1/2. Hence r lies in the relative interior
of the inner side of the real leaf L_A as well as that of P.

The equalities r.e=r.n=d>0 exclude e=-n. For small lambda>0,
the point r+lambda(e+n) lies openly inside both leaves: its two inward
normal increments are lambda(1+e.n)>0, and both transverse coordinates
remain strictly within their actual sides. The two distinct actual
squares overlap. This completes the whole owner and endpoint cover.
