## 1. Exact common chart for the entire both-owner row

Normalize the original U-to-L return channel horizontally to the right,
at its original height H. In actual own axes at L, write
\[
 z_L=(b,a),\qquad a,b>\frac12,\qquad a^2+b^2=R^2.
\tag{2}
\]
The S-incident inward normal is \((0,1)\), and the return outward
normal is \((-1,0)\). This covers either original placement of the
S channel by the appropriate interchange of actual own-axis labels
and reflection of the complete physical chart.

For its original proper concurrence parameter \(0<\tau\le1\),
the genuine means and radial port are
\[
 Q_L=(\tau b,a-1),\qquad p_L=(b-1,\tau a),\qquad
 P_L=Q_L-(0,1)=(\tau b,a-2).
\tag{3}
\]
Put \(H=\tau a>0\). The unchanged return transmitter gives the
strict partner's actual application
\[
                              p_U=(b-2,H).
\tag{4}
\]
All original mean-support constraints remain; \(\tau\) is not a
freely chosen slider.

Let \(\alpha,\beta>0\) be the original two radial-port angles, at L
and U respectively, and put \(\gamma=\alpha+\beta\).
The force turns give
\[
 \theta_S=\pi-\gamma,\qquad
 \theta_U=\pi/2+\gamma\le3\pi/4,\qquad
                         0<\gamma\le\pi/4.
\tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
 k_\xi=d\cos\xi-\frac12\sin\xi,\qquad
 h_\xi=d\sin\xi+\frac12\cos\xi.
\]
Taking the dot product and the positive moment of the actual radial
port in (3) gives the exact identities
\[
 a=2-k_\alpha,\qquad b=\sqrt{R^2-a^2},\qquad
                   \tau b=h_\alpha,\qquad
                   \boxed{H=\frac{a h_\alpha}{b}.}
\tag{6}
\]

In the same return frame, the actual unit direction from S toward U is
\[
                           G=(-\sin\gamma,\cos\gamma).
\tag{7}
\]
At its actual radial port \(P_U\),
\(\det(P_U,G)=-h_\beta\). This is the lower radial-port chart;
it is the same physical chart as (3), rather than an independent
choice of signs.

The strict unique endpoint U has its original proper concurrence
\(q_U=\sigma z_U\), \(\sigma>0\), on the horizontal return line.
Thus \(q_{U,y}=H\). Write
\[
                          z_U=(-A,Z),\qquad Z=H/\sigma>0.
\]
The true incoming channel and original concurrence give
\[
 \det(q_U,G)=\det(P_U,G)=-h_\beta,
\]
hence
\[
             \frac{A}{Z}=\frac{h_\beta+H\sin\gamma}
                                  {H\cos\gamma}>0.
\]
In particular \(A>0\) is proved by this relation; no circle branch
is assumed. Since \(A^2+Z^2=R^2\), put \(v=H/h_\beta>0\) to get
\[
 \boxed{\frac AR
   =\Phi(v,\sin\gamma)
   :=\frac{1+v\sin\gamma}
               {\sqrt{1+v^2+2v\sin\gamma}}.}
\tag{8}
\]
This identity holds for every strict partner feature and every actual
return mean. It uses no assumption that the radial concurrence lies
inside S.

For \(v>0,\ 0\le s<1\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \Phi_v=\frac{v(s^2-1)}{(1+v^2+2vs)^{3/2}}<0,\qquad
 \Phi_s=\frac{v^2(v+s)}{(1+v^2+2vs)^{3/2}}>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus a uniform upper bound on v and lower bound on \(\sin\gamma\)
give a rigorous lower bound on the original A.

The horizontal distance between the actual circle corner and actual
return application is
\[
                           A+b-2.
\tag{10}
\]
We will make it exceed one in the O case and \(\sqrt2\) in the
A/M cases, contradicting their actual adjacent or opposite vertex.

## 2. Every partner with \(\beta\ge2/7\) has \(A+b>3\)

Assume \(\beta\ge2/7\). Equation (5) and \(\pi<22/7\) give
\[
                              0<\alpha<1/2.
\tag{11}
\]
For fixed d, \(2-d\cos\alpha+\tfrac12\sin\alpha\) increases
with \(\alpha\). Replacing d by \(187/200\) raises it. The
signed sine upper polynomial of degree five and cosine lower
polynomial of degree six at \(1/2\) therefore give
\[
                              a<71/50.
\tag{12}
\]
The exact rational reserve in that comparison is \(7613/9216000\).
The same signed bounds, with \(d<61/60\), give
\[
                   h_\alpha<15/16,
\tag{13}
\]
with reserve \(2599/230400\). The function \(h_\alpha\) increases
on \([0,\pi/4]\), since \(d>1/2\) and
\(d\cos\alpha-\tfrac12\sin\alpha>0\) there.
Equations (1),(6),(12) give
\[
 b>7/5,\qquad
 H<\frac{71}{50}\frac{15}{16}\frac57
                             =\frac{213}{224}<1.
\tag{14}
\]
For the b comparison, \(4-(71/50)^2-(7/5)^2=59/2500>0\).

The same monotonicity of \(h_\beta\), now from \(\beta\ge2/7\),
gives
\[
 h_\beta>
 \frac{187}{200}\left(\frac27-\frac{(2/7)^3}{6}\right)
       +\frac12\left(1-\frac{(2/7)^2}{2}\right)
 >\frac7{10},
\tag{15}
\]
with exact final reserve \(887/20580\).
Also \(\sin\gamma\ge\sin(2/7)>1/4\), with sine-cubic reserve
\(131/4116\).

There are two complete analytic ranges.

If \(\alpha\le1/4\), the coarser bounds
\[
 a<2-\frac{187}{200}\frac{31}{32}+\frac18<\frac54,
 \qquad h_\alpha<\frac{61}{240}+\frac12<\frac45
\]

Thus \(b>3/2\), \(H<2/3\), and \(v<20/21<1\).
Equations (8)--(9) then give
\[
                  A>2\Phi(1,1/4)=\sqrt{5/2}>3/2.

Consequently \(A+b>3\).

If \(\alpha\ge1/4\), then

\[
                    \frac{576}{219}-\frac{64}{25}
                              =\frac{128}{1825}>0.
\]
Together with \(b>7/5\), again \(A+b>3\).

This proves the whole-angle statement
\[
                   \boxed{\beta\ge2/7\ \Longrightarrow\ A+b>3.}
\tag{16}
\]

## 4. Complete strict partner feature exhaustion

The accepted strict A/M/O/T cover is unchanged.

For A, the actual S-incident port is adjacent to its circle corner,
so the accepted high-radius near-corner lemma gives
\(\beta>1/2>99/200\). Its actual return port is the opposite
vertex. Equation (24) says the horizontal circle-to-return distance
is greater than \(\sqrt2\), contradicting its exact diagonal length.

For M, the actual S-incident port is its original genuine mean.
Its local chart has
\[
 a_U=2-d\cos\beta+\tfrac12\sin\beta,\qquad
              a_U>b_U>1/2,\quad a_U^2+b_U^2=R^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Hence \(a_U>R/\sqrt2\ge\sqrt2\). The following short exact bound
establishes \(\beta>99/200\) without changing that mean.
It is also the standalone local theorem in
SIX_CYCLE_RADIAL_SHARED_PORT_ANGLE_BOUND.md; its complete fixed
comparison is reproduced here to make the whole-row join explicit.
On \(R\ge2\), one has
\[
 d>\frac{18729}{20000},\qquad
 \frac{15}{4}-\left(\frac{38729}{20000}\right)^2
                         =\frac{64559}{400000000}>0.
\]
If \(0<\beta\le x:=99/200\), monotonicity and signed Taylor bounds give
\[
 \begin{split}
 a_U
 &<
 2-\frac{18729}{20000}
      (1-x^2/2+x^4/24-x^6/720)
      +\frac12(x-x^3/6+x^5/120)\\
 &=\frac{144739320907905703481}{102400000000000000000}
 <\frac{7071}{5000}<\sqrt2.
 \end{split}
\tag{26}
\]
The first final reserve is
\(74759092094296519/102400000000000000000>0\); the squared
last reserve is \(959/25000000>0\). This contradicts (25).
M's return is its actual opposite vertex, so (24) now contradicts
its diagonal length just as for A. All original M endpoint means
remain included in this local argument.

For O, the actual return is adjacent to its circle corner.
SIX_CYCLE_RADIAL_STRICT_PARTNER_SMALL_ANGLE_EXCLUSION.md rules out
the full O chart at \(\beta\le2/7\). Thus any remaining O has
\(\beta>2/7\), and (16) makes its horizontal circle-to-return
distance greater than one, contradicting its exact side length.

For T, both unique endpoints own the original return. The complete
SIX_CYCLE_ONE_RADIAL_LOW_DOUBLE_OWNER_RETURN_OWNER_EXCLUSION.md
already excludes this whole family, including all return means and
the originally allowed low coincident-application boundaries.
It proves \(A_T>8/5\) at any strict return owner, uses the true
return identity \(A_T+b_L=3\), and obtains \(H^2>7/10\) against
the accepted local T bound \(H<4/5\).

All four actual strict partner types have therefore been excluded.
This closes the entire original low-both-inner-axis row, with both
possible S placements and every linked mean and endpoint retained.
