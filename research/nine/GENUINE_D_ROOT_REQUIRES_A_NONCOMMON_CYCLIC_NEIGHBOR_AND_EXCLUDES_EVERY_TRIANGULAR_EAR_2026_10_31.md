## 2. Uniform noncommon-neighbor theorem

**Theorem 1.** There is an actual vertex U of Q, different from H and X, such that HU is an original positive edge and UX is not an original positive edge. More precisely, U can be chosen in one of these two full physical forms:

| Actual U | Retained conclusion |
|---|---|
| Positively circle-loaded | U is physically disjoint from X, not merely nonadjacent in the positive graph. |
| Unloaded | U has full positive degree two, with actual neighbors H and a body other than X. Its original transmission force and two applications remain intact. |

In graph language, the near vertex X cannot be adjacent to every other cyclic neighbor of H. The positive graph contains an induced three-vertex path X-H-U.

*Proof.* Suppose first that X is the unique source of the nontrivial connected moment DAG. Every finite nontrivial DAG has a sink, and such a sink U differs from X: a source that was also a sink would be isolated, contrary to connectedness and nontriviality. D, Section 6, gives the actual positive HU edge from U's full moment equation.

If U is loaded, it physically reaches the original circle. B forbids U touching X, since U is a Q vertex and therefore is not L. Thus UX is not positive. If U is unloaded, D's complete far-sink alternative gives its full degree two and expressly excludes X as its other positive neighbor. Again UX is not positive.

Suppose instead that X is the unique sink. Choose a source U of the finite nontrivial DAG. The same connectedness argument gives U different from X. D makes U a loaded positive H-neighbor. B then excludes any physical contact between U and X. This is the first form in the table.

Both complete near-extremum alternatives have been covered. In every case HX and HU are genuine original positive edges and UX is absent from the positive graph. No physical edge was deleted to obtain that absence. QED.

The argument does not assert that every H-neighbor has either form. It proves the existence of one exact original witness U. In the unloaded alternative, a possible zero-weight physical U-X contact is not excluded by the statement.

## 3. Every triangular true-three D root is excluded

**Theorem 2.** A genuine D leaf cannot have a root H whose full positive star is exactly

\[
H:\{L,A,B\},
\]

where AB is an original positive edge. The complete degrees and loads of A and B, every private graph and all remaining physical squares are arbitrary within the original datum.

*Proof.* The near-core theorem gives the actual positive HX pair with X in Q. Since H has no positive neighbors beyond L,A,B and X is not L, X is exactly A or B. Let U be the other member of the pair A,B. It is the only possible Q-neighbor of H distinct from X.

Theorem 1 requires this U to be nonadjacent to X in the original positive graph. But AB is exactly the positive UX edge. Contradiction. QED.

Thus a D leaf cannot be attached to a degree-two vertex of a cyclic block when that vertex's two cyclic neighbors are adjacent. This is the usual triangular-ear feature, stated here in terms of the full original star. The theorem permits an arbitrarily large chorded block beyond A and B. It does not require the triangle H-A-B to be an intact triangle: A and B may have large full degrees and many other forces.

No two-force equation is assigned to either A or B. If the opposite extremum of the auxiliary graph happens to be unloaded, its full degree two is first derived by D; it is not assumed from the triangle drawing.
