## 1. Entire uniform theorem

Work in the existing physical necessary-certificate setting. Let the
actual packing be contained in a circle of radius R, with

    sqrt(2)<R<2+sqrt(2).                                  (1)

Let V,W be actual units which each have exactly one physical circle
corner, denoted z_V,z_W. Their physical intersection has positive length.
They have entire true positive pair neighborhoods

    V: U,W;             W: V,T.                          (2)

Their original circle coefficients may be positive or zero. Their physical
unique-corner masks, rather than only the active weights, are hypotheses.
The external neighbors U,T have arbitrary degrees, loads, masks and other
forces; they may be the same actual unit. Neither is deleted or shortened.

Suppose both external physical intersections are singletons,

    U intersect V={N},  W intersect T={M},  N!=M.          (3)

**Theorem.** The common necessary physical certificate can be selected
before multiplier extraction so that neither external edge UV nor WT in
any actual pattern (1)--(3) has a free vertex/vertex normal.

This is a simultaneous common-certificate property, not impossibility of
an arbitrarily chosen different equilibrium. It is proved for every
actual mean on the internal positive-length VW side, including its two
endpoints. It does not declare that a mean on a shared external WT side
is a fixed singleton. It does not replace a physical two-corner W by a
unique-corner body when one of its circle coefficients is zero.

It is enough to treat a designated free UV normal at N; interchanging
V,W and U,T gives the other conclusion.

## 2. Fixed physical data and two elementary corner facts

A positive physical side segment gives one geometrically fixed unit
normal m directed from V to W. It is an own side axis of both actual
units. The opposite normal at W is -m. Let P be the original aggregate
VW application, an arbitrary point of its full actual shared segment.
No step below chooses or fixes P before extraction.

At a unique physical circle corner z of a contained unit, each own
coordinate of z is nonzero. Indeed if its coordinate in a side direction
were zero, the adjacent corner obtained by moving one unit in that side
direction would have squared norm R^2+1. This is impossible. In particular

    det(z_V,m)!=0,  det(z_W,m)!=0.                      (4)

For use with a free N, choose the inward signed own-coordinate chart of V
so that its unique far physical corner is z_V=(a,b) and

    V=[a-1,a] times [b-1,b], a,b>1/2,
    a^2+b^2=R^2.                                      (5)

The strict inequalities follow by comparing z_V to each adjacent corner:
physical uniqueness makes both comparisons strict. A contained unit cannot
share an actual circle point with another contained unit. One direct
local proof is that both inward edge directions at such a corner have
strictly negative scalar product with the radius: their other endpoints
have norm at most R, so z dot edge<=-1/2. Each inward right-angle sector
therefore lies strictly inside the same open radial half-plane. Two such
right-angle sectors cannot have disjoint interiors there. Thus N!=z_V.

Since a free N is a vertex of V, the only possible non-circle vertex
collinear with z_V is its opposite vertex. The adjacent determinants
are -b and a, both nonzero, while

    det((a-1,b-1),(a,b))=a-b.

Consequently the only collinear alternative is

    a=b=R/sqrt(2),
    N=(a-1,a-1)=(1-sqrt(2)/R)*z_V,
    |N|=R-sqrt(2).                                    (6)

This is exactly the radial-D inner vertex. It is handled geometrically
next, before any determinant is declared nontrivial.

## 3. The entire radial inner-vertex alternative is impossible here

Assume (6), keeping the original arbitrary shared application P and its
positive force magnitude nu. Torque about the actual N in the entire
V2 star leaves only the internal VW moment. The circle force is collinear
with N, and the external force acts at N. Hence

    det(P-N,m)=0.                                      (7)

The fixed m is one of V's four own side axes. For either of its two near
sides through N, equation (7) makes P=N. This is impossible because N
cannot be a point of the boundary pair V,W. The complete physical contact
bound gives

    |P|>=sqrt(R^2-1)-1 > R-sqrt(2)=|N|.                 (8)

The strict comparison holds for every R>sqrt(2). Both quantities to be
squared are positive, and

    R^2-1-(R+1-sqrt(2))^2
       =2*(sqrt(2)-1)*(R-sqrt(2))>0.

For either far m side, the minimum m projection of the actual W is
R/sqrt(2). Every unit square has width at least one in every unit direction.
Containment of W in the same radius-R disk therefore requires

    R/sqrt(2)+1<=R.

This contradicts R<2+sqrt(2) in (1). It includes the possible far-side
endpoint selected by (7), without any change to the shared mean.

These four axes exhaust the shared VW normal. Thus no actual full V2
star in (1)--(3) with a free N can have det(N,z_V)=0. The proof also
works when the original V circle coefficient is zero; neither force
equation nor moment term has been dropped on that account.

## 4. Whole force equations and cancellation of the internal mean

For the remaining nonradial N, let n be the actual outward unit normal
from V to U at N and g the actual outward normal from W to T at M. Write
mu,nu,delta>0 for the external UV, internal VW and external WT magnitudes,
and alpha,beta>=0 for the original unique-corner circle coefficients,
absorbing the harmless factor two into alpha,beta. The entire two force
equations are

    alpha*z_V+mu*n+nu*m=0,
    beta*z_W-nu*m+delta*g=0.                          (9)

Every original positive force is present. These are the true V2,W2 stars,
not equations obtained by deleting further neighbors.

The sum of their entire origin torque equations is

    mu*det(N,n)+delta*det(M,g)=0.                     (10)

The internal terms are nu*det(P,m) and -nu*det(P,m), and cancel exactly.
They cancel for the original P at every point of the shared interval,
including its endpoints. If the internal edge was obtained by aggregating
several original parallel side atoms, their opposite moments cancel
term by term before aggregation as well. No hypothesis that P is
geometrically fixed enters (9) or (10).

If det(z_W,g)=0, the second equation in (9) would put the positive vector
nu*m on the line spanned by z_W, contrary to det(z_W,m)!=0. Such an
actual positive block is therefore impossible by force alone. In every
remaining block we have

    delta/nu=det(z_W,m)/det(z_W,g).                   (11)

Taking the determinant of the first equation in (9) with z_V also gives

    nu/mu=-det(z_V,n)/det(z_V,m).                     (12)

The denominator in (12) is nonzero by the physical fact (4). Combining
(10)--(12) yields the necessary polynomial identity

    D(n,g):=
      det(z_V,m)*det(z_W,g)*det(N,n)
      -det(z_V,n)*det(z_W,m)*det(M,g)=0.              (13)

In particular (13) contains neither the internal application P nor its
unknown aggregate mean coordinate. No circle-load ratio was selected or
assumed in eliminating the complete equations.

## 5. The bad determinant is genuinely nontrivial

First fix an allowed g with det(z_W,g)!=0. Put

    S=det(z_W,m)*det(M,g)/det(z_W,g),
    A_0=N-[S/det(z_V,m)]*z_V.                        (14)

Then (13), divided by the fixed nonzero factor
det(z_V,m)*det(z_W,g), is exactly

    det(A_0,n)=0.                                    (15)

The vector A_0 is nonzero because the actual fixed N is not collinear
with z_V. As the designated free normal n varies on its actual open
strict interval, det(A_0,n) is a nonzero linear sine/cosine expression.
It cannot vanish on any open angular interval and has at most two zeros
on the whole unit circle.

If g is geometrically fixed and det(z_W,g)=0, the complete force exit
preceding (11) applies. No zero analytic function in that pattern is
incorrectly declared a generic condition.

If g is itself free, its physical singleton M is distinct from N by (3).
Its normal parameter can be varied independently of n within the actual
strict/matched domain. Choose any allowed g not parallel to z_W; an open
normal interval cannot consist of the two parallel directions. Equation
(15) then supplies an allowed n for which D(n,g)!=0. Hence (13) is not
identically zero on the full allowed product of free-normal intervals.

This argument is purely about the original physical points N,M, the two
unique physical circle corners, and the fixed internal side normal m.
It does not depend on eventual positive force magnitudes, a circle-weight
ratio, or any choice of a shared-side mean.

## 7. A complete small-vertex chain reduction

Here is a literal useful consequence in the ordinary nine-square radius

Let V be an actual circle-touching true positive degree-two unit with

    |N|<67/100.                                      (17)

**Conclusion.** In the common necessary certificate, VW must be physically
shared, and either W has two physical circle corners or WT must also have
positive physical length. In particular the entire feature with unique
physical W and singleton WT is excluded, at every original VW mean.

First derive the physical V mask. In the two inward own axes from its
actual vertex N, write N=(A,B) and V=[A,A+1]x[B,B+1]. If A<=0, then |A|<67/100
and the whole first coordinate interval has absolute value at most one.

    1+(167/100)^2=37889/10000<4,

contradicting its physical circle contact and (16). The case B<=0 is
identical. Hence A,B>0 and the unique physical V circle corner is its

    |P|>=sqrt(R^2-1)-1>=sqrt(3)-1>7/10>67/100.         (18)

Suppose now that W is physically unique and WT a physical singleton at
