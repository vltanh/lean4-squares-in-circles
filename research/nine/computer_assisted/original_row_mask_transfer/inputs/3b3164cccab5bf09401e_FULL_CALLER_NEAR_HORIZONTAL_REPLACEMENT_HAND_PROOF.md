# A near-horizontal replacement row excludes both outer-weak callers

November 4, 2026 (UTC). This is a conditional full-physical-caller theorem,
including its closed critical-radius face. It does not close an entire mask.

The new step is a short exact geometric blocker at the equality arrangement
of the accepted all-angle near-horizontal row theorem. It removes the old
requirements that BC be actual, that the original CD height gap be large,
or that the side of the disk containing G have already been determined.
All nine originals, all their corners and physical pairs, and their marked
strict comparisons remain in the caller throughout.

## 1. Statement and retained original caller

Let A,B,C,D,E,F,G,H,I be nine distinct independently oriented physical unit
squares with pairwise disjoint open interiors, contained in the closed disk
of squared radius

\[
Q=1105/256.
\]

Use one orthonormal witness frame. Retain all five original center-cone
comparisons AB, BC, CD, EF, FG. The links AB, EF and FG are actual horizontal
projection separations. The link CD is strictly weak:

\[
x_D-x_C\ge |y_D-y_C|,\qquad
x_D-x_C<h_C+h_D.                                      \tag{1}
\]

BC may be actual or strictly weak. Consequently the theorem applies to
both unchanged full-nine predicates `001/00` and `011/00`. Reflection and
simultaneous reversal of both chains give the corresponding `100/00` and
`110/00` uses, retaining the same nine physical squares.

**Replacement theorem.** Neither unchanged extra X in {H,I} can satisfy

\[
x_B\le x_X\le x_D,\qquad
|y_X-y_B|\le1/8,\qquad |y_D-y_X|\le1/8.                \tag{2}
\]

All angle signs, both physical separator owners, their two axes, all physical
contacts, and the closed boundaries in (2) are included. There is no claim
that a caller must satisfy (2); its complete complement is recorded below.

For the `011/00` caller the same height condition with X=C is also excluded.
That direct specialization has a simpler equality contradiction in Section 5.

## 2. A geometric blocker involving three aligned originals

The following lemma is independent of the containing disk. Its coordinates
are relative to the equality arrangement, and are used only after the actual
original frames have been proved aligned.

**Blocker lemma.** Let D, X and G be aligned unit squares with centers

\[
c_D=(1,0),\qquad c_X=(0,v),\qquad c_G=(1/2,-1),\qquad
0\le v\le1/8.                                        \tag{3}
\]

There is no fourth physical unit square C, of arbitrary independent
orientation, disjoint in open interior from those three squares, whose center
\((x,y)\) satisfies

\[
d:=1-x\ge |y|,\qquad d<h+1/2,                         \tag{4}
\]

where h is C's actual horizontal halfwidth. Its vertical halfwidth is the
same h because C is a square.

Write the larger and smaller absolute side components of C as
\(a\ge b\ge0\), with \(a^2+b^2=1\), and set

\[
h=(a+b)/2,\qquad j=(a-b)/2.
\]

These are the actual frame parameters of C. They satisfy

\[
1/2\le h\le1/\sqrt2<5/7,\quad 0\le j\le1/2,
\quad h+j=a\le1.                                     \tag{5}
\]

The center of a unit square contains an open disk of radius 1/2. Therefore
any two of these physical squares have center distance at least one.
For C,D, (4) gives

\[
1\le d^2+y^2\le2d^2,
\]

so \(d\ge1/\sqrt2>7/10\). On the other side, strict weakness in (4)
and (5) give \(d<17/14\). Hence

\[
-3/14<x<3/10,\qquad |x|<3/10.                        \tag{6}
\]

We now retain every height order of C and X.

### 2.1. C is below X

Suppose \(y<v\). C,X center separation and (6) imply

\[
v-y\ge\sqrt{1-x^2}>\sqrt{91/100}>19/20.
\]

The last comparison has squared reserve \(3/400\). Thus

\[
y<1/8-19/20=-33/40.
\]

The cone in (4) also gives \(y\ge-d>-17/14\). Consequently

\[
-3/14<y+1<7/40<3/14,
\qquad 0<1/2-x<5/7.
\]

The actual C,G centers therefore have

\[
|c_C-c_G|^2
=(x-1/2)^2+(y+1)^2
<(5/7)^2+(3/14)^2
=109/196<1,                                           \tag{7}
\]

contradicting their contained open disks. This case uses no selected
separating axis and preserves every orientation of C.

### 2.2. C is at or above X and lies to its right

Suppose \(y\ge v\) and \(x>0\). Since \(v\ge0\), the cone gives

\[
0\le y-v\le y\le1-x.
\]

By (6), \(0<x<1\). Hence

\[
|c_C-c_X|^2=x^2+(y-v)^2
\le x^2+(1-x)^2=1-2x(1-x)<1,                          \tag{8}
\]

again contradicting physical center separation.

### 2.3. C is at or above X and lies weakly to its left

Suppose \(y\ge v\) and \(x\le0\). A bottom vertex P of the actual
square C has coordinates \((x+\epsilon j,y-h)\), with
\(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\). This statement includes both chiralities;
when the bottom is a horizontal edge, either endpoint has this form.

Strict weakness in (4) says \(x>1/2-h\). Thus

\[
x+\epsilon j\ge x-j>1/2-h-j=1/2-a\ge-1/2.            \tag{9}
\]

Also \(x+\epsilon j\le x+j\le j\le1/2\). Equality with 1/2 would
require \(x=0,j=1/2\), whence \(a=1,b=0,h=1/2\), contradicting
\(x>1/2-h\). Therefore P is strictly within X's horizontal strip.
The center of C is also strictly within that strip by (6) and \(x\le0\).

The cone and the same strict weak inequality give

\[
P_y=y-h\le1-x-h<1/2\le v+1/2.                        \tag{10}
\]

The center of C has height \(y\ge v>v-1/2\). The segment from P to the
center of C therefore contains a point strictly between the heights
\(v-1/2\) and \(v+1/2\), at a parameter strictly between zero and one.
Both of its horizontal endpoints are strictly between -1/2 and 1/2,
so this point lies in the open interior of X. It also lies in the open
interior of C: a strict convex combination of C's center and any one of
its vertices is interior to C. This contradicts physical nonoverlap.

Cases 2.1–2.3 partition every possible C. In particular \(y=v\), \(x=0\),
\(v=0\), \(v=1/8\), and the extreme original angles are included. The only
strict premise used for the vertex obstruction is the original strict weak
comparison in (4), never a strengthened physical contact inequality.

## 3. The inherited all-angle replacement-row inequality

Sections 1–3 of the independently reviewed source
`WEAK_OUTER_NUMERIC_NEAR_HORIZONTAL_REPLACEMENT_ROW_HAND_TERMINAL.md`
prove the following physical inequality for three originals B,X,D satisfying
(2). With

\[
T_D=\max\{2\beta_D/23,
          [9\beta_D-41(1-\alpha_D)]/32\},
\]

one has

\[
x_D-x_B+h_B-h_D+T_D\ge2.                              \tag{11}
\]

Equality forces B,X,D aligned and both horizontal gaps BX,XD equal to one.
Their heights are not asserted equal. The two toll terms are separately
valid full-angle physical pair caps, so their maximum is valid; they are
not added. Every original owner, owned axis and chirality is retained in
that proof.

If one of the weak horizontal order faces in (2) is equality, the
corresponding vertical distance is at most 1/8 and center distance would
be less than one. Thus every physically possible configuration in (2)
has the strict horizontal order needed in the inherited pair argument.

Only the general row and its equality statement in the source's first
three sections are reused here. The old Section 4 original-`001/00` argument
and its G-height or large-CD-gap premises are not used.

## 4. The caps force exactly the blocker arrangement

Actual AB and (11) give

\[
P:=x_D-x_A-h_A-h_D+T_D\ge2.
\]

The original actual EF and FG give

\[
S:=x_G-x_E-h_E-h_G\ge2h_F\ge1.
\]

The ordinary A,E cap in the left direction, plus the physical D,G cap
with the valid toll T_D in the right direction, give

\[
46P+(27/2)S\le211/2=2\cdot46+27/2.                   \tag{12}
\]

All inequalities must therefore be equalities. At a strict subcritical
radius this is already impossible by the strict ordinary cap. At closed Q,
equality in (11) aligns B,X,D, gives \(x_D-x_X=1\), and makes T_D zero.
Consequently both endpoint caps are ordinary-cap equalities. Their accepted
equality classification aligns A,E,D,G; short-chain equality also aligns F.

For the right endpoint pair, in the original disk-centered coordinates,
the accepted equality theorem gives exactly

\[
D=(3/2,\sigma/16),\qquad G=(1,-15\sigma/16),
\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\}.                           \tag{13}
\]

A common vertical reflection, applied to every original frame and center,
preserves all original horizontal cones and bit comparisons. Choose the
orientation in which G is below D. Translate only for the local blocker
argument, taking D=(1,0). Then G=(1/2,-1) and X=(0,v), where (2) gives
\(-1/8\le v\le1/8\).

X,G are aligned physical squares with horizontal center gap 1/2. Their
horizontal projections overlap in their interiors, so vertical projection
separation is necessary. Since \(v+1\in[7/8,9/8]\), it follows that
\(v+1\ge1\), i.e. \(0\le v\le1/8\). This is precisely (3).

The unchanged original C is distinct from X. Its original cone and strict
weak CD comparison become (4), since D is now aligned and has halfwidth
1/2. C remains physically disjoint from X,D,G. The blocker lemma excludes
it. This contradiction proves the replacement theorem in Section 1.

No relation between the two independent signs in the left and right cap
equalities was assumed. No original was replaced by a virtual axis-aligned
object: the original B,X,D,A,E,F,G frames were first proved aligned by
the accepted equality arguments.

## 5. Direct C specialization and complete safe escape families

For the direct choice X=C, the general row (11), actual AB and actual EFG
still force equality in exactly the same way. Equality aligns B,C,D and
makes both horizontal gaps equal one. CD then has gap
\(1=h_C+h_D\), contradicting its strictly weak bit directly.
The blocker lemma is unnecessary in this direct case.

Thus a full `011/00` survivor has no original X in {C,H,I} satisfying (2).
The C order is already settled by the original cone comparisons and
physical center separation. Its complete necessary closed escape family is

\[
y_C-y_B\ge1/8,\quad y_B-y_C\ge1/8,\quad
y_D-y_C\ge1/8,\quad y_C-y_D\ge1/8.                    \tag{14}
\]

For each X in {H,I}, retain all six closed escapes independently:

\[
x_X\le x_B,\quad x_D\le x_X,\quad
y_X-y_B\ge1/8,\quad y_B-y_X\ge1/8,\quad
y_D-y_X\ge1/8,\quad y_X-y_D\ge1/8.                    \tag{15}
\]

The strict failures of (2) are contained in these closed halfspaces, which
retain shared equality faces. Every surviving caller lies in at least one
C member and one member for each of H and I. Their Cartesian product is a
complete optional 4×6×6 cover of the surviving original domain. This is
an exact necessary cover, not 144 proved exclusions or a new denominator
for the unchanged mask register.

If one explicitly applies a common vertical reflection to normalize
\(y_D\ge y_C\), the final member in (14) is impossible. The remaining
3×6×6 family still covers that explicitly normalized caller. No such
normalization is silently imposed on either existing original mask or
