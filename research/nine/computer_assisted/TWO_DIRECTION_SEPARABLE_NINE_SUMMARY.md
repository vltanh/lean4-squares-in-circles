**Proved:** if nine physical unit squares admit two fixed projection directions
separating every pair, then q>=1105/256. The directions may be nonperpendicular.
At equality they are perpendicular, and the complete equality parameter
domain for this cover class is stated in the new source.

The previous proof gaps are resolved explicitly:

1. Greene's theorem is used only for the maxima of unions of two chains and
   two antichains. It supplies a 4+3 chain pair in one of the two directions
   when either order has height four.
2. If both heights are at most three, longest-chain rank pairs are literally
   a bijection onto the 3-by-3 product. Equal-rank rows and columns then supply
   the required actual projection spans. No Greene diagram is assumed to
   have a geometric realization.
3. Both square support minima are derived as triangle bounding-rectangle
   minima. Their four combinations give q>=9/2 by printed elementary
   inequalities over the entire skew-angle interval.
4. The chain branch invokes the existing physical 4+3-chain theorem with
   exactly its stated hypotheses.

The theorem remains conditional on existence of a two-direction cover.
The known attaining example with two oblique upper squares admits no such
cover, as follows from the new equality conclusion and
[NO_GLOBAL_ORTHOGONAL_FRAME.md](../NO_GLOBAL_ORTHOGONAL_FRAME.md). Thus this is
a complete large-family theorem, not the unrestricted n=9 proof.
