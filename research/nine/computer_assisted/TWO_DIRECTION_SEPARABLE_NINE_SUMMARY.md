# Two arbitrary common directions: sharp nine-square theorem

Let Q=1105/256. Suppose nine interior-disjoint unit squares inside a radius-R disk are pairwise separated in projection along at least one of two fixed unit directions e,f (not necessarily perpendicular). Then R²>=Q.

Proof. Define the two projection interval orders. Each antichain in one is a chain in the other. For R²<=Q, no chain has length five. The existing physical four-plus-three chain theorem proves the bound whenever two disjoint chains of lengths four and three exist in either direction. Otherwise the maximum unions C₂ of two chains and A₂ of two antichains in either poset are at most six. Greene's conjugate-partition theorem leaves only the partition (3,3,3) of nine: the complete possibilities with largest row and column <=4 are (4,4,1) [C₂=8,A₂=5], (4,3,2) [7,6], (4,3,1,1) [7,6], (4,2,2,1) [6,7], (3,3,3) [6,6], (3,3,2,1) [6,7], (3,2,2,2) [5,8]. The e- and f-ranks are therefore in {1,2,3}. Each pair of ranks is unique, hence the nine squares form a 3x3 skew projection grid.

For this grid put e=(c,s),f=(c,-s), c>=s>=0, c²+s²=1, t=c+s and d=c-s. Summing the four extremal row/column span inequalities and the four disk support inequalities gives 2R(|e+f|+|e-f|)>=4+Σcorners[h(e)+h(f)+h(εe+ηf)]. For any unit square, elementary support minimization gives h(e)+h(f)+h(e+f)>=min(p1,p2), h(e)+h(f)+h(e-f)>=min(m1,m2), with p1=(3t+d)/2, p2=t(t+d), m1=(3t-d)/2, m2=t². To check all four possibilities set u=√2-t>=0, v=t-1>=0, w=d-u>=0 (since d²-u²=2tu>=0). Then Fij=2+pi+mj-3√2 t has exact nonnegative decompositions: F11=(3√2-4)+3(√2-1)u; F12=(3√2-4)/2+(√2-1)u+u²+w/2; F21=F12+vd; F22=u²+tw. Hence 4Rt>=6√2 t, R²>=9/2>Q, contradiction.

At R²=Q the same dichotomy forces a 4+3 chain. Its equality endpoint pair has centers displaced by (1/2,±1) and axis-aligned frames. Any separating direction n for that pair must satisfy |n_x/2±n_y|>=|n_x|+|n_y|, forcing n_x=0. Thus any two-direction cover at equality is orthogonal.
