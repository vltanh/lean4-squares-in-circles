## 1. What was lost in the previous pairwise bound

The baseline pair test upper-bounded a possible separator projection and
lower-bounded its required support width independently. Those two extrema can
occur at different square angles. The new test keeps the owning square's angle
in both terms until a single affine expression is maximized. It also encloses
the unit-axis arc by an endpoint/tangent triangle instead of an independent
component rectangle.

No original square constraint is removed from the physical root predicate.
The graph remains a necessary relaxation: compatibility does not establish
actual physical feasibility. All conclusions below exclude complete continuous
physical domains, not finitely sampled poses.

## 2. Complete rational frame arcs

As in the existing source, every physical square frame can be represented,
without moving the square, by

    u(t)=((1-t^2)/(1+t^2), 2t/(1+t^2)), v(t)=(-u_y,u_x),
    0 <= t <= 1.

Use closed intervals I_i=[i/k,(i+1)/k], with k=12 or 24 in the two graph families.
Their endpoints and all shared boundaries are included. For each i the entire
unit-circle arc lies in the triangle with rational vertices

    E_i=((k^2-i^2)/(k^2+i^2),2ki/(k^2+i^2)),
    T_i=((k^2-i(i+1))/(k^2+i(i+1)), k(2i+1)/(k^2+i(i+1))),
    E_(i+1).                                                (1)

E_i and E_(i+1) are unit vectors. T_i is the intersection of their tangent
lines, obtained by solving E_i.p=E_(i+1).p=1. The circle arc lies below these
tangents and above its chord, proving its inclusion in the triangle. A linear
functional of the frame components therefore attains an upper bound at one
of these three vertices.

**T_i is not a physical unit axis.** It is used only to bound an affine
expression whose geometric derivation is established on actual unit axes.
Applying a unit-normal separating theorem directly to T_i would be invalid;
that is not done here.

## 3. The coupled endpoint inequality

For two unit squares with relative angle delta, actual interior disjointness
implies that one of their four axes n satisfies

    2 |n.(c_j-c_i)| >= 1+cos(delta)+|sin(delta)|.              (2)

The frames are represented in the first quadrant, so delta lies in
[-pi/2,pi/2]. The complete finite-axis proof and weak-contact convention are
in `UNRESTRICTED_MODEL_AND_ROW_CERTIFICATE.md`.

Take distinct closed frame intervals I_i,I_j. Their angular order fixes the
sign epsilon of the relative sine: epsilon=+1 for j>i and -1 for j<i. For a
fixed owning angle theta, the function

    cos(phi-theta)+epsilon sin(phi-theta)

is concave in the partner angle phi on the whole partner interval: it is
nonnegative there and its second derivative is its negative. Its minimum
therefore occurs at an interval endpoint w=(a,b). At that endpoint it equals

    (a+epsilon b) cos(theta)+(b-epsilon a) sin(theta).        (3)

For each sign and each u/v choice, subtract (3) from the left side of (2).
For fixed centers this is affine in (cos(theta),sin(theta)). By (1), its maximum
is bounded at E_i,T_i,E_(i+1). Thus any actual feasible separator must survive
at least one of these rational tests, with a partner endpoint supplying the
minimum. Maximizing over the finite owner and endpoint choices does not
require the two original extrema to be attained at the same unrelated angles.

For a rational arc-triangle vertex p=(A/q,B/q), define

    h(i,p,j)=min_w [1+p_x(a+epsilon b)+p_y(b-epsilon a)]       (4)

when i!=j. When i=j, use h=2, since cos(delta)+|sin(delta)|>=1 throughout.
The minimum in (4) is only over the two rational partner endpoint vectors.
Both ownership choices must be retained: either square can supply n.
Both p and its quarter-turn are tested, with both projection signs.

To check the logical direction explicitly, an actual separator makes the
actual left-minus-right expression nonnegative. Replacing the partner support
by its endpoint minimum can only increase this expression. Its maximum on
the owning arc is then bounded above by the endpoint/tangent triangle maximum.
Consequently some enumerated test must remain nonnegative. Rejecting all
such tests therefore excludes the entire actual pair.

## 4. Exact polygon and projection arithmetic

The unchanged disk-support construction gives a closed rational outer polygon
for every position-triangle/frame-interval state. It contains every physical
center in that state at q<=Q. Empty polygons have already been excluded by
necessary containment inequalities. All nonempty segments and points remain.
The baseline proof is `DISK_SUPPORT_CERTIFICATES.md`.

Let D=2^20. The vertices of each rational polygon have outward rectangles
with integer coordinates divided by D. For each arc vertex p=(A/q,B/q),
compute lower and upper projections on p and its perpendicular using these
vertex rectangles. A linear functional on a convex polygon is bounded at its
vertices; outward rectangles only enlarge the possible values.

If L_p(P) and U_p(P) denote outward integer projection bounds at scale D^2,
then an upper bound on D^2 times the absolute projection of a displacement
between center polygons P,P' is

    max(U_p(P')-L_p(P), U_p(P)-L_p(P')).                     (5)

Retain the state pair if some owner, tangent/endpoint vertex and u/v choice
satisfies twice (5) at least floor(D^2 h). Equality is retained. Finally intersect
with the old disk graph's edges. Every actual pair satisfied both necessary
tests, so it is not removed by this intersection.

The producer evaluates a directed owner predicate and then takes its union
with its transpose. The independent implementation evaluates both owners
directly in each block. The independent tangent vertices are obtained by
solving two rational line equations rather than using formula (1). Projection
bounds use midpoint/radius arithmetic rather than the producer's endpoint
sign selection. The complete resulting adjacency rows agree.

### Integer overflow bounds

Every outward position coordinate has absolute value at most 3D. Every
unreduced numerator A,B has magnitude at most q, and q<=2k^2<=1152. A numerator
projection has magnitude at most 6qD. Multiplication by D is bounded by
6*1152*D^2<2^63. After outward division by q, projection spans and their doubles
remain far below 2^63. Rational threshold calculations use unbounded integers.
The NumPy int64 operations are exact under these explicitly checked bounds;
there are no floating-point proof comparisons.

## 5. Joint completion by finite exhaustive proof trees

For each required coarse cell c, initialize D_c to all its retained states.
An actual packing selects one state in every D_c, and each actual pair has a
compatibility edge. The sound propagation rule is

    D_c <- {v in D_c : some w in D_s is compatible with v}.  (6)

The actual state cannot be deleted: the actual state in source cell s supplies
its partner and survives by induction. Thus an empty domain contradicts the
existence of a physical packing of the occupied-cell pattern.

Pairwise propagation alone can stop with nonempty domains even when no joint
completion exists. At such a node the certificate splits one cell's current
domain at an explicit integer state-ID cutoff T:

    D_c^left={v in D_c:v<=T},    D_c^right={v in D_c:v>T}.    (7)

Both children are nonempty, disjoint, and have union exactly D_c. Other domains
are unchanged. The parent closes only if both children have complete proofs.
Closed physical states overlapping at boundaries cause no omission: every
actual state belongs to one of the finite ID branches, and all IDs are covered.
This is not a split that assumes an actual physical angle or coordinate is
strictly away from a boundary.

The proof forest uses postorder references. Every child index is smaller than
its parent. Each leaf must exhibit an actually empty domain after its recorded
propagation rows. The independent verifier recomputes each candidate's possible
partners, checks every before/after count, validates both complete children,
and requires all nodes to have been replayed. A compatible assignment, missing
child or exhausted search budget is not a proof and is rejected.

Induction on node index, using (6)-(7), proves each accepted core is impossible
for every physical assignment throughout q<=Q. This gives the real-geometric
meaning of the finite decision forest.
