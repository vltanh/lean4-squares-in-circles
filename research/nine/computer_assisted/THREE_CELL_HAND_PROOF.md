# A three-cell obstruction: complete hand derivation with exact rational tables

**Theorem proved here.** Three independently oriented unit squares with pairwise
disjoint open interiors, all contained in the closed disk of squared radius
`Q = 1105/256`, cannot have their centers in cells **0, 3, 7** of the existing
nineteen-hexagon cover, respectively. The same holds for every common rotation
or reflection of these three specified cells. This is not an assertion that
three squares cannot fit anywhere in the disk.

The proof below uses ten closed orientation cases, four containment directions,
and explicit rational inequalities. It does **not** use a fine position mesh,
a pose-compatibility graph, a numerical optimization result, or the unverified
two-direction draft. Generate the full arithmetic appendix
`THREE_CELL_EXACT_DUAL_TABLES.md` using `render_three_cell_tables.py`; its
machine-readable equivalent is `three_cell_rational_table.json`, generated by
`make_three_cell_table.py`. Both are also in the continuation archive. The
independent standard-library checker `verify_three_cell_table.py` reconstructs
the geometry and checks every entry. No separate human reviewer or kernel
verification is claimed.

## 1. The three physical cells

Put `s=49/100`. For integers q,r, the cell center is

    L(q,r) = (s/2)(3q, sqrt(3)(q+2r)).

The closed cell is the regular hexagon of side s about L(q,r), with offsets

    (s/2)(2,0), (s/2)(1,sqrt(3)), (s/2)(-1,sqrt(3)),
    (s/2)(-2,0), (s/2)(-1,-sqrt(3)), (s/2)(1,-sqrt(3)).

The three axial coordinates and physical centers are:

| Cell | (q,r) | Physical cell center |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | (-2,0) | (-3s,-s sqrt(3)) |
| 3 | (-1,-1) | (-3s/2,-3s sqrt(3)/2) |
| 7 | (0,-2) | (0,-2s sqrt(3)) |

Let the square in cell 3 be the middle square. Write its center as c and
use scaled coordinates `z=(X,Y)=1000c` throughout the tables. No translation
of the disk or change of individual square orientation is performed.

## 2. Ten closed orientation intervals cover every square

A quarter-turn of a square's coordinate frame leaves the physical square
unchanged. Choose its first unit vector in the closed first quadrant. It then
has the rational parametrization

    u(t)=((1-t^2)/(1+t^2), 2t/(1+t^2)),  v(t)=(-u_y,u_x),
    0 <= t <= 1.

The inverse is `t=u_y/(1+u_x)`, with positive denominator. For each square,
cover its parameter by the ten closed intervals

    I_i=[i/10,(i+1)/10],  i=0,...,9.

A boundary parameter may use either adjacent interval. There is no generic
orientation or angular sampling assumption. Write E_i=u(i/10).

The entire unit-circle arc for I_i lies in the triangle with vertices E_i,
E_(i+1), and

    T_i=((100-i(i+1))/(100+i(i+1)),
         10(2i+1)/(100+i(i+1))).                           (1)

To prove this, intersect the two endpoint tangent lines `E_i.p=1` and
`E_(i+1).p=1`; solving them gives (1). The arc lies below both supporting
tangents and above its chord. Those three halfplanes enclose precisely the
stated triangle. Hence any affine function of the axis coordinates is bounded
above by its largest value at these three rational vertices.

**Important:** T_i need not have unit norm. It is used to bound an affine
function, never substituted into a theorem requiring a physical unit axis.

## 3. Four exact containment inequalities per interval

For a fixed orthonormal square frame u,v, containment in the radius-squared Q
disk is equivalent to

    (|c.u|+1/2)^2+(|c.v|+1/2)^2 <= Q.                       (2)

This follows by maximizing the four original corner norms. A disk and a square
are convex, so containing all four corners contains the whole square.

Let `R=sqrt(Q)`, `rho=sqrt(1041)/16-1/2`. For a vector n, set
`a=max(|n.u|,|n.v|)`, `b=min(|n.u|,|n.v|)`, and `ell=|n|`.
The exact support of the center region defined by (2) is

    h(n)=rho*a                    when 2R*b <= ell,
    h(n)=R*ell-(a+b)/2           when 2R*b >= ell.           (3)

The two formulas agree at their shared boundary. To derive (3), by sign and
coordinate symmetries maximize `a x+b y` for x,y>=0 subject to
`(x+1/2)^2+(y+1/2)^2<=Q`. Put `U=x+1/2`, `V=y+1/2`. The unconstrained disk
maximum has `(U,V)=R(a,b)/ell`. It is feasible exactly when `2R b>=ell`.
Otherwise the constraint `V>=1/2` is active and the maximum occurs at
`V=1/2`, `U=sqrt(Q-1/4)`. These give the displayed alternatives.

Over an entire orientation interval, the largest support is attained at an
endpoint or an interior alignment of n with a frame axis. Indeed, if delta
is the angle to the nearest square axis, `0<=delta<=pi/4`, the normalized
support is either `rho cos(delta)` or `R-(cos(delta)+sin(delta))/2`. Both
pieces are nonincreasing and meet continuously. The minimum angular distance
to an axis on an interval occurs at an endpoint or at an actual alignment.
Alignment is tested by rational determinant signs, not approximate angles.

Use only the four directions

    n_0=(-2,-1), n_1=(-1,-2), n_2=(-1,0), n_3=(1,-3).

For every square in interval I_i, (3) gives `n_j.z<=B_ij`, where:

| i | B_i0 | B_i1 | B_i2 | B_i3 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 3275 | 3146 | 1517 | 4761 |
| 1 | 3384 | 3077 | 1487 | 4796 |
| 2 | 3392 | 3119 | 1425 | 4784 |
| 3 | 3368 | 3215 | 1386 | 4635 |
| 4 | 3250 | 3337 | 1378 | 4467 |
| 5 | 3146 | 3391 | 1402 | 4370 |
| 6 | 3088 | 3392 | 1438 | 4349 |
| 7 | 3073 | 3370 | 1481 | 4400 |
| 8 | 3102 | 3293 | 1509 | 4477 |
| 9 | 3146 | 3207 | 1517 | 4570 |

All bounds are outward integers at scale D=1000. For an endpoint, the first
case in (3) is bounded by

    ceil(sqrt(1041*a^2*D^2/256)) - floor(a*D/2),

and the second by

    ceil(sqrt(1105*ell^2*D^2/256)) - floor((a+b)*D/2).

At axis alignment use

    ceil(sqrt(1041*ell^2*D^2/256)) - floor(sqrt(ell^2*D^2/4)).

Take the maximum of the applicable endpoint/alignment bounds. The branch
condition is `1105 b^2<=64 ell^2`. Every number under a square root is rational;
ceiling and floor claims are checked by integer square comparisons. Thus the
table can be reproduced without floating arithmetic.

## 4. Small rational outer polygons for the coarse cells

Use the elementary exact enclosure

    433/250 < sqrt(3) < 1733/1000.                           (4)

For each of the six vertices of a physical cell, bound its scaled x-coordinate
by its floor and ceiling, and its scaled y-coordinate using both endpoints of
(4), then floor/ceiling outward. Take the convex hull of these small vertex
rectangles. This is an outer polygon of the entire closed cell, not a sample
of points in it. Intersect with the four containment halfplanes from Section 3.
Denote the result for cell c and interval I_i by P_(c,i).

Every actual scaled center in that cell and orientation interval belongs to
P_(c,i). All inequalities are weak and all resulting segments and vertices
are retained. The full line lists and exact rational vertices of the thirty
polygons are printed in the generated appendix. They can be checked by
intersecting pairs of lines and testing all inequalities. Boundedness ensures
that every nonempty intersection is the convex hull of its feasible vertices,
also for a segment or a singleton. All thirty polygons here are nonempty.

## 5. Complete coupled separator alternatives

For two physical interior-disjoint squares, a separating axis belongs to one
of their two frames. This follows from the facet normals of the Minkowski sum
of their centered squares: its edges are parallel to edges of one summand.
Touching is included by a weak inequality. For a unit separating axis n and
relative frame angle delta, the exact necessary inequality is

    2 |n.(c_3-c_c)| >= 1+cos(delta)+|sin(delta)|.             (5)

Fix the middle interval i and neighboring interval j. First suppose i!=j.
Their angular order is fixed, including their possible common endpoint. Set
`epsilon=+1` when j>i and `epsilon=-1` otherwise. For fixed owning angle theta,
the support expression is concave in the partner angle on that whole interval.
Its minimum therefore occurs at a partner endpoint w=(a,b), where it becomes

    (a+epsilon*b) cos(theta) + (b-epsilon*a) sin(theta).      (6)

Subtract (6) from the signed left side of (5). For fixed positions and chosen
partner endpoint, the result is affine in `(cos(theta),sin(theta))`. Section 2
bounds it on the three endpoint/tangent vertices of the owning interval.
When i=j, the uniform lower bound `cos(delta)+|sin(delta)|>=1` gives the
same construction with threshold 2.

The owning square may be either member of the pair. Thus enumerate both
owners, each of its three rational arc-triangle vertices p=(A/q,B/q), both
axes p and its perpendicular, both signs, and the two partner endpoints.
For different intervals define

    H=min_partner_endpoints [1+p_x(a+epsilon*b)+p_y(b-epsilon*a)],

and use H=2 for coincident intervals. Let `(a,b)` temporarily denote a signed
integer axis numerator from `(A,B)` or `(-B,A)`. If the neighboring scaled
center belongs to P_(c,j), a necessary alternative for z is

    a X+b Y >= q D H/2 + min_{w in P_(c,j)} (a,b).w.         (7)

The minimum is at a printed rational polygon vertex. Divide both sides by
the positive gcd of a,b. For repeated primitive normals retain the smallest
right side over all alternatives: this weakens their union and cannot lose
an actual packing. Repeat over all ten possible neighbor intervals.

This produces exactly eighty merged alternatives for each i and each neighbor
cell c=0,7. They cover every physical separator, not only separators between
selected numerical poses. The complete eighty-row lists are in the appendix.

## 6. Two linear consequences contradict each other in every interval

For a given middle interval i, membership in P_(3,i) together with at least
one alternative (7) for neighbor cell 0 implies `e_i.z<=U_i`.
Membership together with at least one alternative for neighbor cell 7 implies
`e_i.z>=L_i`. The exact implications are:

| i | e_i | U_i: forced by square in cell 0 | L_i: forced by square in cell 7 | L_i-U_i |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | (-1,0) | 641 | 642 | 1 |
| 1 | (-1,1) | -478 | -463 | 15 |
| 2 | (-1,1) | -424 | -366 | 58 |
| 3 | (-1,1) | -410 | -355 | 55 |
| 4 | (-5,4) | -969 | -898 | 71 |
| 5 | (-3,2) | -244 | -175 | 69 |
| 6 | (-2,1) | 111 | 175 | 64 |
| 7 | (-2,1) | -13 | 67 | 80 |
| 8 | (-2,1) | -84 | 23 | 107 |
| 9 | (-1,0) | 596 | 619 | 23 |

In every row, `U_i<L_i`. A physical middle square would therefore satisfy

    L_i <= e_i.z <= U_i < L_i,

a contradiction. This proves the three-cell theorem, provided the finite
linear implications above are checked. The next section gives their full
arithmetic certificate rather than treating them as an optimizer assertion.

## 7. Exact nonnegative two-line certificates for all table implications

Write each middle-domain inequality as `a_r.X+b_r.Y<=c_r`. For a merged
separator alternative `aX+bY>=B`, append its equivalent line
`-aX-bY<=-B`. Each appendix row has at most two nonnegative rational weights
lambda and mu. Multiplying the indicated domain/branch inequalities by these
weights and adding proves exactly the row's assertion.

There are only two kinds of rows. An **impossible** alternative has a weighted
sum of middle-domain lines with normal `(a,b)` and constant strictly less
than B. It cannot hold. A **bound** alternative has a weighted sum of
middle-domain and branch lines with normal e_i and constant at most U_i,
or with normal -e_i and constant at most -L_i. It gives the required consequence.

The full appendix contains **1,600 rows**:
**1,204 impossible alternatives and 396 bound alternatives**. There are ten
middle intervals, two neighboring cells and eighty merged alternatives per
pair. Each row is verified by rational multiplication, two component equalities,
nonnegativity of its weights, and a weak or strict rational comparison. The
domain/vertex and separator tables make the calculation reproducible without
a numerical optimizer or a trusted pose graph.

The independent checker obtains center polygons by supporting-line intersections,
solves the tangent equations rather than using formula (1), reconstructs all
separator alternatives, and checks the supplied two-line identities. Its exact
arithmetic passes. Corrupting the support table, deleting an orientation case
or separator alternative, inserting a negative weight, changing a separator
threshold, or removing the strict table gap is rejected.
