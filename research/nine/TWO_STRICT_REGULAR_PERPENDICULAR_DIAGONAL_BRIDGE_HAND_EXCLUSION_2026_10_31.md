## 5. The four original diagonal directions all contradict (12)

The following arguments retain whole actual unit sides and the unchanged
points T,U. The interval between two points of any unit side has length at

### 5.1. u=b

Both K and T lie on B's minimum-b side, so

    (K-T).a<1.

L maximizes a on C, so (U-L).a<=0. Taking the a projection of (8),
and using a.b=0, gives

    Delta.a=(K-T).a+(T-U).a+(U-L).a<1.

This contradicts (12).

### 5.2. u=a

K minimizes b on B, so (K-T).b<=0. Both U and L lie on C's
maximum-a side, and L is genuinely interior, so (U-L).b<1.
The b projection of (8) is zero. Hence

    Delta.b=(K-T).b+(T-U).b+(U-L).b<1,

again contradicting (12).

### 5.3. u=-b

T maximizes b on B, whereas K minimizes b; b is an own unit side
axis, so K.b=T.b-1. U minimizes b on C, hence L.b>=U.b.
Equation (8) now gives T.b-U.b=-sqrt(2). Therefore

    Delta.b=K.b-L.b<=-sqrt(2)-1<0,

contrary to (12).

### 5.4. u=-a

U minimizes a on C and L maximizes it, so L.a=U.a+1. T maximizes
a on B, hence K.a<=T.a. Equation (8) gives T.a-U.a=-sqrt(2).
Thus

    Delta.a=K.a-L.a<=-sqrt(2)-1<0,

contrary to (12). The four possibilities (11) are exhausted.
