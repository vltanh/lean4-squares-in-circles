# The entire nonaligned five-body Y family has capacity at most eight

**Theorem (`Y.ALL`).** Put

\[
D=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:|x|^2\le17/4\},\qquad
S(w)=\{tw+sJw:1\le t\le2,\ -1/2\le s\le1/2\}
\]

for every unit direction \(w\). Let

\[
0<\alpha<1,\qquad 0<\theta<\pi/2,
\]

and define

\[
e=(0,1),\qquad u=(\cos\theta,-\sin\theta),\qquad
v=(-\sin\theta,-\cos\theta).
\]

Consider the five physical unit squares

\[
\begin{aligned}
H&=[-\alpha,1-\alpha]\times[0,1],\\
K_0&=\{au+bv:0\le a,b\le1\},\\
&S(e),\quad S(u),\quad S(v).
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{1}
\]

At most three further independently oriented unit squares in \(D\)
can have open interiors disjoint from all five squares in (1) and from
one another. In particular, no member of this entire continuous Y
family can be completed to nine physical unit squares.

The proof uses the complete capacity theorems
`WEDGE_135_CAPACITY_ONE.md` and `HALF_POCKET_TRIPLE_RIGIDITY.md`.
All parameters in the displayed ranges are retained; no shared frame
for the extra squares and no motion of the physical packing are assumed.

## 1. The actual core screens three complete rays

Let \(T\) be any additional square. We first prove that its open
interior avoids all of the positive rays through \(e,u,v\), including
the origin.

The segment \([0,e]\) lies in \(H\), while \([0,u]\) and
\([0,v]\) are sides of \(K_0\). If an interior point of \(T\)
lay anywhere on one of these segments, a small open ball inside
\(T\) would intersect the corresponding core-square interior.
This remains true at the origin, at the segment endpoints, and on
the boundary sides of \(K_0\); every neighborhood of a point of a
closed square meets its open interior. Thus the portions with radial
parameter \(0\le t\le1\) are screened by the physical core.

For each direction \(w\in\{e,u,v\}\), the segment
\(\{tw:1\le t\le2\}\) is contained in the radial terminal
\(S(w)\), and the same open-ball argument excludes it from
\(\operatorname{int}T\). Finally, the outer-face screen
`RADIAL_SECTOR_POCKET_CAPACITY.md`, Lemma P1.1, gives

\[
w\cdot p<2\qquad\text{for every }p\in T
\]

whenever \(T\) avoids \(S(w)\). It excludes the entire remainder
\(\{tw:t\ge2\}\) of the ray. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{int}T\cap
\bigl(\mathbb R_{\ge0}e\cup\mathbb R_{\ge0}u
                 \cup\mathbb R_{\ge0}v\bigr)=\varnothing.
\tag{2}
\]

This is a physical barrier conclusion. A center-only sector condition
has not been substituted for full-square containment.

## 2. Every extra square belongs wholly to one of two pockets

The complement of the three rays in (2) has exactly three connected
open angular sectors. The open interior of a square is connected and
nonempty, so (2) places \(\operatorname{int}T\) in one such sector.
Taking its closure places the whole closed square \(T\) in the
corresponding closed origin wedge. This argument covers squares
touching a boundary ray: only their boundaries may do so, and they
remain assigned by their nonempty interiors. A square interior cannot
be assigned to a ray or to two sectors.

The sector from \(v\) to \(u\) has aperture \(\pi/2\). Its wedge
is exactly the quadrant generated by the orthonormal side directions
\(u,v\) of \(K_0\). The right-angle terminal-pocket theorem
`RADIAL_SECTOR_POCKET_CAPACITY.md`, Lemma P2.1, says that a unit square
in this wedge and in \(D\), avoiding the two boundary terminals
\(S(u),S(v)\), must be the origin-vertex square \(K_0\) itself.
That is impossible for an additional square whose open interior is
disjoint from \(K_0\).

Thus every additional square lies wholly in one of the other two
closed pockets:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{pocket}&\text{boundary terminal directions}&\text{aperture}\\
\hline
P_R&u,e&\pi/2+\theta\\
P_L&e,v&\pi-\theta.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{3}
\]

Every square assigned to a pocket still avoids its two actual boundary
terminals and still lies in the original disk. Distinct additional
squares have disjoint open interiors regardless of their assignments.

## 3. The two uniform capacities finish the entire family

Both apertures in (3) are strictly less than \(\pi\), and their sum
is \(3\pi/2\). Therefore one is at most \(3\pi/4\).

`WEDGE_135_CAPACITY_ONE.md` proves that this smaller pocket holds at
most one additional square, uniformly for all its apertures and all
square orientations. The strict-pocket consequence of
`HALF_POCKET_TRIPLE_RIGIDITY.md`, Section 5, proves that the other
pocket holds at most two additional squares, because its aperture is
strictly less than \(\pi\).

Hence the total number of additional squares is at most

\[
                              1+2=3.
\]

Together with the five core squares, the total is at most eight.
This proves `Y.ALL` for every
\((\alpha,\theta)\in(0,1)\times(0,\pi/2)\).
\(\square\)

## Scope of the completed theorem

The proof closes the whole physical Y completion gate identified in
`REMAINING_TREE_TWO_FAMILY_REDUCTION.md`. The core is allowed to exist;
the excluded event is its completion by four arbitrary additional
squares. The side-origin parameter \(\alpha\) is used only in the
actual ray screen, so no parameter subcase or limiting normalization
has been omitted.

Combined with an independent whole-family `P.ALL` theorem and the
exhaustive P/Y reduction, this closes the positive-tree completion
branch. It does not by itself prove a global cyclic-component theorem
or constitute Lean/kernel verification.
