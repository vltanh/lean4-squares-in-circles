# Three genuine leaves cannot coexist with a true degree-two leaf root

Date: October 27, 2026.

**Status: complete uniform hand original-component theorem, submitted
for full root and separate independent review.** No shared metadata
or coarse count is changed.

Keep all nine original physical units and the same compatible
selected physical certificate in

    79/40<=R<=sqrt(1105)/16,
    d=sqrt(R^2-1/4)-1,       9/10<d<61/60,
    epsilon=max(d-1,0)<1/60.

Suppose one connected positive component has at least three genuine
leaves, and one genuine leaf L has an actual root X of true positive
degree two. Every other original body, degree, force, normal, actual
mean, physical circle mask and individual zero weight remains.

**Theorem.** This complete original situation is impossible.

In particular this closes every one-short-arm plus two-direct-leaf
forest of C1.4.b4, for every choice or repetition of its cyclic roots
and every four-body block profile. The local theorem needs no cyclic
block or prescribed degrees at the other roots. Other four-bridge
forest classes are not closed by this source alone.

## 1. Exact notation and radial geometry

Write H for X's other actual positive neighbor, so the original
short arm is H--X--L. H is not a genuine leaf: otherwise the entire
connected component would be the three-body path H--X--L with just
two leaves, contrary to the hypothesis.

Choose two further distinct original genuine leaves E,F and let
A,B be their actual roots. They are distinct from X, because X's
only other neighbor is the nonleaf H. A and B may equal each other,
and either may equal H. All three original leaves L,E,F are distinct.

X is physically clear as a genuine leaf root and is a true unloaded
normal transmitter. If e is L's actual radial direction, its two
unchanged applications are

    p_XL=r_L e,       p_HX=(r_L-w)e,
    r_L=d for S,       r_L=R-sqrt(2) for D,
    w in {1,sqrt(2)}.                                  (1)

The original D/diagonal alternative is excluded by the retained
strict point-contact convention. The complete remaining types are
D/side, S/diagonal and S/side, with their actual side means kept.

Every genuine root contains O when d<=1 and meets D_epsilon when
d>1, by its actual radial inward unit segment; a D root always
contains O openly. A root supporting two distinct genuine leaves
has the purely physical central-disk alternatives of
TRIANGLE_THREE_BRIDGES_DOUBLE_LEAF_ROOT_EXCLUSION.md, Section 1:
origin-interior for d<1, all of D_epsilon strictly interior for
d>1, and at d=1 either origin-interior or an origin vertex with
the two actual radial S leaves on its two own side rays.

## 3. The complete critical repeated-root case

Let d=1, R^2=17/4, and A=B. Both A and X contain O closed, so
neither can contain it openly. The double-leaf theorem makes
A an origin-vertex unit, with E,F its two genuine radial S leaves
on its own side rays.

The true X cannot be D/side, since that would be origin-interior.
It cannot be S/diagonal either: its two inward own coordinates of
O are both 1/sqrt(2), strictly between zero and one. Therefore it
is S/side, and its actual parent application from (1) is

    p_HX=O.                                            (2)

Thus H also contains O closed. If H=A, the actual double-leaf
root A is positively adjacent to the true unloaded degree-two
transmitter X. This is exactly the full original domain of
TWO_LEAF_ROOT_NO_UNLOADED_TRANSMITTER.md. Its complete critical
P/Y completion proof excludes all actual arm angles and means.

Assume H differs from A. Then A,X,H are three distinct actual
closed-origin units. None has O in its open interior, since
another such unit is present. A is an origin vertex. If all
three are origin vertices, THREE_VERTEX_ORIGIN_CAPACITY.md gives
total physical capacity at most eight.

Otherwise exactly one of X,H has a side-interior origin. Its
occupied half-plane and the other two right-angle sectors have
total angle 2pi and must exactly tile the full turn. Hence their
actual own frames align. There are exactly two possibilities.

    X=[0,1]e+[b-1,b]Je,       0<b<1.

This exhausts every critical owner and origin-sector case, while

## 4. At high radius all three leaves are S and X is a side transmitter

Now d>1. Section 2 has already excluded A=B, so X,A,B are three
distinct genuine roots, each meeting D_epsilon. If any of L,E,F

D_LEAF_ROOT_TWO_NEAR_ORIGIN_UNITS_EXCLUSION.md would apply to

allows arbitrary degrees and forces at all three roots. Thus
all three genuine leaves are S.

X cannot use diagonal width. In its own coordinates an S/diagonal
root has the four origin-side margins

    d/sqrt(2), d/sqrt(2),
    1-d/sqrt(2), 1-d/sqrt(2).

Each is greater than epsilon: the former exceeds 1/2, and the

D_epsilon would lie strictly inside X and overlap the actual

Hence the unchanged arm is S/side with

    p_HX=epsilon e.                                    (3)

In particular H meets D_epsilon. In fact it contains O: from

owned side this is its unit width; for a strictly exposed vertex,
the two inward coordinate displacements have magnitudes at most
one throughout that segment. Apply this to (3).

If H is different from both A and B, the four original distinct

    H=A,       B distinct from H,X.                    (4)

H now supports its actual direct S leaf E and its actual short

## 5. The full high-radius leaf-and-arm chart has only one available near point

Rotate (3) to p_HX=(epsilon,0). The complete force-free chart is
the geometric part of Section 1 of
TRIANGLE_THREE_BRIDGES_MIXED_ARMS_S_EXCLUSION.md, before any
triangle or root-force equation is invoked. We restate it to
make the arbitrary-degree scope explicit.

Suppose H did not own the arm axis. Its actual support point
(epsilon,0) would then strictly expose a vertex. In its own
inward coordinate frame the two coordinates of O are positive
and below epsilon. The near vertex has norm epsilon; both
adjacent vertices have squared norms below 1+epsilon^2<d^2;
and the opposite vertex has squared norm at least

    2+epsilon^2-2sqrt(2)epsilon>d^2.

The last strict inequality follows from
1-2(sqrt(2)+1)epsilon>0. Every own-side distance from O is
below one, whereas d>1. Thus neither a side support nor any
vertex can be the required actual radial support of norm d
for E. This excludes every tilted arm-owner alternative.

H consequently owns the arm axis and has the actual form

    H=[epsilon-1,epsilon] times [l,l+1],
    l<=0<=l+1.

All side distances are at most one, so E's radial support of
norm d>1 must be a vertex. A right-hand vertex would require
y^2=d^2-epsilon^2=1+2epsilon>1, impossible in this transverse
interval. A left-hand vertex requires

    y^2=d^2-(1-epsilon)^2=4epsilon.

After reflection the complete actual chart is therefore

    H=[epsilon-1,epsilon] times
      [2sqrt(epsilon)-1,2sqrt(epsilon)].                (5)

Its two possible signs cannot both be vertices because their
distance 4sqrt(epsilon) is less than one. No original side mean
has been replaced by a midpoint in this derivation.

The three other origin-side margins in (5) are strictly greater
than epsilon. Indeed 1-epsilon>epsilon,
2sqrt(epsilon)>epsilon, and
1-2sqrt(epsilon)>epsilon for 0<epsilon<1/60. Thus

    D_epsilon subset H,
    D_epsilon intersect boundary(H)={epsilon e}.       (6)

The actual third leaf F is S, with some genuine direction f.
Its original root B contains the actual inward point epsilon f.
Since B is interior-disjoint from H, this point cannot lie in
int(H). Equations (6) force

    epsilon f=epsilon e,       f=e.

But the radial S square in a fixed direction at the given radius
is unique. F would be exactly L, contradicting the distinct
original physical leaves. This excludes the final high-radius
case (4).
