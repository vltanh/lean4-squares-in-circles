# One diagonal and two side paths: a common source edge in the unique-endpoint branch

**Status:** reviewed exact geometric normalization; root and independent
full mathematical reviews passed on 2026-10-31.
This is a parameterization of the entire branch in which both D endpoints
have unique geometric circle vertices. It is not a whole-signature
exclusion or a completion-capacity bound. Radial D endpoints are outside
this note's scope, even when one circle coefficient vanishes.

Use the selected physical certificate, `79/40<=R<=3/sqrt(2)`, and the
three-loaded signature `LSLSLD`: a D path A--B and single S paths A--C
and B--C. The
[same-sign normalization](ONE_DIAGONAL_TWO_SIDE_SAME_SIGN_NORMALIZATION.md)
permits a vertical reflection after which

```text
A=[−u,1−u]×[−H_A,1−H_A],
B=[v−1,v]×[−H_B,1−H_B],
u+v=2+sqrt(2),
H_A=sqrt(R²−u²)>1/2,       H_B=sqrt(R²−v²)>1/2.
```

Let h be the common D force height. Its contacts are in the relative
interiors of the facing vertical sides, so

```text
max(−H_A,−H_B)<h<min(1−H_A,1−H_B).                 (1)
```

The outgoing unit S directions are

```text
n_A=(p,q),       n_B=(−r,s),
p,r>=0,         q>p, s>r,
p²+q²=r²+s²=1.                                    (2)
```

The strict inequalities in (2) include the vertical side-normal cases.
The contact position along an endpoint's horizontal side must remain a
parameter in those cases.

## 1. Endpoint contact parameters, including side-normal slides

Write the S starting contacts as

```text
P_A=(1−u−alpha,1−H_A),
P_B=(v−1+beta,1−H_B),
0<=alpha,beta<1,       alpha*p=beta*r=0.             (3)
```

Thus alpha=0 if p>0, and beta=0 if r>0. If p=0 or r=0, the corresponding
parameter records the full possible horizontal-side contact position.
The strict upper bounds follow from torque below; they are not an
assumption that a side-normal contact is an inner-corner contact.

The far S contacts on C are `Q_A=P_A+n_A`, `Q_B=P_B+n_B`. Set

```text
Delta=Q_B−Q_A=(d_x,d_y),
d_x=sqrt(2)+alpha+beta−p−r,
d_y=H_A−H_B+s−q.                                  (4)
```

Since `p,r<1/sqrt(2)`,

```text
d_x>0.                                             (5)
```

In particular, the two contacts on C are distinct.

For completeness, let mu>0 be the D force magnitude and sigma_A>0 the
A-side S magnitude. Force balance and torque about A's unique circle
vertex give

```text
mu/sigma_A=u*q/H_A−p>0,
mu*(h+H_A)=sigma_A*((1−alpha)*q−p).                  (6)
```

Because the left side of the second identity is positive, alpha<1.
The B identities are its reflection:

```text
mu/sigma_B=v*s/H_B−r>0,
mu*(h+H_B)=sigma_B*((1−beta)*s−r),                  (7)
```

and beta<1. Equations (6)--(7) remain valid when p=0 or r=0. They also
recover q>p and s>r in the strict-corner cases alpha=beta=0.

## 2. The two C contacts lie on one common edge

The outgoing support normals on C are `−n_A` and `−n_B`. Their angle is
strictly less than pi/2, because

```text
n_A dot n_B=q*s−p*r>0.                              (8)
```

Two distinct support points of a square whose normals make an angle
less than pi/2 belong to one common edge. Here is the complete feature
check. If a point is in a side's relative interior, its normal is that
side's normal. A normal less than pi/2 from it can expose only that side
or one of its two endpoints. Two different relative side interiors have
normals separated by at least pi/2. If both points are vertices, opposite
vertices have normal cones separated by at least pi/2, so the distinct
vertices must be adjacent. These observations include normals on cone
boundaries.

Equal normals would require p=r=0 and q=s=1. Both C contacts would then
lie on the same horizontal supporting side, but (4) would give horizontal
separation `sqrt(2)+alpha+beta>1`, impossible. Hence the two normals are
unequal as well as acute.

Let e be the unit vector along their common edge from Q_A toward Q_B,
and let `m=(-e_y,e_x)` be the unit normal into C. Then

```text
Delta=ell*e,       0<ell<=1,       e_x>0.            (9)
```

The interior normal is upward. Otherwise the common edge would have
upward outward normal m. A relative-interior contact there is impossible.
If both contacts are its endpoint vertices, their outward normal cones
are generated by `(m,−e)` on the left and `(m,e)` on the right. If
`e_y>=0`, the right cone contains no strictly downward vector; if
`e_y<=0`, the left cone contains no strictly downward vector. Either
case contradicts the two strictly downward normals `−n_A,−n_B`.
The endpoint support inequalities give

```text
n_A dot e>=0,       n_B dot e<=0,
−p/q <= e_y/e_x <= r/s.                            (10)
```

In particular, `|e_y|<e_x`. Also `n_A dot m>0` and `n_B dot m>0`, by
(2) and (10). Thus the only possible boundary incidences in (10) are
the common edge's side-normal incidences; no perpendicular-edge
boundary has been omitted.

## 3. Three disjoint source-edge rows

The following table is exhaustive. A side-normal row includes a contact
at the endpoint of its supported edge. This convention assigns a
shared-edge boundary to that row, rather than assuming the contact is
in a relative side interior.

| Row | Exact conditions on Delta | C as a translated unit square |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E_A | `p*d_x+q*d_y=0`, `0<d_x<=q` | `Q_B+[-1,0]*(q,−p)+[0,1]*n_A` |
| E_B | `−r*d_x+s*d_y=0`, `0<d_x<=s` | `Q_A+[0,1]*(s,r)+[0,1]*n_B` |
| V_V | `d_x²+d_y²=1`, `p*d_x+q*d_y>0`, `−r*d_x+s*d_y<0` | `Q_A+[0,1]*Delta+[0,1]*(−d_y,d_x)` |

In row E_A, `e=(q,−p)` and `ell=d_x/q`; the B contact must be the
right endpoint of the edge since its normal is not the edge normal.
In row E_B, `e=(s,r)` and `ell=d_x/s`; the A contact is the left endpoint.
In row V_V, both support inequalities in (10) are strict, so both
contacts are the edge's endpoint vertices and ell=1.

The first two rows cannot overlap: overlap would make n_A=n_B, already
excluded. Their boundary cases ell=1 are retained within the respective
side-normal rows. The last row is strict at both endpoints and therefore
does not overlap either side-normal row.

This table leaves all possible geometric circle features of C intact.
In particular, C is not assumed to have a unique circle vertex.

## 4. An explicit finite parameter family for center coverage

Normalize mu=1. The positive S force magnitudes are

```text
sigma_A=H_A/(u*q−H_A*p),
sigma_B=H_B/(v*s−H_B*r).                            (11)
```

Equations (6)--(7) can be imposed without division as

```text
(u*q−H_A*p)*(h+H_A)=H_A*((1−alpha)*q−p),
(v*s−H_B*r)*(h+H_B)=H_B*((1−beta)*s−r).             (12)
```

Together, (1)--(4), (11)--(12), positivity of the denominators, and one
row of the table describe a necessary parameter family for every core
in this branch. There are only three endpoint support pair types:
strict/strict, vertical/strict, and strict/vertical. The vertical/vertical
type was excluded in Section 2. Each can be combined with the three
source rows, with any impossible combinations removed by their equations.

All six core centers are explicit. Write `t_A,t_B in [-1/2,1/2]` for
the independent transverse S slides. Then

```text
c_A=(1/2−u,1/2−H_A),
c_B=(v−1/2,1/2−H_B),
c_D=((v−u)/2,h),
c_SA=P_A+n_A/2+t_A*(q,−p),
c_SB=P_B+n_B/2+t_B*(s,r).
```

The C center is respectively

```text
E_A: Q_B−(q,−p)/2+n_A/2,
E_B: Q_A+(s,r)/2+n_B/2,
V_V: Q_A+Delta/2+(−d_y,d_x)/2.                     (13)
```

These formulas do not assert that every parameter choice is a valid
packing or stress. Actual cores must additionally satisfy containment,
all pairwise interior-disjointness conditions, and C's positive circle
load and force equations. A completion certificate may retain those
conditions or prove a bound on a larger parameter family containing
them. Both slide intervals must remain covered.

The remaining goal is to cover this whole parameter family by finitely
many center-region certificates giving at most three additional squares
for a six-body core, using
[FINITE_CENTER_COVER_CAPACITY.md](FINITE_CENTER_COVER_CAPACITY.md).
No such uniform certificate is asserted here. The separate radial
endpoint branch must also be covered before the whole LSLSLD signature
can close.

## Circle-feature and side-source rational refinement

The [strict side-source reduction](STRICT_SIDE_SOURCE_RATIONAL_REDUCTION.md)
now gives five exhaustive C circle-feature rows. E_A and E_B each force
a unique geometric circle vertex; V_V retains two unique-vertex choices
or the upper radial side. The entire E_A geometry is rational in the two
S half-angle parameters, with two residual scalar equations; E_B follows
by reflection. This does not isolate their roots or prove completion.
