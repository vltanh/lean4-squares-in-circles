# Eleven squares: an exhaustive finite separator cover

This note proves a finite polynomial cover of **all** packings of eleven
independently rotated unit squares. It also proves attainment of the least
radius, once any explicit feasible upper bound is supplied. The cover is a
baseline for classification, not a sharp lower bound and not a list of solved
terminal classes. Its deliberately redundant cells preserve every weak contact
and owner tie. Here U=49013/10000 is the certified bound from
[the exact construction](EXACT_UPPER_BOUND.md); it is not a proposed sharp value.

The geometric statement is the repository's `Packing`: closed squares lie in a
closed disk and distinct **open** squares are disjoint. Thus shared edges and
vertices are allowed. The source definitions were read at
[`4bea747b`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-squares-in-circles/blob/4bea747b3efa13c869b956cb28435efaf21526fa/SquaresInCircles/Geometry.lean).

## 1. Normalization without a common-frame assumption

Translate the disk centre to the origin. For square i write its centre as p_i
and its unit frame as u_i = (c_i,s_i), v_i = (-s_i,c_i). Its points are

    p_i + a u_i + b v_i,       -1/2 <= a,b <= 1/2.

Changing an individual frame by a quarter turn preserves this point set; it
does not physically rotate the square. Rotate the entire packing until a
chosen square has frame u_0 = (1,0). Give every other square a representative
frame angle in [0,pi/2), and relabel the squares in nondecreasing order of these
angles, with the chosen square first among any zero-angle ties. The resulting
coordinates satisfy the following closed polynomial conditions:

    c_i^2 + s_i^2 = 1;          c_i >= 0; s_i >= 0;
    c_0 = 1; s_0 = 0;
    B_ij := c_i s_j - s_i c_j >= 0          (i < j).

Use the closed first quadrant, including its two endpoints, for the cover.
This only adds duplicate descriptions of some squares. Conversely, the displayed
conditions describe valid frames whose angles are nondecreasing in [0,pi/2].
No reflection, common orientation, central square, contact graph, or radial
ordering has been assumed.

Set

    A_ij := c_i c_j + s_i s_j;
    B_ij := c_i s_j - s_i c_j;
    H_ij := (1 + A_ij + B_ij)/2.

In this normalization A_ij and B_ij are nonnegative, and
A_ij^2 + B_ij^2 = 1. Consequently

    1 <= H_ij <= (1 + sqrt(2))/2.

The abs-sign cases in a support function have therefore been resolved by a
proved normalization, rather than silently discarded.

## 2. The eight separating alternatives for each pair

For i < j let d_ij = p_j - p_i. The two open squares are disjoint if and only
if at least one of the following eight inequalities holds:

| Tag | Normal n | Inequality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| iX+ | u_i | d_ij dot u_i >= H_ij |
| iX- | -u_i | -d_ij dot u_i >= H_ij |
| iY+ | v_i | d_ij dot v_i >= H_ij |
| iY- | -v_i | -d_ij dot v_i >= H_ij |
| jX+ | u_j | d_ij dot u_j >= H_ij |
| jX- | -u_j | -d_ij dot u_j >= H_ij |
| jY+ | v_j | d_ij dot v_j >= H_ij |
| jY- | -v_j | -d_ij dot v_j >= H_ij |

Here `i` or `j` records the **owner of the axis**, not a claim that the
corresponding pair touches. A legal separator can have positive slack.

Proof. The projection half-width of square j along u_i is
(|u_i dot u_j| + |u_i dot v_j|)/2 = (A_ij+B_ij)/2;
square i has half-width 1/2. The same total half-width H_ij holds along any
of the four owner axes. Therefore each displayed inequality separates the open
projection intervals and implies disjoint open squares.

For the converse, let K_i and K_j be the squares translated to their own
centres. Their interiors overlap after translation by d_ij exactly when
d_ij lies in the interior of K_i + (-K_j) = K_i + K_j. This Minkowski sum
is a planar zonotope generated by u_i, v_i, u_j, v_j; here the interior of
the Minkowski sum is the sum of the interiors, as holds for full-dimensional
convex bodies. Each zonotope edge is parallel to one of its generators.
Perpendicularity permutes the two axes of each square, up to sign, so every
facet normal is among the same four axes, with either sign. The intersection of its supporting half-planes
in those directions is exactly the zonotope. Hence a point outside its
interior satisfies at least one of the eight weak supporting inequalities.
Repeated edge directions merely give repeated inequalities. This proves the
converse, including edge contact, vertex contact and parallel-frame limits.

In particular opposite signs on one axis cannot both be legal: adding their
inequalities would give 0 >= 2H_ij >= 2. Different owners or different axes
can both be legal. Such ties are retained.

## 3. Forty-four corner constraints suffice for disk containment

Put q = R^2. For every square i and each (a,b) in {(-1,-1),(-1,1),
(1,-1),(1,1)}, impose

    ||p_i||^2 + 1/2 + a (p_i dot u_i) + b (p_i dot v_i) <= q.     (D_iab)

This is the squared norm of the corner p_i + (a u_i+b v_i)/2. All four
corners in the disk imply the whole closed square is in the disk because the
disk is convex and the square is their convex hull. The converse is immediate.
Thus these 44 quadratic inequalities are exactly the disk condition.

## 4. Finite-cover theorem

There are binomial(11,2) = 55 unordered pairs. A word omega assigns one tag
from Section 2 to every pair. Define C_omega(U) by:

1. the frame normalization of Section 1;
2. 0 <= q <= U;
3. all 44 corner inequalities;
4. for every pair, the one separator inequality selected by omega.

Every condition is polynomial of degree at most two over the rationals when
U is rational. There are

    8^55 = 2^165

tagged cells before eliminating duplicates, empty cells, or symmetry orbits.
This is a count of words, **not** a count of geometrically realizable or
inequivalent classes.

**Cover theorem.** Every packing with R^2 <= U has a translate, global rotation,
relabeling and point-set-preserving frame representation in some C_omega(U).
Conversely every point of every C_omega(U) defines a valid packing in the disk
of radius sqrt(q).

Proof. Apply Section 1, choose any legal tag for each pair using Section 2,
and use Section 3. Conversely, the chosen separators give all 55 pairwise
interior-disjointness statements and the corner conditions give containment.
The condition q >= 0 supplies a nonnegative radius. No pair is omitted.

The cells overlap intentionally. A total ordering of tags can choose the
first legal tag to provide a unique record, but the preceding tags then need
strict failure inequalities. Those records need not be closed. Use the weak,
overlapping cells for compactness and equality arguments; canonical records
are only an indexing convenience unless their boundary transfer is proved.

## 5. Compactness and existence of a minimizer

For any square, maximizing its four corner expressions gives

    ||p_i||^2 + 1/2 + |p_i dot u_i| + |p_i dot v_i| <= q.

As the frame is orthonormal, the sum of the last two absolute values is at
least ||p_i||. Therefore q >= 1/2 and

    ||p_i|| <= C(q) := sqrt(q - 1/4) - 1/2.                    (C)

In particular the simpler bound ||p_i||^2 <= U - 1/2 is enough for
compactness. The frame coordinates lie in [0,1]. Each C_omega(U) is a closed
bounded subset of a finite-dimensional real space, hence compact. The finite
union is compact. If an exact construction supplies any feasible U, the
continuous coordinate q attains a minimum on that nonempty union. The cover
theorem makes this the unrestricted minimum squared radius for eleven squares.

This is an existence theorem. It does not identify the minimum or imply that
the minimum equals the construction's U.

Two additional consequences hold in every cell:

* ||p_i-p_j|| >= 1. Each square contains the open disk of radius 1/2 about
  its centre; two such disks overlap when their centres are less than 1 apart.
  Equivalently, a legal unit-normal separator has projection at least H_ij>=1.
* The disk centre lies in the interior of at most one square. Otherwise two
  square interiors would intersect there. This does not prove that any square
  contains the disk centre: the zero-central-square branch remains present.

For any common fixed unit direction n, a chain of k successive inequalities

    (p_{i_(r+1)} - p_{i_r}) dot n >= 1

has k <= 2C(q). At q <= 5, 2C(q) <= sqrt(19)-1 < 4, so no four-edge
chain of this form exists. The **common direction** is essential. Summing
owner inequalities with different normals does not justify this exclusion.

## 6. A hand-checkable stress certificate for one cell

Fix the frame variables of a cell temporarily. For each corner define

    w_iab = (a u_i + b v_i)/2.

Choose nonnegative corner weights alpha_iab with total sum 1. For each
selected directed pair separator e=(i,j), with normal n_e pointing from i
to j, choose beta_e >= 0. Write

    A_i = sum_ab alpha_iab;
    m_i = sum_ab alpha_iab w_iab;
    b_i = sum_(e entering i) beta_e n_e
          - sum_(e leaving i) beta_e n_e.

Assume A_i > 0 for every i, or, when A_i=0, require b_i=0 (then m_i=0 as
well) and omit that summand. Then every point in the cell satisfies

    q >= 1/2 + sum_e beta_e H_e
             - sum_(i:A_i>0) ||2m_i-b_i||^2/(4A_i).           (S)

Proof. The corner inequalities give q >= sum_iab alpha_iab ||p_i+w_iab||^2.
Each separator gives H_e - n_e dot (p_j-p_i) <= 0, so adding its beta-weighted
left side can only decrease that right side. Expanding the resulting expression
and using ||w_iab||^2=1/2 gives

    1/2 + sum_e beta_e H_e
      + sum_i [A_i ||p_i||^2 + (2m_i-b_i) dot p_i].

Complete the square separately for every centre p_i. Its minimum is
-||2m_i-b_i||^2/(4A_i), at p_i=(b_i-2m_i)/(2A_i). This proves (S).

The weights may depend on the frame variables, but their signs and the final
bound must then be proved throughout the **whole** cell domain. A successful
numeric stress at one pose or one orientation does not close a cell.

If (S) proves q >= q_star uniformly, its equality conditions retain useful
geometry:

* each corner with alpha_iab>0 lies on the enclosing circle;
* each separator with beta_e>0 is tight;
* each centre with A_i>0 equals (b_i-2m_i)/(2A_i);
* any auxiliary estimate used to prove the uniform bound is also tight.

Zero-weight corners, edges and squares need their residual feasibility domain
tracked separately. They cannot be declared rigid from this certificate.

## 7. What still needs classification

The preceding theorem closes the logical question "is there an exhaustive
finite cover?" It does not turn 2^165 words into a useful proof. The next
classification gate must produce a tractable family of geometric class
contracts and a proved map from every surviving C_omega(U) to at least one
contract. For each contract record:

* the original normalized variables and every retained separator alternative;
* the additional inequalities defining the class, including weak boundary cases;
* every symmetry operation and its inverse on centres, frames and tags;
* the uniform radius certificate, or an explicit unresolved obligation;
* equality conditions and all remaining continuous freedoms.

Sorting orientation is already justified above. Additional identification under
reflection requires keeping the reflected model in any final theorem whose
congruence relation only allows rotations and relabelings. A purely combinatorial
graph isomorphism must preserve the geometric owner-axis data, or be accompanied
by a proof that it lifts to an allowed coordinate transformation.

Closed subregions can share boundaries. This is preferable to omitting an owner
tie in pursuit of disjoint cases. A classification gate is closed only when the
union-cover proof and each terminal contract are written, not when a search has
sampled one representative from each presumed graph.
