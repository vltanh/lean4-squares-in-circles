# Eleven-square minimizers have exact sparse contact stresses

An unrestricted optimal eleven-square packing exists by the compact cover and
the exact construction. This note extracts a nonnegative stress from such a
minimizer; the generic weak dual alone did not provide this extraction.
Every positively weighted pair is an actual touching pair of physical squares.
The stress can be restricted to one connected loaded core, with all other
squares retained as a physical completion.

There are two separate certificates:

* With the frames frozen, at most **23** positive corner and pair terms.
* With all square angles variable, at most **33** positive smooth corner and
  separator-support-vertex atoms, including angular stationarity.

No assertion combines the smaller count with the angular equations. Neither
certificate proves the minimum is the constructed candidate value. The missing
step is an exhaustive sharp theorem for the possible cores **and their complete
eleven-square realizations**.

## 1. Set-up and physical owner selection

Let `(p_i,u_i,v_i,q_0)`, `0<=i<11`, be an unrestricted minimizer normalized to
disk center zero. Write `v_i=J u_i`, where `J(x,y)=(-y,x)`. Its squared radius
satisfies `q_0<=U=49013/10000<5` and `q_0>1469/400>11/4>2`.
The independent elementary center-shell argument in `CENTER_SHELL_BOUND.md`
proves this lower bound: eleven mutually unit-separated centers cannot fit
in radius 27/20, while square centers have radius at most
`sqrt(q_0-1/4)-1/2`. This auxiliary estimate is used to exclude small stress
cores; it is not the desired sharp lower bound.

For each unordered pair choose a signed own-axis separating normal `n_e`
pointing from label i to label j. Make this additional choice:

* If the two **closed** squares are disjoint, choose a strictly separating
  own axis.
* If they touch, choose any weak legal own axis.

The strict choice exists because the center displacement is outside the closed
Minkowski sum of the centered squares. Its polygon facets have own-axis
normals, so an outside point violates one supporting inequality strictly.
Conversely, a touching pair cannot have a positive projection gap in any
direction because its common point projects into both intervals.

For temporarily fixed frames, write

    H_e = h_i(n_e)+h_j(n_e) >= 1,
    g_e(p) = H_e - n_e dot (p_j-p_i) <= 0,
    f_iw(p,q) = ||p_i+w||²-q <= 0,

where w ranges over the four centered corners `(+-u_i+-v_i)/2`.
These are exactly the chosen pair-separation inequalities and all 44 corner
constraints. The minimizer satisfies them. Any point satisfying this system
is a physical packing with the same frames and selected owners, so no such
point has q below q_0.

The strict-choice rule ensures every active selected pair inequality is a
physical contact. A careless weak choice for a noncontacting pair could be
tight and would not have that property.

## 2. A strict direction for every active constraint

Take the variation `dot p_i=p_i`, and choose `dot q=M` with

    M > max_i,w 2(p_i+w) dot p_i.

For each active corner, its directional derivative is strictly negative:

    Df_iw = 2(p_i+w) dot p_i - M < 0.

For each active pair, `n_e dot (p_j-p_i)=H_e`, so

    Dg_e = -n_e dot (p_j-p_i) = -H_e < 0.

There are finitely many constraints, hence this one direction strictly
decreases every active constraint. Inactive constraints remain strict under
sufficiently small perturbations. The argument does not require an assumed
generic contact configuration or independent active gradients.

## 3. Nonnegative multipliers and the 23-term certificate

Let z denote the 23 variables consisting of the 22 center coordinates and q.
Let e_q be the objective gradient. Then `-e_q` lies in the cone generated by
the active constraint gradients.

Here is the finite-dimensional first-order argument. If it did not, separation
from the closed finitely generated cone would give a direction d with
`e_q dot d<0` and every active constraint derivative at most zero. Adding a
sufficiently small positive multiple of the strict direction in Section 2
preserves `e_q dot d<0` and makes all active derivatives strictly negative.
For a sufficiently small positive step, differentiability makes those active
constraints negative, while continuity preserves every inactive strict
constraint. That is a physical packing with q<q_0, a contradiction.

Therefore there are nonnegative corner weights alpha_iw and pair weights beta_e
such that

    e_q + sum_i,w alpha_iw grad(f_iw)
        + sum_e beta_e grad(g_e) = 0.                         (K)

Only active constraints have positive weights. The q coordinate gives
`sum_i,w alpha_iw=1`. Conic Caratheodory in dimension 23 thins this expression
to at most 23 positive terms. Explicitly, if more than 23 positive generators
occur, a linear dependence among them allows moving their coefficients until
at least one becomes zero, preserving nonnegativity and the vector sum; repeat.

Define

    A_i = sum_w alpha_iw,
    m_i = sum_w alpha_iw w,
    b_i = sum_(e entering i) beta_e n_e
          - sum_(e leaving i) beta_e n_e.

The center coordinates of (K) are

    2 A_i p_i + 2 m_i - b_i = 0.                             (F)

If A_i=0 then m_i=0 and hence b_i=0. The exact completed-square identity is

    q_0 = 1/2 + sum_e beta_e H_e
                - sum_(i:A_i>0) ||2m_i-b_i||²/(4A_i).         (D)

Indeed all positively weighted corner inequalities and pair inequalities are
tight at the minimizer. Equation (F) makes every completed square in the
generic weak dual vanish. Thus the dual value is attained exactly, rather
than merely bounding the objective for a guessed stress.

This theorem freezes the actual minimizing frames. It does not make the
right side of (D) an orientation-uniform candidate lower bound.

## 4. A distinct 33-atom certificate with angular stationarity

Allow all eleven angles theta_i to vary in real local coordinates around the
minimizing frames; no half-open angle chart boundary is imposed. Keep the
chosen signed owner and axis for each pair. The chosen normal n_e now rotates
with its owner's angle.

For an owner axis the owner half-width is identically 1/2. If k is the
nonowner, and w_k ranges over its four centered corners, replace the single
pair constraint by four smooth inequalities

    G_ew = 1/2 + n_e dot w_k - n_e dot (p_j-p_i) <= 0.         (V)

Their conjunction is exactly the original pair separator, since
`max_w n_e dot w=h_k(n_e)`. No differentiation of an absolute-value cusp is
used. Parallel-frame contacts can have several active support vertices.

Now use 34 variables: 22 centers, eleven angles, and q. The same strict
direction as before, with all angle velocities zero, gives negative derivative
for each active (V), because its derivative is `-H_e<0`. Therefore the conic
argument gives nonnegative active-atom multipliers with sum of corner weights
one and stationarity in every center and angle coordinate.

Every constraint is invariant under simultaneous rotation of all centers and
all frames about the disk center. Thus its gradient is orthogonal to the
nonzero vector

    (Jp_0,...,Jp_10, 1,...,1, 0).

The objective gradient is also orthogonal to this vector. All generators lie
in a subspace of dimension 33, so conic thinning gives at most **33** positive
corner or separator-support-vertex atoms.

The center equations are still (F), with each beta_e obtained by summing the
weights of that pair's active support-vertex atoms. Since every positively
weighted atom has `n_e dot w_k=h_k(n_e)`, identity (D) still holds exactly.
Positive pair weights again occur only at physical touching pairs.

For clarity, the angular derivatives used by this certificate are explicit.
A corner contributes

    partial_theta_i f_iw = 2(p_i+w) dot Jw = 2p_i dot Jw.

For a pair atom (V) with displacement d=p_j-p_i, owner o and nonowner k,

    partial_theta_o G_ew = Jn_e dot (w_k-d),
    partial_theta_k G_ew = n_e dot Jw_k.

All other angle derivatives vanish. Their weighted sum is zero for each angle.

The atoms must not be called individual physical contact-point forces without
another theorem. For parallel edge contact, an active endpoint of the
nonowner supporting edge can lie outside the actual overlapping segment.
The pair is a physical contact; the active supporting vertex need not itself
be the contact point. No planarity statement is asserted for the body graph
or for this atom representation.

## 5. All normalized multipliers have a uniform bound

The multipliers need not be left as unbounded existential variables. Dot (F)
with p_i and sum over i. Tightness of each positively loaded corner gives
`2p_i dot w=q_0-||p_i||²-1/2`, hence

    sum_e beta_e H_e = q_0-1/2 + sum_i A_i ||p_i||².           (W)

This holds for both certificates, with full-angle support-vertex weights
summed into beta_e. Since every H_e>=1, `q_0<5` and
`||p_i||<17/10`, equation (W) yields

    0 <= sum_e beta_e < 9/2+289/100 = 739/100 < 15/2.

Thus all corner weights lie in [0,1] and all pair-atom weights in [0,15/2].
Moreover the coarse area bound gives `sum_e beta_e H_e>=9/4`, so the pair
stress is nonzero. This identity remains valid after retaining and normalizing
any positive component as in the next section.

Consequently, once the finite support and owner data are fixed, center/frame/
radius/weight variables can all be bounded. Use the four support-vertex
inequalities in the full-angle version, or additional finite closed absolute-
value sign cells in the fixed-frame version. The stationarity equations,
unit-frame equations, and complement constraints are then polynomial in
c_i,s_i,p_i,q,alpha,beta. Encode activity by complementarity equations such as
`alpha_iw*f_iw=0`. To keep every listed pair physical even on chart boundaries,
adjoin a point P_e in the intersection of its two closed squares; its local
coordinate inequalities are polynomial and its coordinates lie in
[-9/4,9/4]. The extracted touching pairs supply these witnesses.

Weak inequalities and zero-weight overlaps now give closed bounded
semialgebraic charts. A chart's positive support may be a
proper subgraph on its boundary; this is why connectedness should be a property
of the extracted positive core, not a strict inequality imposed on a purported
closed cover.

## 6. Retaining one connected loaded component

Make a graph on the eleven square labels with edges exactly the pairs of
positive total beta. A square with positive A_i is circle-loaded.

Every component that carries a nonzero stress has positive total A. Suppose
instead A_i=0 throughout such a component. Then (F) gives b_i=0 there.
Multiplying by p_i and summing gives

    0 = sum_i p_i dot b_i
      = sum_e beta_e n_e dot (p_j-p_i)
      = sum_e beta_e H_e > 0,

a contradiction if any edge weight is positive. A component with no edges
and no circle weight has no stress and is irrelevant.

Every positive component has circle weight on at least **two distinct
squares**. If only one square i were loaded, summing (F) over the component
would give `sum_w alpha_iw(p_i+w)=0`. But every positively weighted corner is
a farthest corner of square i. For p_i nonzero,

    p_i dot (p_i+w)
      = ||p_i||² + (|p_i dot u_i|+|p_i dot v_i|)/2 > 0

at every farthest corner, so that positive combination cannot vanish.
If p_i=0, every corner has norm squared 1/2, contrary to q_0>1/2. This also
excludes an isolated single loaded square as a stress component.

Select one positive component and divide all of its weights by its total
corner weight. The normalizing sum becomes one. All force equations remain
valid; for the angular certificate its angle equations remain valid too,
since there are no positive weighted atoms connecting different components.
All retained constraints remain tight, and identity (D) still equals q_0.

For normalized weights on this component, summing (F) gives

    sum_i,w alpha_iw (p_i+w) = 0.

For any proposed disk center z, the weighted mean of the squared distances
of the loaded corners from z is consequently `q_0+||z||²`. Therefore the
original disk is the **unique minimum enclosing disk of the retained core's
actual position**. This conclusion concerns translation of the disk center,
not arbitrary changes of the core geometry.

A two-square core is impossible. The pair touches at some point P, and
every point of either unit square lies within its diameter sqrt(2) of P.
Their union thus has an enclosing disk of squared radius two, contradicting
the preceding minimum-enclosing-disk identity and `q_0>=11/4`.

The retained component initially has k vertices for some 3<=k<=11. Applying thinning
inside these variables gives at most `2k+1` positive terms in the fixed-frame
case, or `3k` positive atoms in the full-angle case. Thinning can disconnect
the support; if it does, retain one positive component again and repeat.
Each such disconnection reduces the number of retained vertices, so this
process terminates with the stated count for the final vertex number. The final core
is connected, with at least two loaded squares. In the fixed-frame version
it has at most `2k-1` positive pair edges, because at least two corner terms
are positive.

Every square outside the retained core stays in the original physical
packing. Dropping its weight does not delete its geometry. A lower-bound
argument based on a small core must still quantify over all such completions.

Three-square cores are also impossible. Their connected positive pair graph
is either a path or a triangle.

For a triangle, all three pairs physically touch. The distance between any
two centers is at most sqrt(2), since each center is within sqrt(2)/2 of a
shared point. Let z be the centroid of the three centers. Each center is
within `2sqrt(2)/3` of z. Every square point is within sqrt(2)/2 of its center,
so the whole core fits in a disk about z of radius `7sqrt(2)/6`. Its squared
radius is at most `49/18<11/4<q_0`, contradicting the minimum-disk identity.

For a path A-B-C, use the attained **fixed-frame** dual. It only has positive
pair weights on AB and BC. Keep these frames, place B's center at zero, and
place each endpoint center a distance H_e along the signed selected normal
pointing away from B. The two positively weighted pair inequalities are then
equalities. Because each selected normal is a signed own axis,

    H_e <= 1/2+sqrt(2)/2.

All points of the three comparison squares therefore lie within
`1/2+sqrt(2)` of zero. The weak dual with the retained weights applies to
these positions: it needs only the two positively weighted pair constraints
and the corner inequalities, so endpoint overlap does not invalidate this
relaxation. Thus

    q_0 <= (1/2+sqrt(2))^2 = 9/4+sqrt(2) < 1469/400,

contradicting the center-shell lower bound. The same comparison applies to
an angular certificate after freezing its frames and aggregating pair atoms.
The strict last inequality is exact: `sqrt(2)<569/400`, as
`320000<323761` after squaring.

Consequently the retained connected core always has **4<=k<=11**. This
exclusion does not depend on the previously proved n=8 lower bound.

## 7. Unloaded leaves and bounded straight transmission paths

An unloaded square has A_i=m_i=0, hence b_i=0. It cannot have degree one in
the positive pair graph, since a single nonzero positive normal force cannot
sum to zero.

At an unloaded degree-two vertex, the two incident forces are equal in
magnitude and opposite. Along a path whose internal vertices are unloaded
and have degree two, orient the first edge from its first square to its next
square. Balance forces every following edge to have that same signed unit
normal n and the same positive weight. Each physical contact therefore gives

    n dot (p_(r+1)-p_r) = h_r(n)+h_(r+1)(n) >= 1.

Summing along an m-edge path gives

    n dot (p_end-p_start)
       = h_start(n)+h_end(n)+2 sum_internal h_i(n) >= m.

All centers have norm at most
`C(U)=sqrt(U-1/4)-1/2`, with `2C(U)<sqrt(19)-1<4`. Thus m<4: every such
path has at most three edges, hence **at most two internal transmitting
squares**. A closed transmission loop would make a positive sum of widths
equal zero and is impossible. This conclusion uses a single force-balanced
normal along the path; arbitrary successive contact normals cannot be summed
as if they were parallel.

## 8. Exact finite data and the remaining global gate

The minimizer admits a connected core on 4 to 11 actual squares, with:

* actual touching body pairs and their signed own-axis witnesses;
* at least two circle-loaded squares and at most two active corners per
  loaded square (a noncentered square has one or two farthest corners);
* no unloaded leaf;
* straight degree-two unloaded paths with zero, one or two internal squares;
* a normalized attained stress with at most 23 fixed-frame terms, or a
  separate at-most-33-atom full-angle stationary stress;
* a physical completion by all omitted labels, still inside the same disk.

The listed combinatorial data is finite, while centers, orientations and
nonnegative weights remain continuous. It is more structured than arbitrary
55-pair owner words, but it is **not yet an exhaustively solved sharp terminal
inventory**. In particular neither a sparse conic support nor stationarity
forces the selected candidate graph, a common frame, or the seven-plus-two-
plus-two orientation pattern.

An unrestricted lower bound can now be pursued by a theorem covering every
such physically realized core-plus-completion and proving its attained dual
value at least q_c. That theorem, and the equality classification after it,
remain open. This extraction is an unconditional reduction at an unrestricted
minimizer, not a substitute for those missing inequalities.
