# The hard GA0/BT2 class is sharp with arbitrary G/T signs and arbitrary frames

## 1. Original-class theorem

Put
\[
 q_L=\frac{19577}{5000},\qquad
 Q_1=\frac{1957767069}{500000000},\qquad Q=\frac{9789}{2500}.
\tag{1}
\]
Retain an original G0--G2 necessary cycle, sorted contained
corners, true central exclusion, and real frame spread at most
\(7/10\). H,A have their normal positive signed tangents,
B,D their normal negative signed tangents, and E is normalized
nonpositive. The G and T signed tangents are arbitrary.
The original retained witnesses are
\[
 HG,AB,TD\in\{0,2\},\qquad GA0,\quad BT2,\qquad
                         DE\in\{2,3\},\quad EH\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For a necessary system stated apart from an original candidate,
retain also the original zero-owner implications
\[
                  HG2\Longrightarrow y_G<0,\qquad
                  TD0\Longrightarrow y_T>0.
\tag{3}
\]
The H,A,B,D signs and (3) are already global consequences
for original candidates with these owners.

Assume the same E radial alternative as in the normal
hard-class theorem:
\[
                    a_E\le7/12\quad\hbox{or}\quad
                    a_E\ge\max\{b_H,b_D\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Original true-core and sorted outer-circle boundary
representatives satisfy (4).

**Sharp theorem.** Every such system at circle ceiling
\(q\le Q\) satisfies \(q\ge q_*\).
At equality the original G/T signs are normal, the three
original zero differences vanish, the original E frame equals
D's frame, and the original H,A,B,D,E anchors are the unique
stationary five anchors. For an original packing, the remaining
unchanged constraints give exactly the complete weak
five-parameter equality family and its converse.

There is no original frame-order or G/T-sign assumption.
The hard GA0/BT2 owners, original mixed-owner sets, and (4)
remain explicit inputs. This does not capture the other
quarter-owner alternatives or assert the unrestricted theorem.

The enclosure \(q_L<q_*<Q_1<Q\) is already proved.
If \(q>Q_1\), the lower bound is immediate. Otherwise use
the working ceiling \(\max(q,q_L)\), without moving any
original center. The proof below applies on
\[
                              q_L\le q\le Q_1.
\tag{5}
\]
If the original ceiling were below \(q_L\), it would
contradict the conclusion at \(q_L<q_*\). At equality the
working ceiling is the original ceiling.

               A=B=\rho,\qquad T\le\rho\le G,\qquad
                              b_A+b_B=1.
\tag{7}
\]
H,G,T,D,E and all their signed coordinates and frames are

strict A/B losses. A nonzero original AB error creates

The H/D/E budget and both unclipped zero charges remain

## 3. Process the normal large side

### 3.1 The large outer endpoint is not crossed

Suppose \(\theta_H\ge\rho\).
Use the large-side local alternatives in Section 3 of
[the normal arbitrary-frame theorem](HAND_G5_NORMAL_HARD_OWNERS_ARBITRARY_FRAMES_SHARP_REDUCTION.md).
These are three-state comparisons and do not use the
opposite intermediate sign:

* aligned HG already supplies its aligned composite;
* either sign of a canceling HG2 error aligns;
* a positive noncanceling HG0 error aligns with strict
  pair slack and preserved A-circle loss;
* a negative noncanceling HG0 error with \(b_A>1/2\)
  remains its exact true-core composite; at \(b_A=1/2\)
  the small-transverse negative transport may align it.

Each output has \(a_A>1.1\), and therefore
\[
                             b_A<.665,\qquad b_B>.335.
\tag{9}
\]
The concrete comparison G described in that theorem is
normal, satisfies the true core and its actual circle,
and has its frame in \([\rho,H]\). All H,B,T,D,E data
remain fixed. The common frame and both tangents have
not changed. Separate middle-circle losses persist.

### 3.2 The large outer endpoint is crossed

Suppose \(\theta_H<\rho\).
Use Sections 4--6 of
[the signed opposite-envelope theorem](HAND_G5_SIGNED_OPPOSITE_HARD_ENVELOPE.md).
For HG0 it extends the original large crossed
same-circle construction with the full signed-T hard
envelope. For HG2 it retains the original composite
transport and replaces only the one short T-angle estimate.
The bound \(t>-.1\) needed in that estimate is (6).

The result has actual HG0 and GA0, with
\[
                  G=\rho>T,\qquad 0<\rho-H<.04,
                              \qquad b_B>.28.
\tag{10}
\]
H,B,T,D,E and A's frame and tangent are unchanged.
The HG0 construction preserves or creates its strict A
loss after any primary lift. The HG2 construction changes
only G. The opposite wrong T sign, if present, is still
exactly the original one.

This step stops at GA alignment. It does not invoke the
normal-sign common-frame clamp before T's sign has been
corrected.

Thus in either case the sole possible wrong intermediate
is on a middle tangent in
\[
                              .28<b_B\le.5.
\tag{11}
\]
The common A/B frame remains between the two intermediate
frames, all circles use the same working q, and H,D,E
and their mixed contacts have stayed fixed.

## 4. Remove the remaining small wrong sign

If T is already normal, no further sign operation is needed.
Otherwise \(y_T<0\), and the original implication (3) leaves
TD2. Its wrong-sign magnitude is less than .1 by (6).
Apply the reflected statements of
[the small wrong-sign local theorem](HAND_G5_SMALL_WRONG_SIGN_TRANSPORT_AND_CROSSED_EXCLUSION.md),
using (11).

The reflected crossed case would be \(D>\rho\).
That theorem excludes it using the unchanged H/D/E
budget and the full opposite hard envelope. Hence
\(D\le\rho\).

The wrong sign forces the reflected positive zero error
\(D-T>0\). Therefore
\[
                         0<D-T\le\rho-T\le7/10.
\]
The ordered-side transport applies at the actual working q.
It keeps D, B's frame and tangent, and the entire other
side fixed, reaches \(y_T'=0\), and creates a strict
B-circle loss. The T frame remains between D and its
previous frame. The changed points remain sorted and
satisfy the true core. Any inherited A loss is untouched.

Both G and T now have their normal weak signs. Every
required neighboring witness is retained in its allowed
set, and the real frame interval has not expanded.
The only original states used in (4), namely H,D,E,
were never changed. Thus (4) also remains true.

All its actual circle, sorting, core, witness and frame

## 6. Original equality and the full equality family

If an original G or T sign were genuinely wrong, the AB
alignment keeps that signed coordinate unchanged.
Section 2 excludes its occurrence on the large side.
The normal large-side processing retains all separate
middle losses, and Section 4 then creates a strict circle
loss at the wrong side's middle anchor.

At \(q=q_*\), the normal arbitrary-frame theorem requires
all five anchors of its input necessary cycle to be the
stationary anchors, hence to lie on their q-circles.
The descendant's strict middle loss contradicts this.
Therefore both original G/T signs were already normal.

Reapply the normal arbitrary-frame theorem directly to
the original centers. It now gives the original three
zero-error identities, the original E frame at D, and
the unique original stationary H,A,B,D,E anchors.
For an original packing, its unchanged remaining
constraints give the full weak five-parameter equality
family and the converse exactly as in that theorem.

Equality is thus proved for original centers, without
identifying an arbitrary transported state with its ancestor.
The remaining unrestricted task is quarter-owner entry
into this completed hard class, or into another completed
sharp route. No G/T-sign or original frame-order hypothesis
remains within the class (2)--(4).
