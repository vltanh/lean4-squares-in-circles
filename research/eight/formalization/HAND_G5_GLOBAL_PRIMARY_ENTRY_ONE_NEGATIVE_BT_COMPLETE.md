# Complete entry from one aligned negative BT primary, with arbitrary G sign

## 1. Same-radius theorem and its strict equality conclusion

Put
\[
 q_L=19577/5000,\qquad Q=9789/2500,\qquad
 h=7/10,\qquad K=13/12,
 \qquad q_L\le q\le Q.
\tag{1}
\]
Retain sorted true-core necessary states at the actual
circle ceiling q and real frame spread at most h. H,A
are nonnegative, B,D,E are nonpositive, and G has
arbitrary signed tangent. Retain the original actual
\[
 HG,AB,TD\in\{0,2\},\quad GA0,\quad BT3,
 \qquad DE\in\{2,3\},\quad EH\in\{0,1\},
\tag{2}
\]
and the original implication HG2=>y_G<0. Suppose
\[
                         \theta_T=\theta_D<\theta_B.
\tag{3}
\]
Let E be on its true-core boundary or its actual outer
q-circle. Neither H nor D is required to be outer at q.
No AB alignment, G sign, E order, EH-error sign, or
mixed-error bound is assumed.

**Complete one-negative-BT entry theorem.** There is a
necessary descendant at the same q with actual GA0 and
BT2 and all hypotheses of the arbitrary-sign hard-owner
sharp theorem. H,G,A,B, including their coordinates and
frames, remain identical. If BT2 was initially unavailable,
the descendant can be chosen with a strict D-circle loss.
Every preexisting H,A,B circle loss is retained.

Consequently q>=q_*. If BT2 was initially unavailable,
then q>q_*. This strict conclusion uses an anchor loss
at D; it does not use a G/T circle loss or identify a
transported intermediate with its original ancestor.

If BT2 is already actual, select it and apply the hard-owner
theorem directly. All remaining sections concern the
genuinely unavailable case. The construction keeps E
nonpositive and retains its actual mixed owners throughout.

## 2. Original anchor order and the E-free span

The unchanged-state
[one-negative-BT anchor-order theorem](HAND_G5_GLOBAL_PRIMARY_ENTRY_ONE_NEGATIVE_BT_ANCHOR_ORDER.md)
gives H>D. The two E-free negative-GA span theorems give
the other side of the bound:
\[
                         0<\theta_H-\theta_D<.22.
\tag{4}
\]
Here is the exact reflection used for the second inequality.
Exchange H with D, G with T and A with B, and negate
all signed tangents and real frames. The target BT3
becomes the negative GA1 of those span theorems; aligned
TD becomes aligned HG; GA0 becomes BT2; and the AB
owners are interchanged. Original HG0 becomes TD2,
and original HG2 becomes TD0. In the latter case the
retained implication y_G<0 supplies the reflected
positive T sign. The reflected T sign is otherwise
arbitrary, as those theorems allow. The span H-D itself
is unchanged by the transformation.

Thus apply
[the TD0 span theorem](HAND_G5_NEGATIVE_GA_ALIGNED_HG_TD0_SPAN_BOUND.md)
or
[the TD2 span theorem](HAND_G5_GLOBAL_PRIMARY_ENTRY_NEGATIVE_GA_TD2_SPAN_BOUND.md)
as appropriate. Their statements and proofs contain no
E state or E witness. Only this E-free scalar span is
reflected; no E transport is reflected.

The first inequality in (4) is stronger than a one-time
order assertion. The anchor-order theorem needs no radial
equality and applies to every later state satisfying
(2)--(3). Thus a feasible path retaining unavailable BT2
cannot reach H=D. This barrier will handle even an original
nonpositive EH error.

## 3. The common D/T contact, without an H hypothesis

Write u=theta_B-theta_D. The E-independent local comparison
in Section 2 of
[the direct second-primary theorem](HAND_G5_GLOBAL_PRIMARY_ENTRY_BOTH_NEGATIVE_DIRECT_SECOND_TRANSPORT.md)
gives
\[
 0<u<.09,\qquad .1<b_T<.156,\qquad
 b_D>.844,\quad a_D<1,\quad a_T\ge7/12.
\tag{5}
\]
Its derivation uses D,T,B only and does not require that H
be near diagonal. Lower T's primary to 7/12, improving
negative BT3 and leaving aligned TD unchanged. Put
\[
 P=a_B-1/2,\qquad Z(u)=P\sec u-K\tan u,
\]
\[
              b_T(u)=Z(u)-1/2,\qquad b_D(u)=3/2-Z(u).
\tag{6}
\]
These are exact BT3 and aligned TD at contact. Increase
T's tangent and decrease D's tangent to these contact
values, moving D on its own initial circle q_D<=q.

The normalization eligibility is the same local one as in
the cited theorem: before reapplying its sharper exceptional
comparison, Z<=P<.915 gives b_T<.415<7/12 and T's
corner circle is below q_L. Increasing T's tangent makes
BT2 still less feasible. The sharper comparison consequently
applies along the whole normalization and subsequent path:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 .6<Z<\sqrt{Q-K^2}-1<.656,\qquad
 r:=b_D'(u)=K-Z\tan u>1,\\
 .844<b_D<.9,\qquad b_D\le a_D<1,\\
 \mathcal A:=-a_D'(u)
   =\frac{(2-Z)(K-Z\tan u)}
           {\sqrt{q_D-(2-Z)^2}}>1.
 \end{gathered}
\tag{7}
\]
The circle derivative and its strict last inequality are
the D/T/B reflection of the original local H/G/A circle
calculation; no mixed witness enters them.

Normalization preserves the actual DE owner for every
signed error d=theta_E-theta_D. DE3 increases when a_D
increases. For DE2 its D projection has derivative with
respect to b_D, on D's circle,
\[
 -\frac{b_D+1/2}{a_D+1/2}\cos d+\sin d<0,
\tag{8}
\]
because the ratio exceeds .896 and tan d<=tan h<.843.
Decreasing b_D therefore improves DE2. All other original
witnesses remain identical or are the exact contacts in
(6). From now on decrease u, advancing D and T together
toward the fixed B frame, and retain (6)--(7) until BT2
first becomes actual.

## 4. If E is below D, move E to D before advancing D/T

Suppose n=theta_D-theta_E>0. By (4), the EH error
p=theta_H-theta_E satisfies p>n>0. Increase E's frame
alone toward D. Then n and p decrease at the same unit
rate. Write E's actual radial arc as a=a(b), with
v=-a'(b). Its core and outer branches satisfy
\[
 0\le v\le1,\qquad
 b-va=\frac{1-v}{2}\text{ on curved core},\qquad
 b-va=-\frac{1-v}{2}\text{ on outer}.
\tag{9}
\]
The flat core has a=7/12 and v=0. Decreasing b improves
sorting, leaves the outer circle equal to q, and does not
increase the core corner circle.

At fixed b both negative DE owners improve as E advances.
For DE2 the rate is
\[
 C_D(n)=(a_D-1/2)\sin n+(b_D+1/2)\cos n>1.249.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed .344 sin n+1.344 cos n is concave on [0,h], and
its two endpoint lower bounds are 1.344 and
.344(.6442)+1.344(.7648)=1.249496. For DE3 the rate is
(a+1/2)cos n+(b-1/2)sin n>0.

Keep b fixed until retaining the original EH contact
requires a decrease. For EH0 the required nonnegative
decrease rate is
\[
 \beta_0=\max\{(b_H-1/2)\cos p-(a_H-1/2)\sin p,0\}<.4.
\tag{11}
\]
At EH0 contact b<.415. To see this without a near-H
assumption, let rho=sqrt(a_H^2+b_H^2). Containment gives
q>=rho^2+rho+1/2, hence rho<=sqrt(Q-1/4)-1/2<1.415.
The actual contact gives b<=rho-W(p)<=rho-1<.415.
A core E at that contact is therefore flat.

The DE2 rate on this contact is C_D-beta_0>.849.
The DE3 rate is exactly
\[
 (a+1/2-\beta_0)\cos n
                         +(b-1/2-v\beta_0)\sin n.
\tag{12}
\]
On the flat core this exceeds
(K-.4)cos h-.5 sin h>.2. On the outer q-circle,
a>.899 and v<=1, so it exceeds
.999 cos h-.9 sin h>.18. If the untruncated contact rate
in (11) is negative, keeping b fixed improves EH0 and
also preserves the already positive DE rates.

For EH1 the active decrease rate is
\[
 \beta_1=\frac{(a-1/2)\cos p+(b+1/2)\sin p}
                         {\cos p+v\sin p}>0.
\tag{13}
\]
On the core, a<=7/12 and b<=a give the whole-angle bound
\[
                    \beta_1\le1/12+K\tan h<.997.
\tag{14}
\]
This includes curved core; no flatness assumption is
being inferred from H. On the outer circle set X=a+1/2,
Y=b+1/2 and Z_E=X cos p+Y sin p. Then
\[
 \beta_1=X(1-\cos p/Z_E)
    <1.915(1-.7648/1.9788)<1.175.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus C_D-beta_1>0 for either radial kind.

For DE3 under the same EH1 contact, multiply its derivative
by the positive factor (1+v tan p)/cos n. With x=tan n,
y=tan p, the resulting exact expression is
\[
        1-\frac{1-v}{2}(x+y)+(b-va)(x-y)-vxy.
\tag{16}
\]
On the outer arc it is 1-x(1-v+vy)>=1-x>0. On curved
core it is 1-y(1-v+vx)>=1-y>0. On flat core it is
1-(1/2-b)x-(1/2+b)y>=1-max(x,y)>0.
This proves retention of either original negative DE owner
at every original weak contact.

The motion cannot stop at b=0: for n>=0 the actual DE2
projection would be a_D cos n-b_D sin n<1, and the DE3
projection would be a_D-a sin n<1. Both contradict W>=1.
Sorting improves and the contact denominators stay positive.
The flat/curved core derivatives join. Since p-n=H-D>0,
EH alignment is not reached before DE alignment. Therefore
the path reaches actual E=D with p=H-D>0. Every other
state and frame remains fixed during this preparation.

## 5. Positive DE errors are preserved on their entire domain

Now suppose d=theta_E-theta_D>0. Keep E fixed and advance
D/T by decreasing u in (6). This includes E above H and
an original nonpositive EH error; that EH inequality does
not change while E is fixed.

For DE2 put X_D=a_D+1/2, Y_D=b_D+1/2. Its exact
slack derivative with respect to u is
\[
 \frac{L_2'}{\cos d}
 =Y_D-1-rY_D/X_D+\tan d(r-X_D+1)
 <-(2-X_D)(1-\tan d).
\tag{17}
\]
Here r>1, Y_D/X_D>.896>tan h, Y_D<=X_D and X_D<1.5.
Consequently L_2'<-.06 on the full 0<=d<=h interval.

For DE3 the exact derivative, including both half-shifts,
is
\[
 L_3'=-\mathcal A+(a_E-1/2)\cos d+(1/2-b_E)\sin d.
\tag{18}
\]
For a core E, its last two terms are at most
1/12+(sin h)/2<.406<1. Thus every core contact strictly
improves under the forward motion.

For an outer E, first locate all possible contacts.
At fixed d, the DE3 margin increases with b_E on its
sorted q-arc: its derivative is cos d-v sin d>0.
Its minimum occurs at b_E=0, where a_E=a_0(q)>1.4.
Using a_D>.844, for every d>=.18 its margin exceeds
\[
 .344+.9\sin d-.5\cos d
 \ge .344+.9S_7(.18)-.5C_4(.18)>.0132.
\tag{19}
\]
Hence an active outer DE3 contact has d<.18. There
the opposing rate in (18) is at most
\[
 .915\cos d+.5\sin d
 \le .915C_4(.18)+.5S_5(.18)
                         =.98973180942<.990<1.
\tag{20}
\]
The combination increases on [0,.18]. Since A>1,
(18) is strictly negative at every possible contact.
Equations (19)--(20) prove actual DE3 retention on the
whole 0<=d<=h interval by the first-contact principle.
They do not assume a short original DE error.

Stop at the first actual BT2, DE alignment, or H=D.
The last event is impossible while BT2 is unavailable,
by the unchanged-state anchor-order theorem. In particular,
if E>=H, DE alignment cannot precede BT2. If DE aligns
first, it does so with E=D<H and with the positive EH
error less than .22 by (4), since D has only advanced.

If BT2 appears first, the positive movement has made the
actual DE margin strict. For DE2 this follows directly
from (17). For DE3, (19) and the strict inward derivative
at every possible contact prove the same assertion,
including an initially weak contact. Decrease a_D by a
sufficiently small positive amount, keeping b_D fixed.
Strict DE slack absorbs it, aligned TD is unchanged,
and D is strictly sorted after its positive motion.
This records a strict D-circle loss and completes this
alternative. A simultaneous BT2/DE-alignment event has
the same strict DE slack and is handled identically.

## 6. At DE alignment, move D/T/E together

This includes an initially aligned DE pair and the
endpoints of Sections 4--5. Either actual aligned DE
owner has the same expression a_D+b_E>=1. Lower b_E
on its same radial arc to
\[
                              b_E=1-a_D.
\tag{21}
\]
It improves both positive-error EH projections and
preserves the aligned DE inequality until contact.
By (7), 0<b_E<.156. A core E is now flat, and an
outer E is strictly sorted.

Continue the D/T contact motion, carry E with D, and
retain (21). For the forward frame parameter tau,
\[
 a_D'=\mathcal A>1,\quad b_E'=-\mathcal A,
 \quad a_E'=v\mathcal A,
 \quad p=\theta_H-\theta_D,\quad p'=-1.
\tag{22}
\]
While BT2 is unavailable, the order barrier gives p>0;
also p<.22 throughout, by (4). Both actual DE owners
stay aligned contact.

For EH0 the slack derivative is
\[
 \mathcal A+(a_H-1/2)\sin p-(b_H-1/2)\cos p
                                      >\mathcal A-.4>.6.
\tag{23}
\]
For EH1 it is
\[
                     (\cos p+v\sin p)(\mathcal A-\beta_1),
\tag{24}
\]
with beta_1 as in (13). On flat core,
beta_1<1/12+.656(.224)<.231. On the outer arc,
\[
 Y_E<.656,\quad X_E=\sqrt{q-Y_E^2}>1.866,
 \quad X_E<1.915,\quad v<.352,\quad \tan p<.224.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \beta_1=X_E-1+\frac{v\tan p}{1+v\tan p}
                         <.915+.352(.224)=.993848<1.
\tag{25}
\]
Both EH owners thus acquire strict slack along every
positive segment of this coupled motion.

There is no further stopping boundary. The strict cuts
(7) retain T's flat core, D's sorting and containment.
Equation (21) cannot reach b_E=0 while a_D<1. Reaching
p=0 would contradict the anchor-order theorem. At u=0,
BT2 and BT3 coincide. Hence the path reaches an actual
BT2 after positive movement and while EH has strict slack.

Record the strict D loss by lowering a_D by a small
positive epsilon and increasing b_E by epsilon on its
same radial arc. Equation (21) remains exact contact;
strict EH slack tolerates the change. Aligned TD and
BT2 are unchanged. D remains strictly sorted and outside
the true core. Outer E stays on q; core E stays flat and
strictly inside q_L even after the small increase. This
is a strict D-anchor loss at the same actual q.

## 7. Sharp join, weak ties and original radius

The construction leaves H,G,A,B identical. T is positive
on its true flat core; TD remains aligned, so both its
owners are actual. The original HG implication is retained
because G and HG were unchanged. The original DE owner
can be retained even at alignment, where its formula
coincides with the other owner. The original EH owner
has been retained throughout. All weak original contacts
are included in the contact derivative arguments.

Each frame movement lies between original frames:
E's preliminary motion lies between E and D, and D/T
move toward fixed B; after alignment E follows D.
The real-frame range never increases. For original
boundary-neighbor inputs, continuous actual-neighbor
separations and the two-square band retain their cyclic
order and winding, as in the
[boundary-neighbor transport bridge](HAND_G3_BOUNDARY_NEIGHBOR_TRANSPORT_AND_E_ZERO_EXCLUSION.md).

E has stayed on its true core or its actual q-circle.
It therefore satisfies the hard theorem's radial alternative
\[
 a_E\le7/12\quad\text{or}\quad
 a_E\ge\sqrt{q/2}-1/2\ge\max\{b_H,b_D\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The descendant consequently satisfies every hypothesis of
[the arbitrary-sign hard-owner sharp theorem](HAND_G5_ARBITRARY_SIGNS_AND_FRAMES_HARD_OWNERS_SHARP_REDUCTION.md).
It gives q>=q_*. At q=q_* that theorem forces the
stationary D anchor on the q_* circle, contradicting
the strict D-circle loss obtained from every initially
unavailable BT2. Hence the genuine case has q>q_*.

For an original candidate with ceiling below q_L, first
apply the original fixed-witness boundary reduction at
the enlarged actual working ceiling q_L. Its E boundary
representative then has precisely the radial eligibility
used above. An eligible genuine endpoint is impossible
there because q_L<q_*. Merely enlarging the ceiling of
an already constructed descendant is not being used to
assert an outer-circle equality.

This closes the one-negative-BT/GA0 aligned endpoint with
all original G signs and all original mixed witnesses.
It also applies after any first-side primary conversion
that retains these hypotheses and E's radial alternative;
any preexisting H,A,B loss is preserved exactly.
