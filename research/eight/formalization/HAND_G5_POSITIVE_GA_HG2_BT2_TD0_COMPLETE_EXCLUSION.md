# Positive GA1 with HG2, BT2 and arbitrary TD0 is impossible at the sharp radius

## 1. The complete finite entry statement

Let
\[
 q_L=19577/5000,\quad Q_1=1957767069/500000000,
 \quad q_L\le q\le q_*<Q_1,\quad h=.7,
\tag{1}
\]
where q* is the exact hard-chain minimum. Work in the
same-radius boundary-neighbor domain of the
[uniform-radius transport theorem](HAND_G5_UNIFORM_RADIUS_PRIMARY_PAIR_TRANSPORT.md):
actual contained squares, true-core avoidance, one-winding
order, quarter word 0101011, real-frame spread below h,
and both captured variations below h. Every state is on
the true core or its actual outer q boundary. H,A are
positive outer states, B,D negative outer states. G,T may
have either sign and either radial kind. Initially E has
the normalized nonpositive signed tangent; either radial
kind is allowed.

Retain actual witnesses
\[
 HG2,\qquad GA1\text{ at }x>0,\qquad
 AB0\text{ or }AB2,\qquad BT2,\qquad TD0,
\tag{2}
\]
with arbitrary signed errors on HG,AB,BT,TD. Each E edge
retains either actual owner, with arbitrary individual
error signs and arbitrary signed primary total.

**Theorem.** No such boundary-neighbor cycle exists. Thus
this complete positive-GA entry branch forces q>q*.
All comparisons allow weak ties; the strict x>0 hypothesis
is essential when the E-aligned sharp hard configuration
is reached. At x=0 the GA1 projection itself is the actual
GA0 tie, so that case already belongs to the hard-owner
entry target.

The proof below is a finite join of whole-domain statements.
It does not assume that an E-only transport preserves the
normalized E sign, and it does not infer original equality
from a loss at a modified intermediate square.

## 2. Both secondary E owners require no E radial hypothesis

If DE2 and EH0 are both retained, the second regime of the
[sole-DE and all-secondary theorem](HAND_G5_POSITIVE_GA_OUTER_E_SOLE_DE_AND_SECONDARY_EXCLUSION.md)
excludes the necessary system strictly at Q1. That regime
permits either sign and either radial kind of E, arbitrary
signed HG, AB, BT, TD, DE and EH errors, either actual AB
owner, and either G or T sign. Its two E weights are both 1,
so the exact signed E force cancels before any support is
taken. The actual DE2 and EH0 errors remain in the D and H
ordinary blocks, including their possible mixed signs.

Thus this application uses no core-E bound, no E-primary
charge and no E transport. All its hypotheses follow
directly from (1)--(2).

## 3. Core E and negative outer-E primaries

If E is on its true core, the first T row of the complete
[core-E theorem with either E sign](HAND_G5_COUPLED_CORE_E_SIGN_SYMMETRIC_POSITIVE_ENTRY.md)
excludes all four E-owner combinations, every individual
E error sign and both AB owners. Its strict upper ceiling
is Q=9789/2500>Q1, so it applies throughout (1).

Suppose E is outer at the actual q. Each nonpositive
primary E error can be reselected independently at the
unchanged actual state:
\[
 DE3\text{ at }-s\ \Longrightarrow\ DE2\text{ at }-s,
 \qquad EH1\text{ at }-s\ \Longrightarrow\ EH0\text{ at }-s,
 \quad0\le s\le h.
\tag{3}
\]
The [local outer-E comparison](HAND_G3_OUTER_E_NEGATIVE_PRIMARY_WITNESS_COMPARISON.md)
proves these exact projection comparisons. Its outer Q
circle may be replaced everywhere by the same actual q.
For clarity, all bounds in its negative-DE proof remain
uniform: if \(a_q=\sqrt{q-1/4}-.5\) and
\(c_q=\sqrt{q/2}-.5\), then
\[
 a_q>707/500,\qquad c_q<9/10,\qquad
 \tan(h/2)<183/500,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
 q_L-1/4-(957/500)^2=501/250000>0,
\qquad 2(7/5)^2-q>11/2500.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus its sorted-arc support comparison is unchanged. The
negative-EH proof uses the exact outer identity
\(K^2+D^2=q\), containment \(X^2+Z^2\le q\), and the
positive contact-vector square comparison; it has no
additional radius-dependent estimate. This verifies (3)
at q, rather than treating an outer q state as outer at Q.
At s=0 the paired primary and secondary projections are
the same actual weak witness.

After all available reselections (3), every retained E
primary has strictly positive error. No state, frame,
variation or witness at another edge has changed.

## 4. The remaining positive E cases

There are exactly four possibilities after Section 3.

If both E edges are secondary, Section 2 applies.
If the sole primary is EH1, the strict
[EH1/DE2 theorem](HAND_G5_POSITIVE_GA1_POSITIVE_EH1_WITH_TD0_EXCLUSION.md),
with its [signed-coordinate extension](HAND_G5_SOLE_E_POSITIVE_GA_SIGNED_COORDINATE_EXTENSIONS.md),
excludes it. If the sole primary is DE3, E still has its
original nonpositive tangent and the strict
[DE3/EH0 theorem](HAND_G5_POSITIVE_GA_OUTER_E_SOLE_DE_AND_SECONDARY_EXCLUSION.md),
with the same signed-G extension, excludes it.
All these strict ceilings are Q1, and their other ordinary
errors remain arbitrary.

Finally suppose both E primaries are positive. Apply the
[actual same-radius paired-E transport](HAND_G5_POSITIVE_PAIRED_E_SAME_RADIUS_ENDPOINT_REDUCTION.md).
It changes E only, keeps all six other states and all
non-E witnesses, and gives a sorted outer E at the same q.
The E-primary total is unchanged; its frame remains between
D and H; the cyclic gaps and both variations remain valid.
The new E is permitted to have either sign.

At a DE-aligned endpoint, EH1 is the sole E primary and the
signed EH theorem excludes either E sign. At an EH-aligned
endpoint, move E down along its actual outer q circle until
\[
                             y_E=1-a_H.
\tag{6}
\]
The EH0 contact becomes exact and DE3 improves. Sorting,
containment, core avoidance, cyclic order, frames and
variations are verified in Section 6 of the paired-E
transport. If (6) is nonpositive, the sole-DE theorem
applies. Otherwise the endpoint has
\[
 d_{EH}=0,\quad d_{DE}=e>0,\quad y_E>0,
 \qquad a_H+y_E=1.
\tag{7}
\]
The original GA error x>0 remains unchanged, and
\(\tau=x+e\le h\) by the positive-variation bound.

For \(\tau\in[0,.1]\cup[.16,h]\), the strict
[outside-strip theorem](HAND_G5_POSITIVE_GA_EXCEPTIONAL_E_ALIGNED_OUTSIDE_CRITICAL_STRIP.md)
excludes (7) at Q1. For \(.1\le\tau\le.16\), the
[sharp critical-strip theorem](HAND_G5_POSITIVE_GA_EXCEPTIONAL_E_ALIGNED_SHARP_CRITICAL_STRIP.md)
forces x=0 at q<=q*, contradicting (2). These closed
intervals cover every possible tau, including their weak
endpoint ties.

Every radial kind, E-owner choice, E-error sign allocation
and transport endpoint is now covered. The sole sharp
scalar zero in this join has GA aligned, so no positive-GA
case survives at q<=q*.

## 5. Radius and equality bookkeeping

Every constructed E in Section 4 lies on the original
outer q circle. Q1 is used only as an upper ceiling in
strict support inequalities; q* is used as the exact
supporting radius in the critical hard-chain dual.
The auxiliary Q-boundary diagonal contradiction in the
paired transport is not an actual replacement of an anchor
or of the final E state.

The theorem concerns the same-radius domain (1). If this
lemma is used for an original packing with smaller ceiling,
its original fixed-axis radial-boundary construction must
first be performed at a chosen working ceiling at least qL;
merely relabeling pre-existing outer states as outer at qL
would not satisfy the hypotheses. This is the same radius
bookkeeping used by the other global-entry reductions.

At a putative sharp packing q=q*, all x>0 cases are excluded
by this theorem. An x=0 primary is an actual secondary tie
before any E transport is necessary. Consequently this join
introduces no new equality inference from an intermediate
configuration: the original remaining hard-owner problem
retains its actual witnesses for the sharp equality proof.
